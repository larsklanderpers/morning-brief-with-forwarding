**The rule, as amended by Lars on 6 September 2026:**

> **Zero contractions in expository prose. Direct address to the reader is exempt.**

This supersedes the flat "zero contractions outside quoted speech" that had been standing in the
`day-start` house rules and in the panel's voice checklist.

## Why: the evidence that produced the amendment

The Mon 7 Sep piece (`forwarding-address`) came back from his own rv1 edit pass carrying **nine
contractions** where v1 had none: `don't` ×2, `hasn't` ×2, `That's` ×2, `doesn't`, `won't`, `We're`.
Under the old rule that reads as nine violations. It is not. The distribution is the whole finding:

- **All nine sit in direct-address passages** -- the warn-the-reader aside near the top, *"We're barely
  20 lines into this piece,"* the concession that opens the objection section.
- **Not one appears in the expository sections.** Four pages of close document analysis -- the policy,
  the circular, the procurement essentials, the framework update history -- are contraction-free.

That is a register switch, not a typing rate. It is the same move as the length-aside in
[[lars-writing-voice]]: he breaks register to stop being the piece and start being the writer talking
to the reader. Stripping the contractions would have flattened exactly the passages where he is most
present.

## How to apply

- **Never strip a contraction from his own edit pass** to satisfy the old rule. Check where it sits
  first. In direct address it stays.
- **Do not add contractions to expository prose** when drafting for him. The default in the analytical
  register is still zero, and that is where the rule earns its keep -- his document-tracing voice is
  formal on purpose and contractions there read as slack.
- **A panel seat flags the distribution, not the count.** "Nine contractions" is not a finding.
  "Three contractions in the expository middle" is.
- ⚠️ **Mechanical `its` → `it's` typo fixes raise the contraction count.** On this piece seven such
  fixes were applied, which would have taken the total to sixteen. Fixing the possessive typo is
  correct and is not a register decision; count it separately from his deliberate ones, and if he
  ever rules "expand," those seven become `it is` while the deliberate nine stay.
