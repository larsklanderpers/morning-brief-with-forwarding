# Five AI policies every board should require, and no law named anywhere in them

**What it is.** "The Five AI Policies Every Board Should Require", **Victor M. Font Jr.**, LinkedIn
Pulse, **19 September 2026**, filed as **article 8 of 20** in a series called *Governing Intelligence
Before It Governs You*. It tells directors to require an AI Governance Policy, an AI Risk Management
Policy, an AI Acceptable Use Policy, an AI Data Governance Policy, and an AI Third-Party and
Procurement Policy, then gives five questions a board should ask of each: who owns it, how often is
it reviewed, how is compliance measured, what evidence demonstrates implementation, what metrics
reach the board. Its framing line is *"the goal isn't documentation. The goal is governability."*

★ **The finding is what is absent.** Asked directly for every named law, regulation, statute,
regulator, standard or framework in the visible body, the fetch returned **none**. No EU AI Act, no
NIST AI RMF, no ISO/IEC 42001, no SEC, no GDPR, no state statute. The only numbers in the body are
the five policies, the series position and the date. **A five-policy mandate addressed to boards,
answerable to nothing external.**

**The commercial interest, named per the standing rule.** The article closes into the author's own
newsletter, *The Cyber Governance Brief* (1,468 followers), and trails the next installment. The
line *"Governance by Assurance™ enables the board to continuously know and prove whether the
AI-enabled enterprise is actually behaving that way"* came back **attributed to a comment rather
than the body**, and Governance by Assurance™ is his own trademarked methodology; he sells board
governance through CyberGovernanceCenter.com and Victor Font Consulting Group, LLC.

## Observations

- **2026-09-20 - The quiet form of compliance folklore.** [[Ad Hoc Regulatory Regime]] carries the
  pattern as manufactured *facts*: the CLOUD Act agreement dated two years wrong, APRA guidance
  nobody had promised, both traced to firms selling the remedy (see
  [[CLOUD Act Australia - the exposure predates the agreement]], [[APRA CPS 230 voice AI vendors]],
  [[Capability restated as certainty]]). **This one manufactures no facts at all, which is why it is
  harder to argue with.** Nothing in it is false because nothing in it is checkable. The authority
  it runs on is the author's, and the evidence it asks boards to produce is evidence of having
  followed him.

- **2026-09-20 - Every one of the five policies is an internal control at the deploying
  organization.** The only one facing outward is the third-party and procurement policy, and its
  ask is "contractual protections", unspecified. `References/research_incident-label_2026-09-10.md`
  read the five major vendor contracts and found the notification trigger is a breach of the
  *vendor's* security affecting customer data, with "malfunction", "misalignment" and "harmful
  output" appearing in none of them. **So the board is told to govern the vendor through a clause
  that, as written in the market, does not reach the behaviour it is being asked to govern.** That
  is Fri 18's finding arriving from the buyer's side, in a document that does not know it.

- **2026-09-20 - Same shape as the government instruments, without the government.**
  [[UK AI Risk Management Toolkit - guidance, not a gate]] is a method with no stated requirement;
  [[NSW mandates AI registration and assessment across government]] is the same spreadsheet made a
  gate. This is the third position: a method with no issuing body at all, sold directly to the
  people who would have to comply with the other two. Neither ISO/IEC 42001 nor NIST AI RMF is
  named here either, which is the same gap the UK toolkit has.

- **2026-09-20 - It sells governability, and the thread next door found that capability and
  confidence come apart.** [[MIT's GenAI sprint - capability and confidence sold as one product]]
  is six hours and $1,750 with no assessment; this is twenty articles and a newsletter with no
  citation. Different price, same instrument: **a credential for judgment, issued by the seller.**
  [[Ability in AI adoption is judgment, not operation]] is the finding both of them run past.
