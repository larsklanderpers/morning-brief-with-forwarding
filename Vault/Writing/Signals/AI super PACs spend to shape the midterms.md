# AI super PACs spend to shape the midterms — OpenAI vs Anthropic, by proxy

**What happened.** AI-industry super PACs have poured $43.3M into 2026 congressional races.
OpenAI-aligned **Leading the Future** (Andreessen Horowitz + OpenAI president Greg Brockman
money) has raised $75M+ and spent $23.5M through a network including Think Big and American
Mission. Anthropic put $20M into **Public First Action** (Feb 2026), whose affiliated PACs
(Jobs and Democracy, Defending Our Values) spent $16.6M across North Carolina, Texas and Utah.
The two camps hold opposite positions: Leading the Future opposes "policies that stifle
innovation" and pushes *national* AI standards to head off a state-level "chaotic patchwork";
Public First opposes "federal efforts to freeze state progress without adequate federal
safeguards." Meta and Google are also funding state-race PACs. Test case: NY-12, where
AI-safety candidate Alex Bores (co-sponsor of New York's RAISE Act, ex-Palantir) drew heavy
anti-Bores spending from the OpenAI network — and lost the 23 June primary to Micah Lasher,
**39%–35%**. (Lobbying context: 2025 saw OpenAI, Meta, Google and Nvidia spend a combined
$50.9M; Anthropic quadrupled its Q1 lobbying to $1.56M, OpenAI nearly doubled to $1.02M.)

**Why it matters.** This is the Ad Hoc Regulatory Regime thread's cleanest evidence yet that
AI regulation is being shaped *upstream of any statute* — by who gets "at the table," in Issue
One's Michael Beckel's phrase. The striking part is that the fight is intramural: Molly White's
line is that the spending "really mirrors the corporate competition between OpenAI and
Anthropic." The national-standards-vs-state-patchwork split is the federal-moratorium fight
relocated from the floor of Congress to the primaries. For a regulated-industry reader it is a
warning that the rules they will answer to are being priced in campaign-ad markets, not drafted
in the open. And the Bores result is a genuinely ambiguous data point — the targeted safety
candidate lost, but by four points in a crowded field, so "the money swung it" is unproven;
Katie Harbath's "real experiment to see if that sort of money can sway races" is still running.
