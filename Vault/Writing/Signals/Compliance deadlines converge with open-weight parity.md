# Compliance deadlines compress exactly as capable on-prem AI democratises

**What happened.** Four regulatory dates stack up — APRA CPS 230 in effect, Australian
mandatory AI Standards announced 15 July, EU AI Act fully applicable 2 August, OAIC
automated-decision disclosure 10 December — in the same window GLM-5.2 and the 27 July
Kimi K3 weights cross the threshold where local inference stops being a compromise.

**Why it matters.** This is the thesis the whole AI Sovereignty thread has been building
toward, and it is now a dated argument rather than a directional one. The capability
excuse for cloud AI expired at roughly the moment the compliance cost of cloud AI spiked.
The uncomfortable half: the open weights closing the gap are largely Chinese-origin, so
"bring it home" needs an answer on provenance and governance norms, not just latency and
cost.

## Observations

- **2026-07-20** — Framed as the AI brief's synthesis. Consumed in part by *The Compliance Tool Nobody Ordered Just Shipped as a Model* (Mon 20 Jul) and *Bring the Model Home* (Fri 24 Jul, scheduled).
- **2026-07-21** — The thesis has evolved into something better: not "deadlines converge with capability" but **the gap between AI capability and deployable governance is widening**. China shipped the most capable open-weight model on record, freely downloadable and unusable by anyone who cannot document provenance; Australia mandated governance with a 10 Dec ADM obligation that is an engineering problem wearing legal clothing; the EU high-risk deadline is 12 days out; FLI confirms no lab clears C+; and Visa has handed purchasing authority to agents. Firms that navigate it built the governed record and made a documented choice about which weights they trust with which data. The rest are accruing **governance debt that converts to penalty exposure**. That phrase is the keeper.
