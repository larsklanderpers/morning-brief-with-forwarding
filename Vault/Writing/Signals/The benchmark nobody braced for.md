# The benchmark nobody braced for — and the one sector that went the wrong way

**What happened.** The BLS **preliminary benchmark revision to total nonfarm employment for March
2026**, released **28 Aug 2026, 10:00am ET**, is **−79,000 (−0.1%)**. Private employment −178,000.

**Why that is the story.** Last year's preliminary benchmark was **−911,000 (−0.6%)** — the number
that drove a full news cycle about the payroll survey being broken. The **ten-year absolute average
is 0.2%**. This year's is **half the average and a fraction of last year's.** The benchmark
re-anchors the entire employment level to the Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages, so it is the
most authoritative instrument in the series — **and it says the payroll counts have been close to
right.**

## ★ The sector split is the load-bearing part

**Revised UP:** transportation and warehousing · **information** · financial activities ·
construction · government
**Revised DOWN:** retail trade · education and health services · manufacturing · business services

**If AI were eating white-collar and technology work at macro scale, `information` is precisely the
sector you would expect the benchmark to mark down. It was marked up.** The downward revisions are
concentrated in retail and education/health — demand and public funding, not automation.

## Why this is not a new thesis

The corpus has already published this paradox twice, in nearly these words —

*"Record gross cuts and rising net employment, both true at once."* Tech employment up 47,000 in

**This is the next data point in an argument already made, which is a far stronger position than
treating it as a discovery.**

## ⚠️ Non-negotiables

- **The counterweight travels with the number.** Economists surveyed by Bloomberg expected payrolls
  revised **UP by 183,000**. The print missed consensus by ~262,000 to the downside. **Small in
  absolute terms, weaker than expected — both, or neither.**
- **Preliminary benchmark revisions have been downward in seven of the last eight years.** A small
  downward revision is the normal direction, not a surprise of direction.
- 🚩 **Do NOT build on "the BLS was wrong."** A small benchmark is the BLS being **right**. The
  interesting sentence is that the correction everyone braced for did not arrive.
- 🚩 **NEVER conflate the benchmark with monthly revisions.** They are different instruments —
  monthly revisions come from late survey responses and seasonal re-factoring; the benchmark
  re-anchors the level to QCEW once a year for a March reference month. The 28 Aug brief welded them
  and, in doing so, missed this release entirely. See [[Brief Claim Provenance]].
- ⚠️ **The −79,000 is currently footed to two independent secondaries** (Yahoo Finance and Brisk
  Markets, both 28 Aug), because `prebmk.nr0.htm` still served the September 2025 edition when
  fetched. **Re-check the primary before anything ships.**
- **No correction is owed on the two published pieces.** Their 47,000 is *tech occupations*, a
  different series from total nonfarm, and is untouched by the monthly revisions. Checked
  deliberately — the adjacency looks alarming and is not.
