# Kotter superseded his own 8-step model in 2014

**What happened.** John Kotter published *Accelerate: Building Strategic Agility for a Faster-Moving
World* (HBR Press, 2014; preceded by the HBR article "Accelerate!", Nov 2012), replacing the 1996
eight-stage process with eight **accelerators**. The change is not cosmetic. From Kotter Inc's own
2018 eBook, verbatim:

| Original 8-Step (1996) | Enhanced / 8 Accelerators (2014→) |
|---|---|
| "Respond to or effect **episodic change in finite and sequential ways**." | "**Run the steps concurrently and continuously.**" |
| "Focus on doing one thing very well in a **linear fashion over time**." | An ongoing strategic capability, not a project. |
| "Drive change with **a small, powerful core group**." | "Form **a large volunteer army** from up, down, and across the organization." |

Plus the **dual operating system**: the hierarchy stays, because it is critical for day-to-day
operations; alongside it runs "a constantly innovating network." Step 1 becomes "Create a sense of
urgency around **a Big Opportunity**" — "a window of opportunity that is open today but may close
tomorrow," rather than a burning platform. Step 4 becomes "**Enlist a volunteer army**."

**Why it matters.** Almost every "Kotter's 8 Steps" training deck in circulation teaches the 1996
version and presents the *sequence* as the point. Teaching that as current Kotter means teaching a
model its author publicly superseded twelve years ago — and, for AI specifically, teaching the
*wrong* one. The 2014 model was built for change that never resolves, which is exactly the condition
AI adoption creates: the correct practice at 12 months is wrong at 18, so "anchor it in the culture"
aims at a moving target and reinforcing last quarter's discipline is fossilisation, not sustainment.

The shadow-AI economy **is** a volunteer army that nobody enlisted. Kotter-2014 is the only thing
either canonical author offers that has a move for it.

## Observations

- **2026-08-16** — first seen. Kotter Inc's own framing hedges: "Both versions are relevant and
  effective today, yet are designed for different contexts + objectives." Note also that **no
  published empirical validation of the dual operating system could be located** — the 2014 model is
  better-shaped for the problem, not better-evidenced.
