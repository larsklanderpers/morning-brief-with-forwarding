# Council of Europe AI Treaty: signed by 20, ratified by one, in force nowhere

**What happened.** Marc Rotenberg of CAIDP (3 Sep) makes the case that the Council of Europe
Framework Convention on AI (CETS 225) proves global AI governance "has already begun." The
Treaty Office chart, checked 6 Sep at the primary, gives the actual state: 20 state signatures,
one ratification (the EU, 15 May 2026), five ratifications needed including three Council
members, so **not in force**. Zero member states have ratified. Germany, France, Italy, Spain,
the Netherlands and Poland have not signed individually; they shelter under the EU's
ratification. **Australia took part in the negotiation and has neither signed nor ratified.**
Rotenberg's own numbers are accurate; the framing is the advocacy.

**Why it matters.** Two things for the pipeline. First, it is the cleanest example yet of the
Ad Hoc pattern from the other direction: the one instrument that is actually a treaty binds
public bodies, leaves private actors to each government's discretion, excludes defence, qualifies
national security, and relies on a monitoring mechanism that "will depend heavily on the
seriousness with which governments approach implementation." The binding thing is the least
binding thing in the stack. Second, the sovereignty line is a direct foil for this Substack's
inference-sovereignty argument: Rotenberg says sovereignty is "strengthened, not diminished, by
international rules" and the alternative is "private governance by a small number of
multinational technology companies whose technical decisions become global policy by default."
That second half is the thesis of [[You cannot rent an audit trail]] in different clothes; the
first half is the part a piece could argue with, because a treaty in force nowhere strengthens
nobody's sovereignty yet.

**Named interest.** CAIDP has supported the convention's adoption and ratification (author bio).
Treat the article as a brief for the treaty, and cite the Treaty Office chart for status.
