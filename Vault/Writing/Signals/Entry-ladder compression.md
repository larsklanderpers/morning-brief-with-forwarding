# Entry-ladder compression — and the case that it is overstated

*Merged from two flagged items (the practitioner data and the counter-evidence); they are
one argument seen from both ends.*

**The pessimistic read.** Entry-level postings down 35% over 18 months, sharpest in
writing, coding, support and analysis. Unemployment among 22–27 college graduates at 5.6%,
near a decade high. Workers in the most AI-exposed occupations down 16% relatively.
MIT Tech Review: "a looming crisis in entry-level work."

**The counter-evidence.** Altman and Amodei have both walked back earlier displacement
predictions. Yale Budget Lab found no meaningful change in unemployment for AI-exposed
workers since ChatGPT launched. WEF projects 170M new roles against 92M displaced by 2030
— net +78M. Ramp's 21,599-firm study found heavy AI adopters *grew* headcount 10%, and
entry-level 12%.

**Why it matters.** The macro numbers and the entry-level numbers are both true, which is
why the debate stays stuck. Aggregate employment holds; the on-ramp narrows. The
unresolved question is not "will there be jobs" but how the next cohort accumulates the
experience that historically fed senior roles — because every archetype worth having
assumes domain judgment that used to be built on the bottom rung. Meanwhile 85% of
graduating seniors use AI tools and AI-skilled workers earn 28–56% more, so the
compensating mechanism may be that the rung is being skipped rather than climbed.

## Verified 2026-07-20 — the numbers were stale and the entry-level story inverts

PwC's 2026 Barometer (primary, 15 Jun, 1bn+ ads across 27 countries) puts the AI skills wage
premium at **62%**, up from 57% — so the brief's "28–56%" is last year's figure at the top end
and an unattributed number at the bottom. Range by sector: **16% in government, 118% in
consumer markets**.

**The finding that cuts against this note's own framing:** PwC analysed 2.4m US entry-level
jobs and found AI-exposed entry-level roles are 7x more likely to demand traditionally senior
skills — and that those "seniorised" entry-level roles **grew 35% since 2019 while other
entry-level roles shrank 10%**. The bottom rung is not disappearing. It is being seniorised,
and nobody has rebuilt the apprenticeship that used to produce the judgement it now demands.
PwC's Pete Brown says exactly this: "AI is removing some of the routine work that once acted
as an apprenticeship."

**Still unverified:** the "6% premium at entry level vs 70%+ at senior" figure — the single
most useful number for the pessimistic case — is **not in the PwC release**. It appears only in
secondary write-ups with no page citation. Pull the full Barometer PDF before using it.
