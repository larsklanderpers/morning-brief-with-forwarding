# ADKAR — the one external test came back null

**What happened.** ADKAR (Jeff Hiatt, Prosci; book 2006) is marketed as research-based. The research
is house data: Prosci's *Best Practices in Change Management* series, 12 studies since 1998, with
participants **"recruited through thousands of online media outlets plus 90,000 members of Prosci's
Change Management Learning Center."** That is an open-invitation convenience sample of self-selected
change practitioners self-reporting on their own projects, with no sampling frame, no response rate,
and no independent verification. The headline correlations (poor change management → 13% met
objectives; excellent → 88%) are **correlational, same-respondent, and never independently
replicated** — the same person rates both the change-management quality and whether objectives were
met.

Nothing in the benchmarking tests ADKAR's five elements, their order, or the cumulative logic. The
sequential claim is a design assumption presented as a finding.

**The one quantitative external test located:** Caekebeke et al. (2021), "Coaching Belgian and Dutch
Broiler Farmers Aimed at Antimicrobial Stewardship and Disease Prevention," *Antibiotics* 10(5), 590.
Thirty farms, 13 months, farmers profiled with an adjusted ADKAR instrument then coached. ADKAR
scores rose; biosecurity improved ~6%; antimicrobial use fell ~7%. And: **"Despite these
improvements, no significant association could be found between higher ADKAR scores and lower
AMU."** The authors conclude further investigation into sociological models is needed.

**Why it matters.** n=30 is not dispositive. But it is the only attempt on the table to measure
ADKAR scores against an independently observed behavioural outcome, and it came back null.

**The keeper.** ADKAR's barrier point — the first element scoring ≤3, and the rule that you do not
fix what is downstream of it — is genuinely the best cheap instrument in the field for "should this
money go to comms, training, or incentives?" Training a workforce with a Desire barrier is the most
reliable way to waste a training budget. Keep the diagnostic; drop the claim that it is validated.

**Two category errors worth naming.** ADKAR is one of **three** Prosci models (with the 3-Phase
Process and the PCT triangle); saying "we use ADKAR" and meaning "we use Prosci" is a category
error. And **ADKAR contains no leadership at all** — sponsorship lives in the other two models,
which is exactly why teaching ADKAR alone is a mistake.
