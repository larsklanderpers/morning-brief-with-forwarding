# Hassabis calls for a FINRA-style AI standards body

**What happened.** Alongside the Gemini 3.5 Pro launch (17 July, 2M-token context, Deep
Think reasoning layer), DeepMind's CEO proposed a US-led AI standards body modelled on
FINRA — independent testing of frontier models plus a coordinated industry slowdown
mechanism if risks escalate.

**Why it matters.** FINRA is self-regulatory, funded by the industry it oversees, and
carries enforcement power. Proposing that template means regulated AI companies could
set the standards they are then measured against. That is not obviously bad — securities
self-regulation has a real track record — but it is a specific and consequential choice
dressed as a neutral one. For AU and EU regulators still forming their frameworks, a US
self-regulatory body becomes a competing centre of gravity, and one their own firms will
cite as sufficient.

## Observations

- **2026-07-20** — First seen. Note the timing: proposed while the EU AI Act's binding conformity-assessment regime is two weeks from full application. A voluntary body and a statutory one arriving together creates dual compliance tracks.

## Verified 2026-07-20 — analogy holds up, but the story is bigger than Hassabis

FINRA's design flaws are documented from inside the regulator, not just by outside critics.
SEC Commissioner **Hester Peirce**: FINRA "wields governmental powers without the procedural
and disclosure requirements by which a government regulatory agency would be constrained" —
no APA notice-and-comment, no FOIA, no congressional appropriations oversight, no cost-benefit
requirement. It is also a monopoly by construction: formed in 2007 from the NASD/NYSE
regulatory merger, leaving one self-regulator with no competitive discipline.

**Material correction to the framing:** this is not just a lab CEO floating an idea. Forbes
(19 Jul) reports the **Trump administration** is weighing a FINRA-like body to vet advanced
models before public release. "Administration considers it, lab CEO publicly supportive" is a
different and better piece than "Hassabis proposes."

**Handle with care:** the sharpest critique comes from Mercatus and POGO, and the Forbes author
is Mercatus-affiliated. The facts check out; the conclusion ("use voluntary standards bodies
instead") is a policy preference. Cite the mechanism, not the verdict.

## Open

- The strongest line for a regulated-industry audience: a single gatekeeper concentrates risk, and an approver who greenlights a harmful model faces hearings while one who delays a beneficial model faces nothing. That asymmetry is the argument.
