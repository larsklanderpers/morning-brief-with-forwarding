# Governing by order while the statute count falls

**What it is.** The 119th Congress has enacted **111 public laws** (latest PL 119-111, 18 Sep 2026),
**34 of them in 2026**, which is the lowest midterm-year total since at least 1990. The House closed
on **16 September** for a seven-week recess and is gone until after the 3 November election. Over
the same stretch the second Trump administration has signed **284 executive orders** (Federal
Register API, 22 Sep 2026; newest EO 14430, 17 Sep), which is more than Obama signed in eight years
and runs at roughly **171 a year, the highest annual rate since FDR**.

★ **Why this belongs to this thread rather than to a politics file.** Everything else here is
regulation arriving sideways from a party with no standing to write it: a self-regulator proposed by
the industry, a state building machinery the Commonwealth declined to build, a vendor manufacturing
a compliance fact, a lab publishing the gauge it is measured against. **This is the same mechanism
at the top of the system, where the party does have standing.** The instrument that binds is the one
a single office can issue alone, and the instrument that requires agreement has largely stopped
being issued. Every downstream improvisation in this thread is happening in the space that leaves.

**The sharpest version is the smallest.** [[Five board AI policies and no law named]] found an author
telling boards to require five AI policies while naming no law at all. Part of why that is even
possible is that on the federal side there increasingly is no law to name, only an order, which the
next office holder can revoke on day one. An obligation created by order has a different half-life
from one created by statute, and nothing in the corporate governance literature prices that
difference.

## ⛔ What does not survive checking

The item that prompted this got the shape right and three numbers wrong, all in the same direction.
Full working in `References/research_congress-laws-vs-executive-orders_2026-09-22.md`.

- ⛔ **"Most executive orders since FDR" is false unqualified.** Truman signed **907**, Eisenhower
  **484**. It is true only as a **rate**: 171/yr against Truman's 117 and Obama's 35.
- ⛔ **"Fewest laws since Jefferson" / "go back to 1807" does not hold.** The 10th Congress
  (1807-1809) passed about **104 public acts** in its full two years. The 119th is at 111 with three
  and a half months left, so it has already passed more.
- ⛔ **Jefferson's "4 executive orders" is not the same unit as Trump's 284.** EO numbering began in
  **1907**, back-catalogued only to **1862**. Everything pre-Lincoln is a reconstruction and an
  undercount. Never set the two side by side without that sentence.
- ⚠️ **"Congress adjourned until the midterms" is a House fact generalised.** The Senate sat on
  **22 September**.

## ⛔ And the objection that binds any use of it

**Counting laws is a contested measure of legislating, and this cuts both ways.** AEI argues the
metric is simply wrong; the Library of Congress's own material notes that Congress has enacted
roughly **4 to 6 million words of law per two-year Congress since the Second World War**, with the
falling bill count reflecting **consolidation into omnibus vehicles**. Meanwhile Speaker Johnson has
called this the **most productive Congress in 80 years**, which Snopes checked and found wanting.
**Both sides are reaching for the metric that suits them**, which is the observation worth having
and is standing accuracy rule 5 in the wild.

Truman's own label is the cleanest illustration: he called a Congress that passed **906 laws** the
Do Nothing Congress, because it refused *his* programme. The label was always about priorities, not
output.
