# Klarna's 700 agents — the canonical data point, and what Klarna actually said

**What happened.** Reddit still circulates "AI replaced 700 Klarna customer service agents" as the canonical
displacement datum, with the correction that headcount barely moved and contractors were cut, not staff.
Checked against the primary, **the correction is right and the headline was never Klarna's claim.**

**Klarna's own words, 27 February 2024:** *"It is doing the equivalent work of 700 full-time agents."*
**Equivalent work.** The release makes **no layoff claim at all**. "Klarna fired 700 people" is a media
construction — Fast Company's headline welded the AI announcement onto Klarna's **separate 2022 layoff
round**.

The rest of the release, which nobody quotes: 2.3 million conversations in month one, two-thirds of all
chats; resolution time **11 minutes → under 2**; **25% drop in repeat inquiries**; satisfaction *"on par
with human agents"*; 35+ languages, 23 markets; **$40M USD projected profit improvement for 2024**.

## The walkback, in his own words

**Sebastian Siemiatkowski, 8 May 2025, at Klarna's Stockholm HQ:**

> *"As cost unfortunately seems to have been a too predominant evaluation factor when organising this,
> what you end up having is **lower quality**."*

He said the company should be *"really investing in the quality of the human support"*, and Klarna began
recruiting human agents again on a remote, on-demand basis — reported as an *"Uber type of setup."*

**Headcount (REPORTED, CNBC 14 May 2025):** workforce down **~40%, ~5,000 (2022) → ~3,000**, which
Siemiatkowski attributed substantially to **a hiring freeze and natural attrition, not mass layoffs**, with
AI enabling it. Later reporting has a ~2,000 target by 2030.
**The contractor-vs-staff split has no primary source** — the 700-equivalent referred to volume handled by
outsourced CS capacity, but no Klarna document states the split. **Do not assert it.**

## The fair statement of both sides

> Klarna's AI did the work of 700 agents — Klarna's own words, February 2024. Fourteen months later its CEO
> said optimising for cost had produced "lower quality" and started hiring humans back. What Klarna never
> actually said was that it fired 700 people.

**Why it matters.** This is the single most-cited displacement anecdote in the discourse, and it is a
**quality story wearing a headcount story's clothes.** The reversal was not "AI couldn't do it" — the AI
handled two-thirds of chats and cut resolution time by 80%. The reversal was that **cost was the wrong
evaluation function**, which is a management failure, not a capability limit. That is the constructive read
and it is also the more useful one for a regulated-industry reader deciding what to automate.
