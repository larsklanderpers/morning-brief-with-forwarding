# The AI pilot-failure numbers do not survive scrutiny

**What happened.** Three figures dominate every AI-adoption deck in circulation. None is safe.

**MIT NANDA "95% of GenAI pilots return zero."** *The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025*,
Challapally, Pease, Raskar & Chari, MIT Project NANDA, cover date July 2025, circulated from 18 Aug
2025, distributed as **"v0.1."** Method, verbatim: a review of 300+ publicly disclosed initiatives,
structured interviews with 52 organisations, and **153 survey responses "collected across four major
industry conferences."** Its own limitations note: *"These figures are directionally accurate based
on individual interviews rather than official company reporting."*

Its problems: not peer-reviewed, explicitly preliminary; the 153 responses are a convenience sample
of AI-conference attendees; the funnel measures **custom/task-specific tools**, while the same report
says general-purpose LLMs show ~83% pilot-to-implementation — the executive summary generalises a
narrow measurement into a universal claim; and it **cannot hold its own denominators steady** (2 of 8
sectors in the summary vs two of nine in §3.1; 50% of budgets to sales and marketing in one takeaway
and ~70% two paragraphs later). Plus the structural conflict: the diagnosis is that AI fails because
systems do not retain feedback or adapt — and NANDA is MIT's agentic-architecture project.

**Verdict:** *contested* rather than *unexamined*. It discloses its method, which puts it ahead of
most of the field. Not a number to put unqualified on a slide.

**IDC/Lenovo "88% of POCs never reach production."** Lenovo *CIO Playbook 2025*, research by IDC,
Feb 2025 — **no sample size disclosed**. Then the same vendor, same research partner, same annual
series, twelve months later: *CIO Playbook 2026*, **n=3,120** decision-makers, fieldwork Sept–Oct
2025 — **"Nearly half (46%) of AI proof-of-concepts have already progressed into production."**
88% didn't advance → 46% have advanced. Either the world changed radically or the two measure
different things with different denominators. **Both readings disqualify the 88%.**

Note also how it drifts in transit: IDC said 88% of *POCs* don't reach *widescale deployment*;
intermediaries render it as 88% of *pilots* never reaching *production*, a stronger claim.

**Gartner "over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by 2027."** Filed under Gartner's own
label **"Strategic Planning Assumption"** — a house term for a forward-looking prediction. An
independent investigation that obtained the primary note (*Avoid Agentic AI Failure*, analyst
Anushree Verma, 15 May 2025) reports it contains **no survey, no sample size, no case count, no
citation and no calculation**; the only nearby data is a January 2025 poll of 3,412 webinar
attendees measuring *investment posture*, not cancellation. Treat that investigation as a
well-documented allegation rather than settled fact — it is checkable only by a Gartner subscriber —
but the distinction it draws is the right one.

**Why it matters.** The pattern is the story: a directional observation acquires a decimal point,
loses its denominator in transit, and arrives on a slide as a fact. Same mechanism as
[[The 70% change-failure statistic has no source]], one technology cycle later. What survives
scrutiny instead: the **US Census Bureau BTOS** — biweekly, nationally representative, published
methodology — showing AI use at 17–20% of US businesses, 37% for firms with 250+ employees,
Information sector 39.7% (Dec 2025–May 2026). Note the **November 2025 question-wording change**
from AI use "in producing goods or services" to "in any business function" if trending it.
