# Watson for Oncology — the marketing outran the artefact

**What happened.** STAT's reporters sat in on an MSK/IBM bladder-cancer training session and
interviewed dozens of doctors, IBM executives and AI experts. Findings:

- **MD Anderson spent >$60M over three years**, much of it on outside consultants, before shelving
  the project, which "disintegrated amid internal allegations of overspending, delays, and
  mismanagement." A subsequent audit flagged procurement violations.
- **The training mechanism is the load-bearing finding.** MSK physicians manually fed Watson how
  patients *should* be treated. The training *"does not teach Watson to base its recommendations on
  the **outcomes** of these patients, whether they lived, or died."* It encodes a handful of
  physicians' treatment preferences — "Memorial Sloan Kettering in a portable box."
- *"It is by design that there is not one independent, third-party study that examines whether
  Watson for Oncology can deliver."* Published work is conference abstracts showing **concordance**
  with doctors — which proves competence at applying existing care, not improvement on it.
- **50+ hospital agreements across five continents; only a few dozen actual adoptions.** A Danish
  hospital dropped it after **33% concordance** with local doctors, citing over-weighting of
  American studies.

Dr. Marisa Kollmeier, in the observed training session: *"This is the hard part of this whole game.
There's a lack of evidence."*

**Why it matters.** The gap between what was sold and what was built is the adoption failure, and
the mechanism — training on expert preference rather than measured outcome — is exactly the trap
that recurs whenever an organisation encodes its own current practice and calls it intelligence.
Pair with [[Amazon's recruiting engine - the training data was the change]]: same failure, opposite
data source. One encoded its history; the other encoded its experts.

**Two caveats.** The famous **"unsafe and incorrect"** treatment recommendations phrase is **not
from this piece** — it comes from Ross & Swetlitz, 25 July 2018, built on leaked IBM internal
slides. People conflate them constantly. And this 2017 piece claims *absence of evidence of
benefit*, **not proof of harm**; do not let the modal verb slide. IBM sold Watson Health to
Francisco Partners in 2022 — present as history, not current state.
