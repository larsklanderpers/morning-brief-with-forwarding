# Anthropic's Australian capacity tender and the copyright ask

**What happened.** Two real reporting threads, both originating with the **AFR**, both syndicated:

1. **The tender (5–7 Jul 2026).** From confidential tender documents: Anthropic seeking **1.4 GW of
   Australian data-centre capacity**, costed at **as much as US$15 billion**, minimum **1 GW live by
   end-2027**. Documents went to CDC Data Centres, AirTrunk, NextDC, Iren and Stack. Preferred route is
   **partnering with a developer**, possibly split across four or five providers — **not** Anthropic-owned
   construction.
2. **The FOI (13 Jul 2026).** **AFP** ran Treasury briefing notes released under FOI, prepared for Treasurer
   Jim Chalmers ahead of an April 2026 meeting with Dario Amodei. The notes record that investment in AI
   model development capability and associated infrastructure **is contingent on clarity of copyright
   settings**, that Amodei sought meetings on "barriers to AI training in Australia, particularly copyright
   reform", and that Anthropic argued a **"long tail"** of smaller rights holders makes individual licensing
   impractical. **Officials rejected the position**, advising that training requires permission via voluntary
   licensing, not a TDM exception.

**Why it matters.** Investment-as-leverage on a sovereignty question, documented not by a press release but
by the government's own briefing notes. That is a better artefact than anything a vendor would publish.

## Three fixes the brief needs

- **The number.** The primary figure is **US$15 billion**. **A$21.6bn is a derived conversion**
  (US$15bn ÷ 0.694), evidently the publication's own arithmetic. Downstream outlets have already muddled it
  — some render A$21.6bn, some US$15bn, one inflated it to "$150 billion." **Write "US$15 billion (roughly
  A$21.6 billion)" or use no number at all.** Bare "$21.6 billion" is a laundered conversion.
- **It is a capacity tender, not a committed build.** "Anthropic is building $21.6bn of Australian
  infrastructure" overstates it.
- **The attribution.** The copyright link is **Treasury's characterisation in its own briefing notes**, not
  an Anthropic statement. **Anthropic did not respond to AFP's request for comment.** Write it that way.
- **Cut the EU/UK parallel.** No documented basis for Anthropic conditioning EU or UK investment on
  copyright reform. The UK consultation submissions that drew scrutiny were **OpenAI's and Google's**. As
  written it is an inference dressed as a reported fact — see [[Capability restated as certainty]].

## The policy state, which is solid and holds regardless

"Canberra offers no broad TDM exception" is **correct, and understated** — Canberra has refused one
repeatedly. **Oct 2025:** AG Michelle Rowland ruled out a TDM exception. **19 Dec 2025:** the Productivity
Commission's **final** report did *not* recommend one, proposing a three-year wait-and-see (its **interim**
report had floated it — do not conflate the two). The Government rejected even that, saying it "does not
intend to introduce a text and data mining exception" and is consulting **CAIRG** on statutory licensing,
collective licensing, or an enhanced voluntary system. No model chosen.

Albanese, 15 Jul 2026, verbatim:

> "No company should use Australian books, music, art or news to build or train AI without the artist's
> control. That includes the artist's control of the price and value of their work. Anything less, is theft."

> "Australian creators must retain ownership and control of their work. Our laws will spell that out, plain
> as day."

He also conceded: *"No country has got this right yet."* He reportedly rejected a **"shared creative fund"**
alternative attributed to Anthropic.
