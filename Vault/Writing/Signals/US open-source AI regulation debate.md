# US open-source AI regulation debate heats up

**What happened.** A Dean Ball post on regulating open-source AI drew heavy criticism;
Machine Thinking's response ("Who's Afraid of the Big Bad Weights?") argues most of the
discourse misses the point — weights are about to be more capable than anything previously
downloadable, and there is **no export control regime for weights equivalent to the one for
chips**.

**Why it matters.** The chip-control analogy is the useful frame. Export controls on hardware
work because hardware is physical, countable, and shippable. Weights are none of those things,
so any control regime has to operate on distribution rather than possession — which is a
censorship-shaped problem, not a trade-shaped one. For Australia the downstream effect is
direct: a US regime restricting weights would hit sovereign deployments here that assume open
models stay available.
