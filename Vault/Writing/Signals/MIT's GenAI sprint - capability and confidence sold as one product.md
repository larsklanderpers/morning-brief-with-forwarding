# MIT's GenAI sprint - capability and confidence sold as one product

**What it is.** MIT Sloan Executive Education sells a **Generative AI Business Sprint**: **$1,750**,
**six hours**, self-paced and asynchronous, complete inside a 30-day window, stacking toward the
**Digital Business Executive Certificate**. Five named faculty across MIT Sloan and EECS, Paul
McDonagh-Smith as Faculty Director. The artefacts at the end are a completion workbook and a
**certificate of completion**. No assessment is mentioned anywhere in the brochure or on the
course page.

**The sentence that makes it a signal.** The audience section closes by inviting anyone who wants
to *"build capability and confidence in applying generative AI in your work."* **Those are sold as
one thing, and the evidence in this thread is that they are the two things that came apart.**
[[Ability in AI adoption is judgment, not operation]]: METR's developers were 19% slower with AI
and still believed, afterwards, that they had been 20% faster. Confidence was not a proxy for
capability; it moved in the opposite direction. A six-hour asynchronous course can raise the
second reliably. Whether anything raises the first in six hours is the question nobody is selling
an answer to.

**Which is the thread's open question, now with a price on it.** *Adoption and Change* asks whether
calibrated scepticism can be certified at all, *"or is that just a training-completion rate with
better branding?"* Here is the best-branded instance available: the seller is MIT, the curriculum
is four questions (what it is, how it works, when to apply it, where it is heading), and the
credential is attendance. **Knowledge is what this teaches, and knowledge was never the barrier -
the interface is a text box.** The barrier is knowing when the output is wrong, and that is
measured in output quality, not in a completed module.

**Use it carefully, because the honest reading is not "MIT is selling snake oil."** The course
promises orientation and delivers orientation; the brochure makes **no** adoption statistic, **no**
productivity claim and **no** outcome claim of any kind. The only numbers in it are the price, the
hours and the window. **That restraint is itself the exhibit**: the strongest possible seller of
executive AI training does not claim a measured effect, and the market buys the certificate
anyway. The commercial interest is named by the artefact itself, which is the cleanest form of the
standing rule.

★ **The reusable move:** when an organisation's answer to the Ability barrier is a training line
item, ask what the certificate certifies. If the answer is hours elapsed, the barrier point has
not been touched, and the dashboard will go green exactly as it does in
[[Workslop - what adoption without reinforcement produces]].
