# Ability in AI adoption is judgment, not operation

**What happened.** Three studies with real methodology, read together, say the required competence
is not "operate the tool" but "know when the output is wrong" — and that people cannot self-assess
it.

**METR (Becker, Rush, Barnes & Rein, arXiv:2507.09089, July 2025).** RCT, 16 experienced developers,
246 real tasks on repos they averaged five years working in, randomised to AI-allowed or
AI-forbidden. **AI access increased completion time by 19%.** Participants forecast a 24% speed-up
beforehand; afterwards, having actually been slowed down, they still estimated AI had made them
**20% faster**. Expert forecasters predicted 38–39% speed-ups. State the small N out loud — that
disclosure is what earns the citation.

**Dell'Acqua et al., "Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier"** (HBS WP 24-013 / SSRN 4573321,
2023). Preregistered field experiment, **758 BCG consultants**. Inside the AI's capability frontier:
+12.2% tasks completed, 25.1% faster, higher quality. Outside it: subjects using AI were **19
percentage points less likely** to produce correct solutions than those without.

**Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond, "Generative AI at Work"** (*QJE* 140(2), 2025). n=5,172 support agents,
staggered rollout, firm-recorded outcomes. **+15% issues resolved per hour overall — but the lowest-
skill quintile gained ~36%, while the most-skilled agents saw no significant gain and a small
decline in conversation quality.**

**Why it matters.** In Hiatt's own definition, Ability is "demonstrated capability to implement the
change **at the required performance levels**." For AI the required performance level is *can you
tell when the output is wrong?* Knowledge — how to operate it — is nearly free; the interface is a
text box. So the change dies at Ability, and it dies invisibly.

**Which breaks the diagnostic.** An ADKAR assessment asks people to rate their own Ability 1–5. METR
says that number is uncorrelated with reality and biased in the flattering direction. **You cannot
self-report your way to a barrier point on this one.** Measure output quality, not confidence.

**And it inverts the status story.** The workers who gained most were the least experienced; the most
skilled gained nothing and lost quality. The people with the most status to lose are the people the
evidence says benefit least — their resistance is not a Desire deficit, it is an accurate read of
their position. Compare McLaren, van der Hoorn & Fein (2022), *JoCM*, on why manufacturing urgency
by vilifying the status quo backfires against status quo bias: telling a senior expert their craft
is a burning platform is a strategy that paper calls "combative (and futile)."
