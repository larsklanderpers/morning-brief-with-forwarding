# Workslop — what adoption without reinforcement produces

**What happened.** Niederhoffer, Rosen Kellerman, Lee, Liebscher, Rapuano & Hancock, "AI-Generated
'Workslop' Is Destroying Productivity," *HBR*, 22 Sept 2025 — BetterUp Labs with the Stanford Social
Media Lab, survey of **1,004 full-time US desk workers**, Sept 2025.

- **40%** received workslop in the last month
- Workers estimate **15.4%** of the work they receive is workslop
- **54%** of managers report receiving it, vs 38.5% of individual contributors
- **53%** admit some of what they send may be workslop
- Each instance costs **1 hour 51 minutes**
- **~$186 per employee per month**; >$9M/year for a 10,000-person organisation

**Why it matters.** This is what Reinforcement failure looks like when you measure it properly.
Usage went up. Output quality went down. The verification burden was displaced onto colleagues —
note the manager/IC asymmetry: 54% vs 38.5%, so the cost lands hardest on the people whose job is
already review. And the dashboard stayed green, because the dashboard counts seats and prompts.

ADKAR's Reinforcement asks what keeps a change from reverting. It assumes the change is good and the
risk is regression. Here the risk is the opposite: the change sticks, and sticking is the problem.
Neither model has a move for "adoption succeeded and made things worse."

The cost figures are the argument for measuring output quality rather than usage — and $186/employee
/month is the number that makes it a budget conversation rather than a culture conversation.

## Observations

- **2026-08-16** — first seen. Treat the $9M extrapolation carefully: it is arithmetic on a
  self-reported per-instance time estimate, not a measured cost. The 40% / 15.4% / 1h51m figures are
  the survey; the dollar figures are derived.
