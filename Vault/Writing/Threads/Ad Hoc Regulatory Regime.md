# Ad Hoc Regulatory Regime

Regulation arriving by improvisation rather than statute — agencies collapsing, vendors and export controls acting as de facto regulators, obligations landing through the back door.

## State of play

Three live examples of regulation arriving sideways rather than by statute:
[[Hassabis FINRA-style AI standards body]] — an industry-funded self-regulator proposed
two weeks before the EU AI Act's binding regime applies, creating dual compliance tracks;
[[APRA CPS 230 voice AI vendors]], where a technology-agnostic prudential standard reaches
offshore AI inference **without naming AI at all** — via functional materiality plus a
pre-notification duty on material offshoring arrangements *(corrected 2026-07-20 — the earlier
"redefines data residency" framing was wrong and is retired; do not reuse it)*; and
[[NSW mandates AI registration and assessment across government]], where a state government
builds ex ante AI machinery the Commonwealth declined to create.

The pattern worth naming: none of these bodies coordinate, and firms will cite whichever
is most permissive. The FINRA analogy is load-bearing for a piece and unverified — check
whether FINRA works before leaning on it.

**Live tension, promoted 2026-08-06 (Lars).** The CPS 230 / NSW pair is not just federal-vs-state
disagreement — it is **opposite scoping choices on the same instrument, a register.** CPS 230 keys
materiality to *function and reliance*, so vendor AI is caught wherever it touches a critical
operation. NSW keys its register to *the agency's own AI use cases*, so AI arriving inside a
supplier's service is explicitly exempt. The technology-agnostic rule has the coverage the
AI-specific rule lacks, which inverts the standing assumption that AI needs bespoke instruments.
Draft target: this thread's slot, week of 17–23 Aug (or 10–16 if it lands sooner). Carry the
asymmetry openly — prudential standard vs public-sector policy, service-provider register vs AI
register — and argue the mechanism, not a like-for-like.

**New pattern, 2026-08-07 — the market is generating its own legal facts.** Two of this thread's recurring
claims now trace *only* to companies selling the remedy. The Australia–US CLOUD Act agreement is dated
**31 January 2026** across vendor content; it came into force **30 January 2024**, and US extraterritorial
reach predates it by six years under 18 U.S.C. § 2713 — see
[[CLOUD Act Australia - the exposure predates the agreement]]. And "APRA is expected to release updated
cloud guidance addressing AI inference" is nobody's commitment: APRA **rescinded** its cloud information
paper in Feb 2025 and its 30 Apr 2026 AI letter promises only a "forward plan" — see
[[APRA CPS 230 voice AI vendors]]. Both were flagged the same morning, both from sovereign-deployment
vendors. **Regulation by improvisation now has a commercial sibling: compliance folklore, manufactured by
the people selling compliance.** That is a piece, and it is this thread's, not Sovereignty's.

**New instrument, 2026-08-10 — a state reached for a royal commission.** [[SA royal commission into AI]].
Announced this morning by Premier Malinauskas: **three commissioners** (international search, unnamed),
**~$3M**, starting **October 2026**, reporting **by 1 July 2027**, terms of reference **four to six weeks
away**. It is **Australia's** first, *not* the world's — no source makes the world claim, and readers will
hear it as "first to ask", which is false.

The argument is the gap inside it. The *Royal Commissions Act 1917* (SA) gives genuinely coercive powers —
compel attendance, compel production, three months' gaol for refusal, no rules of evidence — and then the
findings **bind nobody**, with SA's own 2016 Nuclear Fuel Cycle commission as the precedent for a central
recommendation simply being abandoned. Set against
[[NSW mandates AI registration and assessment across government]] it is a clean pair: **the instrument with
teeth cannot bite; the instrument that binds cannot see.** Both report into a federal process — mandatory
national AI standards, legislation expected **early 2027** — that is announced policy with a drafting body
attached, not a bill.

**The manufactured-legal-facts pattern now has a fourth instance and a general form.** See
[[Capability restated as certainty]]. On one morning: a prediction restated as a ranking, a figure detached
from the comparison that gave it meaning, two separate incidents welded into one, a capability restated as a
practice, and [[APRA CPS 230 voice AI vendors]] recirculating for the **third** time from the same
sovereign-deployment consultancy chain with the framing this vault retired on 20 July. The engine is
grammatical — *could* loses its modal verb and becomes *does* — which is why it survives editing and why
nobody has to be lying. That is the piece, and it belongs here.

**Promoted 2026-09-06 (Heinlein inbox, Lars).** The regime now has its opposite pole on file: the one
instrument that is actually a treaty. [[Council of Europe AI Treaty - signed not in force]]: 20
signatures, one ratification (the EU), in force nowhere, private actors left to each government's
"other appropriate measures", defence excluded, Australia negotiated and never signed. The improvised
instruments in this thread bite; the formal one does not yet. That inversion is a piece on its own.

**Promoted 2026-09-12 (Heinlein inbox, Lars).** The UK arrives at the soft end of the regime.
[[UK AI Risk Management Toolkit - guidance, not a gate]]: DSIT, 8 Sep 2026, filed on GOV.UK as guidance
for departments, with no stated requirement to use it, an Orange Book 5x5 scoring method and an Excel
workbook. The central risk log is something GDS only "encourages". Against
[[NSW mandates AI registration and assessment across government]] it is the same spreadsheet with the
opposite status: one government made it a gate, the other a suggestion. It names neither ISO/IEC 42001
nor NIST AI RMF.

**Promoted 2026-09-20 (Heinlein inbox, Lars).** The compliance-folklore pattern has a quieter form,
and it may be the common one. [[Five board AI policies and no law named]]: a LinkedIn Pulse article
of 19 Sep, number 8 of a 20-part series, tells boards to require five AI policies and — on the fetch
as read — names **no law, regulator or standard anywhere**, closing into the author's own newsletter
and his trademarked Governance by Assurance™. The earlier instances in this thread manufacture false
facts; this one manufactures none, because nothing in it is checkable. **The authority it runs on is
the author's.** ⚠️ The no-law finding is an absence claim off a summarising reader, not a raw scrape
— read the page directly before printing it.

**Promoted 2026-09-20 (Heinlein inbox, Lars), and it is the opposite face of this thread.** Every
other instrument here was improvised by somebody with no standing to write it. This one was written
well, by the party it measures.
[[Anthropic publishes its own pace metrics]]: three measurements of frontier-lab pace - an AL0-to-AL5
automation index (Claude **leads 26%** of Anthropic's own AI R&D as of Aug 2026, under 1% in Feb),
oversight numbers for ~**30,000** simultaneous agents, and **6%** of AI R&D compute going to safety -
published days after Amodei called for pacing the frontier, and **self-measured, self-judged and
unaudited**, with third-party evaluators still future tense. Its best sentence argues against its own
author's interest: *"the burden of proof should sit with the developer to show that work is
safety-related."* Against [[UK AI Risk Management Toolkit - guidance, not a gate]] and
[[NSW mandates AI registration and assessment across government]] the axis is now three-sided: a
government that made it a gate, a government that made it a suggestion, and **a company that wrote the
gauge because neither of them had.** ⚠️ Read through a summarising fetcher and undated on the page
(secondaries say 17 Sep) - foot both before printing.

**Promoted 2026-09-22 (Heinlein inbox, Lars), and it is the space everything else in this thread
grows into.** [[Governing by order while the statute count falls]]: the 119th Congress has enacted
**111 public laws**, **34 of them in 2026** (the lowest midterm-year total since at least 1990), and
the House closed on **16 September** until after the 3 November election. Over the same period the
second Trump administration has signed **284 executive orders** (Federal Register API, 22 Sep),
more than Obama signed in eight years, at about **171 a year, the highest annual rate since FDR**.
Every other instrument on this board was improvised by a party with no standing to write it; this is
the same move at the top of the system, where the standing exists. **The instrument that one office
can issue alone is the one still being issued, and it is revocable on day one by the next office
holder.** That difference in half-life is not priced anywhere in the governance literature, which is
part of why [[Five board AI policies and no law named]] can tell boards to require policies while
naming no law: federally, there is increasingly an order to name rather than a law.
⛔ Three traps, all recorded in the note: **"most EOs since FDR" is false unqualified** (Truman 907,
Eisenhower 484 - it is true only as a rate); **"fewest laws since Jefferson" does not hold** (the
10th Congress passed about 104 in two years and the 119th is past that); and **Jefferson's "4" is
not the same unit as 284**, since EO numbering began in 1907 and back-catalogues only to 1862.
⚠️ And the metric itself is contested from both directions - AEI says counting laws mismeasures
Congress, the LoC notes 4 to 6 million words of law per Congress since WWII consolidated into
omnibus vehicles, and Speaker Johnson calls this the most productive Congress in 80 years. **The
story is that both sides reach for the measure that suits them.**
