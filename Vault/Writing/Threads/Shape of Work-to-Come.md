# Shape of Work-to-Come

Constructive counterweight: how work is being restructured rather than deleted. Archetypes, orchestration, the entry-level ladder. Fed by the daily AI Work brief.

## State of play

Best current statement of the thread is [[The archetypes converge on one shape]]: FDE,
Agent Orchestration Specialist and Agent Deployer are three job markets independently
describing one person — a domain expert who manages agents and owns output quality. That
is a senior professional with new tools, which reframes the anxiety as a literacy gap
rather than a career change.

[[Replit self-driving company data]] is the empirical anchor (2.9x per engineer, quality
flat), pending a methodology check. The counterweight that keeps it honest is
[[Entry-ladder compression]] — the convergence story reassures people who already have
seniority and offers nothing to a 24-year-old. Any piece here has to carry that caveat
rather than close on the reassurance.

**Updated 2026-08-07 — one number to stop using, one to start.**

**Stop:** the Remote Labor Index "2.5% of tasks at human gold standard" is the **October 2025** launch
figure, and no March 2026 version exists. Current leaderboard: **Fable-5 at 15.80%** — 2.5% → 4.17% →
15.8%, roughly **6x in eight months**. See
[[Remote Labor Index - the 2.5% is nine months stale]]. The constructive read survives and improves:
~84% of real paid end-to-end projects still fail, *and* the edge shrank by two-thirds while everyone argued
about whether it would.

**Start:** BCG's *AI at Work 2026* (n=11,749, 14 markets) — **47% say they spend more time managing and
directing AI than doing the work**, and the buried finding is better still: **42% of regular frontline users
save at least a full workday weekly; 66% get limited or no guidance on what to do with that time.** The
capacity is being created and thrown away. That is a constructive, actionable argument that does not require
predicting anything.

**Reframed:** the chokepoint is **verification, not specification** —
[[Specification is the chokepoint - the evidence is mixed]]. METR's RCT found experienced devs **19% slower**
with AI while believing they were 20% faster; DORA finds adoption raising **throughput and instability
together** ("verification tax"); Stanford's spread shows the **top-to-bottom-quartile team gap widening to
~4x** on the same tools. So the Sweeper / Editor / Risk Steward archetypes are not consolation seats — they
are where the measured cost actually sits.

**Corrected:** [[Klarna 700 agents - what Klarna actually said]] — Klarna said its AI does *"the equivalent
work of 700 full-time agents"* and never claimed a layoff; the 2025 reversal was about **cost being the
predominant evaluation factor** producing "lower quality". A quality story wearing a headcount story's
clothes.

**Updated 2026-08-10 — the debate is settling into data, and the thread's own turn has a name.**

The reassurance side now has figures: Yale Budget Lab (Mar 2026) finding no meaningful unemployment change
for AI-exposed workers since Nov 2022; Goldman (May 2026) revising net job losses down 30%; Altman and
Amodei walking back apocalypse calls; WEF's net +12M through 2030. **All of it is aggregate, all of it is
[VERIFY], and three of the five figures are five to eight months old** — see [[Entry-ladder compression]],
where the caveat lives. The macro can be fine while the entry rung is not; aggregate employment is the wrong
instrument for detecting a broken apprenticeship pipeline.

The constructive turn, which is the more useful finding: **the question moved from "will this happen?" to
"how fast can I shape it in my function?"** The displacement debate is losing oxygen not because the concern
was wrong but because the people who absorbed it fastest have moved on to building. Two supports the same
morning — NLW on **the handoff problem** (deciding what to delegate, verifying what came back) as the durable
human layer, and a new archetype sighting, the **Cost-per-Accepted-Task Analyst**, measuring the business
value of agent outputs rather than tokens consumed. That is Risk Steward meets Maintainer with a P&L, and it
is the same seat as the **AI FinOps** category already parked in `SUBSTACK_IDEAS.md`. Two job markets naming
one seat is the evidence pattern [[The archetypes converge on one shape]] was built on.

**The Ability evidence is the same evidence, read differently.** [[Ability in AI adoption is judgment, not operation]] carries METR (19% slower while believing 20% faster), BCG/Harvard (−19pp

**The status asymmetry is the sharper version of the entry-ladder argument.** The gains concentrate
among the least experienced; the losses land on the most skilled. So [[Entry-ladder compression]]
has a mirror image: the senior end is not merely unhelped, it is measurably degraded on quality.

and reframes the archetype argument from "reassurance for people who already have seniority" to
"the seats where measured cost actually sits." That is the same turn
[[The archetypes converge on one shape]] makes, now with an adverse-evidence leg to stand on.

And the load-bearing gap: both canonical change models sit downstream of job design and assume it
is settled. **The archetype work is the answer to the question ADKAR and Kotter cannot ask.**

**Added 2026-09-20 (Heinlein inbox) - somebody finally published numbers against the rungs of the
ladder, for one real job.**

[[Anthropic publishes its own pace metrics]] scores Anthropic's own AI R&D work on a six-rung scale -
AL0 *no AI involvement*, AL1 *minimal*, AL2 *AI assists*, AL3 *collaborates*, AL4 *leads*, AL5
*is autonomous* - and reports **26% at "leads" as of August 2026, under 1% in February, over 90% at
"collaborates" or above, and nothing fully autonomous.** This thread has argued the ladder for months
without a measured instance of one; here is the measured instance, on the job the measurer holds.
★ **The number that matters for the archetype argument is not 26%, it is the 90%.** Almost all the work
has already moved off the bottom rungs, and almost none of it has reached the top one, which is exactly
the Orchestrator seat this thread has been describing - a job that is neither doing the work nor absent
from it. And it puts a ceiling on the 80% merged-code figure the 19 Aug piece quoted from the same
institute: **authorship is not direction.** ⚠️ Self-measured by the vendor, with a judge model that can
share the errors of the model it judges - the page says so itself. The page also links Epoch's
*"Toward an O\*NET for AI R&D"*, which is Mon 14's classification ground, published and spent.
