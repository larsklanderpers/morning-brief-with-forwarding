# Adoption and Change

How organisations actually absorb AI — and why the change-management canon they reach for was
built for a different kind of change. Sister thread to [[Shape of Work-to-Come]]: that one is
about what the work becomes, this one is about how you get there and who gets it wrong.

Opened 2026-08-16 from a 35-source reading list on AI adoption and change management, plus a
provenance pass on the two frameworks that dominate it.

## State of play

**The canon does not survive a provenance check, and that is the opening.** Three findings carry
this thread, all documented in [[ADKAR and Kotter — a provenance file]]:

The **70% change-failure statistic is unsourced** — see [[The 70% change-failure statistic has no source]]. Kotter's 1995 paper contains no percentage; the number originates in a 1993 reengineering
book that called its own estimate "unscientific" and whose author later disowned it as a rate. Mark
Hughes read all five published sources in 2011 and found nothing behind any of them. It is now in
peer-reviewed nursing journals, sourced to a consultancy blog. Nobody lied at any step.

**Kotter replaced his own model in 2014 and almost nobody teaches the replacement** —
[[Kotter superseded his own 8-step model in 2014]]. *Accelerate* moved from sequential-and-episodic
with a small powerful coalition to **concurrent, continuous, volunteer army, dual operating
system**. That is not a footnote for this thread; it is the whole argument, because the 2014 model
is the one built for change that never resolves.

**ADKAR has never been independently tested, and the one attempt came back null** —
[[ADKAR - the one external test came back null]]. Its genuinely good idea (the barrier point: fix
the first weak element, not what is downstream of it) is worth keeping. Its evidence base is house
data from the vendor's own 90,000-member customer list.

**And the models are pointed at the wrong element.** [[AI adoption has no Desire problem]]: 78% of
AI users brought their own tools; employees at 90% of surveyed companies use personal AI while only
40% bought a licence. The sponsor is not ahead of the workforce, the sponsor is late to it. So the
comms-and-urgency spend goes to two elements that were never the barrier, while the change dies at
**Ability** — [[Ability in AI adoption is judgment, not operation]], where METR's developers were
19% slower and believed they were 20% faster — and at **Reinforcement**, where
[[Workslop - what adoption without reinforcement produces]] shows usage up and output quality down
on a dashboard that stayed green.

**The load-bearing gap neither model can see:** agentic systems move humans to specification and
verification. That is a **job-design** question. Both models sit downstream of job design and assume
it is settled. Connects straight to [[The archetypes converge on one shape]] in
[[Shape of Work-to-Come]] — the archetype work is the answer to the question the change models
cannot ask.

## Cautionary cases

[[Amazon's recruiting engine - the training data was the change]] and
[[Watson for Oncology - the marketing outran the artefact]] are the two canonical AI-adoption
failures, both PRIMARY reporting, both routinely overstated. Use them with the caveats attached —
the overstatements are themselves the point.

## Numbers to never use

[[The AI pilot-failure numbers do not survive scrutiny]] — the MIT NANDA 95%, the IDC/Lenovo 88%,
and Gartner's 40%. All three are in circulation, all three are on somebody's slide this week.

## Open questions

- If Reinforcement aims at a moving target, what replaces "anchor it in the culture"? Kotter-2014's
  concurrency is the shape of an answer, not an answer.
- Is there a defensible way to certify *calibrated scepticism* — the judgment competence — or is
  that just a training-completion rate with better branding?
  - **2026-09-20**: the question now has a price and a seller.
    [[MIT's GenAI sprint - capability and confidence sold as one product]]: $1,750, six
    asynchronous hours, no assessment, a completion certificate, and a sales line offering
    *"capability and confidence"* as one purchase. Notable for what it does **not** claim (no
    adoption statistic, no productivity number, no outcome of any kind), which is the exhibit.
  - **2026-09-20**, second seller the same day, board tier rather than manager tier.
    [[Five board AI policies and no law named]]: twenty articles, a newsletter and a trademarked
    Governance by Assurance™, telling directors to require five AI policies while naming no law,
    regulator or standard as read. Same instrument as the MIT sprint at a different altitude — a
    credential for judgment, issued by the seller. ⚠️ The no-law finding is off a summarising
    fetch, not a raw read.
  - **2026-09-22**, and this one is the counter-example rather than a seller.
    [[The semantic layer without a seller]]: five academics at MIT CISR make the ontology case
    with no vendor quoted and no product named, and the format requirement that every vendor
    attaches to it does not survive the removal of the vendor. Its governance step names one
    owner accountable for what the data means, which is the dull, workable version of the
    judgment problem this thread keeps finding for sale. ⚠️ Its 3x and 2x multiples are
    correlations in a self-reported house survey (N = 349, fielded 2024) and carry nothing.
- Shadow adoption is a security posture problem before it is a change problem. Who owns it?
