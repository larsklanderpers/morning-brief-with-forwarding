## What's contested / watch out
- **J-space — real, but you're overclaiming it as settled.** The finding is genuine and widely covered (MIT Tech Review ×2, Forbes, VentureBeat, TNW): an internal "J-space" of words that influence reasoning but don't appear in outputs. BUT the MIT "does-and-doesn't-show" piece is explicitly a caution against overreach: "better to think of this result as one more step on the path to understanding this technology… than as something that will be useful by itself," and it warns against premature claims about auditability. **Your current line — "the oldest assumption in compliance, that you can audit what a model said, is now literally false"** — goes further than the evidence. Recommend softening to something like: *"is looking a lot shakier than it did"* / *"is no longer safe to assume,"* and framing J-space as an early, contested finding rather than a settled one. The point survives; the overclaim is the only risk. [technologyreview.com/2026/07/13/1140343]
- **Kimi K3 capability — keep it scoped to coding.** Your briefs carry BOTH "tops frontend coding" and "matches Opus 4.8 on measured intelligence." To stay clean, keep the capstone's language to coding/frontend ("beating US frontier labs on coding," "the best open model") rather than "best overall." You already scoped this on Friday — hold the line here too.
