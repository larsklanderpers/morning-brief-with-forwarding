### ★★ The absence claim is FALSE as the plan states it — US

The plan's implied mechanism is that the role is too small to earn a code. **The SOC Policy Committee
says in writing that size is not the test.**

> *"In evaluating collectability and reportability, however, the SOC Policy Committee **does not use a
> specific employment size cut-off**."*
> -- *Revising the Standard Occupational Classification for 2028*, BLS
> [bls.gov](https://www.bls.gov/soc/2028/revising_the_standard_occupational_classification_2028.pdf)

Confirmed independently in the Federal Register notice, which adds why: *small concentrated occupations
may be reportable while larger dispersed ones may not be.* **Classification Principle 9** is the actual
gate: *"For a detailed occupation to be included in the SOC, either the Bureau of Labor Statistics or the
Census Bureau must be able to collect and report data on that occupation."*

★ **So the reason there is no orchestrator code is not that the role is small. It is that nobody can
collect data on it** -- which is Sunday's argument, arriving a day late. Another reason the planned
framing cannot run.

for this job to be named **closed on 12 August 2024.** Lightcast's base year is February 2025. **The role
did not measurably exist until roughly six months after the door shut**, and on a revision cycle that runs
2010 → 2018 → 2028, the next one lands around 2038.

⚠️ **And the committee never asked.** The SOCPC's published input request lists ten categories of
information and **does not mention artificial intelligence, agents, or emerging technology occupations at
all.** Its nearest question is whether *"the tools and technologies mentioned in existing SOC occupation
definitions"* are up to date.
[bls.gov](https://www.bls.gov/soc/2028/input_requested_by_the_socpc_2028.pdf)

### ★★ O*NET can move faster than the SOC — so the door is not even locked

- O*NET-SOC is **updated three times a year at the job level**, and its **New & Emerging** process exists
  precisely to catch *"changes in technology, society, law or business practices."*
- **152 N&E occupations** were added in the O*NET-SOC 2010 taxonomy; **four cybersecurity occupations**
  were added in O*NET-SOC 2019. **Cybersecurity is the worked precedent for exactly this.**
  [onetcenter.org](https://www.onetcenter.org/taxonomy.html)

⚠️ Current taxonomy is **O*NET-SOC 2019, released 17 November 2020**, 1,016 titles. The orchestrator
search still returns nothing -- but "returns nothing" now means *nobody has taken it through a process
that exists and has been used before*, not *the system cannot see it.*

### ★★★ Australia has already drafted the code, and the door closes on 18 September

⛔ **STOP: "no ANZSCO code" MUST NOT BE PRINTED. ANZSCO no longer exists.** It was replaced by **OSCA
(Occupation Standard Classification for Australia)** on **6 December 2024** -- the first major overhaul of
Australia's occupation classification in about twenty years. §12 and the week plan both carry the stale
name. **This is the same class of error as the Achiam affiliation catch: a superseded label taken from a
secondary.**
[abs.gov.au](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/classifications/osca-occupation-standard-classification-australia/latest-release)

**And OSCA is mid-update, in public, right now:**

- **Round 1 consultation: opened 11 March 2026, closed 24 April 2026. 265 submissions**, from *"all levels
  of government, businesses, Jobs & Skills Councils, industry bodies/associations, unions, academics and
  individuals,"* including *"requests for emerging occupations that should be added to the
  classification."* [consult.abs.gov.au](https://consult.abs.gov.au/osca/osca-2027-update)
- **The resulting draft proposes 31 new 6-digit occupations and one new 4-digit unit group.**
- ★★★ **It includes 273334 Artificial Intelligence Engineer.** It also adds **272235 Site Reliability
  Engineer**. **It does not include any orchestration, agent or prompt role.**
- ★★★ **Round 2 consultation on that draft is OPEN NOW and closes 18 SEPTEMBER 2026** -- *"Consultation is
  now open and will continue through to 18 September 2026."* Four days after this piece publishes.
  [abs.gov.au draft](https://www.abs.gov.au/statistics/classifications/consultation-draft-occupation-standard-classification-australia-osca/aug-2026)
  · [abs.gov.au/about-osca](https://www.abs.gov.au/about/key-priorities/about-osca)

★★ **This is the action move, and it is his readers' own government.** Not an audit, not an inventory, not
a write-down. **A named classification, a real code, a public submission form and a deadline four days
out.** Unspent in the window.

## THE ECONOMICS

**The asymmetry is the cost story and it needs no new numbers.** The US classification runs on a roughly
ten-year cycle: a 2024 comment window, a 2027 decision, a 2028 implementation, for a role whose posting
volume moved from about 53 to 739 inside twelve months. **A worker deciding what to train for, and an
employer deciding what to budget a headcount line for, are both waiting on an instrument whose refresh
rate is slower than the half-life of the job title.** Australia's answer is the cheaper one and it is
already built: a rolling consultation, 265 submissions, 31 new codes, and a named AI occupation drafted
inside eighteen months of the technology arriving. **Same problem, two clock speeds, and one of them is
free to participate in.**
