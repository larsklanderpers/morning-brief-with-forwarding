## 1. THE MORATORIUM -- verdict: CONTESTED (the facts are solid; two of the draft's three attributions are wrong)

### What the draft says
Cruz "a year ago tried to attach a ten-year moratorium on state AI laws to a budget bill"; "seventeen Republican governors asked the Senate majority leader to strip the moratorium"; DeSantis called it "bad policy and even worse politics".

### What the record shows

**The provision.** Section 43201(c) of H.R. 1, the budget reconciliation bill later titled the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, as passed by the House on 22 May 2025, barred states from enforcing "any law or regulation regulating artificial intelligence models, artificial intelligence systems, or automated decision systems" for ten years. A bipartisan AG letter of 16 May 2025 (oag.ca.gov PDF, fetched) described it as "an amendment to the budget reconciliation bill that would impose a 10-year prohibition on state enforcement of AI and automated decision-making laws and regulations" and called it "sweeping and wholly destructive of reasonable state efforts to prevent known harms associated with AI."

**Cruz's Senate version.** Cruz, as Commerce chairman, sponsored the Senate-side text. The 25 June 2025 Commerce release ("Chairman Cruz Releases Updated Budget Reconciliation Text", commerce.senate.gov, fetched) describes it as an "optional $500 million state AI program". The mechanism, per the Center for American Progress analysis of the text (fetched): subsection (q) required states to pause AI regulation "during the 10-year period beginning on the date of enactment", with subsection (p) providing that "no amounts made available to carry out this section may be obligated for expenditures... that is not in compliance with subsections (q) and (r)", i.e. the pause was tied to BEAD broadband money. Nextgov (1 Jul 2025, fetched): "Senator Ted Cruz (R-TX) sponsored the decade-long moratorium provision in the reconciliation bill." Cruz's own defence, per Nextgov: "[The AI moratorium] preserves the rights of states to protect consumers without giving the Left a backdoor to push their woke social agenda."

**What the Senate did.** Senate roll call vote 363, 119th Congress 1st session, 1 July 2025, 04:08 a.m. (senate.gov, fetched): S.Amdt. 2814 to S.Amdt. 2360 to H.R. 1, sponsored by Blackburn, "To strike the section relating to support for artificial intelligence." Result: "Amendment Agreed to", 99 yeas, 1 nay. The single nay was Thom Tillis (R-NC). Blackburn had first negotiated a five-year compromise with Cruz over the weekend of 28-29 June, then abandoned it; her statement (via Nextgov): "the current language is not acceptable to those who need these protections the most." Co-sponsors of the strike amendment: Blackburn, Cantwell, Collins, Markey.

**The governors' letter.** Arkansas Governor's office release, 27 June 2025 (governor.arkansas.gov, fetched): 17 Republican governors, led by Sarah Huckabee Sanders, addressed to **both** Senate Majority Leader John Thune **and** Speaker Mike Johnson. Signatory states: Arkansas, Alabama, Alaska, Georgia, Idaho, Iowa, Louisiana, Missouri, Montana, Nebraska, North Dakota, Oklahoma, South Carolina, South Dakota, Tennessee, Utah, Wyoming. Verbatim: "As Republican Governors, we support the One, Big, Beautiful Bill and President Trump's vision of American AI dominance, but we cannot support a provision that takes away states' powers to protect our citizens." Also: "AI is already deeply entrenched in American industry and society; people will be at risk until basic rules ensuring safety and fairness can go into effect." **Florida did not sign.**

**The DeSantis quote: real, but not about this.** Primary: x.com/RonDeSantis/status/2065077339344499059, scraped. Posted **11 June 2026**, 14:22 UTC. Full text: "Preempting states re: AI without enacting a sensible federal framework is just an amnesty for Big Tech. Combined with a potential de facto bailout of OpenAI, it represents bad policy and even worse politics." Context per Washington Examiner, 11 Jun 2026 (fetched): he was reacting to "reports that the White House and congressional allies met to explore reviving legislation that would override certain state-level AI laws, potentially by attaching it to other technology or online safety legislation." So: eleven months after the 99-1 vote, about a 2026 White House/congressional revival effort, not about Cruz's 2025 budget rider. (Yahoo/Politico version of the same story: UNREACHED, 429.)

**"A year ago".** The provision lived from 22 May 2025 (House passage) to 1 July 2025 (Senate strike). As of 14 Sep 2026 that is fourteen and a half months. "A year ago" is a rounding, defensible in conversational register; "last summer" or "in mid-2025" is exact.

In May 2025 the US House of Representatives passed a budget bill containing a clause that would have stopped every US state from enforcing its own AI laws for ten years, with no federal rule offered in their place. [H.R. 1 §43201(c); AG letter 16 May 2025: "This bill does not propose any regulatory scheme to replace or supplement the laws enacted or currently under consideration by the states, leaving Americans entirely unprotected."]

[Senate roll call 363; governor.arkansas.gov 27 Jun 2025.]

### What to print
Keep Cruz, the ten years, the budget bill and the 17 governors (addressed to Thune **and** Johnson). Move DeSantis: he said "bad policy and even worse politics" on 11 June 2026 about a White House-backed preemption revival, not about the 2025 moratorium, and he did not sign the governors' letter. Say "last summer" rather than "a year ago" if precision matters.

**Was it 36 or 40?** Both numbers are real but belong to different letters. The **16 May 2025** letter (opposing the House budget-bill moratorium) was the "40 AGs" letter in contemporary coverage; the NAAG PDF the November letter cites as its predecessor. The **25 November 2025** letter is 36. The draft's "last November ... thirty-six" is correct.

**Addressees:** Speaker Mike Johnson, Majority Leader John Thune, Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries, Minority Leader Chuck Schumer.

**What it was responding to:** The letter itself says only "we ask Congress not to inject an AI moratorium in upcoming appropriations legislation." It does not say "NDAA" and does not mention an executive order. Contemporaneous context: California AG Bonta's own release of 22 Nov 2025 (oag.ca.gov, fetched) opposed "a proposed rider in the National Defense Authorization Act that would strip states of their power"; Bonta's 25 Nov release framed the 36-AG letter as opposing "a proposed provision in the National Defense Authorization Act". ### 7(b) Australian data residency -- verdict: FALSE as written ("illegal in and of itself")
There is **no general Australian law** making it unlawful for data to leave the country. The governing rule for personal information is **Privacy Act 1988, APP 8**, an accountability rule, not a prohibition. OAIC APP Guidelines ch. 8 (v1.3, Oct 2025, fetched), verbatim: "before an APP entity discloses personal information about an individual to an overseas recipient, the entity must take such steps as are reasonable in the circumstances to ensure that the recipient does not breach the APPs"; and s 16C: "Where an entity discloses personal information to an overseas recipient, it is accountable for an act or practice of the overseas recipient that would breach the APPs." OAIC's stated object: to "facilitate the free flow of information across national borders while ensuring that the privacy of individuals is respected." Exceptions in APP 8.2 include consent and substantially-similar-law.

Sector prohibitions do exist and are narrow:
- **My Health Records Act 2012 s 77** ("Requirement not to hold or take records outside Australia", legislation.gov.au, fetched via epub; tool-summarised, verify wording before quoting): participants "must not hold, or take" records including My Health Record health information outside Australia, or process/handle such information outside Australia; de-identified information and activity metadata excepted; 120 penalty units.
- **APRA CPS 230** (commenced 1 Jul 2025, fetched), verbatim: regulated entities must notify APRA "prior to entering into any material offshoring arrangement, or when there is a significant change proposed to the arrangement, including in circumstances where data or personnel relevant to the service being provided will be located offshore." Notification, not prohibition. CPS 234 is information security and does not itself ban offshoring.
- Government: PSPF / hosting certification and procurement policy for agencies, contractual not statutory (not fetched this pass).

**Accurate one-sentence version:** "Australia has no general ban on data leaving the country; the Privacy Act makes you accountable for what an overseas recipient does with personal information, and a handful of sector rules (My Health Record, APRA-regulated entities, government hosting) add outright prohibitions or notification duties on top."
