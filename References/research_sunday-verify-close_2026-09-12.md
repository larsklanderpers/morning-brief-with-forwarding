the March 2026 edition.** Fetched live 12 Sep 2026, `maxAge: 0`, status 200, Last Modified Date

Verbatim, from **USDL-26-1425, for release 10:00 a.m. (ET) Friday, August 28, 2026**:

> "The preliminary estimate of the Current Employment Statistics (CES) national benchmark revision
> to total nonfarm employment for March 2026 was **-79,000 (-0.1 percent)**, the U.S. Bureau of Labor
> Statistics reported today. The preliminary benchmark revision for total private employment was

## VERIFY 2 -- §5.3's denominator against the +279,500 projection. ✅ CLOSED AS A RULING.

**The flag asked for a reconciliation, not a fetch, and the reconciliation is straightforward once
the two numbers are named properly. They are not in conflict, because they are not the same
quantity.**

| | §5.3's denominator | The projections figure |
|---|---|---|
| Instrument | **CPS Table 11b**, household survey, 2025 annual averages (11-month, October excluded) | **BLS Employment Projections 2025–2035**, published 27 Aug 2026 |
| Quantity | Employed persons aged **20–24** in three SOC groups | Net change in **all-ages** employment in the same three SOC groups, over a decade |
| Value | **≈2.4 million** (from a 33,328k all-ages universe) | **+279,500 (+0.78%)**, on a 35,654.8k → 35,934.3k base |

**The ruling, in one line: §5.3's 2.4 million entry-level denominator stands unchanged, and the
characterization that must not survive is any sentence describing this population as contracting.**
BLS projects the three-group aggregate roughly flat and slightly growing, with composition shifting
from clerical toward analytical and technical: Business and Financial **+628.6k** and Computer and
Mathematical **+403.1k** against Office and Administrative Support **−752.1k**. That is a
compositional story. The falsifiability arithmetic in §5.3 does not depend on the aggregate
shrinking, so nothing in it breaks.

### ★ And the reconciliation hands the piece a better paragraph than it had

**Two BLS products give figures 2.3 million apart for the same three occupational groups in the same
year.** CPS Table 11b puts the all-ages universe at **33,328,000**; Employment Projections puts its
2025 base at **35,654,800**. The gap is **2,326,800**, about **7%**, and it is roughly twenty times
the entire eight-month AI-attributed announcement total of 116,175.

**This is not an error in either product, and the draft must say why, or a skeptical reader will
read it as sloppiness.** The two are built on different universes by design: the household survey
counts persons who report an occupation, the projections base is a jobs-based construction with its
own coverage. **That is exactly the estimand argument, arriving inside the piece's own denominator.**
Used well, it is the cleanest available demonstration that the numbers in this argument are not
commensurable even when they carry the same three occupation names and the same agency's logo.

⚠️ **Handle it as a design difference, never as a discrepancy or a mistake.** And name both
instruments in the same sentence or not at all.
