### The second beat — new in this plan: the audit chain exists and the certificate count does not

Verified 14 Aug at ISO's own catalogue and IAPP's own accreditation page. Not a vendor blog anywhere
in this beat.

- **ISO/IEC 42001:2023**, published **2023-12-18**, stage 60.60, 51pp, JTC 1/SC 42. A management
  system standard on Plan-Do-Check-Act, so certifiable in principle. **No 2026 amendment exists** —
  nothing past 60.60, no systematic review, no Amd 1.
- ISO's own sentence, and it kills the most common error in this area: **"ISO does not perform
  certification. Organizations looking to get certified to an ISO standard must contact an
  independent certification body."**
- **★ The document the 14 Aug brief missed, and the actual news: ISO/IEC 42006:2025**, published
  **2025-07-07** — *"Requirements for bodies providing audit and certification of artificial
  intelligence management systems."* It **"supplements ISO/IEC 17021-1 with AI-specific
  requirements"** and names accreditation bodies as an intended audience. Corroborated by a
  non-vendor accreditation body (Standards Council of Canada transition bulletin).
- **So the full conformity-assessment chain exists on paper:** 42001 (requirements) → 42006 +
  17021-1 (auditor competence) → national accreditation bodies → certification bodies → certified
  organisations.
- **★ And there is no public, non-vendor count of who is certified.** Two checkable blockers: the ISO
  Survey is now compiled from IAF CertSearch and sits behind a login, and **42001 is not among the
  standards the ISO Survey covers** — the published coverage list runs 9001, 14001, 45001, 27001,
  22000, 13485, 50001, 20000-1, 37001, 22301, 39001, 55001, 20121, 29001, 44001. Every circulating
  "N companies are 42001-certified" figure traces to a certification body or compliance vendor that
  sells 42001 readiness or audits.
- **AIGP is not accredited, and this is the sharpest single fact in the beat.** IAPP's own
  accreditation page lists exactly which credentials are ANAB/ISO-17024 recognised: **"CIPM, CIPP/E,
  CIPP/US and CIPT."** AIGP — the AI governance credential — **is not on that list**, and its own
  product page makes no accreditation claim. **Holder count not published.**

⚠️ **The trap that will catch a casual re-check.** The only number IAPP has ever published is *"Over
4,000 professionals have already signed up to complete the AIGP training curriculum"* (March 2024).
That is **training sign-ups, not certifications**, and it is two and a half years old. Any "4,000
AIGP holders" claim is a category drop.

⚠️ **"Mirroring post-GDPR privacy" is an analogy and it smuggles in a mandate.** Post-GDPR privacy
had a statutory forcing function — Art. 37 DPO mandate, fines. 42001 is voluntary and confers no
presumption of conformity on its own; AIGP is voluntary and unaccredited. If the comparison is used,
mark it as a comparison and say what differs. **This is the honest frame:** there is no disinterested
party in the certification ecosystem — ISO sells the standards, IAPP sells the exam, IAF CertSearch
sells "Certificate Market Intelligence," and every certification body quoting adoption sells the
audit.

- **Load management:** CPS 230 is the spine and never gets cut. 42006 is the second beat and should

## Tue 25 Aug — DIVERSION #1 (ground game): Read-Only First

- **Register:** short, operational, do it tonight. **Not a word count** — lightness is a state of
  being, not a length metric. The register test is the optimistic take.
- **Thesis:** the first decision you make about an agent is not what it is allowed to *do*. It is what
  it is allowed to *see*. Start read-only, widen on demonstrated reliability, and know in advance
  which rung you are on. Most people skip straight to the top rung because that is the rung the demos
  are shot on.
- **The body — the permission ladder, and where each rung's blast radius ends:**
  1. **Read-only.** It can look at everything and change nothing. Almost all of the value of an
     agent's first week lives here, and almost nobody spends a week here.
  2. **Write to a scratch space.** It can produce, in a place where wrong output costs nothing.
  3. **Write to the real thing.** Now a mistake is a mistake, and you need the check from last
     Tuesday before you promote to this rung.
  4. **Act outward** — send, pay, post, provision. Where a mistake stops being yours.
  The operational move: **write down what has to be true before you promote a rung.** Not a feeling —
  a specific thing you looked at.
- **Whose practice this is, correctly attributed.** Allie K. Miller, in her own words via *Inc.*
  (17 Mar 2026) and *Fast Company* (27 Apr 2026): *"You're not going to give it email access… start
  with read-only access and then earn trust, figure out what is working."* And on failure, which is
  the part nobody quotes: *"Sometimes these things don't work… you just kind of have to say, 'Okay,
  it's broken now. I'll come back to it in an hour.'"* **No recovery machinery. She tolerates

### [BANKED] Thu 27 Aug — Approval Is Not Automatic  [Trusted Access for Cyber]

**The payoff of Mon 17's qualification, and the strongest single find of the 14 Aug dig.**

- **Thesis:** the industry's answer to "how do we ship a model that refuses less" turns out to be a
  human approval process held by the model vendor. That *is* a control. It is also the one kind of
  control Sunday 23's first test cannot see — because it is exercised offshore, by a party you have
  no contractual relationship with, against criteria that are not published.
- **The documented trade, verbatim from AWS, and it is Monday-17's thesis in someone else's mouth:**
  *"a **lower refusal threshold matched by stronger identity verification, monitoring, and access
  controls** improves the speed and depth of an investigation."* You loosen the model and you tighten
  the layer around it. The catch is whose layer it is.
- **The mechanism, verbatim from OpenAI's own help centre:**
  - *"Trusted Access for Cyber is the governance model for OpenAI Daybreak. It helps qualified
    enterprise customers and cybersecurity practitioners use OpenAI models more effectively for
    authorized cybersecurity work…"*
  - **"Approval is not automatic."**
  - **The gate is two-tier, and this is the sharper version of the fact:** *"Daybreak Red requires
