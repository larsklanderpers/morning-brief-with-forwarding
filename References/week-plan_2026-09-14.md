## Fri 18 — [WORKING TITLE] What Your Contract Calls It  [turning a business into something]

- **Thesis, and it is the arc's money beat:** if your vendor files a thing as "misalignment" rather than
  as a "security incident," your contract's breach-notification clause never fires. The label is upstream
  of the obligation, and **you negotiated the obligation without ever negotiating the label.**
- **Why Friday:** it is the only day where the arc becomes a commercial instrument rather than an
  observation, and it is his reseller and CTO ground.
- ✅ **RESEARCH DONE 10 Sep on his word, four parallel digs: `research_incident-label_2026-09-10.md`.**
  NSW/Australian law, US federal commercial law, the journalism hunt and the contract-language dig. All
  primaries fetched live.
- **THESIS RISK: WAS HIGH, NOW LOW ON THE CONTRACT PRONG AND MEDIUM ON THE LEGAL FRAMING.** The dig
  **strengthened the core and broke two things**, and both breaks improve the piece.
- ★★ **What holds, overwhelmingly.** OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft and AWS all define the
  notification trigger with **the same GDPR Art. 4(12) sentence**: a breach of the *vendor's security*
  affecting *customer data*. **"Malfunction" appears in none of the five.** Nor "misalignment," "harmful
  output," "hallucination," "serious incident" or "AI Act" -- zero hits, complete documents. Anthropic's
  **48-hour** clock runs on a data breach and nothing else, while the same contract disclaims that outputs
  are accurate. **Security gets a degradation clause; behaviour does not.**
- ⚠️ **BROKE #1, and the thesis is better for it.** *Bartlett v ANZ* [2016] NSWCA 30: a defined
  contractual trigger presumptively requires **the objective occurrence** of the event, not the party's
  opinion of it. **So "the vendor owns the label" is wrong and must not be printed.** The defensible
  argument is sharper: **nobody ever wrote a definition that reaches behaviour, and the customer has no
  right to the facts that would let them apply one.**
- ★★★ **BROKE #2, and this is the best finding in the dig.** The story is not that the law has a gap. **It
  is that where the law finally addressed this, it wrote the vendor's discretion into the trigger and left
  the customer off the distribution list.** California SB 53's critical-safety-incident definition turns on
  whether the deception was *"outside of the context of an evaluation designed to elicit this behavior"* and
  shows *"materially increased catastrophic risk"* -- **both the developer's call** -- then reports to Cal
  OES, exempt from public records, AG-only enforcement, no private right of action. EU AI Act Art. 3(49) is
  the only definition anywhere that says *"or malfunctioning of an AI system,"* and Art. 73 routes it to
  **market surveillance authorities**. **Neither runs to the customer.**
- ★★★ **THE ACTION EXISTS AND HIS READERS' OWN GOVERNMENT WROTE IT.** DTA **AI Model Clauses v2.0**
  (March 2025, Crown copyright, CC-BY-4.0), clause 2.4.1(g): the Seller must notify the Buyer **immediately**
  where *"the AI System behaves in a way that was not intended or predicted, which adversely affects outputs
  or the delivery of a Buyer service."* Plus an **AI Hazard** near-miss duty, a **circuit breaker**, and a
  **buyer veto on model changes**. **Paste-able into a renewal rider.** Borrow the EU MCC-AI's Annex G
  accuracy-metrics warranty alongside it.
- ⚠️ **The weak prong the piece must concede: the SEC.** 17 CFR 229.106(a) is broader than assumed --
  "unauthorized occurrence" reaching *integrity*, "information systems" extended to third-party and cloud
  resources, and the adopting release says an accidental occurrence is an unauthorized one. **A sharp reader
  attacks here first. Write the concession in.**
- ⛔ **The note carries a 9-item DO-NOT-PRINT list.** Most important: **do NOT say OpenAI classified one
  thing as misalignment and another as a security incident** -- its own 5 Sep statement says the Hugging Face
  event was *also* misalignment and got the security playbook because impact followed. **The line it drew was
  IMPACT, not taxonomy**, which is a better fact. And **do NOT use Replit or Cursor as stonewalling
  examples** -- both vendors apologised and refunded.
- **Centrepiece exhibit:** OpenAI's own published bug-bounty policy, current, not said under pressure --
  *"This exclusion applies even if the agent leaks sensitive data or performs an unwanted action."*
  **Paired with** *Moffatt v Air Canada* [2024] BCCRT 149: *"In effect, Air Canada suggests the chatbot is a
  separate legal entity... This is a remarkable submission."*
