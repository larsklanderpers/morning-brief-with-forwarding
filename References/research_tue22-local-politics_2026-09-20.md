## Bottom line

The national AI argument and the local AI argument are now two different arguments with two different scoreboards,
and the gap between them is the piece. In Washington the fight is abstract, tribal and stalled: the House passed the
first real data center ratepayer bill 417 to 3 on 16 September, and the Senate killed it the next day on a unanimous
consent request, with each side blocking the other's version inside the same hour. In counties and townships the
same question is being settled, repeatedly, by actual votes: moratoria, rejected rezonings, ballot measures passing
at 86 and 90 percent, and at least eighteen incumbents turned out in twelve months over data center decisions in red,
blue and purple jurisdictions. Flock is the same pattern on a different subject, and it is the cleanest example of
the strange bedfellows arc: two Republican governors, a Republican senator, a libertarian legal shop and a gun-rights
group are doing to a police-technology vendor what the ACLU could not.

**Three honest complications, and the piece is better with them in.** First, the causal claim underneath the local
revolt -- that data centers raised your bill -- is real but partial, and the strongest version of it has already been
fact-checked down. Second, the left-right coalition against Flock is ideologically broad but institutionally thin:
Republican voters still support the cameras, and Democratic mayors keep voting to renew them. Third, the people
actually losing their seats are being turned out as much for process and secrecy as for position, which is a different
argument from the one the national press is making.

## Strand 1 — AI in the midterms (3 November 2026, 42 days after publication)

### What is solid

- **AI is not the voting issue. Data centers and electricity bills are.** Pew's July midterm survey (fielded 6-12 July
  2026, n = 3,554 adults including 2,779 registered voters; published 23 July) asked what voters most want
  congressional candidates to discuss: economy NET 29 percent, government ethics 9, immigration 7, health care 5.
  **AI does not appear as a volunteered top issue at all.** Same survey: **51 percent say they agree with neither
  party on AI policy.** https://www.pewresearch.org/politics/2026/07/23/as-the-2026-midterms-approach-economy-is-front-and-center/
- **Data centers are carrying $45M+ of ad spending**, roughly evenly split -- about $22M supporting Republicans and
  $21M supporting Democrats, with $12.8M in the Georgia governor's race alone (AdImpact via NPR, 5 Sep 2026).
- **The NRSC put it in writing.** Internal memo headed "Ohio Data Center Risk", week of 19 Aug 2026: *"More than any
  other thing in this race, data centers are the anchor hanging around Husted's neck"*; *"This has become a sleeper
  issue for the entire election cycle"*; *"If voters' perceptions of data centers are not fixed quickly, the campaign
  against them will expand far beyond Ohio."* https://www.axios.com/2026/08/19/gop-data-center-memo-ai-election
- **The partisan valence flipped inside three years.** Pew, published 16 Sep 2026 (fielded 22-28 June, n = 3,488):
  more concerned than excited about AI -- 2023 Democrats 46 / Republicans 59; 2026 Democrats 56 / Republicans 49.
  https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2026/09/16/democrats-are-now-more-worried-than-republicans-about-ai-and-its-impact-on-jobs/
- **The AI money lost its first marquee fight.** NY-12 Democratic primary, 24 June 2026: Micah Lasher beat Alex Bores
  39-35 after both the pro-industry network (Leading the Future) and the safety-side network (Public First Action)
  spent heavily in the same race. Lasher, victory speech: *"I won't be taking my cues from either of you when it comes
  to protecting our kids, our jobs, our environment."*
  https://www.transformernews.ai/p/alex-bores-defeat-micah-lasher-ai-leading-the-future-public-first
- **The money, at the FEC rather than in press releases.** Leading the Future (C00916114) reported **$75,788,224 raised
  and $31,032,443 cash on hand through 30 June 2026**, with $40M moved to affiliated committees.
  https://www.fec.gov/data/committee/C00916114/?tab=raising Anthropic gave Public First Action a second $20M on
  21 July 2026, cumulative $40M, stating the money *"cannot be used to influence the election of any candidate."*
  https://www.anthropic.com/news/donation-public-first-action
- **Both parties' candidates are running against the industry, not for it.** Michigan Senate: Republican Mike Rogers
  -- *"I believe we must slow down development of AI"*; Democrat Abdul El-Sayed -- *"We need to all hit pause."*
  Rogers backed a statewide data center moratorium around 20 Aug 2026, days after disclosures showed $1.7M-$2.6M in
  AI-exposed holdings. https://michiganadvance.com/2026/08/20/after-data-center-investments-revealed-rogers-backs-statewide-moratorium/
- **Georgia's Public Service Commission is on the 3 November ballot** with two seats and the 3-2 Republican majority
  at stake, and both parties' candidates are running on making data centers pay.
  https://news.ballotpedia.org/2026/08/10/partisan-control-of-georgia-public-service-commission-at-stake-in-november-election/
- **AI-made advertising is the real deepfake story.** Wesleyan Media Project counted 33 AI-generated or AI-enhanced
  political ads by late May 2026 and **164 by mid-September**; fewer than half carried a disclaimer, and the May cohort
  was 70 percent Republican-sponsored. Scott Babwah Brennen, NYU Center on Technology Policy, 16 Sep 2026: *"The
  problem of deceptive deepfakes hasn't really materialized as a widescale problem. But something else is happening."*
  https://www.npr.org/2026/09/16/nx-s1-5953990/ai-political-ads-2026-midterms

### What is contested

- **Who benefits.** Ad spending is near-even, Wesleyan reads Democrats as using environmental framing and Republicans
  anti-big-corporation framing, and NYT/Siena still gives Republicans a within-margin edge on trust to handle AI.
  **There is no settled answer. Do not assert one.**
- **Leading the Future's funding shape.** Public Citizen describes roughly $50M as an Andreessen Horowitz corporate
  contribution; the FEC filing records $75.1M in **individual** contributions and no corporate money. Both cannot be
  right. Likeliest reconciliation is partners giving personally and being attributed by employer.
- **Network totals.** $75.8M federal (FEC) versus $125M-$140M network figures in press coverage. These measure
  different things and are routinely conflated.

## Strand 2 — Data centers as the local issue

### What is solid

- **Public opinion moved 33 points in twelve months.** Heatmap Pro / Embold Research, fielded 8-13 Aug 2026,
  n = 2,045 registered voters, margin ±2.3, verified at the primary 20 Sep: **75 percent oppose** local data center
  development; strong support fell from 13 percent to 4 percent; August 2025 was 43 support / 42 oppose. Net
  opposition: Republicans −43, independents −65, Democrats −75, rural voters −63.
  https://heatmap.news/daily/data-center-opposition-poll-collapse
- **Corroborated by two other pollsters.** Gallup (2-18 Mar 2026, n = 1,000): 71 percent oppose one locally, including
  63 percent of Republicans. Economist/YouGov (28-31 Aug 2026): 63 percent would oppose one locally, and **60 percent
  believe data centers would raise their electricity bills.**
- **Congress passed something and then did not.** H.R. 9340, the Ratepayer Protection Act, passed the House
  **417 to 3 on 16 September 2026**, verified at the House Energy and Commerce majority's own post. It applies to
  loads above **100 MW** and requires state commissions to **consider** -- not adopt -- standards making large loads
  pay their full incremental cost.
  https://energycommerce.house.gov/posts/ratepayer-protection-act-passes-house-with-strong-bipartisan-support
  **The next day, 17 September, Sen. Martin Heinrich (D-NM) blocked it on a unanimous consent request** -- *"Congress
  needs to pass real legislation with real teeth"* -- and Sen. Bernie Moreno (R-OH) then blocked Heinrich's own GRID
  Savings Act by the same mechanism. Moreno: *"The term do-nothing Congress exists for a reason. We absolutely
  accomplished that right now."* Neither bill advanced.
  https://www.cbsnews.com/news/senate-data-center-energy-bill-martin-heinrich/
- **Local bodies are deciding it anyway.** Verified rejections and pauses include Pittsylvania County VA (6-1 deny,
  16 Apr 2025, killing a project projected at up to $120M a year in county tax revenue); Tucson AZ (7-0 reject,
  6 Aug 2025); Prince William County VA (8-0 deny on ~2,000 acres, 8 Jul 2026); Montgomery County MD (**11-0 for an
  18-month moratorium, 28 Jul 2026**); Linn County IA, Boone County IN, York County SC, Imperial County CA and
  Mercer County ND all pausing in 2026.
  https://www.montgomerycountymd.gov/news/council-approves-18-month-data-center-moratorium-places-limitations-data-center-development
- **Voters overrule their own boards when they get the chance.** Monterey Park CA passed Measure NDC banning data
  centers with **over 86 percent** on 2 June 2026. Augusta Township MI **overturned** a rezoning for a $1B project by
  roughly **90 percent** on 4 Aug 2026, and residents then filed to recall all seven board members. Port Washington WI
  passed a citizen initiative requiring a vote on TIF districts over $10M, 66-34, on 7 Apr 2026.
  https://lapublicpress.org/2026/06/monterey-park-data-center-ban-elections-2026/ ·
  https://www.wemu.org/wemu-news/2026-08-05/augusta-township-voters-overturn-data-center-rezoning
- **Incumbents are losing over it, in both primaries.** Box Elder County UT: two Republican commissioners beaten on
  23 June 2026 over the Stratos campus; outgoing commissioner Lee Perry: *"Do I think that the data center vote cost
  me the election? Yes I do."* Calvert County MD: three Republican incumbents lost to challengers backing a pause.
  Frederick County MD: the Democratic council president lost. Festus MO: four incumbents lost, including **both** the
  yes-voters and a no-voter, which points at process anger rather than position. Data Center Knowledge counts
  **18 incumbent losses between August 2025 and August 2026**, with its own caveat that evidentiary strength varies.
- **Regulators are already moving without Congress.** Virginia SCC created a **GS-5 rate class for customers
  requesting 25 MW or more** effective 1 Jan 2027 (order 25 Nov 2025). Pennsylvania PUC adopted a large-load tariff
  framework 5-0 on 30 Apr 2026. Oregon PUC approved PGE's Schedule 96 on 7 May 2026 with a 20 MW threshold, terms up
  to 30 years and a 90 percent minimum demand charge. Indiana's IURC order of 20 Dec 2024 is the template the others
  cite. **300-plus data center bills were introduced across more than 30 states in the first six weeks of 2026**
  (MultiState, 20 Feb 2026).
- **Virginia taxed it.** The $205B budget approved 22 June 2026 created a **$0.011 per kWh** data center electricity
  tax effective 1 July 2026, applying to self-generated as well as utility power.
- **Governors of both parties are running against unconditional support.** Abbott (R-TX): data centers must *"bring
  their own power, reuse their own water and do it in a way that reduces the cost."* Kotek (D-OR): Oregon *"needs to
  stop being a cheap date."* Ayotte (R-NH): *"It does not make sense at all to site a data center in New Hampshire."*
  https://www.multistate.us/insider/2026/9/1/gubernatorial-candidates-are-speaking-out-on-data-centers

### What is contested, and this is the part that protects the piece

- ⛔ **"Data centers raised your bill" is a partial claim, not a settled one.** PJM's independent market monitor puts
  data centers at **9 percent of total PJM wholesale costs** in Jan-July 2026 ($5.1B of $56.7B) and credits data
  center load with **$29.4B** of capacity revenue increases across four auctions. A reconstruction of PJM's internal
  monitor finds data centers explain **53-64 percent of the capacity price spike**, the rest being forecast error,
  methodology changes and retirements. **And PJM's most recent auction, announced 14 July 2026, cleared 2.5 percent
  LOWER than the year before.**
- ⛔ **The strongest public version of the claim has been fact-checked down.** PolitiFact, 12 June 2026, rated
  **Mostly False** Sen. Elizabeth Warren's statement that bills near large data centers rose "as much as 267%": the
  figure described **wholesale nodal prices, not residential bills**, and wholesale is 30-50 percent of a retail bill.
  https://politifact.com/factchecks/2026/jun/12/elizabeth-warren/data-centers-rising-electricity-costs/
- **Utilities are filing testimony that large loads will LOWER residential bills** -- Indiana Michigan Power projects
  about $100 a year in savings for a typical residential customer, OG&E/Google projects $1.83 a month by 2027. The
  source's own caveat is the honest one: these are *"projections, not promised reductions in monthly bills."*
- **The tax-base case is real where it has been measured.** Virginia's JLARC found data centers supply **31 percent of
  Loudoun County's local tax revenue** ($733M) and support about **74,000 jobs** statewide -- while also projecting
  unconstrained growth adds **$23 a month by 2030 and $37 by 2040** to a typical residential bill. Both findings are
  in the same study.
- **Water is the weakest strand.** JLARC, Dec 2024: **83 percent of Virginia data centers used the same water as or
  less than an average large office building**, and data centers were under 0.5 percent of state withdrawals. The
  water fights are real politically; the statewide numbers do not carry the alarm.
- **Abatements are the counter-number to the tax base.** Good Jobs First, June 2026: Georgia $2.5B forgone FY2026,
  Virginia about $1.9B FY2025, Texas $1.3B FY2026, Ohio $1.6B FY2025, and **fourteen states publish no annual
  revenue-loss figure at all.** Do not net these against JLARC's gross revenue figures; they measure different things.

## Strand 3 — The scale mismatch, which is the spine

This strand is synthesis rather than separate research, and it rests on the contrast between two verified facts:

- **Argued globally.** The national argument is about frontier risk, pacing, preemption and an antitrust shield, and
  as of this week it has produced no law. The House's 417-3 bill died on a unanimous consent objection the following
  day, with both parties blocking each other's version.
- **Voted locally.** In the same twelve months, an 11-0 moratorium in Montgomery County, an 86 percent ban in
  Monterey Park, a 90 percent rezoning reversal in Augusta Township, and eighteen incumbents turned out.

**The mechanism worth naming: AI became a local election issue the moment it needed land, power and cameras.** The
argument a reader can actually vote on is a substation, a rezoning, a water permit and a camera on a pole -- and those
are decided by bodies that meet on a Tuesday night, publish an agenda and answer a roll call. ⚠️ **This is adjacent to
Sunday's standing argument** (notice, a place to argue, a date, the facts) and must not restate it. The distinction to
hold: Sunday was about whether there is a route at all; Tuesday is about which level of government is actually using
the one it has.

## Strand 4 — Flock, and the strange bedfellows

### What is solid

- **Scale and the climbdown.** Flock raised **$500M at roughly $8.3B on 31 Aug 2026**, up from $275M at $7.5B in March
  2025, citing 5,000-plus agencies and more than 130,000 cameras. On **30 July 2026** Flock told Fox News its default
  retention was **30 days**; by **17 August** the default was **7 days**, verified at Flock's own trust page on 20 Sep:
  *"In most communities, that's 7 days unless local law says otherwise"*, and *"Every search is tied to a specific user
  and recorded in a log."* The August package also added offense filtering (letting a community block searches for
  specified purposes including immigration enforcement), mandatory case codes, and automatic suspension of anomalous
  accounts. https://www.flocksafety.com/trust/data-privacy
- **The audit record, which is what actually moves councils.** Pflugerville TX: a records request showed **459 outside
  agencies ran about 1.6 million searches** against the city's 28 cameras over six months, versus **5,078** searches by
  local police; the council ended the contract unanimously on 25 Aug 2026. Texas DPS: about **615,000 searches in June
  2026 alone**, 48 percent originating outside Texas. Johnson County TX, April 2025: a deputy ran a search across
  **6,809 networks and 83,345 cameras** logged *"had an abortion, search for female"* -- the sheriff says it was a
  welfare search, the affidavit describes a death investigation, and the DA advised no charge was possible.
  https://www.techbrew.com/stories/flock-pflugerville-texas-data-outside-agencies ·
  https://www.texastribune.org/2026/09/04/texas-flock-cameras-dps-network-search-abbott/
- **Republican state power turned against a police vendor.** Abbott (R-TX), late Aug 2026: *"Flock cameras are being
  reduced dramatically. They're going to be required to be used responsibly if they're used at all."* DeSantis (R-FL),
  Sept 2026: *"I'm all about having law enforcement have tools. But I don't want to have this become a surveillance
  state."* Sen. Josh Hawley (R-MO) opened a Judiciary subcommittee probe on 26 Aug 2026: *"Americans do not surrender
  their privacy rights when they drive to work, drop their kids off at school, or go to church."*
  https://www.texastribune.org/2026/08/28/texas-greg-abbott-flock-cameras-order-state-money/
- **The bill is the bedfellows in one line.** H.R. 10221, the Flock-Off Act, introduced 2 Sep 2026 by **Rep. Thomas
  Massie (R-KY)** with **Boebert (R), Gosar (R), Roy (R), Spartz (R) and Ro Khanna (D)**. Massie: *"The federal
  government shouldn't provide 'security' grants to cities and police departments that have gone beyond the pale and
  turned their communities into a version of 1984."* https://www.govtrack.us/congress/bills/119/hr10221/text
- **The litigation inversion.** The Fourth Amendment cases are being run by the **Institute for Justice** (libertarian,
  *Schmidt v. Norfolk*, lost at district court 27 Jan 2026, on appeal) and the **Wisconsin Institute for Law & Liberty**
  (conservative, suing 11 Waukesha County municipalities on 10 Sep 2026 and asking for a warrant requirement, not
  removal), while the ACLU and EFF do organizing and records work. https://will-law.org/new-will-lawsuit-targets-warrantless-no-limits-government-surveillance
- **The gun-rights frame, which is the sharpest right-wing version.** National Association for Gun Rights DeFlock
  America petition: *"No warrant. No suspicion. No vote."* NAGR's Taylor D. Rhodes, 26 Aug 2026, argues the network
  creates a searchable archive of which vehicles with Second Amendment decals visit gun stores and ranges.
- **Councils are ending contracts across the political map**: Oak Park IL (5-2, Aug 2025), Bend OR (Jan 2026, after
  279 federal immigration queries in three weeks), Denver CO (Feb 2026, a 10-year $22M contract), Harrisonburg VA
  (4-0), Pflugerville TX (unanimous), Asheville NC (5-2), **Knox County TN (unanimous emergency ordinance, 31 Aug
  2026, under Republican Mayor Glenn Jacobs)**, Lynchburg VA (6-0), Sheboygan WI (unanimous), Liberty Hill TX (7-0),
  Waukesha WI (16 Sep 2026).
- **Public opinion crossed over.** Two surveys published 28 Aug 2026 both show **38 percent support**: YouGov
  (n about 20,000) at 46 oppose / 38 support, and Ipsos for Reuters (n = 1,167) at 47 / 38. A year earlier a majority
  favored the technology.

### Where the split does NOT hold, and the piece needs this

- **Cleveland kept its cameras.** Democratic Mayor Justin Bibb backed Flock and the council voted **9-6 to renew** on
  16 July 2026, despite records showing about 2,000 immigration-related searches in 2025.
- **Oklahoma City renewed 5-3** on 18 Aug 2026 with no member of the public speaking in favor.
- **Carrollton TX expanded** on 16 Sep 2026 -- 82 cameras, about $398,000 a year. Police Chief Roberto Arredondo:
  *"It's the best tool we have to reduce crime."*
- **Denver replaced Flock with Axon**, which the ACLU also opposed. Ending a Flock contract is not ending automated
  plate surveillance.
- **Trump backs the cameras**: *"I sort of like them because of that, because of law enforcement."*
- **Texas DPS kept operating about 940 cameras after Abbott's order**, which constrained new state spending rather
  than the existing network.
- **Republican voters are the group most in favor.** In the Reuters/Ipsos survey, Republicans and over-55s supported
  the cameras while Democrats and independents opposed. **Elite Republican opposition is running ahead of Republican
  voters, and that is a more interesting sentence than the coalition story.**

### The money on Flock

Per camera per year runs roughly **$2,500 to $3,750**, median about $3,000. Pflugerville paid **$87,500 a year for 28
cameras**. Denver's cancelled contract was **10 years and $22M**; its Axon replacement was **$150,000 for one year and
50 cameras** with 21-day retention and no federal access. Knox County had spent **$800,000** with a **$2.6M** projected
maintenance ask that had never gone to commissioners, and that procedural failure, not privacy, is what triggered the
unanimous ban. Texas funds its cameras through a **$1 levy on every auto insurance policy** under a 2023 law, which
means nobody ever voted for the cameras. Asheville terminated a contract covering **11 city cameras** while **86
cameras run by other entities inside city limits** were untouched.

## The economics, in one place

- PJM capacity: $329.17/MW-day for 2026/27 (cap), $333.44 for 2027/28, **$325 for 2028/29, down 2.5 percent**.
- Data center share of PJM wholesale costs Jan-July 2026: **9 percent, $5.1B of $56.7B**. Four-auction capacity
  revenue increase attributed to data center load: **$29.4B**.
- Dominion residential increase approved Nov 2025: **+$11.24 a month in 2026, +$2.36 in 2027.**
- JLARC projection, unconstrained growth: **+$23 a month by 2030, +$37 by 2040** on a $90 base.
- US average residential electricity rate: **+7.1 percent in 2025, +42 percent over five years** (EIA).
- Flock: about **$3,000 per camera per year**; a town pays that so that, in Pflugerville's case, 459 other agencies
  can run 1.6 million searches against its residents.
