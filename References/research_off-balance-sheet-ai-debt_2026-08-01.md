## Bottom line
Your memory was right and slightly understated. The piece is **Nikkei Asia, "Five US tech giants'
hidden debts soar to $1.65tn on opaque AI funding"** (~22 Jul 2026). The finding: **Alphabet,
Microsoft, Amazon, Meta and Oracle** are carrying an estimated **$1.65 trillion of off-balance-sheet
AI-related debt** -- grown ~**8x since 2022** -- and, the killer line, that hidden pile is **larger
than their combined ON-balance-sheet debt of ~$1.35 trillion.** So the debt investors *can't* see in a
standard ratio is bigger than the debt they can. It's financed through exactly the SPV-style
structures you suspected: long-term data-centre leases, GPU supply commitments, and joint ventures
with private-credit funds. **One honesty flag:** this is Nikkei's own analysis/estimate (heavily
syndicated, not independently audited), so attribute it to Nikkei rather than asserting it as
established fact -- and "hidden"/"Enron" is partly commentators' gloss (these are disclosed but
off-balance-sheet commitments; Nikkei's own historical parallel is the early-2000s telecom buildout).

## What's solid (Nikkei study, via multiple syndications)
- **$1.65 trillion** in off-balance-sheet debt across **five US tech giants: Alphabet, Microsoft,
  Amazon, Meta, Oracle**; **~8x growth since 2022.** [Nikkei Asia, ~22 Jul 2026; corroborated by
  Futurism, The Next Web, Yahoo Finance, Analysis.org, explainx, TFTC -- all tracing to the Nikkei study]
- **The hidden exceeds the visible:** the $1.65T off-balance-sheet figure is *larger* than the five
  firms' combined **on-balance-sheet debt of ~$1.35 trillion**. "The liabilities investors can't see in
  a standard debt-to-equity ratio are larger than the ones they can." [Analysis.org, summarising Nikkei]
- **The mechanism (the "SPV" part):** the debt is kept off the parent balance sheet via long-term
  data-centre leases, GPU supply commitments, JVs with private-credit funds, and pre-operational lease
  structures. Named examples: **Meta / Blue Owl ($27B JV)** + the Hyperion campus (Louisiana);
  **Alphabet / Blackstone JV** + a **$30B equity derivative** for AI-infra financing; **Oracle**
  project-finance packages on AI sites in Texas and Wisconsin. [Analysis.org, summarising Nikkei]
- **How an SPV keeps debt off the books (mechanism, general):** the parent doesn't borrow directly;
  a separate entity (JV / project-finance vehicle / leasing co, often with a private-credit partner)
  raises the debt and owns the asset, and the parent signs a long-term lease or purchase commitment.
  The obligation is real and disclosed in footnotes, but it doesn't land in the headline debt-to-equity
  ratio the way a bond would. That's the "opaque" in "opaque AI funding."

## What's contested / watch out
- **Single-origin analysis, not an audit or a filing.** Every outlet traces to the one Nikkei study;
  the $1.65T is Nikkei's *estimate* built on disclosures, not a regulator's or auditor's number.
  **Attribute to Nikkei in the text** ("Nikkei estimates," "by Nikkei's analysis") -- do not assert it
  bare. This is exactly the kind of number a CFO-type reader will want sourced.
- **"Hidden" / "Enron" is provocative and partly editorial.** These are *disclosed* off-balance-sheet
  commitments (legal, footnoted), not concealed fraud. Several outlets reach for the Enron accounting
  parallel; Nikkei's own comparison is the **early-2000s telecom buildout** (vendor-financed circular
  capital flows that unwound badly). The defensible framing is "off-balance-sheet / not visible in the
  standard ratio," and the honest historical rhyme is telecom, not (quite) Enron. If you want the Enron
  punch, attribute it as the commentators' read, not the finding.
- **Numbers move and analysts count differently** -- capex/financing figures in this space are
  re-estimated constantly. Date the claim ("as of Nikkei's late-July analysis").

## The economics (this IS the economics beat)
Put the two layers together and the scale is the story. On-balance-sheet: ~$1.35T visible debt, and
~$725B of 2026 hyperscaler capex (up 77% YoY -- that figure is already in your corpus,
`ai_brief_2026-07-18`, via The AI Corner). Off-balance-sheet: another ~$1.65T financed through SPVs,
leases and private-credit JVs, grown eightfold in three years. So the reported trillion-dollar buildout
is roughly the *visible half* of the bet; the other half is structured so it doesn't show up where
investors usually look. When the vendor-financing gets circular -- the chip maker funds the neocloud
that buys the chips that back the loan -- that's the telecom-2001 pattern, and it's the fragility
under the concentration.
