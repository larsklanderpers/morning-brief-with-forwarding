# Week Plan — Aug 3–9, 2026

**Structure:** one Sunday capstone with three tributaries running Mon–Fri, a Saturday
diversion that renders the thesis at human scale, and the capstone that resolves it.

**Capstone spine (Sun Aug 9):** *Guardrails Are How You Give It Away.* Provable control is
the precondition for distributing power, not its enemy. Open weights, local inference and
sovereignty aren't the risk to freedom — they're the version of control that doesn't route
through one vendor's kill switch.

**The foil:** Altman on the *Relentless* podcast (28 Jul): "the fight of the current
moment is: Are we going to head to a world of AI authoritarianism or liberty?" His freedom =
*fewer fences* (deregulation). The week argues the opposite: the right fence is how you
safely hand people powerful tools. *(Quote is from secondary coverage — pull primary before
it ships.)*

**Live hook:** EU AI Act high-risk obligations date lands **Sun Aug 2**, the eve of the week.

## Sat Aug 8 — Diversion: Own the kitchen (build your own rung)
- **Register:** light / hands-on / doom-relief. The thesis at human scale — the reader
  personally doing the sovereign thing this weekend.
- **Thesis (locked — Lars 2026-07-29):** It's less about full-local than about *how you do
  it, period.* Taking back control of your inference is a **ladder of on-ramps**, cheapest
  first. *"The cost of curiosity is approaching zero; the cost of tooling is not. Be
  prudent. Leverage what's already there."* If it's $20/month to start, spend six months,
  then figure out how to get it onto your laptop when it earns its place.
- **The on-ramp ladder (cheapest → most sovereign):**
  1. Routing/aggregator (OpenRouter-style) — pin your model, pay per token; the ~$20/mo
     "curiosity" rung.
  2. Hosted open-weight inference (an open model served for you — e.g. Hermes *models*
     via Nous Portal / Together / Fireworks). NB: the Hermes *Agent* is a different, more
     advanced thing — see bridge below.
  3. Local on a MacBook (Apple Silicon unified memory — Rex runs one for his golf trainer,
     so it's real) — small quantized Gemma/Qwen/Mistral via Ollama / LM Studio.
  4. 24GB workstation — serious on-prem (the MarkTechPost tier below).
- **Honesty:** full frontier-on-a-laptop is a push; be precise about what a MacBook
  actually runs well and what it's good for (Kimi "own the kitchen vs 1.4TB"). Empirical
  anchor now solid — 16GB Mac runs Qwen 9B @ 20–40 tok/s; 24GB does flagship coding
  (Willison, 25.57 tok/s). The [VERIFY]'d ceiling numbers are about the *serving* tier and
  stay OUT of this piece. See `References\research_inference-onramp-ladder_2026-07-29.md`.
- **Control honesty (the Q4 answer):** an aggregator (OpenRouter) buys *visibility +
  configurability* — you can pin the exact model and enforce ZDR, which is the direct fix
  for Monday's "you didn't get the model you paid for" — but providers still *see* the
  data; real containment (data never leaves) only starts at the local rung. "Take back
  control" is a spectrum, not a switch.
- **★ The Hermes dual-use bridge (Saturday ↔ Monday ↔ capstone) — confirmed:** the Hermes
  Lars would recommend is the *same* open-source Nous Research agent that ran YOLO-mode
  post-exploitation at the Thai Finance Ministry (The Hacker News / BleepingComputer:
  "an open-source assistant… It is not a hacking tool"). `--yolo` is a documented flag that
  turns off the approval prompts; it runs locally with no vendor to watch or ban it. The
  flag *is* the guardrail — the capstone thesis in one command-line switch. **Handling:**
  recommend the *ladder* (rung 0 → OpenRouter → local model in LM Studio); treat **Hermes
  Agent as the cautionary top rung, not a beginner's first step** — installing an
  autonomous agent that runs code and reads mail is a big ask, and the honest version comes
  with the YOLO story attached. How prominently to play the bridge = Lars's creative call.
- **Anchor (located):** MarkTechPost, *Best Local LLMs You Can Run on a Single 24GB GPU in
