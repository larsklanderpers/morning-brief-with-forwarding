**The rule: once Lars has answered something, it is answered. Do not re-surface it in a question widget, do not re-confirm it in prose, do not ask it a second way.**

Logged 5 Aug 2026. He had said "tuesday the 11th" for a piece's slot. A later `AskUserQuestion` included a slot question again (with different options). His response: *"you were stuck in a confirmation loop that's why i dropped it"* — he disconnected the device bridge to break out of it. He then repeated "tuesday the 11th for the piece" a third time before work resumed.

**Why:** re-asking reads as not having listened, and it spends his attention on something already settled. He is fast, decisive, and working against a daily publishing cadence; a question he has to answer twice is pure tax. It also stalls the actual work, which is the thing he wants.

**How to apply:**

- Before any `AskUserQuestion` call, scan the conversation for whether he has already answered any of the questions in it. Drop those questions. If that empties the widget, do not call the tool.
- Prefer **one** question round per decision point. Bundle genuinely open forks into a single call rather than trickling them.
- When a fork has an obvious right answer from context, **make the call, state it in one line, and start working** — with an explicit "unless you'd rather X" so he can redirect cheaply. He responds well to this: on the Monday 10 Aug piece I proposed the topic, named the alternative in a sentence, and began research without waiting. No friction.
- Editorial forks he genuinely owns (which spine, which slot, whether a beat is his) get asked **once**. Mechanical choices (file naming, index formatting, which analogy avoids a collision) do not get asked at all.
- If he asks a question mid-work ("what are we doing on Monday?"), that is an invitation to propose, not a request for options. Answer with a recommendation and reasoning, then move.

**Related:** the interviewer stage still needs his first-person material — that is not a confirmation, it is the meat, and asking for it is correct. But ask it in open prose, once per round, and let it sit. On 5 Aug he said of an unanswered [YOUR TAKE] prompt: *"i haven't seen that question, sorry, i will deal with it in the draft, for sure."* Leaving the marker in the draft and moving on is the working pattern — he fills them at his edit pass.
