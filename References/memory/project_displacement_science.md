## ❌ TWO MOVES ARE DEAD. Do not draft them.

1. **"Fourth consecutive LARGE downward benchmark."** The yr-to-Mar-2026 preliminary came in at
   **−79,000 (−0.1%)** — about **9% of last year's −911,000** and a quarter of BLS's 10-yr average.
2. **The "benchmark dwarfs the AI effect" device.** Ran **8.08×** in 2025; runs **0.70×** now. Wrong
   direction. The §2.6 cold-open framing goes with it.

⚠️ **And never write "even the benchmark shows the white-collar sectors were overstated."**
**Information — the most AI-exposed supersector — was revised UP 87,000 (+3.0%)**, the largest
percentage revision in the table. The downward revisions are retail and education/health.

## ★★ The argument is STRONGER, on better evidence — three replacements

1. **The benchmark's point estimate is smaller than its own historical error.** −79,000 against the
   2024 preliminary-to-final miss of **220,000** — an error band ~**2× the entire claimed AI effect**
   (112,713). BLS calls it "a preliminary measure of the **total error**" and, unlike last year,
   **offers no decomposition at all.**
2. **★★ THE STRONGEST RESULT IN THE THREAD — the Displaced Worker Survey cannot record an AI reason.**
   Complete closed-response set: *plant or company closed down or moved · insufficient work · position
   or shift abolished · seasonal job completed · self-operated business failed · some other reason.*
   **No technology, automation or AI category. No causal-mechanism dimension at all.** A job taken by
   a language model codes as **"position or shift abolished"** — same bin as offshoring, restructuring
   and PE cost programmes — and that bin is **44.4% of long-tenured displaced workers**. **No sample
   size fixes a missing question.** BLS mentions AI/automation/technology **zero times** in the
   release and technical note.
3. **BLS says the projections instrument is non-diagnostic, in writing** — it declined to model AI's
   productivity effects because doing so *"would be highly speculative."*

## ⚠️ Three counterweights the draft MUST carry, or it reads as denial

- **The DWS results are not null.** Long-tenured displacement **up ~29%**; reemployment at
  equal-or-better pay **fell ~62% → ~49%**. Something real happened. The claim is that the instruments
  cannot **attribute** it. **Silence is not evidence the effect is small.**
- **BLS DOES name AI in prose, twelve times, both directions.** Sustainable version: *BLS makes
  qualitative, hedged, directional judgements about AI while explicitly declining to quantify it.*
  Its own verb asymmetry: AI *"is expected to support"* demand (confident) vs *"may dampen"* it
  (hedged). ❌ **Never print "BLS says AI will cut 752,100 office jobs"** — BLS says *"automation
  tools, including those powered by AI"* are *"likely to reduce"* demand for *"several"* occupations.
- **⚠️ §5.3's DENOMINATOR NEEDS RECONCILING.** BLS projects the three SOC groups that note uses
  **GROWING +279,500 (+0.78%)** 2025–2035, composition shifting clerical → analytical. Compositional,
  not contraction. Fix before publishing.

## Two spare arguments the releases handed over

- **Scale mismatch from BLS's own tables:** the **top ten fastest-declining occupations lose ~78,400
  jobs over TEN YEARS** — less than the seven-month Challenger figure. Percent-change rankings are not
  magnitude rankings.
- **The declining-occupation roster is the pre-AI automation roster** — word processors, typists,
  switchboard operators, data entry keyers, declining on trend for twenty years.
