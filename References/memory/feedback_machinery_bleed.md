**The rule.** Before drafting day N, read the FINISHED TEXT of every piece within seven days on the
same axis — not the plan's summary of them, not the imagery list. What collides is not only the
controlling metaphor ([[metaphor-bleed]]); it is the **action move** (the thing the piece tells the
reader to go and do, and the device it uses to tell them), the **close**, and — added 13 Sep — the
bare **syntax** of either.

**Why.** Established 23 Aug 2026 on the Tue 1 Sep block of `week-plan_2026-08-31.md`. The plan had
flagged Tue 1 as "the exposed slot" and correctly named the risk as three consecutive Tuesdays on
operational agent practice. The plan was wrong about what would collide. The theses were genuinely
distinct (define what right looks like / limit what it can reach / could you actually tell). What had
been spent was the **apparatus**: Tue 25's finished text already carried "write down what had to be
true for it to earn that," "write down what would send it back down," "do it per task, not per agent,"
and "the human is in the loop as much to take the blame as they are to provide surety." That last one
pre-empted Tue 1's DeepMind authority/accountability beat outright. None of it was visible from the
plan block — only from the draft. Tue 18 and Tue 25 had ALSO both ended on a tonight-header
("Twenty minutes tonight" / "What to do tonight"), so a third was a slot, not a series.

**★ THE CLOSES ARE THEIR OWN CHECK, added 30 Aug 2026 — and this one produced a CATEGORICAL FAIL that
two separate week plans missed.** Mon 31's drafted close: *"Who in that room can tell a good answer
from a confident one."* Sat 29, two days earlier, closes: *"you will be the only person in the room
who knows."* Same image, same payoff position. The Sat 29 file had explicitly asserted Monday's close
was protected — but it had only checked the *formulation* ("a good answer from a confident one"),
never the room the formulation was standing in. **A plan can bless a close and still be wrong about
it, because plans compare planned wordings and readers experience finished ones.** Caught at panel
Round 1, not at plan.

## ★★ SHARED SYNTAX IS A COLLISION. 13 Sep 2026, and it beat three separate sweeps.

Mon 14 was the most heavily guarded boundary of the month. **Three sessions checked it: on THESIS
(Sunday is four instruments that cannot resolve a question; Monday is one instrument being revised in
public), on CONTROLLING IMAGE (truck scale versus dictionary edition), and on ACTION VERB (Sunday
READ, Monday WRITE). All three held.** The piece was built deliberately so that the words *count,
measure, instrument, denominator* and *ruler* appear nowhere in Monday's body, and that was verified
mechanically.

**The panel failed it categorically anyway, on the one thing nobody had diffed.**

- Sun 13: *"You do not need permission, a subscription, an analyst or a vendor briefing to read it...
  on a fixed date and it is free."*
- Mon 14, the next morning: *"You do not need standing, an institution or a submission template"* plus
  *"the form is free."*

**A negated-prerequisite tricolon followed by a free-and-dated pairing, in the same slot, on
consecutive days, with ZERO SHARED SUBJECT WORDS.** Every sweep that looked for a repeated noun, a
repeated image or a repeated verb passed it, because there was nothing lexical to find. What repeated
was the **shape of the sentence and the rhythm of the reassurance** — which is exactly what a reader
who has just read yesterday's piece actually notices.

★ **The general form: a collision can live one level up from vocabulary.** Diffing words catches
metaphor bleed. Diffing verbs catches machinery bleed. **Neither catches a borrowed cadence.** When
two adjacent pieces both hand the reader a low-friction instruction, the risk is not that they name
the same thing — it is that they *sound* like the same paragraph.

**How to apply.**
1. When two pieces inside the window share an axis, diff their ACTION SECTIONS first, before the theses.
2. **Read the last two sentences of every piece in the window out loud, next to each other.** Register,
   image and payoff position all have to differ — not just the wording. A protected formulation can
   still collide on its setting, its noun, or its posture.
3. ★★ **Then diff the SHAPES, ignoring the words entirely.** Strip both action sections and both closes
   to their skeletons — "negated tricolon + reassurance", "question + date", "imperative + cost" — and
   compare those. **If the skeletons match, it is a collision even when no word is shared.** Watch
   especially for the reassurance move ("you do not need X, Y or Z"), which is seductive precisely
   because it is invisible to a word-level check.
4. Watch for a body passage that pre-states its own close. Mon 31 ran "cannot tell a good answer from
   a confident one" mid-piece AND at the close — a competing landing sitting on top of the collision.
   One formulation, one place, and the close gets it.
5. Ask what device the reader is being handed. Three consecutive "write down X" closes is the formula
   failure his charter names, even when the three X's differ. The fix is a different *kind* of
   exercise (a sort, a read-back, a single named check), not a reworded write-down.
6. A thesis surviving is not the same as a piece surviving. Re-pitch: keep the spine, strip the spent
   machinery, and let the beat that is genuinely unspent carry the weight.
7. This is a SEPARATE check from imagery and from [[forward-dated-drafts]]. Imagery bleed is a word.
   Machinery bleed is a move. A close collision is a posture. **A syntax collision is a rhythm.**
   Plan-thesis refutation is research. All of them run.
8. ⚠️ **The later piece resolves it.** Monday recast, not Sunday, because Sunday had already gone to
   Scheduled. Check the window against `Substack Scheduled\` and `Substack Published\` as FINAL text —
   Sunday's scheduled version had grown substantially past the draft an earlier sweep had read.
