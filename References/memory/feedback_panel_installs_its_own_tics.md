**Rule: after the panel applies or proposes replacement text, re-run the repetition sweep over the panel's own words. A seat that removes a tic can install a new one in the fix, and nothing in the loop catches it.**

**Why:** On 10 Sep 2026, Round 3 on the Astra piece found the construction *"the part that…"* at five occurrences — over the charter's threshold of three. **Four of the five were drafter or panel text, and two had been written by the panel itself in earlier rounds**, including *"the part that is thinning"* (a replacement the panel wrote for a cut sentence) and *"the part I find troubling"* (the panel's own relocation of one of Lars's lines). The panel had retired a different formula in an earlier round and seeded this one in the replacements. It only surfaced because a later round counted the construction from scratch rather than diffing against what it had already changed.

This is a blind spot with a specific shape: **a seat that is optimizing away repetition is producing new text under time pressure, in one voice, across many small edits in the same pass — which is exactly the condition that generates a house tic.** The drafter's tics get counted. The panel's do not, because the panel is the thing doing the counting.

## ★★ SECOND CASE, 12 Sep 2026 — the Sat 12 Grok Bot piece. Four rounds, and the fix was the defect three rounds running.

The categorical gate failed **three consecutive times**, and **four of the nine total hits were the panel's own replacement text**:

- **Round 2** rewrote the opener to clear `"Last Saturday … This Saturday"` (a Sat 5 collision) and **landed on Fri 11's frame instead** — `"A week ago I wrote about…"` against Friday's `"Yesterday I wrote about…"`, one day apart, same slot. Round 2 also copied a **fourteen-word frame verbatim off one of Lars's own sentences** (`"which matters more than it sounds like it should once…"`) into a new paragraph 200 lines away.
- **Round 3** cut thirteen words into the security paragraph and made `"this is where"` a **third** instance eight lines from another one; and added a fifteen-minute-schedule warning that **already existed two sections earlier**, same interval, same consequence, in better words.

### The two new rules this case adds

1. **A RELOCATED FRAME IS NOT A RETIRED FRAME.** Round 2 moved the Sat 5 hinge out of paragraph 3 and into the close. It failed again in the new position, because the collision was with the *shape* (compress last week's thesis, pivot to this week's), not with the slot. When a frame is flagged, ask what the frame IS and kill that — do not relocate it and re-run.
2. **DIFF EVERY REPLACEMENT AGAINST THE WHOLE DRAFT, NOT AGAINST THE LINE IT REPLACES.** Three of the four panel-caused hits were invisible locally and obvious globally: a duplicate warning two sections up, a scaffold at two elsewhere in the file, a frame lifted off the author's own prose. Before writing a replacement in, grep the draft for its distinctive four-to-six word span.

### And one thing to resist

By round 4 the seat was returning findings of genuinely diminishing value, and one of its proposed fixes **reintroduced the exact cardinal-of-cardinal header form it had failed the piece for in round 2**. It had also misquoted the window file it was comparing against. **Check the seat's own evidence before applying its fix**, apply what holds, and **decline the rest in writing with reasons** so the decision is visible to Lars rather than silent. Four rounds was the right place to stop.

**How to apply:**
- **Every round, count constructions across the WHOLE draft, not just the parts that changed.** Do not scope the repetition sweep to the drafter's original text.
- **When a round applies more than two replacements, diff the replacements against each other** before writing them in. Three fixes that all reach for the same scaffold is a tic being born.
- **Name the provenance of each flagged repetition** — drafter, Lars, or panel. The report should say which. A repetition the panel installed is cheaper to fix and carries no voice risk, so it should be applied, not proposed.
- **Watch the scaffolds specifically**, not the content words: `the part that…`, `the question is not…`, `it is not X, it is Y`, `what X does not say`, `the thing I keep returning to`, `That is the whole…`, `the one that…`, `[time] I wrote about…`, `the [adjective] half`. These are the shapes that spread across a revision pass.
- **Prefer cuts to rewrites in late rounds.** A removal cannot install a new frame. Round 4 on the Sat 12 piece was almost entirely cuts and was the only round that did not cause a new hit.
- Same discipline applies to the drafter across revisions: rv1→rv5 on the Astra piece introduced its own repetitions that no single round would have caught.
