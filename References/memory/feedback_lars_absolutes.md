**The rule: an unearned absolute is a voice defect, not a style choice.** Universal quantifiers asserted
about what people do — *nobody writes this down*, *everybody skips this*, *no one ever explains it* — read to
Lars as overconfidence, and overconfidence is the root objection. His words, 16 Aug 2026, on a line in his
own draft:

> *"I might change that line and its absolute surety -- which is the root of my problem with it,
> overconfidence, because 'nobody' is complete bs in that statement."*

**Why: it is the same failure as a stripped modal verb, pointed at a population instead of a mechanism.**
See [[brief-claim-provenance]] — the modal-verb drop turns *can* into *does*. An unearned absolute turns
*some people* into *nobody*. Both buy emphasis with accuracy, and both are the exact failure his writing
spends its time indicting. It is worst in a piece whose own argument is about claims being overstated.

**His fix is not hedging — it is relocating the claim from a census to a secret.** He rewrote the flagged
line himself, and the instruction that came with it is the useful part:

> *"'The second half is the one that MBA schools teach and no consultant ever explains' or something of the
> kind. Its secret sauce. Introduce it as such, not as a system-wide failure, which is just flat out isn't."*

So the move is: **frame the gap as valuable and under-explained, not as universal incompetence.** Shipped as
*"the part business school teaches, consultants resell, and hardly anyone ever actually explains"* — three
concrete populations doing three specific things, and one soft quantifier carrying what "nobody" was
overclaiming. Nothing was hedged away; the claim got more specific, not weaker.

## How to apply

- Treat any universal quantifier about human behavior as a flag: *nobody / everybody / no one / everyone /
  always / never*, when the subject is people rather than a mechanism.
- Ask whether the sentence is a **census** (a claim about a population — needs to be true) or a **figure of
  speech** (an analogy setup — fine). *"Nobody renovates a bathroom by calling a builder and saying 'fix the
  bathroom'"* survives because it is illustrative, not demographic. *"Nobody writes the second half down"*
  does not.
- The repair is **specificity, not qualification.** Name who actually does the thing, or who actually skips
  it. The drafter stacking impersonal hedges ("in many ways", "it could be argued", qualifier chains) on its
  own claims is the *other* failure — the charter's over-hedging tell.
- ★ **AMENDED 25 Sep 2026 (his ruling, voice recalibration C2): first-person stance markers are HIS voice,
  not the over-hedging tell.** "I think", "in my opinion", "pretty sure", "Perhaps.", and "arguably" used as a
  judgement appeared in 23/23 of his edit passes where counted; he adds them and never cuts them
  ("In my opinion, the obvious version of this argument is wrong." -- forwarding-address rv1:67; "Arguably the
  most capable model ever released" -- approval-not-automatic rv1:59; his own absolute fix "arguably the most
  capable AI company in the world" -- rule-written-nine-days-later rv7). Never strip them. This note's
  anti-absolutes rule is unchanged: his stance markers are one of the ways he softens a census claim.
- Bounded absolutes are fine and should survive editing: *"Nobody in that study was lazy"*, *"nobody ran the
  two arrangements side by side"*. The bound is what makes them true.

## The distinction that keeps this from over-firing

**Do not turn this into a ban on emphasis.** Two things in the canon would otherwise get flagged wrongly:

- `lars-about-me.md` lists **"specific-number hyperbole; 17 and 37 recur"** in his phrase bank — deliberate,
  his, and the panel must never sand it. He is not against overstatement as a rhetorical move.
- His long rolling sentences and blunt fragments are protected by the Prime Directive regardless.

The target is narrow: **a universal claim about people, presented as fact, that he could not defend if
challenged.** That is it.

## What the canon already had, and the gap this fills — worth knowing, because it explains the miss

`reference_mds\lars-about-me.md` already carries an anti-certainty law, and it is **scoped to taste only**:

> *"Never call something an 'aesthetic crime' or write from aesthetic certainty. I reject the stance that I
> know better than everyone; taste is developmental."*

plus *"Do not infer certainty about taste; I hold that as an open, developmental thing."* The interview
records the same disposition (Q8: he neither dismisses nor **overclaims** what AI changed; A69: *"no
fabricated positions"*).

**So the instinct was documented — but only about aesthetic judgment, never about empirical assertion.**
Nothing in the voice rubric or the profile prohibited *nobody writes this down*. That is why an absolute
reached a draft and he had to catch it himself, and why he then asked whether the rubric covered it. It did
not. This note is the missing half.

**Process lesson, and it is the recurring one:** he gave this correction on 16 Aug in general terms, it was
applied to the draft, and it was not logged until he asked. A correction applied to one piece and not
written down regenerates on the next one — the same mechanism as [[charter-drift-across-three-copies]].
Log at the moment of correction, not when asked.
