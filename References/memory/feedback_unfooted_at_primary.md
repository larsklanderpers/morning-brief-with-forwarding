**The rule:** a regulatory claim needs a primary. When only secondary sources exist, **the draft says so
in the sentence**, and **names the commercial interest**. That is `CLAUDE.md` accuracy rules 1 and 2, and
they are the direct output of the 2026-07-20 failure.

## Why this needed its own entry: it hides in the gap between two checks

On Wed 9 Sep (`cheaper-and-metered`) the governance hinge was the incoming APP 1.7 to 1.9 automated-decision
disclosure obligation, stated flat as fact. Every source in `research_oaic-adm-guidance_2026-07-27.md` is a
**law firm** — HSF, JWS, Allens, Norton Rose, MinterEllison, Bird & Bird — and that note's own method line
records that legislation.gov.au **returned a table of contents only**. The claim is almost certainly true.
That is exactly the problem.

- **It is not a `[VERIFY]`.** Nothing is unsupported, so no flag was ever raised and the 7.0 cap never fired.
- **It is not a scored defect.** The Skeptic seat reads six independent corroborating sources and is satisfied.
- **It would still have blocked Scheduled**, because the house rule is about *where the claim is footed*, not
  about whether it is true.

Two checks both pass and the breach ships between them. **This is the same shape as
[[killed-claim-false-positive]]** — a category of error that rigour makes invisible rather than catches.

## How to apply

- **Sort every regulatory claim by SOURCE TYPE before scoring it**, not by confidence. Ask "what is the most
  primary thing anyone actually opened," and check the research note's own method line for a truncated or
  table-of-contents-only fetch. Per [[absence-verification]], a truncated fetch is not a read.
- **The fix is one sentence in his register, not a hedge.** It says where the reading comes from and who is
  selling what. On 9 Sep: the obligation described as law firms read it, the interest named, and the cheap
  shot explicitly declined ("does not make them wrong"). Hedging the claim instead would trip
  [[lars-absolutes]] from the other direction.
- **Law firms are a commercial interest.** Advisories about an incoming obligation are marketing for
  compliance work. That is rule 2, and it is easy to miss because law-firm writing reads neutral.
- **A corroborated-but-unprimaried claim is more dangerous than an uncertain one**, because six agreeing
  secondaries feel like verification and are frequently one another's source.
