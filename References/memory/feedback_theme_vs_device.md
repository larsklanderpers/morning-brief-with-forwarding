**The rule.** Established 30 Aug 2026, in his words:

> "i think the whole week could be about the things only a human can do and still be ok. it is a beat
> that bears repeating... I am unwilling to consider this any kind of categorical fail."

**What may NOT repeat across days** — controlling images, signature devices, closing phrases, named
exercises, sentence frames, section-header shapes. See [[metaphor-bleed]], [[machinery-bleed]].

**What MAY repeat, and sometimes should** — the argument. A week is allowed to make one case from
seven positions. That is what a week is *for*, and the Sunday capstone exists to collect it.

**Why this needed saying.** The panel flagged Mon 31 and Tue 1 (wk of 31 Aug) as closing on the same
posture — a human's capacity to tell whether something is right — and pre-emptively constrained Wed 2's
close. He lifted the constraint. The flag had drifted from policing repetition into policing the
week's own spine, which is the opposite of the job.

**Same shape as the 23 Aug encyclopaedia-of-skills ruling, one level up.**
- 23 Aug: the same *category* of skill piece is fine; the same *skill* is not.
- 30 Aug: the same *theme* is fine; the same *move* is not.
Both rulings say: the thing being repeated has to be examined at the right altitude before it is called
a failure. Convergence toward a thesis is a series working. Convergence toward a phrasing is a tic.

**How to apply.**
1. When a cross-piece flag fires, ask first: is this a WORD or an IDEA? Only words fail.
2. Never write a plan constraint that forbids a day from reaching the week's own conclusion. Constrain
   the wording, never the destination.
3. A theme running the whole week is a signal to make the CAPSTONE name it explicitly, not a signal to
   suppress it. His follow-on instruction the same day: *"we might want to look at the capstone for next
   sunday and make sure it explicitly calls out the recurring theme."* **A recurring theme is capstone
   material. Harvest it, do not police it.**
4. Naming a theme is still not licence to overstate it — the source's own hedges and counter-evidence
   travel with it (see [[brief-claim-provenance]] on modal drift).

**Safe-practice steps are never bleed (his ruling, 1 Oct 2026).**
- [stated] "the step-by-step build and the caution guidance are always acceptable. they are not a collision freshness risk, they are prudent usage" -- e.g. shadow first, test each step, checkpoints, kill switch, humans keep irreversible actions. Never flag these as machinery bleed; only images, phrases, closes and headers count.
- [stated] "verbatim reuse is always something to watch for and call out" (1 Oct 2026) -- the safe-practice steps may recur, but reprinting an earlier piece's exact wording of them is still flagged.
