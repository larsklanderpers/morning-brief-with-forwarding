**The rule:** check the whole publication window for a controlling image, a signature device or a
closing move before choosing one. He will cut a good line to stop an image crossing days — that is
his standard, not a concession. Seat 5 scores any cross-piece repetition inside the window as a
**categorical fail**, independent of the numeric score.

**Why:** on 6 Aug a kitchen/house-keys image bled from the Sat 8 draft into the Mon 10 draft and was
caught only because Saturday happened to sit two slots back. The window was widened from "the last
two pieces" to the **full seven-day publication arc, checked in BOTH directions** — `Substack
Published` / `Substack Scheduled` for what has run, `Substack Drafts` for what is already written for
later days. He drafts three to four days ahead, so the files nearest in the folder are NOT the ones
his reader just read. **Anchor to the publication slot, not to the draft order.**

**How to apply, cheaply:** you need closes, section headers and controlling images — not full
re-reads. `grep` the neighbouring files for the distinctive phrase you are about to use.

## ★ SATURDAY-TO-SATURDAY IS THE HIGHEST-RISK ADJACENCY (established 23 Aug 2026)

The Sat 29 draft failed categorically on a phrase nothing but a neighbour-file read would surface. It
closed on ***"That is the whole skill."*** **Sat 22 — the immediately preceding Saturday — closes on
*"The whole skill is telling the two apart."*** Same phrase, same payoff position, consecutive
Diversions.

**Consecutive Saturdays share a register, a job and a closing shape**, so they converge on the same
vocabulary far harder than two consecutive weekdays do. Two further echoes surfaced in the same read:
*blast radius* (Tue 25's controlling frame, four days out) and *muscle* + *discipline* (Sat 22's close
and Sun 23 twice — **both** candidate words were already spoken for, so the clause had to drop both).

**How to apply: read last Saturday's close before writing this Saturday's. Every time. First.**

## ⚠️ A DARK MID-WEEK STRETCHES THE WINDOW, IT DOES NOT SHRINK IT (23 Aug 2026)

In the 24–30 stand-down, Wed/Thu/Fri were dark. So Sat 29's real neighbours were **Mon 24 and Tue 25**
— four and five days out — not the usual one or two. **Count the window in PUBLISHED SLOTS, not in
calendar days.** A week with gaps reaches further back than it looks.

## ⚠️ THE CHECK'S OWN FAILURE MODE: RETIRING A FORMULA IS NOT REMEMBERING IT IS RETIRED (23 Aug 2026)

The panel cut a section header — *"The part nobody quotes"* — from the Mon 24 draft at rv1, because it
collided with Fri 21's *"The part of the report I am not seeing people discussing."* **Two drafts
later it reinstalled the identical frame in the Tue 25 draft.** The charter already warns *"when you
retire a formula, check you haven't installed a new one in its place"* — this is the sharper version:
**check you have not reinstalled the SAME one.** Keep a running list of what this session has already
retired and re-grep it before each new draft.

## The related shape — a NEW piece's material can collide with an OLD piece's frame

Not only images. Watch also for:
- **Header noun-frames** (*"The part that…"*, *"The honest…"*, *"What I would do about…"*) — these
  repeat far more readily than images and are easier to miss.
- **Declared-return openings** (*"Coming back to X, because Y"*). Two in a window is an arc; three is
  a device. Thu 20 and Sat 22 both ran it, which put it off-limits for everything in between.
- **Closing moves** — the imperative triple, the weekday recap, "Bring the list", the
  afternoon-sized task. All of these ran twice inside 17–23 alone.
- **A close that pre-empts a LATER piece's close.** Mon 31 ends on *"who on your board can tell a good
  answer from a confident one"* — which is Sat 29's territory, two days earlier. Sat 29's close was
  written to avoid that formulation so Monday keeps its ending. **Check forward as well as back, and
  when two pieces want the same landing, the LATER one keeps it.**

## Faint blends count, and he will fix them himself

He cut *"building the walls / do it safely"* partly because it pulled Monday's containment-*walls*
metaphor into Tuesday as *enablement* — same word, different sense, consecutive pieces. **A shifted
sense is still a bleed.** Flag it.
