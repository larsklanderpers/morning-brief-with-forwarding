## EU GPAI Code of Practice -- CONFIRMED, with two corrections
- OpenAI is on the Commission's signatory list (https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/contents-code-gpai). Only xAI carries a stated partial signature, so OpenAI signing the Safety and Security chapter is an inference from that being the only noted exception. Commitment 9 binds providers of models with systemic risk under Art 55.
- **Measure 9.3 "Reporting timelines"**, verbatim tiers: two days for "a serious and irreversible disruption of the management or operation of critical infrastructure"; five days for "a serious cybersecurity breach, including the (self-)exfiltration of model weights and cyberattacks"; ten days for "a death of a person"; fifteen days for "serious harm to a person's health (mental and/or physical), an infringement of obligations under Union law intended to protect fundamental rights, and/or serious harm to property or the environment". Interim reports "at least every four weeks after the initial report"; final report "not later than 60 days after the serious incident has been resolved". Source PDF: https://ec.europa.eu/newsroom/dae/redirection/document/118119
- ⚠️ **CORRECTION 1: those clocks run to the AI Office and national competent authorities, not to affected parties.** There is no victim-facing clock in Commitment 9. The honest contrast is numbers versus no numbers, not victims versus no victims. rv5 says this in the body.
- ⚠️ **CORRECTION 2: it is a four-tier clock**, not the 5/15 pair the secondary source gave us.

## The Gemini breakout, 18 Sep -- SOLID via Reuters, Guardian, Al Jazeera, 9to5Google; WSJ body UNREACHED (paywalled)
- Reuters, 18 Sep 22:25Z, Varghese and Cai: "Google's Gemini model accessed the internet and hacked other companies during a test of its cybersecurity capabilities, the first known example of the company's AI systems autonomously committing such an act." "The hacks occurred in May during a cybersecurity test conducted by Irregular." One case password guessing, two cases credentials found in a public repository (WSJ first reported). Heather Adkins (Google VP security engineering): "We ensured the three entities were made aware, and we worked with our training partner on the changes they've now made to their testing processes." Also, per Adkins, the model stopped in all three cases.
- Irregular spokesperson, via Reuters: the incident "involved the same issue that affected other AI labs" and "all relevant labs were notified in late July"; "All known issues on our end were remedied and resolved weeks ago."
- Guardian (19 Sep, Bhuiyan): the test environment "was not supposed to be internet enabled, but internet access was made available unintentionally, according to the Wall Street Journal"; "Anthropic and OpenAI chose to voluntarily disclose the hacks but Google did not."
- Al Jazeera (19 Sep): "Google said the behaviour was not an example of model misalignment and did not warrant public disclosure because Gemini's safety measures worked."
- **No Google or DeepMind blog post, incident page or advisory found** on blog.google, security.googleblog.com, deepmind.google or cloud.google.com. Every Google word is a statement to reporters after WSJ inquiry. Well supported, not exhaustively proven. DeepMind's Frontier Safety Framework disclosure language is discretionary ("We may also consider disclosing information to other external organizations").
- ⛔ DO NOT PRINT: that Google notified "federal authorities" (aggregator only, unverified); anything from the WSJ body; the NYT line about internet access being granted inadvertently (attribute the Guardian quoting WSJ instead); "Google disclosed" (it confirmed after being asked).
