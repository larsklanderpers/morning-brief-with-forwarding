# Research Note — the Saturday studies, footed at their primaries
For: `substack_draft_ask-dont-answer_2026-09-05` (Sat 5 Sep) · Depth: deep · Date: 2026-09-03

## Bottom line

**Four of five reached at a primary. Three are solid. One is partial. And one of the solid ones CUTS
AGAINST the piece as planned.** Budzyń (deskilling is real, measured, −6.0 points ADR) and Vicente &
Matute (people detect the AI's error and follow it anyway) are the strongest evidence on the
PROBLEM side and both stand up — with a correction to how the plan phrases V&M. But the DESIGN-FIX
side, which is the whole constructive payload of a Saturday, is thinner than the plan believes:
**Everett's RCT shows AI-as-FIRST-opinion beat AI-as-second-opinion on accuracy** — the opposite of
"make the tool ask before it answers" — and Danry, the study that actually supports question-framing,
could not be reached past its title. **The honest piece is a different piece: accuracy-with-AI and
skill-without-AI are different outcomes, Everett measured the first, Budzyń measured the second, and
almost nobody measures the second.** That is a sharper Saturday than the one in the plan, and it is
constructive without pretending the evidence says what it does not.

## What's solid

- **Budzyń et al. (2025), *Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology* — VERIFIED at the Warwick repository
  copy of the abstract.** Retrospective observational study, **four endoscopy centres in Poland,
  8 Sept 2021 – 9 March 2022**. **1,443 colonoscopies** (795 before AI introduction, 648 after). In
  non-AI-assisted colonoscopies, adenoma detection rate fell **from 28.4% (226/795) to 22.4%
  (145/648)**, absolute difference **−6.0% (95% CI −10.5 to −1.6; p=0.0089)**; exposure to AI was an
  independent factor, **odds ratio 0.69 (95% CI 0.53–0.89)**. Authors' interpretation, verbatim:
  *"Continuous exposure to AI might reduce the ADR of standard non-AI assisted colonoscopy, suggesting
  a negative effect on endoscopist behaviour."*
  ⚠️ Note the modal: **"might."** Retrospective, observational, six-month window, one country. The
  Nature Reviews and ACG commentaries raise the obvious confounds. Print it as the authors print it.
  ⚠️ The number of endoscopists is NOT in the abstract. Do not print one.
  [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40816301/) · [Warwick WRAP](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/191005)

- **Vicente & Matute (2023), *Scientific Reports*, "Humans inherit artificial intelligence biases" —
  VERIFIED at nature.com.** The 80.7% is real and verbatim: *"It is important to note that in this
  experiment 80.7% of participants detected mistakes in the AI recommendations."* That is **Experiment
  3** (n = 98 + 99, Prolific, international, English-fluent), a **fictitious medical tissue
  classification task**. Experiment 2 found **58%** detected. And separately: *"Although participants
  found that the AI was not entirely accurate, many of them still relied on the AI recommendations to
  perform the task."*
  **⚠️ CORRECTION TO THE PLAN AND THE CORPUS — A WELD.** The plan says *"80.7% of participants detected
  the error in the AI's recommendation — and followed it anyway."* That fuses two findings into one
  number. **80.7% is the DETECTION rate. "Many" is the follow-anyway finding, and it is qualitative.**
  The paper does NOT say 80.7% detected-and-followed. Write: *"eight in ten noticed the AI was making
  mistakes; many of them kept following it anyway."* That is what the paper says and it is still the
  finding that stays with readers.
  **⚠️ "Surface validation functioned; substantive validation failed" is NOT in the paper.** It is
  Lovett's gloss. Attribute it to Lovett or do not use it as a quote.
  **⚠️ POPULATION.** Psychology students (Exp 1) and online Prolific participants (Exps 2–3) doing a
  made-up diagnosis task. **Not professionals. Not real stakes.** Lovett uses it as evidence about
  professional validation; the piece must not inherit that stretch. It is evidence about human
  behaviour under AI advice, and it is good evidence of that.
  [nature.com](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-42384-8)

- **Everett et al., "From Tool to Teammate: A Randomized Controlled Trial of Clinician-AI Collaborative
  Workflows for Diagnosis" — VERIFIED at PMC.** medRxiv preprint **8 June 2025**; peer-reviewed
  version since published in ***npj Digital Medicine* (2026)**. **70 US-licensed physicians** (39
  residents, 41 attendings; 97% internal medicine), **vignette-based**. ⚠️ 4 Sep: 39 + 41 = 80, not 70. The paper's own Results text says "70 unique U.S.-licensed physicians were included in the analysis, 39 residents and 41 attendings" and never reconciles it (one exclusion for prior vignette exposure is mentioned; nine unexplained). DO NOT PRINT THE SPLIT. Print "seventy physicians, residents and attendings." Three arms: **AI as first
  opinion / AI as second opinion / conventional resources only.** Results: **first-opinion 85%**
  (p=0.00039 vs control), **second-opinion 82%** (p=3.3e-6 vs control), **control 75%.** Authors'
  own limitation: *"vignettes are not representative of real-world clinical practice."*
  **★★ THIS CUTS AGAINST THE PLAN. READ THIS TWICE.** The plan (via Lovett) describes Everett as
  showing that *"structuring physician-AI workflows to require active clinician engagement with the
  AI's reasoning, rather than passive acceptance, preserved and even improved diagnostic
  performance."* **That is not the headline. The headline is that giving the physician the AI's
  answer FIRST produced the highest accuracy.** The second-opinion arm — the one where the clinician
  forms their own view first, which IS the "attempt first, then let it critique" design the plan wants
  to recommend — scored lower than answer-first. Both AI arms beat control; the paper is good news
  for AI-assisted accuracy on vignettes. It is NOT evidence that engagement-first is the better
  design, and citing it for that is citing it against its own result.
  ⚠️ I do not have the first-vs-second-opinion significance test, only each arm vs control. Do not
  print "first opinion significantly beat second opinion." Print the three numbers and let them sit.
  ⚠️ CITATION-YEAR TRAP: "Everett et al. (2025)" is the preprint. The journal version is 2026. Cite one
  or the other explicitly.
  [PMC (preprint)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12155023/) · [npj Digital Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41746-026-02545-1)

- **Choudhuri et al. (2025), "AI Where It Matters: Where, Why, and How Developers Want AI Support in
  Daily Work" — VERIFIED at arXiv and at Microsoft's own publication page.** *"a large-scale,
  mixed-methods study of N=860 developers,"* finding *"clear limits for identity- and
  relationship-centric work (e.g., mentoring)."* October 2025, arXiv 2510.00762.
  **⚠️ THIS IS A MICROSOFT RESEARCH STUDY — Developer Experience Lab.** Standing rule 2: name the
  commercial interest. Microsoft sells Copilot to exactly these developers. The finding is still
  usable — a vendor documenting where its own customers push back is, if anything, more credible on
  that specific point — but it ships as *"a Microsoft Research study of 860 developers,"* never as
  independent academic research.
  [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.00762) · [Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/ai-where-it-matters-where-why-and-how-developers-want-ai-support-in-daily-work/)

## What's contested / watch out

- **The design-fix thesis is now carried by one partially-footed study.** Once Everett is read
  correctly, "assistance that makes you attempt, evaluate, question and justify is itself
  developmental" rests on Danry alone — and Danry's abstract could not be reached (below). The
  PROBLEM half of Saturday is well-evidenced. The FIX half is a hypothesis with one supporting study
  I cannot fully quote. **Write the fix as a hypothesis and the problem as a finding.**

- **Everett and Budzyń are not in tension — they measure different things, and that is the piece.**
  Everett: accuracy WITH the tool, on the day. Budzyń: skill WITHOUT the tool, after months of use.
  Both can be true at once: the tool makes you better today and erodes what you can do alone. **Nobody
  in this set measured both on the same people.** The Lovett table also lists **Wiles et al. (2024)**
  — *"no significant advantage on unassisted assessment after AI-assisted training"* — which is the
  study that would close the loop. **NOT CHECKED in this pass. If Saturday wants to make the
  two-outcomes argument load-bearing, Wiles needs its own quick check first.**

## ★ UPDATE 3 Sep, later -- DANRY FOOTED AT THE PRIMARY. Lars supplied the ACM PDF (doi 10.1145/3544548.3580672).

**Danry, Pataranutaporn, Mao & Maes, CHI 2023 -- now SOLID, and it is better than the corpus had it.**
- **Design:** 3-condition between-subjects, pre-registered (aspredicted.org #94860). **234 enrolled, 204
  analysed** after attention checks. Prolific, US, English-fluent, mean age 35.3, 71.2% white. Control
  n=62, causal AI-explanation n=63, AI-framed questioning n=79.
- **Task:** discern the logical validity of **10 socially divisive statements** (IBM Debater
  claims+evidence; hasty-generalisation fallacies vs valid), on topics like violent video games,
  affirmative action, Gaza, refugees, circumcision. Explanations generated with GPT-3 from templates,
  manually checked.
- **★ THE HEADLINE NUMBERS (§5.1, verbatim):** *"The participants' raw discernment accuracy (Mean =
  44% accuracy, SD = 26) were lower than the random guess success rate... participants supported by
  causal AI explanations or AI framed questioning achieved a raw discernment accuracy of 57%, 67%
  respectively."* **Unassisted humans scored BELOW CHANCE on spotting fallacies.**
- **The asymmetry that matters (§5.2):** on **INVALID** statements, questioning (62.5) > causal (55.0)
  > control (46.9), questioning-vs-causal **p = .009**. On **VALID** statements, questioning (74.7) and
  causal (78.2) **did not differ** (p = .126); both beat control (68.2). **So asking beats telling
  specifically on the flawed statements** -- which is where discernment is the whole job.
- **★ A mechanism line, and it links straight to V&M (§5.3):** causal explanations **lowered
  "perceived information insufficiency"** -- being TOLD made people feel they had enough information
  and less inclined to look further. Questioning did not. In the authors' words: *"when supported by
  causal AI explanations, individuals are more likely to be satisfied with the given information and
  potentially would not seek further information to verify the claim."*
- **⚠️ TWO LIMITS THE PAPER STATES ITSELF, and both must travel with it:**
  1. **The AI was ALWAYS CORRECT.** §6.1 verbatim: *"our experiment compared AI-framed Questioning to
     always correct Causal AI-explanations... since our experiment does not compare AI-framed
     questioning with an AI system that can be wrong, the results do not contribute much to
     discussions on over-reliance."* So it says nothing about V&M's problem directly.
  2. **Within-session only.** Single sitting, no retention or transfer test. Participants
     *self-reported* learning to recognise the fallacy pattern (§5.5.3) -- that is a quote, not a
     measurement. **"Within session" is now CONFIRMED as the scope**, which the corpus had flagged.
- **Correction to the corpus's rendering:** the 16 Aug source note said Danry showed improvement
  "within session." Correct, and now footed. Lovett's use of it as evidence that question-framing is
  "itself developmental" over-reads a single-session study of an always-correct AI. **SAFE:** asking
  beat telling on fallacies, 67 vs 57 vs 44, one session, AI always right. **NOT SAFE:** any claim
  about durable skill-building, or about behaviour when the AI is wrong.

**What this does to the piece:** the design-fix half of Saturday is no longer carried by a title. It is
carried by three real numbers with the two caveats stated in text. It still does not rescue "make the
tool ask before it answers" as a proven rule -- Everett is unchanged -- but it earns "the design space
is real" honestly, and the perceived-information-insufficiency finding gives the piece its
mechanism: being told makes you stop looking.

## The economics

Not a pricing story this week, but the cost framing is the point of Saturday and it is clean:
Everett's 10-point accuracy gain is **free, today, on the vignette**. Budzyń's 6-point loss is
**deferred, invisible on the day, and shows up only when the tool is absent** — which for a control
function is exactly when it matters. That is the storage-unit shape from Friday, one more time: the
gain is booked now, the cost arrives later as a decline nobody attributed. Do not re-use the storage
unit image; the shape is licensed, the image is Friday's.

## Angles this opens

- **★ THE REFRAME FOR SAT 5.** Not "ask, don't answer" as a proven design rule. Instead: **two
  outcomes, one of them unmeasured.** Every vendor study and most trials measure accuracy WITH the
  tool. Budzyń is one of very few that measured skill WITHOUT it, afterward. The design rule that
  survives the evidence is not "attempt first" (Everett does not support it) — it is **"measure the
  second thing"**: keep a periodic unassisted rep on the work you are accountable for, because the
  decline is real, deferred, and invisible until the tool is gone. That is constructive, it is
  footed, and it is the plan's third takeaway promoted to first.
- **This also lands on the Sun 6 capstone harder than the original would have.** Lovett's Validation
  Tether says oversight depends on the expertise adoption erodes. Budzyń is the erosion, measured.
  Everett is the adoption, measured. Nobody measured the person who has to do both. That is the
  missing person the whole week has been about.
