# Sketch — the encyclopaedia of skills

**Not a proposal. A thinking aid, written 23 Aug 2026 at Lars's request after his ruling that
"if each skill is part of an encyclopaedia of skills, its a whole different animal."**
He was explicit that he is not ready to commit: *"it puts a bit more form around it than I probably
[am] completely comfortable with but its worth taking a look and thinking it through."* So this is
tiered from zero commitment upward, and the costs are named honestly rather than sold past.

You have written six entries in three weeks without calling it anything.

**Six distinct skills. No repeats.** Which is the evidence that the encyclopaedia framing is right
rather than aspirational — you have been writing entries and the entries have not collided.

So the real question is not *should there be a series.* It is **whether the reader can see the one
that is already there.** Right now they cannot, unless they read every piece and assemble it
themselves.

---

## Four tiers, cheapest first

### Tier 0 — bookend only. No structure at all.

One clause in each entry naming the previous one. *"Two Saturdays ago the skill was reading a claim.
This one is about how the work gets designed."*

- **Cost: nothing.** It is one sentence and it is already required by your own ruling.
- **Buys: most of the benefit.** The reader perceives a set from the inside, without you having
  declared one. Entry three stops reading as "he is on about this again."
- **Reversible: completely.** There is nothing to undo.

### Tier 1 — a quiet marker. Still no name.

A single consistent line in the standing footer area, unnumbered: *"One of an occasional series on
the skills this work now needs."*

- **Cost: near zero, and it is the last genuinely free step.**
- **Buys: findability of intent.** A new reader who lands on one entry learns there are others.
- **⚠️ Do not number them.** "Part 4" creates an expectation of Part 5. Unnumbered means occasional
  really means occasional.
- **Reversible: yes.** Drop it and nobody writes in to ask.

### Tier 2 — a name.

The set gets called something, and the name appears in each entry.

- **Cost: real, and this is where the discomfort you are feeling starts being correct.** A name is a
  promise. Readers who like it will expect it on a cadence, and the weeks you do not have a genuine
  new skill to hand over become weeks where something is missing.
- **Buys: identity.** People can recommend the series rather than a piece.
- **Reversible: not really.** Retiring a named thing is a visible act.

### Tier 3 — an index post.

A living page listing every entry with one line each, updated when a new one lands, linked from each.

- **Cost: maintenance, plus it is the step that makes this feel like a product rather than a habit.**
- **Buys: the actual encyclopaedia.** Somebody can go and look up "how do I check a claim."
- **⚠️ Worth knowing: you already have the raw material.** `SUBSTACK_INDEX.md` carries every piece
  with its thread and beat. A public index is a filtered view of a thing that already exists
  internally, which makes Tier 3 much cheaper than it looks — but it does not make it lower risk.

## ★ The risk you are actually sensing, and it is your own history

**A named series becomes a slot. A slot gets filled whether or not the story is there.**

This pipeline's worst repeated failure was exactly that shape: the reader's-calculus close ran four
straight weekdays in late July, not because four pieces called for it, but because a stale charter
listed it as a requirement rather than a callback. Your own correction at the time is the relevant
law: *"we need to make sure that we're not just repeating a formula where we're actually delivering
to what the story is about."* And: **devices are seasoning, never a slot to fill.**

**Tier 2 and above risk doing to an entire piece-type what charter drift did to a single device.**
That is not a reason never to do it. It is the reason your instinct is telling you to wait, and the
instinct is correct.

The protection, if you ever go past Tier 1, is to write the retirement condition down at the same
time you write the name — *this runs when there is a real skill to hand over, and a month with none
is a normal month.* Cheap to write now, nearly impossible to add later once readers expect a cadence.

---

## One question to settle before any of it

**Is this one encyclopaedia or two?**

The entries currently split across two registers: **Tuesday ground-game** (short, do-it-tonight) and
**Saturday long-form** (the weekend read). If it is one set, that is fine — encyclopaedia entries
vary in length. If those are actually two different things — a drill and an essay — then naming them
together will feel wrong later and be awkward to unpick.

You do not need to answer that at Tier 0. You cannot avoid answering it at Tier 2.

---

## Recommendation

**Do Tier 0 now.** It is free, your ruling already requires it, and it is the whole of what Sat 5 Sep
needs.

**Revisit Tier 1 in about a month**, when there are ten entries. By then you will be able to look at
the list and see whether they genuinely cohere or whether you have been calling three different
things by one name. That is a much better basis for the decision than making it today.

**Leave Tier 2 and 3 alone until the set has proved itself.** Nothing is lost by waiting and the
option stays open. The entries accumulate either way.
