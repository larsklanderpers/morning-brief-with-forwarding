# Research Note — Nvidia "circular financing" (companion to the Nikkei $1.65T note)
For: Fri "mood flipped / bloodbath walkback" (pairs with `research_off-balance-sheet-ai-debt_2026-08-01`) · Depth: deep · Date: 2026-08-01

## Bottom line
Real, current, and the credit market -- not just commentators -- is pricing it. Nvidia's ~$750B wave of
AI-customer deals revived "circular financing" fears and pushed its 5-year **CDS to a record 82bps
(late Jul, largest single-day jump since the contract began trading Nov 2025)**; the stock shed ~5%
(~$250B of market value) the same day. The mechanism is genuine: Nvidia takes equity stakes in and/or
guarantees debt for customers (OpenAI, CoreWeave), who spend it on Nvidia hardware -- supplier +
investor + backstopper, which "makes it impossible to cleanly separate organic demand from manufactured
demand." **Crucial attribution discipline:** the eye-popping OpenAI figures (~$250B compute guarantee +
~$350B chip financing) are REPORTED / IN-NEGOTIATION ("early stage, could collapse," neither company
commented) -- not closed deals. What's confirmed is the SK Group >$500B partnership, Naver $1B, and
$5B into Sutskever's Safe Superintelligence. And the honest part Lars asked for: **the bull case is
strong** -- scarcity not glut, GPUs aren't stranded, monetization is real, Nvidia backstops from
confidence not weakness. This is not a clean Enron/telecom re-run. But it IS the perfect Friday
counter-beat: while the podium sells optimism, the credit market flinched -- and the coverage already
ties it to the Nikkei $1.65T off-balance-sheet number as one story.

## What's solid
- **Nvidia 5-year CDS hit ~82bps, a record, late July** -- largest single-day intraday jump since the
  contract began actively trading Nov 2025; stock -~5% (~$250B market cap erased) the same session.
  [Bloomberg via Yahoo Finance / Investing.com / The Next Web] -- **this is the concrete "the market is
  pricing it" fact; lead with it, not the deal totals.**
- **The confirmed deals:** SK Group partnership **>$500B** (2+ GW of AI data centres on the Korean
  peninsula; bidirectional -- SK sells Nvidia memory, buys Nvidia supercomputers); **Naver $1B**;
  **Safe Superintelligence (Ilya Sutskever) $5B**. Nvidia has announced **>$540B** of such deals in 2026
  alone, excluding any OpenAI arrangement. [The Next Web, late Jul]
- **The mechanism (solid):** "Nvidia takes equity stakes in or guarantees debt for customers such as
  OpenAI and CoreWeave, which then spend the money on Nvidia hardware." Nvidia's stakes span OpenAI,
  CoreWeave, Marvell, IREN, Nebius. [The Next Web]
- **It's not only Nvidia:** Google has extended **$35B in lease backstops for Anthropic** -- the
  vendor-financing pattern is spreading across the buildout. [The Next Web]
- **Regulators/institutions have flagged it:** the **IMF and the BIS** have both named AI circular
  financing a systemic downside risk. [The Next Web] -- useful "not just perma-bears" cover.
- **CoreWeave is the sharper credit signal:** its CDS reportedly implies **~50% default odds** ("bond
  market calls time on the GPU debt spiral"). [TechTimes, 30 Jul] -- confirm the exact figure before
  asserting; it's one outlet, but it's the vivid version of the same stress.

## What's contested / watch out
- **The OpenAI $250B + $350B are REPORTED, NOT CLOSED.** "The OpenAI negotiations are early stage and
  could collapse or change terms, and neither Nvidia nor OpenAI commented." Do NOT let "reportedly
  negotiating up to ~$600B" become "gave OpenAI $600B." The clean, defensible line: *confirmed* >$500B
  (SK) + *reported/in-talks* up to ~$600B (OpenAI). The ">$750B" total mixes closed and rumoured --
  attribute it as "the wave of deals and reported talks total north of $750B," not a balance-sheet fact.
- **The telecom/dot-com (Lucent/Nortel) parallel is the analysts' framing, and it's genuinely
  contested.** Give the counter its full weight (below). The strongest honest position is NOT "it's
  Enron/telecom again"; it's "the market is nervous and the demand signal is muddied, even as the
  fundamentals may hold."
- **Jensen Huang's rebuttal, for fairness:** "It's a small percentage of the amount of money that they
  ultimately have to go raise... the idea that it is circular is ridiculous." Include it; it's the
  honest other voice.

## The counterarguments — given full weight (Lars: "regardless of whether it's as bad as Enron")
From the Investing.com analysis (Lango), which lands on "fear legitimate but overblown":
- **Scarcity, not glut.** TSMC is *raising* prices (up to ~10%, some reports 25%) for 2027; foundries
  are supply-constrained. The opposite of 2001, when dark-fibre supply overwhelmed demand and bandwidth
  prices collapsed.
- **GPUs aren't stranded assets.** A cluster built for OpenAI has "instant next-best-use demand from
  every compute-starved frontier lab, enterprise, and sovereign AI program on earth" -- not decade-long
  idle fibre.
- **Monetization is real, not hypothetical.** Enterprise AI spend is accelerating (Alphabet Cloud +82%);
  usage-based pricing captures value the subscription-era internet couldn't.
- **Confidence, not desperation.** Lucent/Nortel financed customers to prop up *weak* order books;
  Nvidia -- with the best demand visibility in the industry -- backstops because "the math works," not to
  move inventory.
- Verdict to quote if useful: "when specific fear runs into a specific intact thesis, the fear usually
  breaks first."

## The economics (this is the whole beat)
Stack the two research notes and you get the Friday economics: **on-balance-sheet** ~$725B/yr hyperscaler
capex (your corpus, AI Corner) + ~$1.35T visible debt; **off-balance-sheet** ~$1.65T (Nikkei); and now a
**demand side** where the biggest supplier is funding its biggest customers to buy its own chips, so
even the *revenue* is partly manufacturer-financed. The credit market's response: Nvidia CDS at a record,
CoreWeave CDS at ~50% implied default. The honest synthesis for a control/concentration writer (not a
bubble-caller): you are being asked to route your business through a handful of players whose true
leverage is partly off the books AND whose true demand is partly self-funded -- and even the people who
insure their debt can't cleanly price it. That's the argument for a hedge you can locate, not a
prediction of a crash.

## Angles this opens — FRIDAY, paired with the Nikkei note
- **The counter-beat to the walkback.** Friday's frame is the men who forecast apocalypse now selling
  optimism ("delighted to be wrong"). The turn: the credit market didn't get the memo. Nvidia's own CDS
  hit a record; CoreWeave's implies coin-flip default odds; the IMF and BIS are flagging it. The mood
  flipped on stage; the people closest to the money are quietly buying insurance.
- **Two obscured halves = one honest thesis.** Nikkei = the *leverage* you can't see (~$1.65T off the
  books). Nvidia = the *demand* you can't cleanly see (manufacturer-financed). Together, not "it's a
  bubble," but "the true size and solidity of the bet are structured to be hard to read" -- which is
  exactly why a control you can locate beats depending on players whose books and order-books both blur.
- **Hold both sides (Lars's steer).** The bull case is real -- scarcity, reusable GPUs, accelerating
  spend, a strong balance sheet. So the honest Friday line isn't a crash call; it's: even if the thesis
  holds, you should not want your business's continuity riding on a concentration this leveraged and this
  self-referential without a hedge of your own. That threads Friday back into the week's control argument
  instead of turning it into a market-timing take.
