## What's solid

- **YALE — no aggregate unemployment break for AI-exposed workers.** The Yale Budget Lab's labor-market tracker finds no meaningful change in unemployment rate, occupational mix, or unemployment duration for high-AI-exposure occupations since ChatGPT launched, through ~March 2026. This is carried repeatedly across Lars's own work briefs (7/07–7/27) with primary links. Primaries: [Budget Lab — "AI is probably not (yet) the reason the labor market is weakening"](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/ai-probably-not-yet-reason-labor-market-weakening); [Budget Lab labor tracker](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/tracking-impact-ai-labor-market). **Honest nuance to preserve:** Yale does find real displacement in *translation and some adjacent fields* — just not the broad-based spike the apocalypse thesis predicted. The draft's phrasing ("no meaningful change in unemployment for AI-exposed workers since ChatGPT shipped") is accurate and safe.

- **RAMP — heavy AI adopters grew headcount, including entry-level.** Ramp Economics Lab + Revelio Labs, published **30 June 2026**: firms in the high-intensity AI-adoption group grew **overall headcount ~10% (10.2%)** and **entry-level headcount 12%** over the two years following adoption, while low-adoption firms flatlined. Primary quote (Ramp): "Companies that use AI grow their headcount 10% over the two years following AI adoption"; "Entry-level headcount grew 12% in the high-intensity group." Primary: [Ramp — "Does AI eliminate jobs? Heavy adopters hire more"](https://ramp.com/data/heavy-ai-adopters-hire-more); [full paper](https://ramp.com/data/ai-jobs-impact/paper); corroboration [CoinDesk, 30 Jun](https://www.coindesk.com/business/2026/06/30/companies-spending-the-most-on-ai-are-growing-jobs-ramp-study-finds), [PRNewswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ramp-economics-lab-finds-companies-that-invest-heavily-in-ai-hire-more-302814151.html). Also in Lars's briefs: work_brief_2026-07-19 (line 125) + work_brief_2026-07-09 (line 8). **Draft is accurate**; the "around 12 percent" entry-level figure is correct and specifically entry-level (not just overall).

- **ALTMAN — walked back his entry-level forecast, to CommBank's CEO.** Confirmed venue and quote: at **Commonwealth Bank's "Accelerate AI" event, Sydney, 26 May 2026**, Altman spoke with **CBA CEO Matt Comyn via video link** and said he had been "wide of the mark" on AI's short-term impact on entry-level white-collar jobs, which "had not been nearly as bad as he had once predicted." Exact words: **"I'm delighted to be wrong about that,"** and "we've been roughly right on technological predictions and pretty wrong on the social and economic implications." Primary: [CommBank Newsroom, May 2026](https://www.commbank.com.au/articles/newsroom/2026/05/sam-altman-close-ai-gap.html); coverage [Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/news/investing/the-negative-question-openais-sam-altman-gets-most-from-ceos/), [Fortune, 26 May](https://fortune.com/2026/05/26/sam-altman-dario-amodei-walking-back-ai-jobs-apocalypse-prophecies-ipo/). The draft's "told the CEO of Commonwealth Bank that his own earlier forecast was wrong" is **exactly right** — the once-only appearance of the CBA venue in the 7/21 brief checked out against the primary.

## The economics
The counter-evidence is itself the cost argument in the piece's favor: the firms spending *most* on AI are the ones adding headcount (Ramp), so "cut juniors to capture AI savings" is contradicted by the behavior of the heaviest adopters — they're buying the tool *and* hiring the rung. The draft's "the redesign is close to free / the deletion is the expensive option" framing is consistent with the Ramp behavioral data: deletion isn't even what the AI leaders are doing.
