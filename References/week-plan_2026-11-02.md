## The arc: what it still opens after you hand it over  (working name: THE AFTERLIFE OF A HANDOVER)

Last week was friction: rules priced on the other side's effort. **This week changes axis from the other side's effort to your own handovers, and from the moment of the exchange to what happens after it.** Every organization hands things out: a remote-support tool with standing access, a function run by a contractor, a workload sent to someone else's model, years of records kept by a vendor. The cost of each handover used to be paid at the handover. It is now paid at the end of it: when the tool is breached, when the contractor goes down, when the vendor is investigated or goes broke and its data is sold. Wednesday shows it on the access side, Thursday shows the up side (you can now keep a frontier-class workload where you can see it), Friday shows what your data is worth on someone else's liquidation schedule, and Sunday merges them.

- **Wed opens with 2-3 points:** (1) the court did not get hacked, its outsourced post room did (BAE / US Bankruptcy Noticing Center); (2) a remote-support appliance is a set of keys handed to strangers on purpose, so CISA's fix is to rotate every credential it ever stored or injected, not to patch (CISA alert, 16 Oct); (3) the exposure is what each handover still opens, not when it was granted.
- **Thu adds:** the handover is now optional for more work than it was a month ago. Open weights at 816B on two desktops (Meta Muse Spark Open, 15 Oct), and a government choosing on-device so chats never leave the phone (DeepMind / GovTech Singapore, 14 Oct). A workload you never send has nothing to come back and bite you.
- **Fri adds:** the records you handed a vendor are an asset on its balance sheet. Google's reported $10M bid for Spirit Airlines' emails, Teams messages and HR records in bankruptcy, and 120+ lawmakers arguing de-identification does not survive a model. What it costs, and who pays.
- **Sun merges:** a handover is not finished when it is granted; it is finished when everything it opened is closed. The fix is to price each handover by its end state: what it opens, where it goes when they fail, and whether it needed to leave at all.

If Wednesday cannot foot the BAE outage at the US Courts' own notice and the rotation advice at CISA's own alert, the arc is wrong and gets re-pitched.
