# Research Note -- Paid by the Action (Fri 9 Oct, commercial)
For: substack_draft_paid-by-the-action_2026-10-09 (not yet drafted) · Depth: deep · Date: 2026-10-07
Seat: NONE (his word, 7 Oct). Re-angled to pricing as the per-action story. Four parallel primary sweeps, 7 Oct.

## Bottom line
- The plan's thesis survives, but only in a narrower form. **OpenAI is not selling per action.** ChatGPT Pro is a three-step usage ladder ($100 / $200 / $500), Codex bills by tokens, and the allowance unit is "times the Plus allowance". The per-action and per-outcome meters are in agent products: Salesforce, Microsoft, Intercom, Zendesk and Sierra. Robinhood is the cleanest exhibit. It passes model tokens through at "the model's public token cost" and earns on the trades the agent places; transaction revenue was about 59% of Q2 revenue.
- The thesis that survives is "the meter moved from the model to the action, and whoever counts the action sets the margin." That foots better than "a delegate paid per action wants more actions." Intercom caps billing at one outcome per conversation, which cuts against "more actions" there.
- The bank-run leg is well footed as a *threat* and unfooted as a *fact*. Slok's note is two sentences. No 2026 data shows agent-driven deposit outflows, and BofA's analyst calls the thesis "conceptual".

## What's contested / watch out
- **"Fifth of the price"** is against Astra, NOT against the previous Sol. GPT-6 Sol was already $2 / $10; only cached input halved. Do not write "Sol got cheaper."
- **Pro 200 going from 20x to 10x** comes from TNW only. It is not on any OpenAI page. Attribute it. https://thenextweb.com/news/openai-devday-pro-200-usage-cut-pro-500-plan
- **Kabra's "150% more for ... 25%" is wrong** against the new 10x Pro 200 allowance (2.5x price for 2.5x allowance). She concedes in her own post that a more efficient model "could complete more work within a smaller allowance." Her question is the usable part: "which cost are we measuring, and what have we assumed stays unchanged?" Correct URL: https://nehakabra1.substack.com/p/more-intelligence-for-less-check
- **Anthropic's "up to 30% less per task"** for Sonnet 5.5 is a claim. WinBuzzer cites Artificial Analysis at $7.60 vs $5.09 per benchmark task, which is higher, not lower. Label it "Anthropic says".
- **"Tokens at cost"** is Linas's characterisation. Robinhood's own wording is "public token cost", which is list price, and its wholesale cost is not disclosed. Never write "zero margin on the AI."
- **150,000 accounts** counts the May external-agent (MCP) product. The in-app Agents are "coming soon to eligible U.S. customers". Write it that way.
- **Slok's 5.0% top end** has no support in current listings (top HYSA ~4.2-4.4%, Bankrate / Newtek / Investopedia, 6 Oct). The 3.3% matches SoFi's base rate.
- **Bank-run counter-evidence.** BofA's Poonawala: "Until deposit costs rise faster than can be explained by rates or competition, the disruption thesis remains conceptual." Maurer (TWIF; note Plaid owns TWIF): agents need Plaid coverage that does not exist everywhere. Catalini (for the thesis): "the Netflix moment for the banks." Banks are responding with rate incentives at Citi, PNC and BofA, and JPMorgan's "Smart Cash" was floated. https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/banks-have-profited-from-idle-cash-for-years-ai-agents-could-change-that-154215138.html ; https://www.thisweekinfintech.com/p/an-agentic-bank-run-is-more-likely-a-stroll-for-now-experts-say
- **The deposit franchise is not pure profit.** DSS say it pays for branches. "Inertia was a revenue line" is safe. "Inertia was free money" is not.
- **Intercom "assumed resolution" dispute.** It happened, but it is one customer's community thread (22 Jan 2025): Fin "'assumes' it resolved an issue way too often." Intercom only promised to pass on the feedback. Use it as one example, not as a pattern. https://community.intercom.com/ask-the-intercom-team-about-fin-54/fin-s-flawed-assumed-resolved-pricing-design-8929

## The economics
- **Robinhood.** The model is sold at list price; the business is the $776M transaction line (Q2). An agent that trades "around the clock", with per-trade approval switchable off, is a volume engine pointed at that line. The incentive is structural. It does not need intent.
- **Banks.** ~$4.04T sits at 0% and checking averages 0.07%, against ~4.2% available. On $10k that is roughly $410 a year (4.20 - 0.07; our arithmetic, not Slok's). The margin was paid for by not shopping. An agent that shops takes the not-shopping away.
- **Per-action vendors.** $0.10-0.60 per Agentforce action, 5 credits per Copilot "agent action", $0.99-2.00 per outcome. In each case **the seller defines and counts the unit**: Zendesk's own LLM judges "resolved", Intercom counts a silent exit as resolved, Salesforce actions cost 20 to 120 credits.
- **OpenAI.** Here the meter is a ladder of usage multiples. The unit is still the seller's ("25 times the Plus allowance") and was cut for new subscribers at the same price. Kabra's question stands: which cost are you measuring?

## Angles this opens
- **Sun 11 pt 1:** "what it is paid on" is the third delegation term. The FINRA quantitative-suitability rule is the ready-made human version: we already wrote a rule for delegates paid per transaction, and agents have none.
- **Proposed three points for the draft:**
  1. The AI is sold at list; the money is in what it does (Robinhood).
  2. Inertia was a revenue line, and a delegate that shops removes it (FDIC / Slok / DSS, with BofA's caveat).
  3. Whoever counts the unit owns the margin (Agentforce, Copilot credits, Intercom's assumed resolution, Zendesk's LLM judge, OpenAI's allowance multiples).
- The action for readers: read the unit definition in your agent contract before the price.
