# Research Note -- Non-starters: who has not started with AI at work, and what moves a first use
For: Tue 6 Oct, *Before the First Prompt* (working) · Depth: deep · Date: 2026-10-04

## Bottom line
- **The thesis survives, but only in a narrowed form.** The single biggest stated reason non-users give is that they cannot see a use for it in their own job (Pew 36%; Gallup 39% of non-users). That is the "no first task" problem, and it is the leading reason. Permission is murky for most people: half of US workers say their employer neither encourages nor discourages use (Pew), and only a quarter say their company has told them a clear plan (Gallup).
- **"Not short of desire" is too strong as written, and the evidence will catch it.** Gallup's April 2026 holdout study finds that 43% of non-users in AI-enabled organisations are ethically opposed, and 46% prefer to keep working as they do now. Cui et al. find that 30-40% of engineers given Copilot never tried it, and they put that down to preference. **The fix is SPECIFICITY, not hedging:** most non-starters are missing a task and a signal, and a real minority object. The act is aimed at the first group, and it should not pretend to convert the second.
- **The act itself is well supported.** In the strongest causal evidence available (randomised rollouts), a nudge plus manager encouragement moved first use a long way: Microsoft take-up went from 8.5% to 42.5% within two weeks after follow-up emails, and Accenture's treated group reached 69.4% against 24.4% in control. Gallup: 78% of people whose manager actively supports AI use are frequent users, against 44% of those whose manager does not.

## What's solid
- **48% of US employees never use AI at work; 15% use it daily** -- Gallup AI Indicator, May 2026. [https://www.gallup.com/699797/indicator-artificial-intelligence.aspx]
- **Only 25% say their company has communicated a clear integration strategy. 20% do not know whether their organisation has adopted AI at all. 47% say it has integrated AI tools** -- same Gallup indicator, May 2026. *This is the permission vacuum, in Gallup's own numbers.*
- **The manager effect** -- Gallup, Andy Kemp, "AI in the Workplace: What Separates Adopters and Holdouts," 12 Apr 2026 (n=23,717 US employed adults, fielded 4-19 Feb 2026, ±0.9pp). The base is employees in organisations that offer AI tools:
  - 78% of those who strongly agree their manager actively supports AI use are frequent users, against 44% of those who do not strongly agree.
  - 72% of those supported in experimenting are frequent users, against 44% without that support.
  - 68% of those with a clear AI policy are frequent users, against 47% without one.
  - 24% of individual contributors are non-users, against 8% of leaders.
  - [https://www.gallup.com/workplace/704252/workplace-separates-adopters-holdouts.aspx]
- **Why non-users do not use it (Gallup, same study; non-users in AI-enabled organisations)** -- 46% prefer to keep working as they do now, 43% are ethically opposed, 43% worry about data privacy, security or compliance, and 39% "do not believe AI can assist with the work they do." *Among infrequent users (people who did try it), 27% found it unhelpful.*
- **Pew's reasons for never using AI chatbots for work** -- Pew, "Workers' experience with AI chatbots in their jobs," 25 Feb 2025 (ATP Wave 157, **fielded 7-13 Oct 2024**, n=5,273 employed adults):
  - **36%** say a major reason is that there is no use for them in their job.
  - 22% are not interested.
  - 10% do not know how.
  - 9% say their employer does not allow it.
  - Half of workers who are not self-employed say their employer **neither encourages nor discourages** AI chatbots; 12% say it encourages them and 8% say it discourages them.
  - [https://www.pewresearch.org/social-trends/2025/02/25/workers-experience-with-ai-chatbots-in-their-jobs/]
  - ⚠️ The data is nearly two years old. Use it for the *ranking of reasons*, not as a current level.
- **Randomised rollouts: access alone does not start people; a push does** -- Cui, Demirer, Jaffe, Musolff, Peng, Salz, "The Effects of Generative AI on High-Skilled Work" (Feb 2025 draft):
  - Microsoft: 8.5% uptake in the first two weeks, rising to 42.5% within two weeks of follow-up emails.
  - Accenture: training sessions plus managerial encouragement took the treated group to 69.4%, against 24.4% in control.
  - All three firms: "around 30-40% of the engineers not even trying the product," which the authors attribute to "individual preferences and perceived quality of the tool."
  - Short-tenure developers were 9.4pp more likely to adopt.
  - [https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/draft_copilot_experiments.pdf]
- **What the employer does matters more than who the worker is** -- Dillon, Jaffe, Immorlica, Stanton, "Shifting Work Patterns with Generative AI," NBER w33795 (rev. Nov 2025; 7,137 workers, 66 firms). "Firm fixed effects alone explain nearly twice as much variation in individual workers' Copilot use as workers' pre-period work behavior." Coworker access added little. Over 90% of those given Copilot touched it at least once, but the median user used it in only 39% of weeks. [https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33795/w33795.pdf]
- **Telling people it saves time does not start them** -- Humlum and Vestergaard, "The Adoption of ChatGPT" (IZA DP 16992, May 2024; ~100,000 Danish workers, 11 occupations, fielded Nov 2023 to Jan 2024):
  - 43% say they need training to use it.
  - 35% say their employer actively restricts use.
  - An information treatment about time savings "shifts workers' beliefs but with muted effects on adoption behaviors."
  - Women were about 20pp less likely to use it than men in the same occupation.
  - [https://docs.iza.org/dp16992.pdf]
- **Worker-level adoption is 45% (US, May 2026), "widespread but shallow"** -- Bick, Blandin, Deming, Schumacher, "What Work Does Generative AI Do?", St. Louis Fed working paper 2026-017, Aug 2026 (Real-Time Population Survey).
  - Individual fixed effects explain about 44% of adoption variation at the worker-task level. In plain terms: two people doing the same work differ a lot.
  - Clerical and admin roles adopt well below their predicted potential.
  - [https://s3.amazonaws.com/real.stlouisfed.org/wp/2026/2026-017.pdf]
- **Firm side: 19.8% of US firms used AI (BTOS, period ending 3 May 2026)**; 37% at firms with 250+ employees; no significant change under 20 employees from Dec 2025 to May 2026 -- Census, May 2026. [https://census.gov/library/stories/2026/05/ai-use-businesses.html]

## What's contested / watch out
- **Desire vs opportunity.** Gallup's own framing is that non-users show "a motivation gap rather than an access problem." That cuts against the plan's thesis sentence, so do not print the thesis as written. Defensible line: *the largest group of non-starters cannot see the task, and the second-largest has never been told it is allowed; a real minority object, and the piece should leave them alone.*
- **"Cannot see a use for it in my job" is ambiguous.** It reads as a missing first task, but it could equally be an accurate judgment (Bick et al.: receptionists 7.6%, LPNs 10.4%). Say "many," not "most," when claiming it is a missing task.
- **Pew's base.** One fetch said 55% "rarely or never"; another said 69% "do not use" (40% never plus 29% not heard). Both appear in Pew's material for different cuts. If quoting a level, quote the PDF exactly or skip the level and keep the reasons ranking.
- **The Gallup multipliers (9.3x, 7.8x, 7.4x)** compare "strongly agree" against everyone else on perceived transformation. They are correlational and self-reported. The 78/44 split is cleaner; prefer it.
- **Shadow use is the inverse problem.** The Fed note (3 Apr 2026) thinks shadow use is "unlikely ... still a major driver" of the worker/firm gap. Do not claim non-starters are secretly using it.

## Couldn't verify
- **A "Mercor effect" in survey data.** No primary measures "I saw someone punished for using AI, so I stopped." The nearest evidence is Gallup's 43% privacy/compliance worry and Humlum's 35% employer restriction. **Do not assert that fear of punishment is a measured driver.** If Mercor appears, it is an illustration (and the payoff of Tue 29's "I will come back to it"), not data.
- **Pairing or sitting-with as a tested intervention.** I found no RCT of one-to-one pairing on first use. The closest is Accenture's "training + managerial encouragement." The 30-minute sit-with is a proposal built on that evidence, not a tested protocol; write it as his practice (`[YOUR TAKE]`), not as a finding.
- Not reached: the Census BTOS supplement asking firms *why* they do not use AI (it exists per Census, but no figures were fetched).

## The economics
- **The cost of the act is about one manager-hour per person.** The benchmark is Accenture: training plus encouragement moved first use by roughly 45pp over control.
- **The cost of not acting is paying for seats nobody opens.** Microsoft's own engineers took up the tool at 8.5% in the first two weeks with access and no push. Dillon et al.: even among adopters, median use was 39% of weeks. A licence with no first task is shelfware, and the per-seat bill arrives anyway.
- The firm-level difference (firm fixed effects ~2x individual behaviour) means the variable a manager controls is bigger than the one they do not.
