### The second beat — `inference_geo`, and the wording is load-bearing

**Dead sentence, strike on sight:** any form of *"you can't control where the model runs."* True on
6 Aug, false on 7 Aug, refutable with one link.

Three facts the docs keep separate, and collapsing them is how this goes wrong. Verified verbatim:

1. `inference_geo` sets where the model computes, per request. Values are **`"global"` or `"us"`
   only.** No EU, no AU.
2. `workspace_geo` sets where data sits at rest. *"Currently, `"us"` is the only available workspace
   geo"* and *"Workspace geo is set when you create a workspace and can't be changed afterward."*
3. They are independent. Pinning one says nothing about the other.

**Shape is concede-then-sharpen.** The control exists and answers the wrong question: for a non-US
regulated buyer the menu is "United States" or "unspecified," both offshore. **Build the paragraph
around the fixed-at-creation clause** — that is the sharpest thing in the finding, and it is a
decision the organisation already made without knowing it. Straight onto CPS 230 / AUSTRAC turf.

**Cost clause, on his own frame:** US-only inference is *"priced at 1.1x the standard rate across all
token pricing categories"* and *"draws down 1.1 tokens from your committed TPM."* Residency has a
meter on it.

### The third beat — inference hooks, and it is what the capstone needs

Point Claude at **your** AI security server; each governed prompt is held for your allow/deny verdict
**before inference proceeds**. You operate the server. This is the most literal available instance of
governance you build rather than receive — a box you draw, that you run, in the request path.

- Fires once per governed inference request, event type `prompt`, before inference begins.
- Your server sees transcript text, tool calls and results, and text from attachments. Never raw
  file bytes, system prompts, or Anthropic-internal context.
- **Verdicts are allow or deny only.** No rewriting, no redaction. 5-second default timeout.
- Limits that must travel: Claude **Enterprise only**, beta, excludes voice mode and title
  generation, image-only content not inspected, Platform organizations out of scope, **not available
  on Bedrock or Google Cloud.**
- ⚠️ Because it is Enterprise-and-signed-in only, it does **not** reach the personal-Pro-on-a-work-
  laptop population. It therefore does not weaken Fri 14's `permissions.deny` argument — it
  strengthens it. Do not write it as an exposure.

**★ The sentence that pays the capstone: fail-open or fail-closed is the customer's setting.** The
vendor ships the hook; you choose whether it is actually a control. A hook set to fail open is a
guardrail you can point at in a system diagram that stops guarding on timeout — which is **Friday
21's "control that was never a control" arriving as a config flag.** Signpost the Mon↔Fri pairing
explicitly, or the two days read as one argument told twice.
