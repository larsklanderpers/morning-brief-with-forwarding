## The economics (Lars's cost lens)
The seniorisation data *is* the cost argument. The market is **pricing judgment explicitly**: a 62%
wage premium for AI skills, and a two-track split where the judgment-keeping "professionalised"
track grows ~2x faster with ~42% faster wage growth. Read against the entry rung, that's the market
saying it will pay a large premium for judgment-at-entry -- and won't hire the judgment-*less*
entrant at all (7x more likely to demand senior skills; AI-exposed entry roles +35% while others
-10%). So the rung didn't get cheaper, it got **repriced above what a beginner can supply.** The
counter-move's economics: the **cost of curiosity has collapsed to ~0** (free 6-hour courses,
$20/mo tooling) while the **cost of tooling has not** -- which is precisely why "build your own
ladder" is now possible but must be done prudently, cheapest-rung-first (hands to Saturday's on-ramp
ladder). The honest guardrail: the 62% premium rewards judgment, and judgment is still
manufactured by doing the work under someone good -- which is why build-your-own is additive
(an escape hatch) and not a substitute for the apprenticeship the employer still owes.
