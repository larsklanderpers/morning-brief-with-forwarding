# Handoff — the week of 17–23 Aug, reinterpreted against where 10–16 actually landed

Written 12 Aug 2026, after the 10–16 week was drafted, panelled and scheduled end to end.
Reads: `References/week-plan_2026-08-17.md` (status EXPLORATORY v0, written **2 Aug**) against
`References/week-plan_2026-08-10.md` (LOCKED v2) and the seven pieces actually shipped.

**The one thing to understand before reading the 17–23 plan again.** It was written on 2 August,
before a single day of the 10–16 week existed. Its own Open Item #1 says so: *"Aug 10–16 map is not
on disk. Needed to settle the capstone fork (A vs B)."* That map now exists, and the week it
described has been written. So the 17–23 plan is not wrong — it is **uninformed**, and almost every
open question in it is now answerable. This document answers them.

---

## 1. The capstone fork is resolved, and neither branch survives intact

The plan offered two spines and could not choose without the 10–16 map.

**Spine B — "What Is the Governor, and Who Can Reach It?" — is spent.** Fri 14 Aug spent the
governor argument completely: `permissions.deny` in managed settings as the single control the
customer holds outright, the 31 Jul sign / camera / governor ladder paid off by name, and the
closing move that the seat was never anybody's job. Running it again on 21 Aug is a third outing of
the same frame in three weeks. **Do not run Spine B as the capstone.**

**Spine A — "The Window Just Changed Shape" — was spent as a Saturday.** Sat 15 Aug ran its core:
the question shifting from *will this happen to me* to *how fast can I shape this in my function*,
the archetype convergence, the domain-knowledge finding, the entry-level caveat, and the close on
positioning rather than prediction. The planned Sun 23 capstone summary — *"every lane this week
shows capability arriving ahead of position… what you do is take a position"* — is now Sat 15's
argument with a week of different evidence under it.

**Worse, there is a three-way collision the plan cannot see.** Sat 15 (shipped), Sat 22 (planned,
closing on *"the useful move was never predicting. It is positioning"*) and Sun 23 (planned,
*"the gap is positional"*) all land in the same place. Two of the three have to move.

**What the capstone should be instead.** Sun 16 ends with an explicit, printed promise:

> *"The argument I have been circling for three weeks is that governance is something you build
> rather than something you receive, and that a guardrail you cannot point at in a system diagram
> is not a guardrail at all. This week was the case for why that matters. Next week is the part
> where we build it."*

That is a debt, in public, and it is also the spine the LOCKED 10–16 plan reserved for exactly this
week. **The 23 Aug capstone should be the build-it capstone.** Not "take a position" — *here is
what you build, and here is how you know it is real.* It has the strongest possible setup: six
instruments across the previous week that were strong in one dimension and empty in the other, and
one (AUSTRAC) that was not.

## 3. The strongest asset 10–16 produced, and it is a tool rather than a topic

The Sun 16 capstone established a frame that the whole of 17–23 can use without re-running it:

> **Every governance instrument has two independent properties — how far it can see, and how hard
> it can hold. And sight is never chosen directly: you choose what the instrument is *for*, and the
> scope falls out of the purpose.**

This is worth more to next week than any single story, because it **rescues Friday 21 without
re-running the governor**.

Fri 21's planned thesis is that Article 50 requires labelling not control, the OAIC sweep measures
disclosure not auditability, "data in Frankfurt" is a disclosure claim not a sovereignty guarantee,
and open weights give on-prem inference not sovereign compute. Every one of those is **sight
without grip**, stated four times in four domains. Written in the capstone's vocabulary, Friday
becomes *the disclosure layer is fully built and it is the sight half; nobody built the grip half* —
which is the same argument, sharper, in the week's own language, with no third outing of "governor."

**Recommendation: keep the Friday slot and the material. Change the frame from governor to
sight/grip.** That also makes Friday a genuine bridge into the build-it capstone instead of a
repeat.

## 4. Day-by-day, what changed

**Mon 18 Aug — "the vendor's machine outpaced the vendor's humans" (Chrome CVE triage).**
⚠️ **Thesis collision, and it is a real one.** Fri 14 Aug's central number is that a classifier
caught 89% of dangerous commands where humans caught 13.6% — *the machine outperformed the human it
replaced*. The Sun 16 capstone re-ran that number as the week's honest exception. Mon 17's planned
sharpest beat is *"the vendor's own machine is outpacing the vendor's own humans."* That is the
same claim, third outing, inside a seven-day window. Options: (a) move this piece later in the week
and open with the collision declared, the way Thu 13 declared the Wed 12 callback; (b) re-point it
onto blast radius alone and drop the machine-vs-human beat entirely, which the plan's own "the
answer is not faster triage, it is blast radius" line already supports; (c) swap the slot for
**The model isn't the product. The harness is.** Recommend (b) or (c).

**Tue 18 — "Write the Brief, Not the Prompt."** Survives, and it got *stronger*. Sat 15's action
section already told readers to write down three things they would catch that a new hire would not,
hand one to an agent, watch it fail, then write the tell down. That is a specification exercise
without the name. Tuesday can pick it up explicitly: *last Saturday you wrote a tell; here is what
it is called and how to write the whole brief.* One caution: the plan sets "under 1,000 words on
purpose," and Lars ruled on 12 Aug that **lightness is a state of being, not a length metric**.
Do not treat 1,000 as a gate.

**Wed 19 — "Open Weights From the House That Closed Them."** Survives cleanly, and the capstone
hands it a better question than the one it has. The plan frames it as *the last procurement
objection dies*. Under the new frame the sharper version is: **what is the purpose your procurement
rule was scoped to?** A rule written to keep models off-prem was scoped for a world where that was
the only option. Same piece, and it inherits the week-before's argument instead of standing alone.
⚠️ The Sat 8 Aug on-ramp ladder callback is still available but "ladder / rung" is a Seat 5 hazard
if anything else that week touches it — check before drafting.

**Thu 20 — "The Roles Aren't Going. The Job Is."** Survives, and 10–16 handed it a genuinely new
number the plan does not have. The Ramp × Revelio Labs study (21,559 US companies, published 30 Jun
2026) found the heaviest AI adopters grew **entry-level** employment by around **12%**, and
entry-level workers became a larger *share* of their workforce, while light adopters trimmed their
junior ranks. That is the hardest evidence yet for "who gets redefined at" versus "who gets to
redefine," and it says the answer is partly *which employer you pick*. It also pairs directly with
Filby's trust-erosion caution rather than being cancelled by it. Sat 15 already spent one paragraph
of it; do not re-run the paragraph, do run the argument further.

**Fri 21 — see §3. Keep it, re-frame it.**

**Sat 22 — "A Better Map, Not a Better Prediction." This is the one in real trouble.** Its close
duplicates Sat 15's close. Its evidence base is largely spent or killed: the archetype convergence
ran on Sat 15, the FDE +729% figure was used there as the *provenance discipline benchmark*, the
Agent Orchestrator comp band was killed as single-source and method-free, and the "eight role
archetypes" figure was killed for untraceable provenance. What is left is 1.3M new roles, the wage
premium (still unpinned — see §6) and 60% degree-free by 2030, none of which has been through the
research assistant. **Recommend: re-scope or replace.** The strongest available replacement is the
Anthropic 400k-session study — see §5.

**Sun 23 — the build-it capstone. See §1.**

## 5. Unspent material 10–16 generated, in rough order of value

1. **The Anthropic 400,000-session study (16 Jun 2026, ~235,000 users, Oct 2025–Apr 2026).**
   Sat 15 spent two paragraphs of it. The headline nobody has written: **verified success ran
   around 26–30% overall, and even expert-rated sessions topped out around a third.** Two thirds to
   three quarters of sessions produced nothing anyone could confirm worked. That is the vendor's own
   measurement of its own product, published, and essentially unread. It is a Trust Boundaries piece

   the constructive read is that the human interrogating the output is where the other 70% lives.
   Source: `References/research_saturday-verify-items_2026-08-12.md`.
2. **AUSTRAC as the design principle.** *Sight by function, grip by statute.* The 10–16 week found
   exactly one instrument that has both, and the reason is that it was scoped by *what is being
   done and who relies on it* rather than by technology. That is the constructive spine of the
   build-it capstone and it is on Lars's own turf.
3. **The banked CPS 230 piece** (`substack_draft_cheapest-way-to-comply_2026-08-12-rv1.md`,
   ~3,100w, unpanelled). Still reserved. Its payload — *the technology-agnostic instrument catches
   vendor AI; the AI-specific one only registers what the agency runs itself* — is now considerably
   stronger, because the capstone built the vocabulary that explains why. If any 17–23 slot opens,
   this is the first thing to reach for. **Do not spend it as a subordinate clause.**
4. **The provenance case study.** The lawyer-vs-engineer claim travelled comment → brief (31 Jul,
   1 Aug) → signal note → draft in eleven days, and the 1 Aug brief printed the tell in a
   parenthesis while treating it as evidence anyway. That is a piece about how claims launder, with
   a worked example from inside his own pipeline. Handle carefully — it must not become a piece
   about the newsletter.
5. **SA royal commission terms of reference, mid-to-late September.** Diary item, not this week.
   That is when it becomes writable in detail.
