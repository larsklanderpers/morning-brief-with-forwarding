# Research Note — OAIC final ADM guidance status
For: OAIC ADM / clubs audience · Depth: deep · Date: 2026-07-27

## Bottom line
As of today (Mon 27 July 2026), the OAIC's **final** ADM transparency guidance has **not** been published — it remains at the issues-paper/consultation stage. The OAIC released an **Automated Decision-Making Issues Paper on 18 May 2026**, ran consultation to **15 June 2026**, and has publicly said it **intends to publish final guidance by September 2026**, ahead of the obligation's commencement. The underlying statutory obligation — new **APP 1.7–1.9**, requiring privacy policies to disclose ADM use — commences **10 December 2026** (24 months after the amending Act's 10 December 2024 assent); there is no separate grace/transitional period beyond that lead time. For a clubs/gaming audience the live risk is that automated **exclusion and risk-scoring** decisions on patrons are the kind of "significant effect on rights or interests" the regime is built to capture, and "licensed venues" are already a named OAIC privacy-policy enforcement target.

## What's solid
- **Final guidance is NOT out yet — still consultation/draft.** The OAIC's own page is titled "Consultation on Guidance for Transparency in Automated Decision Making" and describes an Issues Paper open for feedback, not final guidance. (oaic.gov.au consultation page; corroborated by Norton Rose, MinterEllison, Allens, HSF, JWS — all June 2026, all still describing it as consultation).
- **Issues Paper released 18 May 2026; consultation closed 15 June 2026 ("Monday 15 June 2026").** (OAIC page; Allens; JWS).
- **OAIC intends to publish final guidance "by September 2026," before commencement.** Verbatim from HSF: "the OAIC has confirmed that it intends to publish regulatory guidance on this obligation by September 2026." Corroborated by Allens ("The OAIC intends to release guidance by September 2026, before the commencement date…") and JWS. [4 independent sources].
- **The ADM transparency obligation commences 10 December 2026.** OAIC's own page: "From 10 December 2026," APP entities must disclose ADM in privacy policies. Corroborated by JWS, Norton Rose, Allens, MinterEllison, HSF.
- **Statutory basis:** the *Privacy and Other Legislation Amendment Act 2024* (Cth) (assent 10 Dec 2024) inserts new **APP 1.7, 1.8 and 1.9** into Schedule 1 of the *Privacy Act 1988*. The ADM measure sits in **Schedule 1, Part 15 ("Automated decisions and privacy policies")** of the amending Act (note: not "Schedule 2" as sometimes cited). (legislation.gov.au TOC + JWS/Allens/Norton Rose).
- **What must be disclosed:** (1) the kinds of personal information used in the ADM; (2) the kinds of decisions made **solely** by a computer program; and (3) the kinds of decisions where a computer program does something **substantially and directly related** to making the decision. (OAIC Issues Paper; Norton Rose).
- **Trigger test (three limbs):** an entity arranged for a computer program to make, or do a thing substantially and directly related to making, a decision; that decision could **reasonably be expected to significantly affect the individual's rights or interests**; and personal information is used in the program's operation. (Issues Paper; JWS; HSF).
- **"Licensed venues" are a named OAIC privacy-policy enforcement target.** The OAIC's high-risk-sector privacy-policy review lists licensed venues (identity verification for entry) alongside pharmacies, car rental/dealerships, pawnbrokers and property/rental. (MinterEllison).

## What's contested / watch out
- **Scope is deliberately broad and unsettled.** Bird & Bird's headline is that the "OAIC signals a broad reading." The OAIC reads "substantially" as the automated output being "a key factor" in a human's decision, so **human-in-the-loop / recommendation** systems can still be caught — it is not limited to fully automated decisions. Where the line falls for advisory risk scores is genuinely contested.
- **"Significantly affects rights or interests" threshold.** The Issues Paper says the effect must be "more than trivial… potential to significantly influence the circumstances of the individual," with heightened sensitivity for **vulnerable persons and minority groups** — a framing that matters a lot for problem-gambling risk scoring. The exact threshold is what the final guidance still has to pin down.
- **Statutory examples are public-sector-flavoured.** APP 1.9's illustrations (grant/refuse a statutory benefit; affect rights under a contract; affect access to a significant service or support) map awkwardly onto commercial venue operations — application to loyalty/gaming is an inference from the framework, not something the OAIC has spelled out for this sector.
- **Commencement day precision.** OAIC states "from 10 December 2026." A strict "day after the end of 24 months" reading of the assent-based mechanism could put it at 11 December 2026. Immaterial in practice; if a draft states a hard date, use OAIC's "10 December 2026."

## The economics / compliance cost angle
- **The cost is front-loaded into a discovery exercise, not the privacy-policy edit.** The real effort for a club group is mapping *where* automated or automation-assisted decisions touch patrons — loyalty engines, cashless-gaming and card-based play systems, AML/CTF transaction monitoring, and increasingly automated problem-gambling / harm-minimisation risk flags — then classifying each as "solely" vs "substantially" automated. The two-line privacy-policy update is trivial; the systems inventory behind it is not.
- **Human-in-the-loop is not a free pass.** Because "substantially" catches systems where an automated score is a "key factor," a club can't dodge disclosure by having a staff member rubber-stamp an automated exclusion or risk flag. That removes a cheap compliance escape hatch and pushes cost toward genuine model/documentation transparency.
- **Overlap with AML/CTF and state gaming reforms compounds the spend.** Venues are already building automated monitoring for AUSTRAC/AML and state harm-minimisation regimes; the ADM obligation adds a *transparency* layer on top of systems being stood up for other reasons — so the marginal cost is documentation and disclosure, but the reputational cost of a mismatch (policy says X, the risk engine does Y) is where enforcement bites.
- **Enforcement backdrop raises the stakes.** The OAIC is actively reviewing privacy policies in named high-risk sectors including licensed venues, and post-reform penalties are materially higher — so a thin or inaccurate ADM disclosure is a live enforcement exposure, not a paperwork nicety.

## Angles this opens
- **"The guidance clubs are being asked to comply with doesn't exist yet."** The obligation lands 10 Dec 2026 but final guidance is only promised "by September 2026" — a ~3-month runway to re-paper systems, and it's already running late.
- **Self-exclusion by algorithm.** If a venue's system auto-flags or auto-decides exclusion/intervention on a patron, that's close to the core of "significantly affects rights or interests" — and it collides with harm-minimisation duties.
- **Loyalty tiers and differential pricing.** OAIC scope examples include differential pricing and restricted access to goods/services — does a loyalty engine that gates comps, offers or access count?
- **Human-in-the-loop theatre.** The "substantially automated" limb means a staff rubber-stamp won't launder an automated decision out of scope.
- **Overlap map:** ADM transparency vs AUSTRAC AML/CTF monitoring vs state cashless-gaming/harm-minimisation — three automated-surveillance regimes converging on the same patron data.
