## WHAT'S SOLID (new in this note)

### Leg 1, the ad hoc framework: the senators' own word
- **Warner, Gillibrand, Coons, Kelly, Schiff (all D) to Rubio, Bessent, Lutnick, Wiles, Kratsios,
  Cairncross, 4 Aug 2026.** Primary: warner.senate.gov press release with full letter text; PDF at
  `warner.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/Senate-AI-Model-Access-Letter.pdf`. Verbatim:
  - *"the Administration's ad hoc and unpredictable approach undermines U.S. competitiveness,
    heightening market incentives to adopt open weight models from vendors based in the People's
    Republic of China"*
  - *"Ultimately, a rigorous, predictable, and competitiveness enhancing process for evaluating frontier
    models requires a statutory framework."*
  - On Commerce's 12 Jun directive to Anthropic: the resolution was negotiated *"outside of public
    view"* over eighteen days.
  - Asked for an answer within 30 days, including *"Whether opportunities exist for independent,
    third-party experts to participate in the benchmarking process."*
  ★ **Why it matters for the piece: the case for a public, statutory framework is being made on
  competitiveness and China grounds, by Democrats, in the administration's own vocabulary.** That is
  the no-doom version of the argument, already on letterhead.
- **EO 14409, re-footed at the Federal Register** (91 FR 34565, 5 Jun 2026, doc 2026-11415): "Executive
  Order 14409 of June 2, 2026." §3 sentence confirmed verbatim: *"Nothing in this section shall be
  construed to authorize the creation of a mandatory governmental licensing, preclearance, or permitting
  requirement for the development, publication, release, or distribution of new AI models, including
  frontier models."* Also confirmed: *"design a voluntary framework"* and *"develop and maintain a
  classified benchmarking process."* The earlier dig's quote holds exactly.

### Leg 3, standards commitment: the good bill exists, and nobody has held a hearing on it
- **H.R.9925, FRONTIER Act, 119th Congress, introduced 23 Jul 2026.** Primary: congress.gov, text tab
  (the summary tab says "A summary is in progress"). Full name: *"Frontier Risk Oversight, National
  Transparency, Independent Evaluation, and Reporting Act."* Sponsor Obernolte (R-CA-23); cosponsors
  Trahan, Houchin, Peters, Scott Franklin, Subramanyam (three R, three D by name; party labels from
  member records, the cosponsor tab was not opened). **Latest action: referral, 23 Jul. Status:
  Introduced.** What the text does:
  - Creates an **Under Secretary of Commerce for AI Security.**
  - **Mandatory** annual independent third-party audit for large frontier developers; the auditor may
    have no financial interest in the developer and payment may not depend on results.
  - **Licensed independent verification organizations** conducting ongoing assessment of very large
    frontier developers (defined as over **$5B** gross revenue and at least **$10B** AI development
    spend over 36 months).
  - Developer must **publish** a high-level summary and a redacted copy of each assessment report within
    30 days, with a redaction-dispute process.
  - **Civil penalty up to $1,000,000 per violation, each day a separate violation**, enforceable by the
    US Attorney General and by **opted-in State Attorneys General**.
  - IVO must refer an imminent catastrophic risk to the Secretary within **72 hours**; §8 lets the
    Secretary issue an **emergency order suspending or restricting** development or deployment.
  - **Preempts** state laws imposing "new substantive obligations on artificial intelligence developers"
    in Covered Subject Areas (including frontier AI risk transparency), with carve-outs for generally
    applicable law and for regulating deployers and users.
  - FOIA-exempts materials given to the Under Secretary.
  ★★ **This is the sharpest standards-commitment fact available.** A bipartisan House bill that builds
  exactly the standards machinery he says Washington won't commit to, including the pre-release power EO
  14409 §3(c) disclaims, has sat for seven weeks with **no hearing and no markup**, while the leadership
  bill that has momentum is the one Transformer's source calls voluntary. That is a statement about
  priorities, and it can be made without a word about extinction.
- **California SB 813 (McNerney), Chapter 179, approved and filed 9 Sep 2026.** Primary: leginfo, chaptered
  text. By **1 Jan 2028** the Government Operations Agency must set criteria for designating
  **independent verification organizations**, including conflict-of-interest and independence rules and
  a ban on payment contingent on results; designated IVOs report annually to the agency and Legislature.
  ⚠️ **It is optional for developers:** §8898.4(a)(3), the chapter does not *"Require any person... to engage
  an IVO or to undergo a covered AI audit."* An audit to the standard is *"relevant to, but not conclusive
  of"* a harm action. ★ **Honest framing: the state that went furthest built a register of approved
  auditors and made using it voluntary.** It still out-commits Washington (a statute, a date, an agency),
  but do not print "California requires independent audits." Transformer's "lays the groundwork" is the
  accurate verb.
