# Research Note — "Guarded the Secret Sauce for Decades" (analogy pool for the drain concept)
For: Ambient AI Privacy Drain thread (Tue "Every Correction…" + Pieces 2-4) · Depth: deep · Date: 2026-07-23

## Bottom line
The mirror image of the exhaust/drain argument: companies that guarded a trade secret *vigorously, for decades, on purpose.* Five best-sourced, most vivid: **Coca-Cola** (secret since 1886, its own vault), **KFC** (Sanders' pencil-written 11 herbs, split across suppliers), **Chartreuse** (only two monks alive know it, 250+ years), **WD-40** (deliberately never patented, 70+ years), and **Google's ranking algorithm** (litigated to keep sealed inside a federal antitrust trial). The killer sub-angle for the piece: two of them (Coca-Cola, WD-40) *chose not to patent* precisely because a patent means public disclosure and a 20-year clock -- a trade secret lasts forever, but only if you never let it out. Leaking your know-how into an AI is the accidental version of that same disclosure, with none of the monopoly you'd at least get from a patent.

## The five cases

### 1. Coca-Cola -- "Merchandise 7X"
- Secret since **1886 (~140 years)**. Written formula sat in an Atlanta bank vault (SunTrust) for **86 years**, then moved **Dec 2011** to a purpose-built "Vault of the Secret Formula" at the World of Coca-Cola. Deliberately **never patented**.
- Source: [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2012/01/06/vault-with-coca-cola-trade-secret-formula-on-public-display/id=21588/) · [Africa Check (who knows it)](https://africacheck.org/fact-checks/meta-programme-fact-checks/no-more-two-people-know-coca-colas-secret-formula)
- **Drop-in:** the recipe spent 86 years in a bank vault before Coca-Cola built it its own bomb-proof shrine -- a company that turned *keeping* a secret into a ticketed attraction.
- ⚠️ **LORE, do NOT print as fact:** the "only two executives, each knows half, never fly on the same plane" story is a **myth** (Coca-Cola says "a few people"; Snopes/Africa Check rate it false).

### 2. KFC -- Original Recipe, 11 herbs and spices
- Sanders finalized it ~**1940 (~85 years)**; the original is **handwritten in pencil, signed by Sanders**. In **2008-09** KFC sealed it in a Louisville vault (concrete, motion detectors, 24-hr cameras); moved it in a lockbox **handcuffed to a security consultant's wrist**. Ingredients blended by **multiple suppliers who each know only part**.
- Source: [AP via Deseret News](https://www.deseret.com/2009/2/10/20301211/colonel-s-secret-recipe-in-new-safer-vault-at-kfc/) · [KFC](https://global.kfc.com/stories/often-imitated-never-duplicated)
- **Drop-in:** the security consultant called it "an onion of security -- many layers"; the one-page recipe rode back to its vault handcuffed to a courier's wrist.
- ⚠️ The 2016 Chicago Tribune "leaked recipe" (nephew Joe Ledington's scrapbook) is real reporting but **KFC disputes it's authentic** -- don't call it *the* recipe.

### 3. Chartreuse -- the Carthusian monks' elixir
- Manuscript **1605**, recipe finalized by **1764 (250+ years)**, through the French Revolution and two World Wars. **Only two monks** know the full recipe at a time; the manuscript sits in a **safe only one monk has the key to**; the monks **pre-mix the botanicals themselves** so even the distillery never sees the full list.
- Source: [Chartreuse (official)](https://www.chartreuse.fr/en/story/) · [Chowhound](https://www.chowhound.com/1632848/chartreuse-liqueur-monks/)
- **Drop-in:** when demand recently outran supply, the monks refused to scale up -- secrecy and their vows mattered more than the money, so the world's bartenders simply ran short.
- ✅ **SOLID** and genuinely documented (unlike the Coca-Cola two-person myth -- this two-monks detail is real).

### 4. WD-40 -- the formula that was never patented
- Secret since **1953 (70+ years)**; named for "Water Displacement, 40th formula." **Deliberately never patented** to avoid the disclosure a patent requires. Formula lives in a **San Diego bank vault**; on its 65th anniversary (2018) WD-40 moved it via **Brink's**, CEO **handcuffed to the briefcase**.
- Source: [WD-40 press release (PR Newswire)](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/wd-40-company-enlists-armored-security-to-move-top-secret-formula-to-new-location-celebrates-65th-anniversary-300711731.html) · [Klemchuk law analysis](https://www.klemchuk.com/ideate/how-wd-40-keeps-its-famous-secret-formula-protected)
- **Drop-in:** WD-40 could have patented its formula in 1953 and owned it for 20 years -- instead it chose the vault, and 70 years on still owns it outright. A patent would have handed the recipe to every rival by the mid-1970s.
- ✅ SOLID on never-patented + trade-secret strategy (the handcuff move is a PR stunt -- note that if used).

### 5. Google -- the search-ranking algorithm
- Trade secret since **1998 (~28 years)**. In the **2023 US v. Google** antitrust trial, Google got roughly **a third of trial days closed** and ~**1,000 exhibits sealed**, arguing disclosure would cause irreversible harm (an Apple exec testified 10 min in public, 4 hours behind closed doors).
- Source: [Slate](https://slate.com/technology/2023/10/google-trial-secret-redactions-trade-secrets-closed-door-testimony.html) · [DOJ trial exhibits](https://www.justice.gov/atr/us-and-plaintiff-states-v-google-llc-2023-trial-exhibits)
- **Drop-in:** Google guards its ranking formula so tightly it persuaded a federal judge to shut the public out of a third of its own antitrust trial rather than say out loud how Search works.
- ✅ SOLID. ⚠️ minor: a 2024 internal-docs leak exposed *some* ranking feature names -- frame as "treated as a trade secret and litigated to keep sealed," not "never revealed anything."

## The trade-secret-vs-patent angle (sharpest hook for the piece)
A trade secret can last **forever -- but only if you never let it out.** A patent is the opposite bargain: you publish the recipe to the world in exchange for a ~20-year monopoly, then it's public domain. **WD-40** (invented 1953, never patented, still exclusively theirs) and **Coca-Cola** (a patent would have gone public before WWI) are the textbook illustrations. The drain argument lands here: leaking your operating know-how into an AI is the *accidental* version of a patent's disclosure -- all of the loss (your secret is out), none of the 20-year monopoly you'd at least have bargained for.

## Honorable mentions
- **Dr Pepper's "23 flavors"** (trade secret, "two halves in two banks" -- but that split detail is closer to lore; source carefully).
- **Twinkies / Listerine / Angostura bitters** -- long-running formula secrets, good for a quick "the pattern is everywhere" list.
- **McDonald's Big Mac special sauce** -- ⚠️ actually a **cautionary counter-example**: reportedly lost and re-created, and McDonald's later **published a step-by-step recipe video**, arguably destroying any trade-secret protection. Perfect as a "how to LOSE your secret sauce" story (the exact behavior the piece warns against) -- a bad example of *guarding* one. The cleanest structure: open on one guarded-for-decades secret (Chartreuse or Coca-Cola are the most vivid), land the trade-secret-vs-patent point (a secret lasts forever only if you never let it out), then turn: your operating know-how is your Merchandise 7X, and feeding it into an AI is leaking the formula out of the vault one correction at a time -- the accidental disclosure with none of the patent's upside.
