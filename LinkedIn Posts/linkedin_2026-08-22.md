The trust infrastructure of modern IT doesn't fail all at once. It erodes at the seams — and this week, every seam got tested simultaneously.

Three malicious Rust crates shipped from a compromised maintainer account on crates.io. A GitHub Actions workflow injection flaw sat inside Snowflake's public connector repo. N-able N-central — the RMM platform that gives managed service providers access to hundreds of client environments — is being actively exploited in Australia right now. An AI testing vendor misconfiguration exposed OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta model outputs to external systems. All in the same week.

None of these are the perimeter you've been hardening.

They're the maintainer who holds the key to a dependency used by 40,000 projects. The CI/CD pipeline that runs automatically on every pull request. The MSP that has privileged access to your entire fleet. The third-party evaluation partner that processes your most sensitive AI outputs.

We've spent fifteen years building better perimeters. Adversaries responded by going above them — into the supply chain, the tooling, the trusted intermediaries. The attack surface isn't your network anymore. It's everything you depend on.

And the adversary side is keeping pace. CISA this week confirmed that Gunra ransomware — a Conti-derived RaaS operation — is targeting healthcare, critical infrastructure, financial services, and government simultaneously, using internet-exposed firewalls and VPN gateways as the entry point. DeadLock ransomware now uses Polygon smart contracts to route around law enforcement infrastructure takedowns. Researchers documented the first fully autonomous AI agent executing a ransomware attack — exploit, credential theft, lateral movement, encryption — without a human in the loop.

The economics of attack are improving faster than the economics of defence.

So what does this mean for decision-makers?

Three things.

First, your security posture is now inseparable from your vendor security posture. Your patch cadence doesn't matter if your MSP's RMM platform is compromised. Your secure coding practices don't matter if a dependency in your package graph ships malicious code from a hijacked maintainer. Vendor security reviews need to graduate from annual checkbox exercises to continuous monitoring.

Second, AI infrastructure needs to be treated as production infrastructure — not a lab. The Irregular testing incident (same vendor, three separate AI companies exposed) is a preview of what happens when AI evaluation, red-teaming, and model testing run on infrastructure that doesn't meet the same controls as production. The attack surface is growing faster than the security culture around it.

Third, autonomous attack tools change the required response window. If an AI agent can move from initial access to encrypted database without human pacing, the detection-to-isolation window that was acceptable against human operators becomes dangerously insufficient. Organisations still running 24–48 hour mean-time-to-detect against lateral movement are already behind.

The threat is industrialising. The trust assumptions underpinning modern IT are the target. Neither of those trends reverses on its own.

The organisations that get through the next 12 months without a material incident will be the ones that treated their dependency graph as a security perimeter — not as background infrastructure.
