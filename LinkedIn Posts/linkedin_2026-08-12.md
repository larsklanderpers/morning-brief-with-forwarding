Your AI security agent just became an insider threat. And it happened through a Cloudflare firewall block.

That's the finding Tenet Security dropped at DEF CON this week. Researchers demonstrated that an AI agent asked to review a blocked web request would read the attacker's embedded instructions — logged word-for-word by the firewall — and execute them. DNS hijacked. Credentials exfiltrated. The block that was supposed to stop the attack became the delivery mechanism. They called it "Ghostjacking." It worked nine times out of ten against AI coding tools.

This is happening on the same week that North Korea's Lazarus Group got caught exploiting a Windows zero-day (CVE-2026-68820) against defence and aerospace organisations in Europe and India — through fake LinkedIn recruiter messages. The zero-day gave them SYSTEM-level access. The campaign deployed two previously unknown implants and hid C2 traffic inside compromised webmail servers to look like legitimate email traffic. Microsoft patched it yesterday, 421 CVEs into their August release. CISA has a federal deadline of 26 August.

Three things are happening simultaneously, and they connect:

**First**: Attackers are finding and chaining zero-days faster. The Lazarus campaign had this vulnerability in operational use before Microsoft knew it existed. OpenAI's new GPT-5.6-Cyber model — just announced, access restricted — completes exploit chain development prompts at a 95% rate. That capability will not stay exclusive.

**Second**: The tools we're deploying to manage the first problem are becoming attack surface themselves. Ghostjacking targets Cloudflare, Datadog, and Sentry — the exact platforms that security teams use for triage and alerting. An AI that trusts its logs is an AI that can be instructed through its logs.

**Third**: Critical infrastructure is being pre-positioned against. Iranian hackers have now confirmed access to industrial control systems at water utilities across at least 12 US states since late July. No major disruptions yet. That's not reassurance — that's reconnaissance.

The Cisco firewall zero-day (CVE-2026-20349) patched today has a federal remediation deadline of **14 August**. Two days. If your network perimeter runs Cisco ASA or FTD and you haven't deployed the hotfix, your SSL VPN can be taken offline without authentication right now.

The pattern across today's intelligence isn't complexity — it's speed asymmetry. Adversaries are operating at a tempo that assumes defenders are managing 421 CVEs across a 30-day patch cycle while simultaneously trying to vet whether their AI triage tools are reading malicious instructions from their own logs.

The question I'd be asking your security team today isn't "are we patched?" — it's "do our AI-assisted tools treat log data as a trusted input or an adversarial one?" Because right now, for most organisations, the honest answer is the wrong one.
