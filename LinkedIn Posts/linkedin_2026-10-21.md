At 14:00 UTC today, the PHP project ships fixes for two critical flaws. One security firm expects mass exploitation to begin within 15 minutes of the patch going public.

Fifteen minutes. Most change advisory boards take longer than that to find a meeting room.

That number is the thread running through this week's security news, and it should change how we think about defence.

Details of the two PHP flaws leaked before coordinated disclosure. Every PHP version since PHP 5 is affected. That covers WordPress, Drupal, Magento and a long tail of member portals, booking systems and intranets that nobody has on their critical asset list. The moment the patch lands, attackers can compare old and new code and know exactly where to aim.

The same week, someone used a hijacked developer account to swap OVHcloud's official command-line tool for a secret stealer. The poisoned release was live for about 94 minutes. Researchers say the account had been compromised since March. Seven months of patience, then an hour and a half of action, aimed at the machines that hold cloud keys.

Google's threat team described an even tighter pattern at AOMEI, a backup software vendor. Its update server pushed a backdoor in bursts of five to ten minutes at a time, between April and July. Short enough that a once-a-day check would never see it. And the vendor dismissed the first warning it received.

Then there is the automation. Researchers found criminals running an open-source AI agent platform called Cairn against more than a thousand targets, mostly e-commerce and payment sites in the US, Japan and Australia. They succeeded about one time in ten. Reuters reports more than 100 companies were breached. A 10% hit rate would get a human fired. For software that works around the clock for the price of compute, it is a business model.

Now set that against the defenders' clock.

CISA's new advisory on SafePay ransomware counts at least 600 victims. The newest vulnerability on its list of entry points is two years old. A healthcare billing firm that detected a breach in October 2025 took until September 2026 to notify 970,000 people.

So attackers are working in minutes, and plenty of defenders are still working in months.

Buying another tool does not close that gap. Three things do.

First, know your exposure before the clock starts. If you cannot say today which of your systems run PHP and who patches them, you will lose the race this afternoon.

Second, treat software updates as an attack surface, not just a fix. Release immutability, signed builds you actually verify, and behavioural monitoring of the tools with the most privilege: backup agents, CLIs, remote management.

Third, measure the time it takes you to act, not whether you have the control. A patch policy that says "critical within 14 days" was written for a world that no longer exists.

The question for every board this quarter is not "are we protected?" It is "how many minutes does it take us to respond, and who is awake when it matters?"
