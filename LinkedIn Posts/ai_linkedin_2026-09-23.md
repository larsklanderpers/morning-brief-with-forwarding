# LinkedIn Post — 23 September 2026

**Theme:** The week AI governance went from theory to enforcement — and the three signals your board needs to see

---

## Post

The EU AI Act just became active enforcement — not "coming soon."

Most organisations still treat it as a 2027 problem. But Article 50 has been live since August 2: right now, today, you must disclose when users interact with AI, and label synthetic content. Penalties run to €35M or 7% of global turnover.

This week also brought the Australian Privacy Amendment Bill out of consultation. It introduces something new to privacy law: a "fair and reasonable" test for data practices. That's not a form you can optimise. It's a judgement call applied after the fact — and AI-generated inferences about people are explicitly in scope.

Three things I'd want every board to sit with this week:

**1. The benchmark-to-deployment gap is now measurable.**
METR's study found AI-assisted developers took 19% longer on tasks than unassisted developers — even though they expected to be 24% faster. Stanford's 2026 AI Index confirms benchmarks are maxing out. The question isn't "what can this model do on a test?" It's "what happens when it touches your workflows?"

**2. Shadow AI is not an emerging risk — it's the current state.**
67% of UK organisations have shadow AI active today. 36% of corporate professionals globally are using unapproved tools. Only 5 of 73 organisations surveyed have an AI kill switch policy. Boards are being named as accountable when AI fails. Most boards don't yet have the tools to exercise that accountability.

**3. The platform-vs-agent access war just started.**
Amazon blocked Meta's AI agent from its retail infrastructure to protect $76B in annual ad revenue. Shopify made the opposite bet. This isn't a tech story — it's a distribution story. Every organisation building agentic AI workflows needs to ask: which platforms will gate our agent's access, and what's our contingency?

The window between now and early 2027 — when Australian legislation is expected — is the engagement window. The organisations that shape these rules are the ones at the table now.
