# LinkedIn Post — August 4, 2026
**Status:** Draft (review before posting)

---

The attacker's new strategy isn't to breach your systems.

It's to breach the systems that manage your systems.

Today, three critical exploits are active simultaneously: N-able N-central (MSP remote management), SonicWall SMA VPN appliances, and Cisco Firewall Management Center. All three are management-layer infrastructure. All three have CVSS scores of 9.3 or higher. All three are under active exploitation right now.

This isn't a coincidence. It's a doctrine shift.

---

**Why management layer?**

When an attacker compromises an MSP's N-central console — rated CVSS 8.2 but granting what researchers are calling "god-mode" — they don't get one organization. They get every organization that MSP manages. In the N-able case, attackers established persistence using Cloudflare tunnels registered as services on managed endpoints. Those tunnels survived credential resets. The blast radius of a single compromise multiplied across every downstream client.

Same math applies to SonicWall SMA (CVSS 10.0) and Cisco FMC (CVSS 10.0). The VPN gateway that controls who gets into the network. The firewall manager that controls what the network's rules are. Compromise those and you don't need to find a gap in the perimeter — you own the perimeter.

The INC Ransomware group is now listing SonicWall victims at volume, with 885 claimed to date and the pace accelerating in August.

---

**The AI angle makes this worse.**

CrowdStrike's 2026 Threat Hunting Report, released yesterday, documents something that should reset every security team's assumptions: one eCrime campaign sent 200,000 AI model requests in two minutes.

That's not a human operator probing your environment. That's machine-speed reconnaissance — faster than any human analyst can detect, triage, and respond to in real time. The same report shows China-nexus adversaries are exploiting critical vulnerabilities within 24 hours of public PoC release.

Your quarterly patching cycle is no longer in the same time zone as the attack.

---

**What this means for decision-makers:**

If you use a managed service provider: ask them today what version of N-central they're running, and whether they've run a compromise assessment — not just patched. The Cloudflare tunnel persistence means patching alone isn't proof of clean.

If you run SonicWall SMA 1000 series appliances: assume active targeting, validate your TOTP secrets and active session tokens were not exfiltrated before patching. Attackers extract those credentials before the patch lands.

If you're on Cisco FMC: a public PoC for CVE-2026-20079 is already on GitHub. The window between "exploit exists" and "commodity attack tool" is measured in days, not months.

The broader lesson is one I keep coming back to: the security industry built an entire layer of management and monitoring tooling, and then largely stopped treating that layer as part of the attack surface. The adversaries noticed before we did.

---

The question for every CISO this week isn't whether your systems are patched. It's whether the systems that *manage* your systems are patched — and whether anyone checked if the attackers were already there first.
