# AI LinkedIn Post — Sunday, July 12, 2026
**Status:** Draft (review before posting)

---

For three weeks in June, the most capable AI models on the planet simply switched off.

Not because of an outage. Because of a government directive. A US export-control order forced Anthropic to suspend Fable 5 and Mythos 5 — and the practical effect was that every customer, everywhere, lost access overnight.

Access was restored on July 1. The lesson wasn't.

Here's what that episode actually proved: your AI vendor's promises are subordinate to their government's priorities. Every data processing agreement, every zero-retention clause, every "we don't train on your data" commitment — all of it sits downstream of a jurisdiction you don't vote in and can't appeal to.

Watch what happened next.

Mistral is closing a round at roughly €20 billion — nearly double its valuation from nine months ago — while building 200 megawatts of European compute. The EU's OpenEuroLLM consortium ships its first open-source models this month. The European Investment Fund just launched a €15 billion vehicle for sovereign tech. That's not policy talk. That's capital moving in response to demonstrated risk.

And quietly, the compliant alternative stopped being the compromised alternative. The best MIT-licensed open models now score within touching distance of the frontier — GLM-5.2 posts 91% on GPQA Diamond with a licence that lets you run it entirely on your own hardware. Ollama just made local inference nearly 90% faster on Apple Silicon. The "capability tax" for keeping customer data inside your own perimeter has almost vanished.

This matters urgently for anyone in Australian financial services, health, or government. APRA's CPS 230 went live on July 1, and it draws a distinction most AI deployments fail: storage residency versus processing residency. Your database can sit onshore in Sydney while your LLM inference — the part actually reading your customers' personal information — runs offshore. Regulators have noticed. Have you audited where your inference happens?

A risk practitioner put it perfectly this week: contracts promise, architecture proves. A zero-data-retention clause is a promise enforced by your vendor's internal controls. An on-premise model is an architecture where the promise is unnecessary.

Layer on the deadline: from 10 December 2026, Australian privacy policies must disclose which decisions are substantially automated and what personal information feeds them. You cannot disclose what you haven't mapped. You cannot confidently map what you don't control.

So here's the strategic question for every board balancing AI capability against PII obligations — and it's no longer "which vendor do we trust?"

It's: which of our AI workloads still need to leave our jurisdiction at all?

Run the inventory. Price the local option — it's cheaper than you think, and it just survived the stress test the cloud didn't.
