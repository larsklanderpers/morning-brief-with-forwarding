The trust perimeter didn't collapse overnight. It was systematically dismantled, one intermediary at a time.

Today's cybersecurity picture isn't a list of separate incidents. It's a single story told from five angles.

---

**Angle one: the kernel.** CVE-2026-68820 — a Windows use-after-free in the Ancillary Function Driver for WinSock — is being exploited right now by the Lazarus Group. North Korea is burning a kernel zero-day against Windows infrastructure. CISA's patching deadline for federal agencies is today. This is not a drill, and it is not the weekend.

**Angle two: the MSP layer.** Australia's Cyber Security Centre has confirmed active exploitation of authentication bypass vulnerabilities in N-able N-central, the RMM platform that sits at the centre of thousands of managed service provider networks. One compromised MSP is potentially the key to hundreds of downstream SMB clients simultaneously. The threat actor doesn't need to breach each business — they breach the company those businesses trust to manage their security.

**Angle three: the supply chain.** A GitHub Actions workflow injection vulnerability in Snowflake's public connector repository can be triggered by a maliciously crafted GitHub issue. The attack surface is the contribution mechanism itself — the collaborative infrastructure that makes open-source software development possible. And an `isolated-vm` sandbox escape in a widely-used Node.js library means that developers who used it specifically to run untrusted code safely were doing neither.

**Angle four: the document layer.** The World Economic Forum reports that 94% of organisations now identify AI as the dominant force reshaping their threat landscape. The specific escalation that stands out: deepfake-driven fraud targeting legal documents and personal media. When the integrity of a court filing or a financial instrument can no longer be verified by eye, the trust model for institutional processes breaks down in ways that no firewall addresses.

**Angle five: the healthcare floor.** ProHealth Medical Group hit by ransomware. Questal losing 21 million Salesforce records to ShinyHunters. These aren't just breach notifications — they're the raw material for every follow-on campaign that runs against the individuals in those records for the next three years.

---

The question I keep coming back to: which trust relationships does your organisation depend on that you haven't stress-tested?

Not your systems. Your relationships. The MSP you handed privileged access to. The open-source package your team added to the build pipeline last quarter. The CI/CD workflow that accepts input from external contributors. The document verification process that was designed before AI-generated media existed.

Technical controls protect assets. But sophisticated adversaries in 2026 are targeting the connective tissue between organisations — the mechanisms you use to trust each other, delegate authority, and operate at scale.

If your threat model still starts with "an attacker trying to breach our perimeter," it's missing the game being played against you.
