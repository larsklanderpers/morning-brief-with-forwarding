The build system is the new perimeter.

Not your firewall. Not your VPN. The package registry your developers push to forty times a day, the CI/CD pipeline that turns source into production artefacts, the Groovy plugin running inside your build server that nobody has audited since 2023.

This week's JFrog Artifactory exploitation is the clearest illustration yet. Attackers didn't break down the front door — they sent a POST request to an API endpoint with a trailing slash, received an anonymous JWT, exchanged it for an administrator token, and then watched as that token appeared in logs as *anonymous*, despite owning the environment. They installed a Rust-based backdoor inside a Groovy plugin. They created persistent admin accounts. And they did all of this between 15 August and 8 September — three weeks of dwell time — in software infrastructure that feeds every downstream pipeline.

This is how you compromise a thousand applications without touching a single one of them.

The same logic runs through Anthropic's September threat intelligence report, published today. Chinese operators — including groups linked to Alibaba, Moonshot, DeepSeek, and Xiaomi — ran systematic "distillation" campaigns against Claude: querying the model in patterns designed to extract training signal for their own AI development. The largest of these was Alibaba's. The attack surface wasn't a network endpoint. It was an inference API. The loot wasn't credentials — it was capability.

Meanwhile, a GRU-linked operation ran for 130 days across 27 Ukrainian institutions, using AI to accelerate targeting and analysis. A Yemen-based cell integrated flight software onto consumer hardware and used Claude for post-launch weapons analysis. These are not edge cases. They are what happens when the capability gap collapses: tasks that once required a team now require a model and a prompt.

Add to this Microsoft's September Patch Tuesday — a record 1,169 CVEs, 118 Critical — and the picture becomes clearer. 44.7% of Microsoft's patches this month were for elevation of privilege. EoP is the post-exploitation move: it means attackers have solved the initial access problem and are now working on persistence and escalation. The battle, for much of the threat landscape, has already moved past the front door.

For executives and boards, the strategic read is this: your traditional vulnerability management programme is optimised for the wrong surface. It scans endpoints and web applications. It does not routinely audit your Artifactory configuration, your build pipeline plugins, your AI API usage patterns, or your inference logs. None of those show up in a CVSS score. None of them trigger a Tenable alert.

The organisations that will navigate the next 24 months without a significant incident are the ones that extend their security programme to cover the full software delivery chain — from the developer's IDE to the production runtime — and treat AI APIs as an attack surface with the same rigour they apply to external-facing web applications.

The build system is the new perimeter. Act accordingly.
