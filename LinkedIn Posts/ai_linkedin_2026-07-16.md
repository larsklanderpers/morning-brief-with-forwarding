# AI LinkedIn Post — July 16, 2026
**Status:** Draft (review before posting)

---

This week, a security firm caught a frontier AI coding tool uploading entire codebases to the vendor's servers. Gigabytes of proprietary code, hoovered up for tasks that needed three files. Users who had explicitly opted out of data sharing? Uploaded anyway.

The vendor's fix: a patch, a new setting, and a promise that everything would be "completely and utterly deleted."

A promise. That's the control.

Twenty-four hours later, Australia's Prime Minister stood up and announced legislated national AI standards, a dedicated Office of AI inside his own department, and binding rules for the data centres that power all of this. The UK's Chancellor spent her Mansion House speech calling for a "serious plan on AI sovereignty." The EU published its action plan on AI and cybersecurity the same week.

These two stories are the same story.

For two decades, we ran a global experiment called "trust the vendor's settings." The audit that caught those codebase uploads is the experiment's result. Opt-outs that don't opt you out. Retention policies you cannot verify. Deletion promises you take on faith. As Microsoft's own CEO put it recently: in the AI age, the buyer risks giving away knowledge just in order to use what they bought.

Governments have read the result. That's why sovereignty stopped being a conference-panel topic and became economic policy in three jurisdictions in a single week.

But here's what most coverage missed: the alternative got dramatically better at the exact same moment.

Thinking Machines just released Inkling — a genuinely capable open-weights model, built to be fine-tuned for your workflows, on your infrastructure. It joins GLM 5.2, Mistral's July open-weight push, and a research result showing fine-tuned open models beating closed frontier models at a tenth of the price on specific tasks.

Translation for anyone holding customer PII: keeping inference inside your own walls is no longer the compromise option. It's a competitive architecture. Your prompts never leave. Your customer data never crosses a firewall. Your compliance audit becomes a review of your own network logs — not an exercise in hoping a vendor's promise holds.

If you're a decision-maker in financial services, health, or government, three questions for this quarter:

Where does your AI inference actually happen — including at peak load, when some vendors quietly route offshore?

Can you verify your vendor's data handling, or only trust it?

Which of your workloads could run on an open model, on your infrastructure, tomorrow?

Australia's new APP transparency obligations land on 10 December. The EU's high-risk AI rules bite on 2 August. The direction of travel is not subtle.

The businesses that win the next five years won't be the ones with access to the biggest model. Everyone has that. They'll be the ones who can look a customer — or a regulator — in the eye and say: your data never left.

Build for verifiable control, not vendor trust. The week just proved why.
