**Status:** Draft (review before posting)

---

Your regulator is not waiting for you to figure out AI governance.

APRA made that clear in April. In a formal letter to every bank, insurer, and super trustee in Australia, they flagged two things: AI governance practices are "not keeping pace" with adoption, and there is systemic concentration risk from reliance on offshore AI providers. That second point is the one that should stop you mid-meeting.

If your organisation runs on US-based frontier AI — and most do — APRA is now asking what happens if that provider changes terms, exits a market, or goes down. Do you have an answer?

The EU AI Act's high-risk compliance deadline is August 2. Australia's Privacy Act amendments on automated decision-making transparency land December 10. The OAIC is already running a compliance sweep across retail and digital services.

Three regulators. Three hard deadlines. All in 2026.

Here is the irony: the infrastructure to respond has never been more capable.

This week, GLM-5.2 — a fully open-weight model anyone can self-host — became the first locally-runnable AI to beat GPT-5.5 on software engineering benchmarks. Ollama v0.30.8 now runs on commodity hardware, not just Apple Silicon. Anthropic's Claude is available in AWS Sydney, keeping inference onshore for Australian data residency requirements. Mistral AI, valued at €20 billion and now contracted to the French and German governments through 2030, processes all data inside the EU by default.

The tools to do AI properly — without sending customer data offshore — exist right now.

The businesses getting this right are not waiting for mandatory federal guidance (which Australia just converted to voluntary anyway). They are building AI architectures where the answer to "where does our customer data go?" is a boring, documented, defensible one. On-premise. AWS Sydney. EU-resident infrastructure. Not "somewhere in Virginia, probably."

For decision-makers balancing AI capability with PII obligations, here is the frame that cuts through the noise:

Every AI call that sends a customer's data to a third-party server is a data processor agreement you need to have documented, a jurisdiction you need to have assessed, and a concentration risk you need to have disclosed to your board. Not eventually. Now.

The gap between what regulators expect and what most organisations have documented is closing fast — but not in the direction most people assume. The regulators are accelerating. The question is whether your governance is moving at the same speed.

The businesses that treat this moment — maximum AI capability, maximum regulatory clarity, maximum infrastructure maturity — as a window to get ahead will look very different from their peers in 18 months.

The ones that don't will spend 2028 explaining to an auditor why they didn't act in 2026 when the signals were this clear.
