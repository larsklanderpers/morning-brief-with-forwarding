# AI LinkedIn Post — 20 August 2026
**Status:** Draft (review before posting)

---

France got hacked last week.

700,000 taxpayer records. The government's response? Deploy AI to hunt for the next vulnerability.

Then Budget Minister Amiel added one condition: the AI has to be sovereign. Mistral, yes. OpenAI, explicitly excluded.

That's not protectionism. That's infrastructure policy.

---

Here's the logic: if you're going to hand an AI system your government's internal network — its documents, its vulnerabilities, its most sensitive data — you want some control over where that AI lives, who can compel it to hand over information, and what happens if the AI vendor itself gets breached or acquired.

You wouldn't outsource your military communications network to whichever foreign company currently has the best app.

AI is now critical infrastructure. France just said so out loud.

---

The same week: the EU AI Act's Article 50 enforcement regime went live. For the 78% of organisations that hadn't taken meaningful compliance steps as of April, the grace period is over. Fines now reach €15 million or 3% of global turnover for GPAI providers.

And in Australia, the December 10 deadline for automated decision-making transparency obligations is 112 days away. OAIC guidance won't be finalised until September. That leaves a 90-day implementation window — for rules the regulator is signalling it will interpret broadly.

---

Meanwhile, Mistral has started selling a "10% sovereignty surcharge" — inference endpoints that pin traffic to EU or US regions, with a 99.5% SLA. They're underwriting 200 megawatts of European compute by end-2027.

The market has priced sovereignty. Regulated enterprises are paying for it.

---

For decision-makers in financial services, health, and government: the question of *where your AI runs* is no longer separable from the question of *whether you're compliant*.

Your AI vendor's data residency claims are not the same as demonstrated data routing. Many "Australian-hosted" AI providers route inference through Singapore or the US during peak loads. Under APRA CPS 230, that makes the AI vendor a material third-party risk — and the burden of proof is on you, not them.

The organisations that get this right aren't the ones with the best AI models. They're the ones that treated deployment location as a compliance decision from day one.

---

Three things to do before the Australia December 10 deadline:

Map every AI system in your organisation that makes or assists with customer-facing decisions — hiring, lending, insurance, customer analytics. That's your ADM exposure. Then verify where inference actually runs, not what the contract says. Then scope your privacy policy disclosure obligations now, before OAIC guidance lands in September and you have 90 days to implement.

France didn't have a plan before the breach. Now they do.

You don't have to wait for yours.
