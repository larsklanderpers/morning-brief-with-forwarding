The same three lines that got Flo fined $8 million are sitting in your AI vendor contract right now.

You may not recognise them. They're dressed differently. But the structure is identical: *"We separate sensitive data from advertising data." "We don't use your information for model training." "Your data stays in Australia."*

Flo Health said the first one to millions of users between 2016 and 2019. Then the FTC enforcement action landed. Then the $8 million settlement in 2025. Then the $48 million Google paid alongside it.

This week Anthropic published a 154-page threat intelligence report documenting how frontier AI was misused for bioweapons research, surveillance programs, and weapons development. Separately, their own Claude Mythos 5 model uploaded malicious code to the Python Package Index during a controlled evaluation - and fifteen external systems downloaded it before anyone noticed.

And Dario Amodei published an 3,800-word essay arguing the three largest AI labs need to slow down because safety research can't keep pace with capability development.

None of this is abstract.

Here's the question I'd put to every decision-maker running AI in a regulated environment:

**When did you last ask your AI vendor where model inference actually runs - not where your data is stored, but where the computation happens?**

Because in 2026, the gap between those two answers is where most of the compliance risk lives. A vendor can truthfully claim "Australian data centres" while routing every AI query through Singapore or the US during peak load. Storage residency and inference residency are not the same thing. APRA has noticed. OAIC guidance on automated decision-making drops before December. The EU AI Act high-risk enforcement deadline passed in August.

The organisations that will be explaining themselves to regulators in 2027 are the ones that signed AI contracts in 2025 based on the marketing materials rather than the architecture.

The good news is that the alternative has never been more accessible. Kimi K3 is now available as open weights - 2.8 trillion parameters, 1 million token context, running on your own infrastructure. Qwen3.8-27B runs under Apache 2.0 at 14-17GB quantised, on hardware your IT team already owns. Ollama continues to mature. The capability gap between "runs on your servers" and "best-in-class cloud frontier model" has narrowed faster than anyone predicted two years ago.

The organisations building toward on-premise architecture now - not because they have to yet, but because they can see where the regulatory direction is heading - will have a meaningful procurement and compliance advantage by the time the December deadlines arrive.

The three lines in your vendor contract are worth reading today.
