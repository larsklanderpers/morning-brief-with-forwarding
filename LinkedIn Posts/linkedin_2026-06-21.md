86,644. That's how many working FortiGate VPN credentials a Russian-speaking threat group extracted — from Samsung, AT&T, Chevron, Toyota, and hundreds of government agencies — without exploiting a single zero-day. Just relentless, automated credential stuffing at a scale most security teams can't conceptualise.

FortiBleed broke this week. It should break something else too: the comfortable assumption that perimeter defences are holding.

Here's the thread that connects today's headlines into something that should worry every executive and board member.

**The 24-hour problem**

CISA's new Binding Operational Directive 26-04 mandates risk-based patching for federal agencies — because 28.3% of CVEs are now being exploited within 24 hours of disclosure. That's not a trend. That's a structural collapse of the reactive security model that most organisations still operate.

This week's Microsoft Patch Tuesday landed 209 vulnerabilities. Three of them — Windows Kernel, HTTP.sys, and DHCP Client — scored CVSS 9.8 and require zero user interaction to exploit. The average enterprise remediation time for critical vulnerabilities is 74 days. Do the maths: attackers consistently have a 73-day head start.

**The EDR blind spot**

The Gentlemen ransomware group deployed GentleKiller this week — a framework purpose-built to disable endpoint detection tools before the ransomware payload arrives. EDR-killing isn't new. What is new is that it's now a modular, commoditised component in mainstream RaaS operations, not a specialised capability reserved for nation-state actors.

If your security architecture assumes endpoint visibility will hold under attack, that assumption is now officially wrong.

**The permanent problem**

Security firm Paradigm Shift dropped usbliter8 — a working exploit for the SecureROM in Apple A12 and A13 chips. iPhone XS through iPhone 11. Millions of devices. No software patch will ever fix it. Affected hardware will carry this vulnerability for as long as it stays in use.

This introduces a category that strips the entire security industry's core value proposition: the patch. There is no patch. Your MDM policies, your device attestation, your endpoint compliance checks — all of them operate above the OS layer. usbliter8 runs before the OS loads.

**The pattern**

FortiBleed. GentleKiller. usbliter8. Simultaneous CVSS 9.8 RCEs. AI-generated phishing up 89% year-over-year.

What's striking is that none of these require exceptional attacker capability. They require patience, automation, and the knowledge that most organisations' security operations tempo hasn't fundamentally changed in a decade.

The gap isn't tools. Every large enterprise has the tools. The gap is operational velocity.

Attackers have automated their kill chains. Defenders are still running approval processes designed for when exploits took months to arrive. Until security operations match the attacker's tempo — automated triage, continuous credential hygiene, zero-trust segmentation that limits blast radius when perimeter controls fail — the arithmetic doesn't change.

86,644 credentials. No zero-day required. Just time.
