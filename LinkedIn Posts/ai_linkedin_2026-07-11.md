# AI LinkedIn Post — July 11, 2026
**Status:** Draft (review before posting)

---

On July 1, a regulation quietly took effect that reclassifies your AI vendor as critical infrastructure. Almost nobody noticed.

APRA CPS 230 is now in force. If you're a regulated financial institution in Australia, every AI service you call — including that "harmless" API integration your team shipped last quarter — is now a material service provider. Formal register. Continuity planning. Enforceable contractual accountability. Board visibility.

And it's not arriving alone.

The EU AI Act becomes fully enforceable on August 2 — three weeks away — with penalties up to 7% of global turnover. Higher than GDPR. In December, Australia's Privacy Act amendments require you to disclose what personal information feeds your automated decisions.

Here's the uncomfortable question: could your organisation produce, today, an inventory of which customer PII flows into which AI model?

Most can't. I've watched teams discover mid-audit that "we use one AI tool" was actually eleven, routed through four jurisdictions.

Now hold that against what happened on the supply side this same fortnight.

Ollama — the tool that makes running AI models on your own hardware almost boringly easy — raised $88M from Docker's founders. GLM-5.2, an MIT-licensed model you can download and own outright, is within single-digit percentage points of the frontier on reasoning benchmarks. GitHub put an open-weight model inside Copilot. Mistral is pouring €4 billion into European data centres because, after June's US export ban briefly switched off the world's best model for every non-US customer, "sovereign AI" stopped being marketing and became procurement policy.

Read those two trends together and something remarkable emerges:

The capability cost of running AI you control has collapsed to near zero — at precisely the moment the regulatory cost of AI you don't control is being written into enforceable rules with published deadlines.

Two years ago, keeping customer data out of third-party AI clouds meant accepting a painful capability gap. That gap is now a rounding error for most business workloads. The trade has inverted.

One caution for those already shopping: vendors will sell you "data residency" that means your data is *stored* onshore while inference quietly routes through other regions at peak load. Storage residency is not processing residency. The risk lives in that distinction. Ask the second question.

If you hold customer PII and you're planning your FY27 AI roadmap, the strategic move isn't picking the smartest model. It's building the architecture where models are interchangeable and your data never has to trust any of them — enterprise data, your control layer, then whichever model earns its place this quarter.

The window where "we'll sort out the data flows later" was a defensible answer is closing. On a published schedule.

Map your AI data flows now — before December makes the map a legal requirement and an auditor draws it for you.
