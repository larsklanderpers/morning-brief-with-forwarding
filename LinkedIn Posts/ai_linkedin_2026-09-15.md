# AI LinkedIn Post — 15 September 2026
**Status:** Draft (review before posting)

---

The frontier AI gap just tripled.

In January, the top 10% of enterprise AI users produced 2.6x more output than the median firm. By June: 8.3x.

Same models. Same vendors. The difference is entirely governance and process — whether anyone has decided which work agents may do, in which systems, and who signs off on the actions that carry consequences.

That number came from OpenAI's own Enterprise Signals data, released this week. And it should concern anyone in a regulated industry who's been treating AI governance as something to sort out later.

Here's the irony: the industries most cautious about AI — financial services, government, health — are the ones where getting it right matters most. But "getting it right" is not the same as "going slow." The firms building that 8x lead aren't being reckless. They're being deliberate. They've made choices about which processes get automated, under what controls, with what audit trail.

Meanwhile, this week also brought two regulatory deadlines that most organisations aren't ready for.

The EU AI Act's Annex III requirements became enforceable in August. Employment decisions, credit assessments, insurance underwriting — if AI touches these and you haven't done a conformity assessment, you're operating in breach. As of April, 78% of organisations hadn't taken meaningful steps. That number hasn't improved.

In Australia, public consultation on the Privacy Amendment (Personal Data Protection) Bill 2026 closes in three days — September 18. From December 10, any APP entity using AI in automated decisions affecting individuals must disclose this in their privacy policy. Not vaguely. Specifically: the kinds of personal information used, the nature of decisions, broadly how the process works.

And there's a gap that isn't getting enough attention: data residency versus inference residency.

Many AI vendors legitimately claim "Australian hosting." But check where inference happens — where your customer's data is actually processed by the model — and you'll often find it routes through Singapore or US regions at peak load. The Australia-US CLOUD Act Agreement, which entered force in January, gives US law enforcement treaty-backed access to compel that data. APRA-regulated entities in particular need to audit this: not where data rests, but where it's computed.

The good news is that the gap between "cloud AI capability" and "privacy-safe AI" is closing fast.

Alibaba's Qwen3.8-27B is now the best-rated open-weights model for local deployment, running on a single workstation at 14GB. Mistral has signed framework agreements with France and Germany for sovereign AI infrastructure through 2030. The "local model as AI firewall" pattern — where a small on-premise model scrubs PII before content goes to a cloud model — is a practical architecture that several privacy-conscious organisations have already deployed.

The question isn't whether to use AI. It's whether you're making deliberate governance decisions or inheriting someone else's defaults.

The 8x gap is a governance gap. And it's compounding every month.
