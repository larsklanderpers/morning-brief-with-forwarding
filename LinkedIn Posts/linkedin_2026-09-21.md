Your organisation has network access control. It has MFA. It has endpoint detection. And this week, all three can be bypassed before your SOC analyst finishes their morning coffee.

That's not hyperbole. It's what the threat intelligence is actually saying.

**The Cisco ISE zero-day (CVE-2026-76460, CVSS 10.0)** allows any unauthenticated attacker on the internet to bypass your network access control system and gain root command execution. The system that decides who gets on your network — compromised by anyone who can send an HTTP request. CISA's patch deadline already expired. Active exploitation confirmed. No workaround.

**The Microsoft 365 phishing kit defeating MFA in 78 seconds** isn't some exotic nation-state tool. It's being sold as a service. An adversary-in-the-middle attack captures your session token before you finish reading the "sign in" prompt. The MFA event you reported as "enabled" in your last board update is a speed bump, not a barrier — unless you've deployed FIDO2 or passkeys.

**Twelve of Microsoft's September Patch Tuesday RCE vulnerabilities** trigger through Outlook's Reading Pane. Not a click. Not an attachment open. Just preview. 972 vulnerabilities in one patch cycle, 113 critical, and the ones that will actually hurt organisations are the ones where the victim doesn't have to do anything wrong.

Here's the thread connecting these stories: **the gap between "we have the control" and "the control actually works against current attacks" has never been wider.**

Boards are being told "we have MFA." Security teams know AiTM kits routinely defeat TOTP in under two minutes. Boards are being told "we have NAC." Security teams are looking at a CVSS 10.0 auth bypass in the most widely-deployed NAC platform. Boards are being told "we have endpoint detection." The first confirmed agentic ransomware attack — autonomous, no human operator, from exploit to encrypted production database — completed its entire attack chain faster than most detection tools can fire an alert.

This isn't a technology problem. Technology is being deployed. It's a measurement problem. We are measuring the presence of controls, not their efficacy under current attack conditions.

The hardest conversation security leaders need to have in Q4 2026 isn't about budget. It's about honesty: **do the controls we report as "in place" actually hold against the attack classes that are actively being used against organisations like ours, right now?**

If the answer requires more than three seconds of thought, that's the gap to close before year-end.
