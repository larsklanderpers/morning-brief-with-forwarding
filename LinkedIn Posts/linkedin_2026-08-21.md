Every week, someone senior asks me: "What's the one thing I should be worried about?"

This week, the honest answer is the abstraction problem.

---

Four stories landed within 24 hours that, on the surface, look like routine CVE disclosures. They're not.

**Microsoft Entra ID** has a confirmed remote code execution exploit in the wild. Entra is the identity layer that half the enterprise world has consolidated onto. One exploited vulnerability doesn't just compromise a server — it puts every downstream resource that trusts Entra for authentication in scope. Cloud identity was supposed to reduce attack surface. Right now, it's centralising risk.

**N-able N-central**, the remote monitoring platform MSPs use to manage hundreds of client environments, has two vulnerabilities under active exploitation in Australia — confirmed by the ACSC. An MSP's unpatched N-central instance isn't one breach. It's dozens of breaches, waiting. The ACSC's advisory explicitly called out SMBs to "check with your MSP," which is the polite way of saying: your security may depend entirely on your service provider's patch cadence.

**MLflow**, near-ubiquitous in enterprise machine learning pipelines, hit the CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog this week via a server-side request forgery flaw. Most organisations treat their data science infrastructure as a development environment. Attackers treat it as a pivot point into production.

And **Cl0p** claimed more than 40 victims through PTC Windchill, the product lifecycle management platform used across manufacturing and defence supply chains. Same playbook as MOVEit in 2023: find the trusted enterprise software that connects supply-chain participants, exploit it once, harvest dozens of targets in parallel.

---

The pattern isn't coincidence. It's strategy.

Every one of these platforms — identity, RMM, ML pipelines, PLM — exists because it makes operations simpler. It abstracts complexity. It centralises control. Those are features. They're also exactly what makes them high-value targets.

Attackers aren't looking for the most complex vulnerability. They're looking for the highest-leverage one. The platform that, once compromised, unlocks access to ten things instead of one. The abstraction layer that everyone trusts but no one's watching closely.

CrowdStrike's 2026 Threat Hunting Report landed the same week with a number that should change how you think about patching: **88% of vulnerability exploits with published proof-of-concept code were weaponised within 48 hours of release**. The 30-day patch window is a fiction. For internet-facing systems, you have two days. In most cases, you don't even know the clock has started.

---

What should decision-makers do with this?

Stop asking "which vulnerability do I patch first?" Start asking "which abstraction layer in my stack has the least security visibility?"

Your identity platform. Your MSP's tooling. Your AI/ML infrastructure. Your supply-chain software. These are load-bearing systems that rarely receive the same scrutiny as your firewalls and endpoints.

The organisations that are getting hurt right now aren't the ones ignoring security. They're the ones who invested heavily in the right platforms, then assumed the platforms were covered.

That assumption is what's being exploited.
