# LinkedIn Post — 1 July 2026
**Status:** Draft (review before posting)

---

Your patch window is now measured in hours. Not weeks. Hours.

This morning's threat intelligence confirms that 28.3% of CVEs published in 2026 are being actively exploited within 24 hours of disclosure. Let that land. The moment a vulnerability goes public, a quarter of the time attackers are already in networks before most organisations have even read the advisory — let alone scheduled a patching sprint.

And yet, enterprise patch cycles still run on 30-day cadences.

We have a structural mismatch at the heart of enterprise security, and it's not getting better.

---

Three stories from today illustrate exactly how this plays out in practice.

**Citrix NetScaler CVE-2026-3055** — a CVSS 9.8 memory overread in the SAML Identity Provider configuration — has been under mass exploitation for months. Fortinet is blocking thousands of attack attempts daily. Patches have been available since March. The attackers aren't exploiting a novel technique. They're exploiting the gap between "patch shipped" and "patch deployed." Compromised NetScaler appliances hand attackers the keys to the identity layer — meaning SSO tokens, session hijacking, and lateral movement without credentials. This is how you breach an enterprise without ever needing a zero-day.

**The July 1 ransomware wave** saw five separate ransomware groups — Akira, LockBit, Genesis, BlackNevas, and TheGentlemen — claim victims simultaneously across local government, legal services, and international business. These aren't sophisticated APT actors choosing high-value targets. This is commodity ransomware at industrial scale, sweeping up every unpatched, unmonitored endpoint in the kill zone. The question for every board member reading this isn't "would we be targeted?" It's "would we know within hours, or within weeks?"

**The Bitdefender 2026 Assessment** confirmed something security practitioners have known for years but struggled to communicate upward: organisations have never had higher cyber risk awareness, and have never been further from operational resilience. 84% of high-severity incidents in 2026 leveraged Living off the Land techniques — attackers using your own legitimate tools against you, invisible to signature-based detection. Boards can now articulate cyber risk in earnings calls. But can they answer "how quickly can we detect lateral movement in our own network?" Most can't.

---

The security spending story of the last five years has been awareness, detection tools, and compliance frameworks. The story of the next five years has to be **operational resilience and patch velocity**.

This means:

Continuous exposure management — not quarterly scanning. Behavioural detection — not signature-based tools that miss LOTL. Tested incident response — not a plan that's never been run against a realistic scenario. And board-level appetite to resource the gap between "we know we're at risk" and "we can actually respond."

The attackers aren't more sophisticated than they were last year. They're faster. And they're exploiting an enterprise security model that was designed for a threat landscape that no longer exists.

If your security posture is built around patching within 30 days, you are already behind.

The window is hours. Govern accordingly.
