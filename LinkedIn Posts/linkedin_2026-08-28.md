CISA just published what might be the most useful document in government cybersecurity this year. They ran the same red team against two critical infrastructure organisations — same attackers, same techniques, same tradecraft — and got completely different results.

One organisation never detected the intrusion. The red team compromised domain admin, pivoted to sensitive business systems, reached cloud infrastructure, and left. The SOC didn't know until CISA told them.

The other organisation caught the initial access, quarantined it, and forced the red team to adapt. Not perfectly. But detectably.

The report is called "A Tale of Two SOCs." The difference between the two organisations wasn't their budget. It wasn't their tooling stack. It was whether they could actually use what they had.

This matters beyond the US. CISA's red team findings against Water/Wastewater infrastructure are directly applicable to every critical infrastructure operator globally. The attack patterns don't change at the border.

Meanwhile, this week also gave us a masterclass in what happens when detection fails earlier in the chain.

Nevada's government confirmed that ransomware attackers were inside their systems for **three months** before they detonated. May 14 was the initial breach. August 24 was when 60 state agencies went offline. In between: credential theft from 26 accounts, systematic deletion of backup volumes, and patient preparation for maximum disruption.

Nevada refused to pay the ransom. Recovered 90% of data over 28 days. Published a full transparency report on everything that went wrong.

They deserve credit for the transparency. But "we didn't pay" is not the metric that matters. Three months of undetected dwell time is.

Separately, CISA has given federal agencies until tomorrow — August 29 — to patch CVE-2026-8452, a Citrix NetScaler RCE that's being actively exploited in the wild. Attackers are dropping web shells on unpatched appliances right now. This is the edge device that sits in front of a huge portion of enterprise and government network access.

The question for every organisation reading this is not "are we patched?" It's "how long would it take us to know if we weren't?"

Three things I'd be doing this weekend if I ran security for a mid-to-large organisation:

1. **Check your NetScaler versions.** CVE-2026-8452. This week, not next sprint.
2. **Pull CISA's AA26-237A.** Read the detection gap section. Ask honestly which of the two orgs yours looks like.
3. **Audit your backup deletion controls.** Nevada's attackers deleted their backups before encrypting anything. That's the real leverage — not the ransomware.

The headlines this week are all about breaches and bulletins. The actual story is simpler: we keep building detection infrastructure we can't operate, and adversaries keep having more time than they should.
