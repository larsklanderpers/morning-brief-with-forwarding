Your AI data centre has a 9.8-severity hole in its spine.

Cisco disclosed CVE-2026-20212 on Tuesday — a critical flaw in Nexus 9000 switches running Silicon One ASICs. Unauthenticated attackers who can reach two TCP management ports can execute code as root. No credentials. No user interaction. Root access to the switching fabric of your AI training cluster.

This is not a peripheral box. The affected models are the high-bandwidth switches that move data between GPUs at scale. An attacker who owns root on that fabric doesn't just get a foothold — they get eyes on every packet flowing through your AI infrastructure.

Meanwhile, the same week: SonicWall confirmed its SMA1000 remote access appliance is being actively exploited via a CVSS 10.0 zero-day pair. Chain the two CVEs and you get unauthenticated remote code execution — no authentication, no victim action required. This is the third SMA1000 compromise of 2026. MFA seeds stolen in July are reportedly still valid through September's patches.

And CISA just added seven vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, including LiteLLM and JFrog Artifactory — AI pipeline tooling and software supply chain infrastructure. That's the first time AI platform components have appeared on the actively-exploited list alongside traditional enterprise targets.

Here's what I keep coming back to: organisations are racing to build AI infrastructure without asking who's on the other side of the race.

The attacker economy doesn't wait for your AI transformation to complete before developing capabilities against it. The same infrastructure being stood up to run AI workloads — the edge appliances for remote access, the switching fabric, the LLM orchestration platforms — is being probed and exploited now, while it's still new and the security controls are still being figured out.

IBM's 2026 breach data puts AI-driven attacks up 56% year-on-year. The NIST position is that traditional cybersecurity frameworks don't adequately address AI agent risk. Attackers are already exploiting malicious .git configurations to make AI coding agents execute arbitrary code.

Three things decision-makers should do before next week's Patch Tuesday (which has three CVSS 10.0 Microsoft vulnerabilities landing on 8 September):

Audit what your AI tooling can access. LLM agents, coding assistants, and orchestration platforms that have access to credentials, API keys, or internal system context are target-rich environments for infostealer campaigns. Most organisations have not inventoried this exposure.

Treat edge infrastructure as critical infrastructure — not IT. SonicWall's third breach of the year is a procurement signal, not just a patching problem. If a product has been compromised three times in twelve months, the conversation should be about replacement, not remediation.

Don't let AI infrastructure build outpace AI infrastructure security. The teams designing AI clusters are not the same teams thinking about what a compromised switching fabric means. That gap is where the next major incident will live.

The perimeter has always been the battleground. We're just building a new perimeter faster than we're securing it.
