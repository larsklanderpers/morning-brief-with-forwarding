---

Two weeks.

That's how long it used to take a skilled ransomware operator to fully compromise an enterprise network — from initial access to domain admin, data exfiltrated, backups encrypted.

This week, it happened in under 10 hours. The operator used frontier AI models and agentic frameworks.

The constraint on attackers was never skill. It was time. AI has removed that constraint, and most security programmes were not designed for a world where the clock runs 30 to 50 times faster.

---

That's the headline. But the week has three other stories that are all part of the same structural shift, and decision-makers need to see them together.

**The tooling is under attack, not just the network.**

Attackers are now embedding invisible Unicode characters in phishing emails — not to trick the user, but to disrupt the NLP-based pattern matching that security products use to flag suspicious language. The security tool becomes the blind spot. This is adversarial machine learning applied to your email gateway, and it works against most products in market today.

**AI infrastructure is attack surface.**

CVE-2026-0768 in Langflow — an open-source AI pipeline framework that data science teams deploy for "internal prototyping" — allows unauthenticated remote code execution. No credentials required. Network access is the only prerequisite. If your organisation ran any AI pipeline experiments in the last eighteen months, you may have exposed infrastructure you don't know exists.

**The supply chain runs through your MSP's RMM.**

The Australian Cyber Security Centre this week confirmed active exploitation of N-able's N-central remote monitoring platform in Australia. N-central is how managed service providers administer their clients' environments. Compromising the RMM is compromise of every downstream customer — simultaneously. The upstream exposure point is your MSP's patch cadence, which you probably don't audit.

---

Here is the pattern: the attack surface in 2026 is not just your network perimeter or your user endpoints. It is your security tooling, your AI infrastructure, and your third-party service providers' infrastructure. And the tempo at which adversaries can move through it has compressed dramatically.

The organisations that will navigate this aren't necessarily the ones with the largest security budgets. They're the ones that have updated their threat model to account for what changed — and stopped planning their AI security response as a future initiative.

The future arrived this week. It's running on agentic frameworks.

---

What's your organisation's current assumption about attacker dwell time? If it's still measured in days, the conversation needs to happen before the incident, not after.
