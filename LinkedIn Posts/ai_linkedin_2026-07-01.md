# AI LinkedIn Post — 1 July 2026
**Status:** Draft (review before posting)

---

Today, two things happened simultaneously that every business leader running AI should sit with.

APRA CPS 230 went live. And Anthropic's Fable 5 came back online.

Most people will treat those as separate news items. They're not.

---

**Let's start with what happened in June.**

On June 12, the US Commerce Department sent Anthropic a letter. By the time Australian businesses opened Monday morning, the world's most capable AI models — Fable 5 and Mythos 5 — were dark. Globally. Not for foreign nationals. For everyone.

Eighteen days later, they're back. Anthropic agreed to proactively flag security risks, share threat intelligence with Washington, and collaborate on standards for future models. A new safety classifier blocks the triggering jailbreak in 99%+ of cases.

The models are back. The precedent is permanent.

---

**Now let's talk about what APRA is actually asking.**

CPS 230, effective today, classifies AI vendors as material service providers for every bank, insurer, and superannuation fund in Australia. That means formal service provider registers, business continuity plans for AI outages, and enforceable contractual accountability.

Here's the thing: APRA already ran the audit. In April, they published the findings from a review of large banks, insurers, and super trustees. What they found wasn't a technology problem. It was a governance problem.

No AI inventory. Unclear ownership. Weak monitoring. Absent decommissioning.

The regulator's exact words: *"This is not an emerging expectation. It is a current obligation with current gaps."*

---

**Put those two stories together and a single question emerges:**

What is your continuity plan when the AI goes dark?

Not if. When.

The Fable 5 outage lasted 18 days. It was triggered by a researcher's report, resolved by a geopolitical negotiation, and reinstated with new conditions on how Anthropic must operate going forward. Your procurement contract with your cloud AI vendor almost certainly does not cover that scenario. Your business continuity plan almost certainly doesn't either.

And APRA now requires you to prove it does.

---

**The good news is the options have never been clearer.**

This week, GLM-5.2 — a 753-billion-parameter open-weight model from Z.ai — hit the benchmarks. MIT licensed. One-sixth the cost of closed frontier APIs. Deployable on hardware you control. It beats GPT-5.5 on coding benchmarks.

OpenAI released open-weight models under Apache 2.0. Mistral is building 200 megawatts of EU-sovereign compute capacity. Ollama's local inference stack just got materially faster on Apple Silicon.

The "we have to use cloud AI" argument is getting harder to sustain in every direction simultaneously.

---

**Here's the question worth taking into your next board meeting:**

You wouldn't accept a single-vendor dependency for your core banking system without a tested failover. You wouldn't accept a critical supplier who could go dark based on a foreign government's letter without 24 hours notice.

Why are you accepting that for AI?

CPS 230 isn't asking you to avoid cloud AI. It's asking you to govern it like the critical dependency it is — with inventory, oversight, continuity planning, and contractual accountability.

The organisations that do that work now won't be scrambling the next time a government letter arrives on a Friday afternoon.

---

The kill switch exists. It was used. And the governance framework to manage it just became legally required in Australia.

Start there.
