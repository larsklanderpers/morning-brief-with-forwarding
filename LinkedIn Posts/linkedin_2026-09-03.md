The systems you trust most are the ones being targeted.

This morning's advisories make the pattern hard to ignore: SonicWall's secure access gateway — the appliance sold specifically to protect your perimeter — has two vulnerabilities being actively chained for unauthenticated remote code execution. VMware vCenter, the control plane for your entire virtualised infrastructure, has a CVSS 9.8 flaw with an APT actor already inside, using a reverse shell to maintain persistence. Siemens and Schneider Electric ICS products, running operational technology in energy and manufacturing, are the subject of six concurrent CISA advisories.

These aren't obscure edge cases. They're the products organisations have built their security architectures around.

The trust hierarchy is the attack vector now.

There's a second thread running through today's intelligence that makes this worse: the response window has effectively closed. AI-assisted attack tooling has compressed eCrime breakout times — the interval between initial access and lateral movement — to a 29-minute average. The fastest recorded this year is 27 seconds. No human analyst catches that. No alert-investigate-respond workflow stops it. Security architectures designed around human-speed detection are architecturally mismatched to the current threat cadence.

The third thread is supply chain, and it's moving upstream. Hugging Face responded to a supply chain compromise targeting AI development tooling this week. A BGP hijack was used to serve malicious updates through the Softaculous update channel. TeamCity CI/CD servers are under active exploitation for the same reason: compromise the build pipeline and you're not attacking one organisation, you're attacking everyone downstream of that build.

What connects these three threads is that the attack surface being targeted is infrastructure — not end-users, not misconfigured S3 buckets, not phishable employees. Infrastructure that organisations bought, certified, and trusted.

The question every board and CISO should be sitting with right now: which systems in your environment are treated as implicitly trusted? Where do you not apply zero-trust principles because the asset is "inside" or "managed" or "enterprise-grade"?

Those are your highest-risk assets this week.

CISA's federal civilian agencies have until Friday to patch vCenter and the SonicWall chain. That deadline exists because the exploitation is confirmed and active. Private sector organisations don't have a regulator setting that deadline for them — which means they either set it themselves or they don't.

The organisations that treat this week's advisories as a Friday afternoon maintenance window are making a different bet than they realise.
