# AI LinkedIn Post — 30 August 2026
**Status:** Draft (review before posting)

---

The question has changed.

For two years, the AI privacy conversation in regulated industries has been: "What data does the AI see?" That's the wrong question now.

This week Perplexity shipped a fully local AI agent that runs the entire stack — model, conversation history, task execution — on the device itself. Zero token costs. Cloud access only when you explicitly permit it. Built on NVIDIA hardware with a 27 billion parameter model that, in August 2026 benchmark testing, rivals GPT-4o across most enterprise tasks.

And Ollama updated to make a local model speak Anthropic's API natively. One command to connect Claude Code to inference that never leaves your building.

Meanwhile, Apple's Mac mini M6 at $1,299 with 32GB of unified memory can now run those same 27B models at production quality.

Three separate products. Same week. Same message: **the tooling argument for cloud-first AI just collapsed.**

Here's what makes this urgent rather than interesting.

On December 10 — 102 days from today — Australia's automated decision-making transparency obligations come into force under APP 1.7, 1.8, and 1.9. If your AI touches decisions that affect a customer's rights or interests, your privacy policy must say so, describe the types of personal information involved, and explain broadly how it works.

The OAIC ran its first compliance sweep earlier this year. Enforcement posture is shifting.

The EU AI Act's GPAI enforcement went live August 2. Fines up to 3% of global turnover. 78% of organisations reportedly haven't taken meaningful compliance steps.

And here is the thing that keeps coming up in client conversations: most organisations have checked the "Australian data centre" box and assumed that settles data residency. It doesn't. Data centre location covers where the data is stored. It doesn't cover where the LLM inference happens — where the model actually processes the customer information. For APRA-regulated entities, CPS 230's material service provider obligations extend to that inference layer.

The question is no longer "what does the AI see?" The question is **"where does the thinking happen?"**

If the answer is a data centre in Virginia or Oregon, and your customers are Australians whose financial or health information is involved, you have a gap.

The good news is that the gap now has a practical solution — not a workaround, not a research experiment. Production-quality open-weight models, on infrastructure you control, processing data that never leaves your jurisdiction.

The compliance argument and the capability argument have finally arrived at the same answer.

For decision-makers in financial services, health, and government: the window for treating this as a future-state architecture question is closing. December 10 is a hard date.

**Where does your AI's thinking happen?**
