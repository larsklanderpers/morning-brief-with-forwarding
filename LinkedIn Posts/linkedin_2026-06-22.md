87,000 network devices. Compromised not by a zero-day. Compromised by generic admin accounts and default credentials.

That's the FortiBleed campaign — a Russian-speaking threat actor group that has systematically worked through 86,644 internet-exposed Fortinet firewalls and VPN gateways, logging in with credentials that should never have existed, or should have been rotated long ago. CISA and Australia's ACSC both issued emergency advisories this week. Five Eyes-level concern. For what is, fundamentally, a credential hygiene failure.

At the same time, the June 2026 Microsoft Patch Tuesday landed with 206 vulnerabilities — including a Windows Kernel RCE with a CVSS score of 9.8 that allows unauthenticated remote code execution at SYSTEM level. 571 CVEs across vendors this month alone. The largest monthly patch release since 2017.

And North Korea's Sapphire Sleet group quietly poisoned more than 140 npm packages in the Mastra AI ecosystem — the kind of supply chain attack that spreads silently through developer pipelines before anyone notices.

Here's what these three stories have in common: **scale**.

FortiBleed isn't novel tradecraft — it's opportunism at industrial scale. The June patch wave isn't a single critical flaw — it's 571 flaws simultaneously demanding triage. The npm supply chain attack isn't targeting one organisation — it's targeting every developer who installed those packages, downstream.

Attackers have automated. They've scaled. They've commoditised. The Gentlemen ransomware-as-a-service platform is now distributing EDR-killer tools to affiliates as a subscription feature. The barrier to entry for bypassing enterprise endpoint detection just dropped again.

Meanwhile, Britain's NCSC issued a warning this morning that AI-generated code is becoming a hidden security liability in production systems. Developers are shipping AI-written code they don't fully understand into systems that their security teams haven't audited. The "move fast" culture of AI adoption is on a collision course with the "understand what you're deploying" imperative of security.

92% of security leaders are now concerned about AI agents across the workforce — that's from the Cloud Security Alliance's latest survey of 1,500+ security professionals. But Darktrace's data shows 67.3% of threat actors are already using AI for reconnaissance and malware development. The adoption gap isn't theoretical anymore.

So what does this mean for decision-makers?

The organisations that will navigate 2026 are the ones that have matched attacker automation with defender automation. Automated patch prioritisation tied to live exploit intelligence. Automated credential rotation and MFA enforcement — not policies, actual enforcement. Software bill of materials practices that extend into AI tooling and open-source dependencies, not just traditional software.

The organisations that won't are the ones still responding to automated, scaled attacks with manual processes, quarterly reviews, and advisory emails that nobody acts on.

FortiBleed is a test. 86,644 organisations failed it. The question for your organisation is whether you'd pass it — and whether you know the answer.
