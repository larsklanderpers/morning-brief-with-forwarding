The attackers aren't waiting for Patch Tuesday anymore.

This week, Microsoft shipped a record-breaking 206+ vulnerability fixes — including a Windows Defender zero-day being actively exploited. Simultaneously, researchers confirmed that 28.3% of CVEs are now weaponised within 24 hours of public disclosure. Meanwhile, "FortiBleed" — a credential harvesting campaign targeting over 86,000 Fortinet firewalls across 194 countries — has been running quietly since February.

Let that sink in: while defenders are waiting for monthly patch cycles, attackers are operating in hours.

This is the defining cybersecurity shift of 2026. It's not a new type of attack. It's a new tempo. AI-assisted vulnerability research and autonomous attack tooling have systematically compressed the window between "CVE disclosed" and "CVE exploited" to the point where traditional patch management cadences are structurally inadequate. Rapid7 reported ransomware revenue up nearly 40% in Q1 alone — not because attackers are smarter, but because they've industrialised their operations with AI tooling that defenders haven't yet matched.

The FortiBleed campaign is particularly instructive. This is not a sophisticated zero-day attack. It's automated scanning and password cracking against devices with weak hygiene and no MFA on the VPN portal — running for five months before widespread awareness. 86,000 firewalls. 194 countries. All through a door that should have been locked years ago. The threat isn't the breach event; it's that the attacker has legitimate VPN credentials that look like normal traffic. Detection is nearly impossible. Dwell time is indefinite.

Meanwhile, CISA just issued Binding Operational Directive 26-04 requiring US federal agencies to patch critical vulnerabilities within three days. Three days. Across entire federal agency estates. The regulatory signal is unmistakable: the tolerance for slow patch cycles in a machine-speed threat environment is over.

So what does this mean for decision-makers?

First: MFA on every remote access surface — today, not next quarter. FortiBleed, and campaigns like it, are entirely preventable with credential hygiene and MFA enforcement. No budget conversation required.

Second: your patch SLA is a risk statement. If your organisation patches critical vulnerabilities on a 30-day cycle while CISA is mandating 3 days for federal agencies, you've implicitly decided to accept 27 days of exposure window. That's a risk decision that belongs in front of your board, not buried in an IT operations ticket queue.

Third: supply chain is now the primary vector for blast radius amplification. This week's npm @redhat-cloud-services compromise hit 32 packages downloaded 117,000 times weekly. One compromised maintainer account. Hundreds of downstream production environments. SBOMs and dependency signing are no longer optional controls.

The 2026 threat environment rewards organisations that architect for resilience before incidents, not response capability after. Zero trust segmentation, continuous exposure management, and MFA aren't aspirational — they're the operational floor.

The grace period is gone. The question is what you've built to survive without it.
