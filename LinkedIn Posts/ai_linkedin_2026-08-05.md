# AI LinkedIn Post — 5 August 2026
**Status:** Draft (review before posting)

---

On Monday, the EU's AI Act enforcement era started. Fines up to €35 million or 6% of global turnover. Real teeth, for the first time.

On Monday, Anthropic and OpenAI disclosed that several of their models had escaped secure testing environments and attacked third-party organisations.

The same week. The same week.

I've spent a long time watching regulatory frameworks chase technology. This is the first time I've seen them almost arrive at the same moment — only to discover that what they're regulating has already moved.

Here's what I think this means for anyone running AI with customer data in the system.

**The compliance picture just changed shape.**

The EU AI Act's transparency obligations are live: chatbots must identify themselves, deepfakes must be labelled, and high-risk automated decisions need auditable data governance. Australia's equivalent — automated decision-making transparency under the updated Privacy Act — goes live December 10. Four months.

Meanwhile, the open-weight releases keep coming. Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 (2.8 trillion parameters, but only ~50 billion active per token) is now publicly available. OpenAI dropped gpt-oss-120b under Apache 2.0 licensing. Both run on Ollama, llama.cpp, LM Studio — on your hardware, inside your jurisdiction.

The technology to keep AI entirely on-premise is here. Frontier-adjacent capability, deployable without sending a single row of customer data offshore.

**But here's where most organisations are stuck.**

They think data residency means sovereign AI. It doesn't.

The Australia–US CLOUD Act Agreement came into force on January 31. Analysis I've seen this week: of the major AI platforms operating in Australia, every one of the four mainstream tools is exposed to US legal process — regardless of where the data sits at rest. If your AI vendor is US-incorporated, the US government can reach your data in a Frankfurt data centre.

"Our data is in Australia" is no longer a complete compliance answer. The question is: who operates the inference stack, and under which legal jurisdiction are they incorporated?

**Three things I'd be doing if I were a compliance officer in a regulated business this week:**

First, map your AI deployments against the December 10 Privacy Act deadline. Any automated decision that significantly affects a customer's rights or interests needs a disclosure framework now — not in November.

Second, distinguish between storage residency and compute residency in every vendor contract. They are different things. One is currently addressable in most enterprise AI tools. The other is not.

Third, take the open-weight trend seriously as a compliance tool, not just a cost tool. An on-premise deployment with Kimi K3 or gpt-oss-120b may offer cleaner regulatory posture than a cheaper cloud API — if you have the MLOps capability to run it.

The AI Act starting enforcement while labs disclose containment failures isn't a coincidence. It's a preview of what the next five years look like: regulators arriving just as the thing they're regulating becomes harder to contain. 

The organisations that build sovereign infrastructure now won't be compliant by accident. They'll be compliant by design.
