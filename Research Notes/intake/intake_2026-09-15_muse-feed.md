## What checks out, and what does not

- **The feed is not in Meta's own launch copy.** Not in the newsroom post (research note), not in the App
  Store description (fetched 15 Sep: no mention of a feed, Facebook/Instagram updates, local events or
  nearby). Only secondary explainers describe it ("a feed in the app, pulling updates from your connected
  Facebook and Instagram accounts alongside articles from the web", usecarly.com). **So in print it is his
  first-hand observation, in the first person, not a stated Meta feature.** Nothing in this screenshot
  shows Facebook or Instagram content; both cards are public local events.
- **His "you can graph API your way into the same level of stuff" does not hold for a personal account,
  and the break strengthens his point.** Meta for Developers, 4 Sep 2024: "Starting on December 4th, 2024,
  Instagram Basic Display API will no longer be available", and after that "there will no longer be a set
  of Instagram APIs for consumer developer apps." The surviving Instagram APIs serve business and creator
  accounts. (https://developers.facebook.com/blog/post/2024/09/04/update-on-instagram-basic-display-api/,
  fetched 15 Sep.) So an ordinary person building their own control plane cannot pull their own Instagram
  the way Muse does. Pair with the Verge line already in the research note: Muse read her interests
  "through the account's API data, which surfaces more than the UI does." **The seamlessness is first-party
  access that Meta closed to everyone else nine months before Muse shipped.** That is the commercial
  interest to name, and it is his "much harder to do on your own" point with a date on it. Facebook-side
  Graph API access for personal data was not re-checked this tick; do not generalise past Instagram.
- **His "harder to do on your own" framing is the aside's hinge**, not a new beat: it is exactly the
  set-up for "why I built my own anyway" that the research note's gate section asks for.

## Lars's note

I also really like this muse feed structure that is very reminiscent of Facebook and Insta. They're sort of playing into what you're already comfortable with, but that highlights not only stuff you've identified, but other things it can see in your area and around you, and stuff that's up on the book and all the rest of it.

I think that's a really smart piece of engineering and makes it even more approachable, at least as far as the story goes, compared to the build-your-own-plane issue, right? They've clearly spent some time making sure that there are things that are much harder to do on your own than they already do from here. That does not mean for one minute that you can't graph API your way into the same level of stuff, but for the ordinary user, they have made it far more seamless.
