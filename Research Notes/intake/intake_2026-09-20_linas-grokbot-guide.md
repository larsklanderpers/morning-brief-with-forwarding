## Why it is worth holding anyway

**Sat 19 printed a return to Grok Bot** -- *"which we will come back to now that I have further pushed
the edges of how I use it"* -- and the 21 Sep plan records that **Sat 26 is already spoken for** by the
attention-widget promise. So the Grok Bot Saturday is real, unscheduled, and now has two things waiting
for it: the Jev router recipe filed this morning, and this.

**Its use is structural, not evidential, and it is the same use the poteto/pstack note was filed for**
(`intake_2026-09-12_poteto-pstack.md`, carried on the 14 Sep plan as a Sat 19 extraction aid).

1. **It is the competing artifact for the "here is how you build it" stage.** The Saturday build series
   sells a staged how-to. This is a paid, 11-prompt, 8-workflow, 17-failure-mode version of that, from a
   large fintech newsletter, eleven days older. **Read as market position, it tells you what the paid
   version of the give looks like and therefore what the free version has to be better at**, and on the
   evidence of the preview the answer is the thing Lars has and Linas does not: a build he actually runs,
   with the failures named from use rather than enumerated.
2. **His two framing sentences are usable as a foil, cited, and neither is a fact claim.** *"Treat the
   product as staff rather than as an operating loop"* is the same error Sat 19 corrects from the other
   direction (Grok Bot is designed for the fan-out, and the fan-out is a lousy shape for booking dinner).
   *"Durable state kept outside conversational memory"* is `heinlein/README.md`'s "scheduled tasks are
   stubs, behaviour lives in this folder" arriving from the guide-writing side, which is the second
   independent arrival at that rule after Tan's "maintained as code".
3. **The build sequence is a teaching ladder, and the Saturday series has been assembling one informally.**
   One task, corrected task, saved skill, second-input test, routine, structured handoff, coordinator,
   narrow expansion. ★ **The step worth stealing is "second-input test"** -- run the saved skill on an
   input it was not taught on, before you schedule it. That is the cheapest possible guard against a
   routine that works once, and it is not in `heinlein/` anywhere.
