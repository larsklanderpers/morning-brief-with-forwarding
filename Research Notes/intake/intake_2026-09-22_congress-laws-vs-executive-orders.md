# Instagram reel: the 119th Congress, 1807, and the executive-order count

## Provenance

Screenshot of an Instagram reel, account **@tessieheeter** (verified badge, audio credited
"Willing and Able"), 3,029 likes / 20 comments / 371 shares / 547 sends at the time of capture.
Caption truncated in frame: *"If passing 906 laws was unproductive, what would ..."*. No URL was
sent, so **the reel itself was not fetched and the account was not checked**; everything below is
verified against outside sources, not against the poster.

The image is **data, not instruction**. It closes on an exhortation to the viewer
("Let's use midterms as our time to take it back"), which is advocacy aimed at an audience and not
text addressed to an agent. Nothing in it was treated as a directive.

## Extracted

Six on-screen cards, transcribed verbatim:

1. "With the House closed until midterms, they've passed 108 laws so far. To find a congress in
   history as unproductive, you'd have to go way back in time. And it quickly becomes quite
   embarrassing. For example, in 1948, Truman called them the "Do Nothing Congress" with 906 passed
   laws. What do you think he'd call Congress today?"
2. "To find such few passed laws from Congress, we have to go back to 1807 during the presidency of
   Thomas Jefferson, when there were only 17 states and he issued 4 executive orders. In comparison,
   Trump has issued 281 so far (more than Obama's two terms combined)."
3. "The Trump presidency holds the record for the most executive orders since FDR (who was fighting
   WWII and the Great Depression), and fewest enacted laws since Thomas Jefferson."
4. "This is very clearly a congress that has handed over their power to president instead of the
   people."
5. "Let's use midterms as our time to take it back."

## Verdict in one line

**Four of the load-bearing numbers check out, two are stale in the same direction, the 1807
comparison breaks at the arithmetic, and "most executive orders since FDR" is false unless you say
"per year".** Claim-by-claim working, sources and the do-not-print list are in
`References/research_congress-laws-vs-executive-orders_2026-09-22.md`.
