# Generative AI Business Sprint (MIT Sloan Executive Education brochure)

## Extracted

**How it was read.** The PDF would not render through the fetch tool (binary returned, no text
layer extracted). Pulled locally with `pdftotext -layout` off the saved copy. Everything below is
from that text unless marked as from the catalogue page.

**The brochure, verbatim on the facts:**

- **Duration:** "6 Hours / Complete within 30 days"
- **Price:** "$1,750"
- **Format:** "Self-Paced Online"
- **Executive Certificate track:** "Digital Business"
- **Faculty:** "Paul McDonagh-Smith, Faculty Director"
- Conducted by "world-leading generative AI faculty from the MIT Sloan School of Management and
  the MIT Schwarzman College of Computing"
- Structure: "Inspired by Agile product development sprints, this self-directed course is an
  opportunity to explore and better understand the transformational power of generative AI."
- The four questions it answers: "What is GenAI and why is it relevant to your business? How does
  GenAI work? When should our business apply GenAI? Where is GenAI heading?"
- Premise: GenAI "can generate unstructured outputs, such as images, text, and code. This ability
  aligns with the tasks of knowledge workers, offering potential to automate many business
  processes, enhance operational efficiency, and create new opportunities in the business
  landscape. Businesses need a clear and compelling GenAI strategy today if they want to be
  industry leaders tomorrow."
- Audience: "all levels of management", "particularly beneficial to those responsible for, or
  participating in, the design and delivery of AI and other transformative technology
  initiatives in any industry", plus "Chief Learning Officers and senior HR leaders".
- The closing sentence of the audience section, and the one that matters: "Regardless of whether
  you are currently in a technical or non-technical function, if you are looking to support your
  organization's digital transformation, or you want to **build capability and confidence in
  applying generative AI in your work**, this course is for you."
- Self-declared shelf life: "Please visit our website for the most current information."
- Brochure's own link, `executive.mit.edu/genai`, **404s as of 20 Sep 2026.**

**From the live catalogue page** (`executive.mit.edu/course/generative-ai-business-sprint/a054v00000r9S4fAAE.html`,
fetched 20 Sep 2026), which confirms the brochure is current and adds four names:

- Tuition **$1,750**, 30 days, 6 hours, On-Demand Online, Digital Business Executive Certificate.
- Faculty: **Jacob Andreas** (Assoc Prof, EECS), **Phillip Isola** (Assoc Prof, EECS),
  **Paul McDonagh-Smith** (Visiting Senior Lecturer, MIT Sloan, Faculty Director),
  **Rama Ramakrishnan** (Prof of the Practice, AI/ML, MIT Sloan), **Antonio Torralba** (Delta
  Electronics Professor, EECS).
- Deliverables named: a completion workbook and a "Certificate of completion from MIT Sloan".

**What is NOT in it, and this is the usable part.** No adoption statistic. No productivity number.
No pilot-failure rate. No claim of measured outcome anywhere in the brochure or on the course
page - the only quantities are the price, the hours and the window. An assessment is not
mentioned; the credential is a completion certificate. **So nothing here needs footing against
the corpus's numbers-to-never-use list, because it makes no numeric claim at all.**

⚠️ **Extraction artefact, not a finding about MIT:** the cover line came out of `pdftotext` as two
interleaved titles, "GENERATIVE AI BUSINESS SPRINT" overlaid with "THE GOOD JOBS STRATEGY:
DELIVERING SUPERIOR VALUE TO CUSTOMERS, SHAREHOLDERS, AND EMPLOYEES" (a different MIT Sloan
programme). That is a shared cover template in the catalogue's PDF layer, not a claim either
course makes. **Do not print it as an observation about the brochure.**

⚠️ **Naming:** the body text says "The Generative AI for Business Sprint"; the cover, the file name
and the catalogue page all say "Generative AI Business Sprint". Use the catalogue form.

**No text in the PDF or on the course page addressed an AI assistant or tried to instruct one.**

## Why it was filed where it was

`Threads/Adoption and Change` carries an open question in its own words: *"Is there a defensible
way to certify calibrated scepticism - the judgment competence - or is that just a
training-completion rate with better branding?"* This is that question with a price on it, from
the most prestigious possible seller: six asynchronous hours, no assessment, a completion
certificate, and a sales line that offers **capability and confidence together** when
[[Ability in AI adoption is judgment, not operation]] is the finding that those two came apart -
METR's developers were 19% slower and believed they were 20% faster.

**Not proposed for a day this week.** The 21 Sep plan runs liability, local politics, detection,
the shield, channel pricing and the widget build. Adoption training touches none of them, and the
Saturday slot is a printed promise. This is thread material and holds.
