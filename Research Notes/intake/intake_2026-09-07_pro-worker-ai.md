# Pro-Worker AI, Explained (slide deck on Acemoglu / Autor / Johnson)

Eight-slide explainer deck, every slide headed "ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE". Summarises work by
"MIT economists Daron Acemoglu, David Autor, and Simon Johnson". Johnson quoted twice and
captioned "MIT Sloan Professor and Nobel Laureate". Reads as MIT Sloan communications output.

**What is NOT on the artefact:** no publication date, no byline, no publisher logo in the
extracted text, no URL, and **no citation to the underlying paper**. It is a secondary explainer
that does not name its primary. Under standing accuracy rule 1 it can source nothing on its own.

The underlying paper is almost certainly Acemoglu, Autor and Johnson, *"Building Pro-Worker
Artificial Intelligence,"* NBER Working Paper 34854, Feb 2026, which is already logged in
`References/research_displacement-science_2026-08-24.md` at the end of section 4.3.

## Extracted

**Definition.** Most corporate AI adoption aims at automation: existing tasks faster, cheaper,
fewer people. AI can instead extend human judgment, create new tasks, accelerate skill
acquisition and raise the value of human expertise. The researchers call that pro-worker AI.

> "Pro-worker AI means deploying AI in a way that increases the demand for human expertise.
> People become more valuable, their expertise becomes more valuable, and you pay them more as a
> result." - Simon Johnson

**Not every productivity gain is pro-worker.** Technology can make a firm more efficient while
reducing the need for expertise, narrowing a role, or shifting value from labour to capital.
"That distinction depends not simply on whether AI improves productivity, but how those
productivity gains are achieved."

> "Replacing people with machines is just too easy. It's the path of least resistance that does
> not require much management imagination." - Johnson

**The five categories.** Labor-augmenting, capital-augmenting, automating, expertise-leveling,
new task-creating. "Only one category is unambiguously pro-worker: new task-creating
technologies."

**Distinguishing them.** A labor-augmenting tool speeds up a current task. An automation tool
moves the task to the machine. An **expertise-leveling tool lets a less-experienced worker
perform work that once required a specialist.** New task-creating technologies expand the range
of valuable work humans can do. Worked example: Ethernet and fibre-optic cabling raised building
complexity and created demand for specialised electrical expertise to plan, install and maintain
it.

**The business case.** Automation raises productivity by lowering the cost of existing work. New
task-creating technology raises it by expanding what people can productively do, letting firms
meet new needs and deliver things previously too difficult or expensive.

> "Do you want to amaze your customers and expand what they can expect from you, or do you want
> to putter along doing the same things as before? AI creates potential for breakthrough customer
> experiences, which is where companies should focus." - Johnson

**Four evaluation questions**, offered against a narrow time-and-labour-cost business case: what
new capabilities will AI create; what new work will employees be able to perform; what expertise
will become more valuable; how will the technology improve the product, service or customer
experience.

**Implementation.** Wider adoption "may depend on public investment," but leaders need not wait
for policy. The direction depends on choices made now: how work is redesigned, what AI systems
are asked to optimise for, and "whether workers are treated primarily as costs to reduce or as
sources of expertise to extend."

> "The priority is finding new things for humans to do and finding new ways to expand human
> capabilities across levels of education and skill." - Johnson

**Zero numbers appear anywhere in the deck.** No estimate, no study result, no n, no date.

## The take, against the corpus

**1. We already logged this paper, and we logged it as conceptual.**
`research_displacement-science_2026-08-24.md`, end of section 4.3: "No 2025-26 quantitative
revision found. Acemoglu/Autor/Johnson, *Building Pro-Worker Artificial Intelligence*, NBER
34854, Feb 2026, is conceptual and contains **no updated estimate**." The deck is that paper
dressed as a management prescription. The absence of numbers is not the deck compressing for
slides; there are no numbers in the source either.

**2. The register has been laundered, and section 4.4 has the receipt.**
Autor's own hedge on the adjacent argument is on file: *"My thesis is not a forecast but an
argument about what is possible."* The deck converts that into "firms that use AI to expand human
capability rather than to replace labor **may gain a competitive advantage**" on the cover, then
spends seven slides in the imperative. Standing accuracy rule 5 cuts both ways: the hedge should
survive editing, and the deck has smoothed it away. If any of this is quoted in a piece, the
hedge goes back in.

**3. Same authors whose macro arithmetic we already hold.** Acemoglu's published TFP figure is
**0.66%** over ten years, not 0.71% (killed claim `acemoglu-tfp-071`, 24 Aug). Nothing in the
deck touches it, and nothing in the deck is a killed claim - there is nothing in it specific
enough to kill. Worth noting for its own sake: the economist whose central published finding is
that the arithmetic cannot deliver large macro numbers is here making a case that does not
require any.

**4. It is the Sunday capstone's argument arriving from the optimistic side.**
Sun 13 Sep is "Four Numbers, No Instrument": the instruments people use to argue about AI and
work were built by someone else for a different question. The deck's five categories are a
taxonomy **with no instrument that codes into it.** There is no dataset, no survey item and no
statistical series that records whether a given deployment was labor-augmenting,
expertise-leveling or new-task-creating. The capstone's second and strongest support is that the
Displaced Worker Survey has no category that can record an AI reason. This is the same hole seen
from the other end: the optimists have five bins and no way to count what falls into any of them,
and the deck asks executives to self-classify. That is a better use for it than as a source.

**5. Its "expertise-leveling" category is Wednesday's depletion mechanism, named from economics.**
The deck defines expertise-leveling as a tool that "might allow a less-experienced worker to
perform work that once required a specialist," and pointedly declines to call it pro-worker. The
scheduled 6 Sep piece (`substack_scheduled_validation-tether_2026-09-06.md`) runs Lovett's
Cognitive Commons argument to the same place from HR development, and closes on his real thesis:
*"the question for HRD is not whether AI weakens expertise but under what design conditions it
builds or erodes it."* The deck's closing move is the identical question in the economists'
vocabulary: how work is redesigned, what the system is asked to optimise for, workers as costs
or as expertise to extend. **Two unconnected literatures landing on design conditions rather than
capability is a real convergence and it is the usable finding here.** It is corroboration for a
piece already scheduled, not a contradiction of it.

**6. Cross-links in the corpus.** `Vault/Writing/Signals/Ability in AI adoption is judgment, not
operation.md` carries the QJE support-agent result: +36% at the bottom quintile, no gain and a
quality decline at the top. That is expertise-leveling actually measured, and it points the same
way the deck's taxonomy does without needing the taxonomy. `Entry-ladder compression.md` is the
counterweight any use of this must carry: "finding new things for humans to do" is exactly what
nobody has found for the 24-year-old. And `The archetypes converge on one shape.md` is the
nearest thing the corpus has to an observed new-task-creating category.

**Bottom line.** Usable as framing and as a named-source foil, not as evidence. It contains no
claim that can be verified because it makes no claim specific enough to check, which is itself
the point worth making about it. If it is used, cite NBER 34854 as the primary, restore Autor's
hedge, and say plainly that the deck carries no data.
