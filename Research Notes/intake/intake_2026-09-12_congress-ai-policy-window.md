## Extracted

**The lead essay (about 600 words).**

- Frame: last week Transformer said Congress's mind was elsewhere; this week the Coxon resignation
  and Hubinger posts went viral, "Congress has duly snapped into gear," Democrats are discussing an
  AI select committee, Hawley is leading a subcommittee investigation.
- A bipartisan bill from **Cruz, Thune and Klobuchar** "may be introduced as soon as next week," with
  the hope of passing by January (linked to Semafor, 10 Sep).
- The charge, sourced to "a source who has seen the text": the bill "lets AI companies grade their
  own homework"; no safety requirements; a voluntary regime under which companies certify that models
  have "advanced threat capabilities"; no required independent evaluation; companies need only
  "reasonably address" risks. The only real government power, per WP Intelligence (3 Aug): the
  Commerce Secretary may request a court injunction. Paired with **broad preemption** of state AI
  laws. Cruz's office did not respond.
- Two problems named: preemption only works if the federal replacement is at least as strong as the
  state laws it replaces; and a weak bill now reduces appetite for a meaningful one later, "likely
  part of the strategy" for Cruz and "industry-friendly Republicans" given the GOP is likely to lose at
  least one chamber in November. (That motive claim is Hashim's inference.)
- Contrast offered: the Obernolte/Trahan **FRONTIER Act** "establishes an independent auditing
  scheme." (Checked at congress.gov in the research note: true, and it also preempts; Transformer
  omits the second half.)
- Cantwell on X, 10 Sep: the answer is "not a weak federal standard that becomes a backdoor for wiping
  out stronger state protections."
- Close: OpenAI's Chris Lehane (blog, Wed 9 Sep) urged "meaningful action over policy perfection";
  Hashim agrees perfection is too high a bar but says action must actually be meaningful.

**The newsletter items that bear on his angle (summarised, links as given by Transformer).**

- *Transparency.* The "secret White House AI framework": Axios reported it lacks public incident
  reporting guidelines; The Information reported the "trusted partner" early-access program lacks
  clear criteria; the White House declined to answer Senate Democrats' questions about it.
- *Inside the administration.* Politico: Treasury Secretary Bessent reassigned CIO Sam Corcos off AI
  policy after Corcos warned OpenAI and Anthropic the White House was pursuing a burdensome
  licensing regime.
- *States.* Newsom signed a spate of AI laws including **SB 813**, groundwork for an independent
  verification organization regime. Bonta opened an investigation into OpenAI over the Hugging Face
  hack. More than 10 states paused or cancelled data-centre tax breaks.
- *Congress.* Hawley announced a Homeland Security subcommittee investigation into the OpenAI Hugging
  Face hack; Blumenthal wrote to Altman (9 Sep letter, PDF on blumenthal.senate.gov). Democrats plan to
  investigate AI companies if they win control in November; a House AI select committee is floated.
  Sanders hosts a senators' briefing next week. OpenAI reportedly asked Congress whether a coordinated
  industry slowdown would violate antitrust law.
- *Money.* A new Democratic PAC, **Tomorrow Together**, launched with $10m+ (NYT). **Build American
  AI** launched a $10m Midwest data-centre ad campaign. Americans for Responsible Innovation launched a
  state-level policy push. Data for Progress polling: 68% of voters, 63% of Republicans, support the
  Sanders/Casar bill to pause AI development and ban superintelligence.
- *Industry.* OpenAI called for mandatory national AI safety regulation and endorsed four California
  bills including SB 813. OpenAI statement: "We and the larger AI community do not yet have a clear
  standard for how to report misalignment..." Anthropic withheld Mythos 5.1 from the UK AI Security
  Institute's pre-release evaluation, "reportedly" under White House pressure (FT).
- *The discourse section* is where the extinction material lives: Coxon, Hubinger, Leo Gao, Andreas

  "American killer robots." **This is the half he rejects.** The lead essay itself does not depend on

- *Australia, in passing.* US tech firms eyeing Australia for data centres partly because it is
