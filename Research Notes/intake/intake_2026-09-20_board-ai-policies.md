# The Five AI Policies Every Board Should Require (Victor M. Font Jr., LinkedIn Pulse)

## How it was read - READ THIS BEFORE QUOTING ANYTHING BELOW

⚠️ **Firecrawl is not available in this session** (`ToolSearch` for it returned nothing), so the page
was read through **WebFetch, which is a summarising reader, not a raw scrape**. Two passes were run
against the same URL: one for the body, one asking specifically for named instruments, verbatim
opening and closing sentences, and every number and date. **Every quotation below is the reader's
transcription, not a captured primary.** Both passes report the body is fully visible, not paywalled
and not truncated. **Nothing here is footed to print standard. Re-read the page directly before any
sentence of it enters a draft**, and treat the absence finding in particular as unconfirmed.

## Extracted

**The artefact.** "The Five AI Policies Every Board Should Require", **Victor M. Font Jr.**, LinkedIn
Pulse, **19 September 2026**. Filed as **"AI Governance Series | Article 8 of 20"** under the series
banner **"Governing Intelligence Before It Governs You"**.

**The five policies, as named:**

1. **AI Governance Policy** - governance objectives, roles, responsibilities, decision authorities,
   executive oversight, board reporting, governance committee responsibilities, review cadence.
2. **AI Risk Management Policy** - how AI risks are identified and classified, approval requirements
   by risk level, residual risk acceptance, monitoring expectations, escalation.
3. **AI Acceptable Use Policy** - approved platforms, prohibited activities, confidential
   information, IP, customer information, human review requirements, employee responsibilities.
4. **AI Data Governance Policy** - data quality, ownership, privacy, retention, classification,
   access controls, data lineage, regulatory compliance.
5. **AI Third-Party and Procurement Policy** - vendor due diligence, security reviews, privacy
   assessments, contractual protections, regulatory compliance, ongoing monitoring, exit planning.

**The framing line, as transcribed:** *"the goal isn't documentation. The goal is governability."*
Policies are said to be worth having only where they generate governance activity - risk
assessments, approval records, meetings, monitoring reports, exception reviews, audit results,
board reporting, compliance evidence.

**The five questions it tells boards to ask:** who owns each policy, how often is it reviewed, how
is compliance measured, what governance evidence demonstrates implementation, what metrics reach
the board.

**Opening two sentences, as transcribed:** *"Every organization adopting artificial intelligence
needs policies. The challenge is deciding which ones matter most."*

**Closing, as transcribed:** *"Those questions distinguish mature governance from administrative
compliance. Policies define expectations. Governance assigns ownership, manages risk, produces
evidence, and demonstrates that those expectations are consistently being met."*

★ **What is NOT in it, and it is the whole reason this was filed.** The second pass asked directly
for every named law, regulation, statute, regulator, standard or framework in the visible body, with
examples offered (EU AI Act, NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001, SEC, GDPR, Colorado AI Act). The answer was
**"None appear in the visible article body text."** The only numbers in the body are **5** (the
policies), **8 of 20** (the series position) and the publication date. **No statistic, no deadline,
no jurisdiction, no citation of any kind.** A five-policy mandate addressed to directors, and
nothing external that any of it answers to.

⚠️ **That is an absence claim read off a summarising reader, and the standing rule applies.** It is
the same class of claim as Mon 14's "no occupational code", which was false at the primary. **Do not
print it until the page has been read directly.**

**The commercial interest, which the standing rule says to name.** The article ends on a
subscription prompt for the author's newsletter, **"The Cyber Governance Brief" (1,468 followers)**,
and promotes the next installment, *"Your AI Dashboard Probably Measures the Wrong Things"*. The
reader also returned, **attributed to a comment rather than to the article body**, the line
*"Policies define how AI should behave; Governance by Assurance™ enables the board to continuously
know and prove whether the AI-enabled enterprise is actually behaving that way."* **Governance by
Assurance™ is a trademarked methodology, and it is the author's.** Web search (20 Sep) puts him at
**CyberGovernanceCenter.com** and **Victor Font Consulting Group, LLC**, describing a
"boardroom-focused blueprint for directors, executives, and compliance leaders", and as the author
of 20+ books. ⚠️ **Do not conflate him with the other Victor Font who came back in the same search,
a Senior Managing Director at FTI Consulting. Different person, and the search results interleave
them.** The trademark line is the piece that needs re-reading at the page: if it is in the body
rather than a comment, the article is a twenty-part funnel that names no law and one product.

**Nothing on the page was addressed to an AI assistant**, and nothing on it attempted to instruct
one. Both passes were asked and both said so.

## Why it was filed where it was

`Threads/Ad Hoc Regulatory Regime` already carries the pattern this belongs to, promoted 7 Aug:
*"Regulation by improvisation now has a commercial sibling: compliance folklore, manufactured by the
people selling compliance."* The instances on file are all false-fact instances - the CLOUD Act date
that was two years wrong, the APRA guidance nobody had committed to. **This one is the quiet version
and it may be the more common one: nothing in it is false, because nothing in it is checkable.**
Five policies, a trademarked assurance product, and no statute, regulator or standard anywhere to
be answerable to. The thread's own open question is *"who is actually setting the rules when the
agency that should is gone"*, and one live answer is a consultant with a newsletter and a series.

**Not proposed for a day.** Mon 21 wants the money visible in a primary and this has no money and is
not a primary; Fri 25 is his own channel seat. Adjacent to both, footing for neither, so the
21 Sep plan and its status board were left untouched.
