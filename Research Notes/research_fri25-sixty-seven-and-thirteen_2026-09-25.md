## Bottom line
**The angle holds, and it got a news peg today.** The MSP 501 report (Informa, sponsored by Ingram Micro, published **23 Sep 2026**) says 91% of top MSPs offer or use AI, nearly three-quarters run it as a core managed service, and 57% expect it to be a top revenue gainer within a year. Kaseya's survey of the wider market (n=1,061) says **13% are generating meaningful revenue from it now.**

**Microsoft moves the meter under all of them.** From **2 Nov 2026**, new Copilot Business licenses sold through CSP default to pay-as-you-go usage billing (Partner Center, 16 Sep 2026, primary). Partner incentives are shifting from selling seats to driving usage.

So the channel is being asked to sell AI as a consumption meter, on a margin it cannot yet see, while doing readiness work that is mostly unscoped. **The money half is still his:** what he bills for AI, what he absorbs, and what the Nov 2 default does to his invoices.

## What's contested / watch out (secondary; not re-fetched by main session)
- **Counter-evidence, and the piece must carry it:** GTIA State of the Channel 2026, UK and Ireland (n=130, May 2026; via intelligenttechchannels.com, 13 May 2026).
  - 35% of ITSPs generate 11-25% of revenue from AI-related services.
  - 7% get over half.
  - 30% identify as "AI-driven."
  - "AI-related products and services" likely includes resale. UK and Ireland only.
- **The Channel Company, "The Tech Channel's AI Frontier", 22 Jan 2026** (n=579 MSPs plus 396 solution providers). Solution providers reporting "significant positive impact" from AI rose from 13% to 29%. 83% hold a "wait-and-see" investment stance. The sameness of this 13% to Kaseya's is a coincidence. Do not conflate them.
- **Channel Dive, 21 Jan 2026 (James Anderson):**
  - Copilot Business list price dropped from $30 to $21.
  - A partner CEO says the bundle discount of about 20% drops to 5% after year one: "They basically are charging us a 10% premium by bundling it."
  - "Selling the full subscription is not enough. Microsoft expects full usage if partners want to get the most out of rebates and competencies."
  - Peter Fidler (WCA Technologies): "We can't twist somebody's arm."
  - https://www.channeldive.com/news/confusion-clouds-microsoft-partners-csp-discount-copilot/810181/
- **Channel Dive, 15 May 2026:** Sherweb's Michael Slater says MSPs "see [Microsoft licensing] as a cost of tooling rather than a profit center". Ntiva reports clients buying "closer to five licenses per organization". Anecdotal, but the best thin-margin voices found.
- **Channel Dive, 12 Jun 2026:**
  - Pax8's COO: there is "no good way for a partner to actually see the usage on anything they build."
  - Omdia's Jessica Davis: "Most MSPs are still early in figuring out how to package, price, govern and support agentic AI."
  - ★ **This quote pairs with the Nov 2 usage-billing default.** The partner will bill a meter it says it cannot see.
- **Readiness work (unpaid, mostly):**
  - Gartner via Computerworld (27 Sep 2024, n=132): 64% said governance and security risks "required significant time and resources"; oversharing concerns led "40% to delay rollouts by three months or more." The sample is large enterprises and it is two years old.
  - Microsoft's own deployment blueprint (Learn, updated 18 Aug 2026) lists "remediating oversharing" and "fix access issues" and gives no time estimate.
  - Microsoft did fund some partner deployment through the FY26 CSP Deployment Accelerator, but it was invitation-only, non-profit and K-14 only, and ran 1 Apr to 30 Jun 2026. Use it to avoid saying "never paid".
- **Adoption:**
  - Nadella, Q4 FY26 call (29 Jul 2026): "over 30 million paid Microsoft 365 Copilot seats." (Transcript via stockanalysis.com. Re-fetch before printing.)
  - Directions on Microsoft put Jan 2026's 15M seats at about 3.3% of about 450M commercial seats.
  - Gartner 2025 (via techpartner.news, n=187): only 5% of completed pilots moved to larger deployments.

## The economics
- **Expectation against ledger.** The best of the channel forecasts AI as a top revenue gainer (57%). The broad channel reports meaningful AI revenue at 13%. The spend in between is readiness work, pilots and support, and it is carried on existing managed-services margin. (Inference; the spend is not measured anywhere public.)
- **The meter moves on 2 Nov.** Seats are a number the partner quotes and knows. Pay-as-you-go is a number the customer's staff generate and the partner invoices. Pax8's own COO says partners cannot see that usage. Microsoft's incentives now reward usage, not the sale.
- **The arc tie (Sunday):** the cost of the transition lands on the party in the middle. The vendor books the consumption, and the customer assumes the MSP is managing it. The MSP carries the unbilled readiness work and the bill-shock conversation. (His to confirm from the seat.)

## Verdict
**Stronger than "What the Transfer Costs", and it carries without a war story.** It is footed at primary on three sides: Kaseya's 13%, the MSP 501 forecast released today, and Microsoft's 2 Nov billing change. It still needs his seat for the money and for credibility. Four questions for him:
1. What does he actually charge for AI (per-seat add-on, bundled, project, retainer), and what does he eat?
2. How much readiness and oversharing cleanup goes unbilled on a typical Copilot rollout?
3. What does the 2 Nov pay-as-you-go default do to his invoicing and his client conversations?
4. His commercial interest, named in the text.

**Proposed spine.**
- **Opener:** the two numbers.
- **Problem:** the forecast is in, the ledger is not, and the gap is funded from existing margin.
- **Turn:** on 2 Nov the product becomes a meter the partner bills but cannot see.
- **The seat:** his figures.
- **Counterweight:** GTIA UK and Ireland, 35% at 11-25% of revenue. Some firms are earning, and what they did differently.
- **Actions** in bold lead-ins. Candidates: price the readiness as a line item; decide your PAYG ceiling before 2 Nov; stop bundling what you cannot meter.
- **Two-paragraph close.**
