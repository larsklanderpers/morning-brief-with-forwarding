**Opener style:** Two short declarative sentences. No "As if..." or subordinate clause openers. State the fact, then escalate. Example: "The sheer volume of threats has spiked horrifically over the last 6 months. The threat arc is increasing constantly, sometimes with exponential jumps."

**Why:** He trimmed a long hedging opener to two punchy declarations across multiple revision rounds.

**Analogy-first:** Reaches for a concrete analogy before stating the abstract argument. Analogies are colloquial and slightly irreverent ("Jimmy in the mailroom", "modern-day Obi-Wan"). Pop culture references are welcome when precise.

**Problem-before-fix:** US East Coast instinct — names the threat clearly and fully before prescribing solutions. Do not restructure this; it's intentional and correct for his audience.

**Jargon handling:** Explains technical terms inline with parentheticals rather than separate explanatory sentences. Example: "context explosion (creating lots of junk that the AI has to interpret...)"

**Hollywood vs. grind contrast:** Recurring instinct to contrast the "cinematic" framing of threats with the reality of diligent, repeatable defensive work. A signature observation — but a conditional callback, not a slot (see signature-devices rule below).

**Sentence rhythm:** Short punchy sentences mixed with longer structured ones. Comfortable with semicolons. Uses normal dashes (-) and double dashes (--) for emphasis. Never use em-dashes (—) — he explicitly rejected them. Do not convert his dashes.

**Section openings:** Leave light — Lars will add his framing. Don't front-load section openers with my own argument; leave a natural drop-in point.

**How to apply:** Draft Substack articles with a light, open intro structure and punchy declarative section openers. Leave `<!-- [YOUR TAKE]: -->` prompts only at spots where his personal experience or opinion would sharpen the piece. Calibrate for CISO/decision-maker audience, not purely technical practitioners.

**Closes — CORRECTED (2026-07-24; supersedes the 2026-07-19 "default" rule):** The reader's-calculus/news-cycle close is a rationed signature callback, NOT the default weekday closer. His words: the beat is real, "it just doesn't always land in the piece and it doesn't always align to the topic, so it should be very much conditional as a signature device, not a requirement." Once or twice a week at most, only when it aligns with the topic, worded fresh each time — "the calculus idea is right, the deluge of content is right on, but saying it a different way, consistently, is also very much right on." Close shape: two short paragraphs, resolved line first; the last sentence lands one of two ways — "a meaningful conclusion that people go 'yeah I get it'" or, when genuinely open-ended, "an open question that indicates they need to take action because no one else is going to hand them easy answers to this stuff." "What I'm going to do" forward-guidance closes rank a distant third ("these are less about me and more about the space"), licensed mainly when the piece carries a personal/professional anecdote, and even then sparingly. **How to apply:** derive the close from what the story is about; before deploying the calculus close, check the last two scheduled/published pieces; keep an escalating-tool metaphor concrete when one is genuinely running, never force one.

**Signature devices are conditional callbacks (2026-07-24, general rule from Lars):** ALL signature devices — "Read that again.", the escalating-tool metaphor (hammer → bandsaw), Hollywood-vs-grind, the reader's-calculus close — are callbacks rationed across the week, deployed only when the piece calls for them, never slots to fill. Device repetition across consecutive pieces is slop (the editor panel's Seat 5 now checks the last two pieces for it). His framing: "we need to make sure that we're not just repeating a formula where we're actually delivering to what the story is about." A draft loses nothing for lacking a device; it loses for forcing one.
