## ★ AMENDMENT, 3 Nov 2026 (his word) -- register decides

> *"Write formal pieces without contractions, things in a more casual voice can use contractions but still pretty minimally."*

- **Formal pieces** (default reading: Thursday's one argument, monthly capstones, anything regulatory or analytical): the drafter writes **zero** contractions.
- **Casual-voice pieces** (default reading: Saturday Shop Hours, Notes, first-person check-ins): the drafter **may** contract, **sparingly** -- a handful per piece, where speech cadence wants it, not as a default.
- **Tuesday Changing Management:** judge by the piece; when unsure, treat as formal.
- **Unchanged:** nobody expands a contraction he wrote, in any register. The `its` -> `it's` typo fix stays mechanical.
- This replaces "zero contractions in exposition" below as the drafter rule. Any older note saying "no contractions by default" (e.g. [[agent-propagation-dig]], 20-21 Aug) is superseded.

## The 25 Sep 2026 ruling (still governs his own text)

**The rule, as ruled by Lars on 25 September 2026 (ruling "c" of the voice recalibration):**

> **The drafter writes zero contractions in expository prose. Nobody -- drafter, panel, mechanics pass -- ever expands a contraction Lars wrote, in any register.**

This supersedes the 6 September amendment ("zero contractions in expository prose; direct address is exempt"), which in turn superseded the flat "zero contractions outside quoted speech." The 6 Sep rule was applied to HIS text as well as the drafter's, and that is what went wrong.

## Why: the evidence that produced the ruling

The 25 Sep recalibration diffed 38 pieces (12 Aug - 22 Sep) plus the adoption-speed rv16 -> rv17 pair, drafter-only file against his edit-pass file:

- **He added contractions in exposition in 38 of 38 passes. He removed none.** They are not confined to direct address: "I don't mean to imply that the model improvements aren't *more* than that" (adoption rv17:123); "The document's been edited twice in 30 days; pretty sure the government's content management system could keep up" (forwarding-address rv1:91 -- the same piece the 6 Sep note described as contraction-free in its expository sections).
- **The pipeline was expanding them.** From 24 Aug the drafter and panel expanded his expository contractions under the 21 Aug ruling and the 6 Sep amendment. His "I'm lucky enough" (window-closes-friday) was expanded before rv2 and shipped as "I am lucky enough." Several of his files were de-contracted before they were even saved.

The 6 Sep observation (in forwarding-address his nine contractions clustered in direct address) was true of that piece and is still a useful description of where contractions cluster. It was wrong as a rule for his text.

## How to apply

- **Drafting:** see the 3 Nov amendment above -- zero in formal pieces, sparing in casual ones.
- **His text:** every contraction he wrote stays, wherever it sits. No expansion by the drafter, the panel, or any mechanics pass. The panel does not flag them, count them, or score them.
- **Mechanical `its` -> `it's`:** his missing-apostrophe "its" (~33 passes) is a typo. Fix it silently. That fix is not an expansion and is not a register decision; count it apart from his deliberate contractions.
- **Merging:** when a panel fix collides with a sentence of his, keep his contractions in the merged sentence.
