## The spine of it
The enterprise's obligation to build rungs STANDS -- and the firm is best served by meeting it.
"Build your own ladder" is an ESCAPE HATCH that didn't exist before, not an excuse for the
enterprise and not abandonment of the kid. The optimism is additive, not substitutive. The moral
weight stays on the employer; the individual just gains an option so they're not trapped.

## The beats (Lars's, from the interview)
- If you take a job with someone, they owe you a real set of climbing rungs -- and they're better
  off building them than not. That obligation does not move.
- If the rungs aren't there, building your own ladder used to be near-impossible unless you were in
  a hands trade (be a plumber for yourself). You needed compute, structure, people to sell and
  market it. Now the **cost of curiosity is ~0** -- you can find out what you can and can't do on
  your own without that scaffolding. The **cost of tooling still isn't 0**. Be prudent; use what's
  already there. (ties to Saturday's on-ramp ladder, research_inference-onramp-ladder_2026-07-29.)
- Saturday-optimism = two things: (1) impatience -- when he was that age the rungs weren't coming
  fast enough; now you can add your own rungs / a whole new ladder if you want. (2) The ability to
  build your own ladder WITH discipline and structure, so if you decide the guardrails in place
  aren't serving what you believe in, going out on your own is genuinely possible now -- which it
  wasn't before. "Not a caveat, not papering over -- that's alternatives," and alternatives you can
  pursue without moving into a house with eight other people like the old Microsofties did.
- The discipline point: he learned to structure answers, build things, AND discipline about "what
  people are asking me for versus what it does" precisely BY working for a lot of other people.
  Working for others is where the judgment came from -- worth keeping honest against the
  go-it-alone romance (his son wants to go into business for himself "because he doesn't know he
  can work for other people").

## Candidate analogy (Lars flagged doubt -- use with care or cut)
Corporate-controlled vs. socialized health insurance: employer-controlled = trapped in place (can't
leave, don't want to be uninsured); socialized = maybe not as good, but doesn't lock you where you
are. He said himself he wasn't sure it holds under analysis. Termite/house is the keeper analogy;
this one is optional.

## Usable verbatim lines
- "It's not the kid's responsibility to build the rung -- it's the enterprise's responsibility... [telling the kid to build their own] is simply giving the kid a better option so they're not trapped."
- "Keeping the ladder down and not pulling it up behind me."
