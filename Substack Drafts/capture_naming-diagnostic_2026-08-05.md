## Argument stress-test (Pass 2)
- **Thesis:** the ability to name the task-level split is the diagnostic that separates a company transforming its work from one using AI as cover for a decision it had already made.

- **Best counterexample (this is the sharp one):** you cannot diagnose internal rigor from external comms. A press release is a communications artifact written by people who were not in the decision. Plenty of genuinely rigorous companies say nothing useful publicly, and plenty of companies that *can* produce a beautiful task inventory still got it badly wrong — Ford almost certainly had detailed engineering plans. **Naming is necessary, not sufficient.** The piece must concede this or a sharp reader kills it in the first paragraph.
  - **Our answer:** the diagnostic is not the press release. It is whether anyone inside can produce the list on demand. That test is internal, and it is available to the reader about their own employer. For outsiders, the careers page works as a *behavioral* signal rather than a stated one — hiring is expensive and slow, so what a company staffs is revealed preference against what it announces. Cheap talk versus costly signal. That distinction is worth making explicit; it is the load-bearing move of the piece.

- **Best skeptic rebuttal:** "Rehiring after layoffs is ordinary and always has been. Visier's baseline boomerang rate is 5.3% with no AI variable at all. You have found the business cycle and dressed it up as a morality tale."
  - **Our answer:** the claim is not that rehiring is new. It is that the *stated reason* for these cuts was uniquely falsifiable — "AI can do this job" is a testable proposition, and a third of the managers who made it have already tested it and been wrong. Concede the baseline honestly, then note that the baseline is precisely why the rehire number is the hook and not the argument. The argument is the 23% who cut without role-level analysis.

- **Hidden assumption:** that task-level decomposition is knowable in advance. Mollick's tasks-vs-jobs point cuts both ways — if a job is a bundle whose seams only appear in practice, then "name which tasks moved" may demand a precision nobody has ex ante.
  - **Our answer / the concession that strengthens it:** you cannot know perfectly in advance, and that is exactly the point. The honest orgs name it provisionally and revise. Naming is a practice, not a document. An org that produced the list once in January and never revisited it is washing on a longer timescale.

- **So-what / Monday-morning change:** ask your own organization for the list. Which tasks moved, which judgment calls stayed, who signed for each, when was it last revised. If nobody can produce it inside a week, the answer is not that AI is doing the work. It is that a decision got made and AI was handed the microphone.
