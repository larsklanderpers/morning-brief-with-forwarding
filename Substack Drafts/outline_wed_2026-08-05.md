## Thesis
Monday showed every inherited wall failed and nobody controls the models. The reflex answer is a
taller fence announced from a podium. But look at the two answers on offer in public and they are the
same answer wearing different clothes: *more regulation / kill-switch bills*, and Altman's
*"liberty" = fewer fences / deregulation*. One routes control through a government's switch, the
other leaves it in a vendor's hands. Neither is control **you** have. The third answer -- the only
one that actually responds to Monday -- is control you can **locate**: a barrier that lives in
infrastructure you hold, doesn't route through one vendor's kill switch, and is hard to switch off
because it's yours. Open weights and local / in-jurisdiction inference aren't the risk to freedom.
They're the version of control that doesn't depend on someone else's goodwill.

## The spine

### Open — analogy-first: the keep
Monday's outer walls fell. The keep is the tower at the center of the castle you can still hold when
the curtain wall is breached -- the part that's yours, that you don't share, that no besieger's memo
opens. (Your own phrase-bank has "the castle and its keep.") Control you can locate is the keep: not
the biggest wall, the one whose key is in your pocket. Alt analogies if the keep feels tired: a safe
in your house vs. a safety-deposit box the bank can drill or freeze; a generator in your basement vs.
depending on a grid someone else can cut.

hedge (run it yourself, no vendor kill switch, data never leaves) but NOT full transparency,
