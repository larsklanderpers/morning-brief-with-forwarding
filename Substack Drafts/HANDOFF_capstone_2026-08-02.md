## ⚠️ THE PLAN'S PUBLICATION-DAY PEG IS WRONG — READ THIS FIRST

The week plan says *"the EU AI Act becomes fully applicable Sunday 2 Aug — the day this publishes."* **It does not.** The Digital/AI Omnibus deferred the high-risk obligations. Do not write "fully applicable" — the phrase was never precise and is now actively wrong.

**Verified (see `References\research_capstone-2aug_2026-07-27.md` for sourcing):**

- Stand-alone Annex III high-risk: 2 Aug 2026 → **2 December 2027**
- High-risk embedded in Annex I products: 2 Aug 2026 → **2 August 2028**
- The delay followed **Big Tech pushback** — reported plainly, not an inference
- **2 August is NOT cancelled.** What lands that day is **Article 50 transparency** — the obligation to disclose when an AI system is interacting with a person or generating/manipulating content
- Narrow carve-out: Art 50(2) machine-readable marking deferred to 2 Dec 2026 for systems already on market before 2 Aug; new systems comply immediately
- New prohibited practices from 2 Dec 2026
- **Penalties unchanged:** up to EUR 35M / 7% of global turnover (prohibited), EUR 15M / 3% (transparency and high-risk)

**Why this makes the piece better.** The original peg was "a designed framework switches on." The true version is sharper and lands the week's thesis harder: the most carefully designed framework anybody has written put a date on the hard part, then moved it sixteen months under industry pressure — and what actually arrives on 2 August is a duty to *tell people*. A disclosure obligation. **That is a sign, not a governor** — Friday's exact distinction, arriving in the one jurisdiction everybody holds up as the adult in the room. Even the EU shipped the sign and deferred the engine.

## Voice rules that bite on this piece

Load `feedback_lars_writing_voice.md` from project memory first — it is authoritative and beats any skill file that disagrees.

- **Dashes:** hyphens `-` and double hyphens `--`. **Never em-dashes.** Automatic voice-fail.
- **Opener:** two short declarative sentences.
- **Analogy-first**, colloquial, slightly irreverent. Fresh for this week: Mon used lifeguard/nurse ratios, Tue the apprentice + Chartreuse, Wed the door/building, Thu Dune, Fri the speed-limit sign. **Do not reuse those.**
- **THE READER'S-CALCULUS / NEWS-CYCLE CLOSE IS OUT.** It ran Mon 27, Tue 28 and Wed 29 and was deliberately retired from Thu 30 and Fri 31. Five-plus in a week is the exact failure the 24 July rationing rule exists to prevent. Close shape is two short paragraphs, resolved line first, last sentence landing either a meaningful conclusion or an open question that pushes the reader to act.
- **Check the last two pieces for any device before using it** — cross-piece repetition is slop.
- **Appositive-gloss-then-consequence:** name the abstraction, stack disqualifying traits in a `--` appositive, break the consequence into its OWN following sentence. Do not collapse it.
- **Action sections:** bold lead-in + paragraph, never bullets.
- **Capability + cost:** name the economics. Here that is the unchanged penalty ceilings.
- **Audience glosses:** CISOs and decision-makers, not AI insiders. Spell out acronyms and firms — "BCG" got caught bare in Friday's draft.
- **US spelling.** Length is Lars's call, never a hard gate; if it runs long he prefers a two-beat aside to the reader over cutting the argument.
