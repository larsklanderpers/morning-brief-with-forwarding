## WORKING THESIS

**Washington has finally started paying attention to AI, and that is good. But the argument worth having now
is not how much to regulate. It is whether the rules will be written down, in public, by Congress, where
anyone can read them and someone independent checks them. So far the federal answer has been no: an
executive order that disclaims the power to compel, a review rubric that five companies have been briefed on
and nobody else has seen, and a standards centre with no statute. The people who agree that this is the
problem are not who you would expect, and that is the political opening.**

Reader action (Tuesday afternoon, one line): **before you ask how strict this week's AI bill is, ask two
questions of it: can I read it, and who checks?** (A different verb from Mon 14's WRITE and Sun 13's READ:
this is a test you apply, not a filing or a lookup.)

## FIVE-LINE OUTLINE

1. **They finally noticed.** A week of senators, investigations and a Cruz who wants "substantially more
   regulation"; say plainly that attention is good, and say plainly he is not here to sell the doom version.
   The Casar deadline lands the morning this runs.
2. **What they have built so far is improvised, private and unfunded.** EO 14409 §3 disclaims compulsion;
   the review framework is finalised and unpublished, briefed to five companies; CAISI has no statute. Five
   senators call it "ad hoc" in writing.
3. **The coalition nobody expected.** Americans for Prosperity and R Street on the same letter as Public
   Citizen and CAP; Democratic senators arguing for a statute on China grounds; a three-and-three House bill
   with mandatory audits and published reports that has not had a hearing. The split is written-down versus
   improvised, not left versus right.
4. **The honest case against me.** The patchwork is real for small firms; some secrecy has real
   national-security reasons (the coalition letter concedes it); the state AGs moved faster than Congress;
   the leadership bill may drop Monday and be better than reported.
5. **The test, and the close.** Whatever drops this week, ask can I read it and who checks. For AU readers:
   Australia made the same choice in December 2025. Declarative close, no question in the last two
   paragraphs.
