# The Junk Drawer

*A dot keeps one memory for everything you give it. That is what makes it useful, and it is the thing you have to manage.*

Every kitchen has one drawer that holds everything. It is the most useful drawer in the house, right up until it isn't.

You know the one. Batteries, takeaway menus, three sets of keys to locks nobody can identify, a tape measure, the warranty card for a toaster you threw out in 2019, and somewhere near the back, the one thing you actually need. The junk drawer works because it takes anything. It stops working for exactly the same reason. Nobody ever cleans it out one item at a time; you live with it until the day you tip the whole thing onto the counter and start over.

This is the first entry in Shop Hours, and it is about the agent in my stack that works most like that drawer: Freya, my dot.

## What a dot is, briefly

If you missed it in October: dots are OpenAI's always-on agents. A dot lives inside ChatGPT, has its own cloud computer, connects to the apps you choose, and keeps working between conversations. I wrote about the computer side of that on 8 October, so I will leave it there. Today is about the other half, the memory, because that is where my opinion has changed.

The basics, from OpenAI's own help page. Your first dot is included with ChatGPT Pro or Business Premium. Pro now starts at $100 a month on OpenAI's pricing page (it was $200 when I wrote about dots in October, so if that number put you off, look again). Pro dots are not available in the European Economic Area, Switzerland or the UK; Business Premium is available more widely. You get one dot for now; OpenAI says that "in the future, you'll be able to add more." You can name it, give it an avatar, talk to it in the app, on a call, or in Slack, and (in a limited US beta) text it.

Last week I described Freya as the business manager standing at the bar while the band plays, the one with opinions about everything who does not play an instrument. That is still the best description I have. Both of my chiefs of staff talk to her. She sees across everything. And that is exactly the property that needs looking after.

## One drawer for everything

Here is the part of the help page I wish I'd read on day one. Your dot "receives memories from ChatGPT and can create its own memories, including from connected apps." It can review what's in those apps "proactively and form memories from it, even when you haven't asked." That is the always-on bit, and it is great: she notices things.

Then the catch, also in OpenAI's words: "To delete your dot's own saved memories, you need to delete your dot." You cannot open her memory and take out the one wrong thing. Disconnecting an app "does not delete information your dot has already obtained from it." And deleting the dot removes her memories and her scheduled tasks together, archives the main conversation, and "can't be undone."

So a dot is a junk drawer with a lid that only comes off all at once. Everything you hand it goes in the same drawer, it stays there, and the only way to clean it is to tip it out and start a new one. If you use one dot for your business, your household, your side project and your kid's school schedule, all of that is now context for every answer. Sometimes that is brilliant (she connects the late invoice with the client meeting on Thursday). Sometimes it's pollution: a preference from one part of your life quietly steering a decision in another, and you cannot see it happening.

## How I'd set one up now

None of this is a reason not to have a dot. It is a reason to decide what goes in the drawer before you start filling it. If I were setting Freya up again from scratch, here is what I'd do.

**Clean ChatGPT's memory first.** Your dot starts from your existing ChatGPT memories. Open Settings and look at what ChatGPT thinks it knows about you before you create the dot, and delete anything stale or anything you would not want an always-on agent acting on. This is the only point where you can take things out one at a time.

**Connect apps on purpose, one at a time.** Each app you connect becomes something she reads in the background and remembers. Disconnecting later does not make her forget. So connect the one or two apps the job really needs, live with it for a week, and add the next one when you have a reason, not because the list is there.

**Use tasks as dividers.** Each task you give a dot gets its own conversation, and OpenAI says a task "doesn't automatically receive every conversation you've had with your dot." That is the closest thing you get to dividers in the drawer. Keep the business work in one task, the house in another, and do not do everything in the main chat.

**Write the rules down where she can see them.** Under Custom rules, every action can be set to "take action without asking," "take action if pre-approved," "ask before taking action," or "hand off to you." Start with anything that spends money or sends something in your name on "ask before." One line from the help page I like a lot: "Asking for a draft doesn't authorize sending it."

**Know when to tip it out.** If the drawer gets bad enough, reset. Save anything you need from the main conversation first, because it is archived and you cannot continue it, and remember the separate Work and Codex chats she created stay where they are. Then create a new dot and do the four steps above properly this time.

## Where a dot fits

My view, for what it's worth: a dot is at its best as the one agent that sees across everything else, and at its worst as the one agent that does everything else. The manager at the bar is valuable precisely because she is not also playing guitar. Let your other tools do the narrow jobs, give the dot the job of noticing, and keep the drawer tidy enough that what she notices is still true.

If you are only going to have one agent, a dot is a reasonable choice. Just decide what goes in the drawer before it fills itself.

**Addendum: the prompts, if you want to start from mine.**

*Before you create the dot (in a normal ChatGPT chat):*

List everything you currently remember about me, grouped by topic. Mark anything that looks out of date, contradictory, or like something I would not want an always-on assistant to act on. Do not change anything; I will review and delete myself.

*The first message to a new dot:*

Your job is [one sentence]. The apps you can use are [list]. You may read anything in them. Ask me before you send, buy, book, or delete anything. Every [day/time], tell me in three lines what you noticed, what you did, and what is waiting on me. If something from one part of my life seems relevant to another, tell me you are making that connection rather than acting on it quietly.

*A divider task for the business:*

This task is only for [business]. Do not use anything from my personal or household tasks here unless I bring it in. Track [the two or three things that matter] and flag anything that changes.

*A divider task for the house:*

This task is only for the household: [calendars, school, bills]. Keep it separate from work. Remind me of anything due in the next seven days every Sunday evening.

*The weekly check on the drawer:*

Tell me the ten most important things you believe about me and my work right now, and where you learned each one. Mark any you are unsure of. I will correct you here.

*The question for the manager at the bar (if you have other agents):*

Here is what my other agents reported this week: [paste or point to the channel]. What is falling between them, what are two of them doing twice, and what would you change? Opinions only; do not act on them.
