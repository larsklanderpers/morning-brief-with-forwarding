## 🚨 Government Advisories

**CISA lists two more Ivanti Connect Secure flaws as exploited, and the fix for this month's first Ivanti bug does not cover them**
Source: CISA | [Link](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/10/30/cisa-adds-two-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)
On 30 October CISA added CVE-2026-111357 (missing authentication, CVSS 8.8) and CVE-2026-118527 (predictable filenames, CVSS 4.3) to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. Ivanti's advisory says exploitation in the wild has hit a limited number of customers when both are chained with CVE-2026-104935, the flaw CISA urged agencies to patch on 13 October. The fix for these two is 25.1.1.3 or 22.8R2.6, one release past the versions that closed the first bug. Neither Ivanti nor CISA has named an actor.
💡 Strategic take: A team that patched fast on 13 October and closed the ticket is still exposed, because the chain had two more links. And a medium-severity score sits on one of those links, which tells you what a score is worth once a flaw is part of a chain. On any edge device, treat "patched" as a state you recheck, and hunt for compromise as well, since the bug was live before the fix.

**UK watchdog says GCHQ cannot account for thousands of USB sticks and hard drives, some possibly holding intercept data**
Source: The Record | [Link](https://therecord.media/gchq-cannot-account-for-thousands-of-data-devices)
The Investigatory Powers Commissioner's annual report for 2025, published Thursday, says it is "very concerned" that GCHQ cannot establish the scale, location or recoverability of removable media it estimates at "in the thousands". GCHQ calls it a record-keeping flaw and says it has since audited its holdings; The Record says it is unclear whether that audit established how many items are lost. The report does not allege adversary access. It separately records five port stops of innocent people after a software application misled GCHQ's target discovery. The report itself was not read for this brief.
💡 Strategic take: If the agency that designs the UK's security guidance cannot count its thumb drives, the rest of us should stop being embarrassed and go and count ours. An asset register is the dullest control there is, and it is the one every other control assumes. The misidentified travellers matter just as much, because they show what happens downstream when a confident system is quietly wrong.

**DOJ's bankruptcy office asks for a privacy ombudsman before Character.AI's chat histories can be sold**
Source: The Record | [Link](https://therecord.media/doj-raises-privacy-concerns-over-character-ai-sale)
The Executive Office for US Trustees filed on Tuesday asking the court to appoint a consumer privacy ombudsman as Character.AI goes through bankruptcy. The filing, as reported, notes the 12 million monthly users, a chatbot telling users their conversations stay confidential, a privacy policy it calls "ambiguous", and a policy update on 1 July while the company was "actively seeking to sell its assets". Character.AI did not respond to The Record. The filing itself was not read for this brief.
💡 Strategic take: Every privacy policy has an exit clause, and it only gets read when the company fails. Intimate chat logs have just become a line on an insolvency schedule, the same road 23andMe's genetic data took. Before putting customer data into any AI vendor, read what the contract says happens to it in a sale, because the buyer of last resort is not the partner you did your due diligence on.

**ACSC alerts page unchanged, with the Fortinet notice of 23 October still the latest**
Source: Australian Cyber Security Centre | [Link](https://www.cyber.gov.au/about-us/view-all-content/alerts-and-advisories)
The page, checked this morning, shows "Critical vulnerabilities in Fortinet products" (23 October) as the most recent entry, then PHP (21 October) and "Protecting operational technology and edge devices" (14 October). Nothing material from ACSC in the last 24 hours, and nothing on the Ivanti additions.
💡 Strategic take: That 14 October alert names refrigeration and cooling technology, which looked niche until this week's reporting on US commissary freezers. Australian clubs and hospitality venues run exactly this kind of equipment, usually on a contractor's remote access. Check who can reach yours.

## 📰 Top Security News

**Linux kernel flaw from 4.4 onward gets a working remote root exploit, and the two severity scores are nowhere near each other**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/critical-linux-kernel-flaw-allows-remote-root-attacks-on-mptcp-servers/)
CVE-2026-98432 is a race-condition use-after-free in IPv4 TCP handling, rated 9.8 by the Linux kernel CVE team and 5.9 by Red Hat, which classes it as a potential denial of service. Anthropic says an unreleased Claude model wrote an exploit reaching root on a server with a Multipath TCP listener in about 53 minutes. Go 1.24 and later enable MPTCP by default on Linux, so some services may have it without anyone choosing it. Ubuntu fixes were still in progress and RHEL fixes planned as of 30 October; CIQ warns disabling MPTCP is not enough. No exploitation is confirmed. Anthropic sells the model family.
💡 Strategic take: A 5.9 tells you how hard the bug looked to a human; a retry loop that fails harmlessly until it works does not care. The Go default is the part to act on, because your developers may have turned this on without knowing, so the first job is an inventory of what is listening. Where patches are not out, use CIQ's firewall rules for packets with IP options rather than waiting for the distro.

**Palo Alto confirms PHP2Shell rooted its firewalls, while Wordfence has yet to see one WordPress site fall**
Source: SecurityWeek | [Link](https://www.securityweek.com/php2shell-attacks-root-palo-alto-firewalls-but-fail-against-wordpress/)
Moved since Friday's brief, when the evidence was Dream's alone. Palo Alto's Unit 42 says it independently verified the chain, has patched PAN-OS, and that about 0.02% of its installed base had an internet-facing management interface and was hit. SecurityWeek now describes Dream's 25 victims as firewalls out of a pool of hundreds, and says the attackers were AI-assisted. Palo Alto counts 22.6 million exposed PHP instances, potentially vulnerable. Defiant, which sells Wordfence, knew of no WordPress compromise as of 28 October despite 77,000 requests, because exploits need tuning per server.
💡 Strategic take: The gap between 22.6 million exposed and a handful confirmed is not comfort; it is the time a generic exploit takes to get tuned. Attackers skipped the long tail and went for appliances where the build is identical everywhere. An internet-facing management interface was the real entry requirement here, and that is a setting, so it costs nothing to fix before Monday.

**A failed attack on Alpine Linux shows upgrading GitLab does not fix the GitLab flaw if you run your own Ruby**
Source: SecurityWeek | [Link](https://www.securityweek.com/max-severity-gitlab-vulnerability-exploited-against-alpine-linux/)
Moved since Friday's KEV listing. Alpine Linux says an attacker hit its GitLab instance with a CVE-2026-97229 exploit on 20 and 21 October, and that its downloading of the container image suggests tuning to the exact build. It found no evidence of code execution, "but we cannot rule it out", rebuilt from scratch and reset all passwords. The bug lives in Ruby's regex engine, so Alpine stayed vulnerable after updating to GitLab 19.3.6; custom builds need Ruby 3.3.13, 3.4.12, 4.0.9 or later. Alpine says its packages and 21 October releases were not tampered with.
💡 Strategic take: Alpine is what a good incident looks like: it rebuilt on doubt and published the part that makes it look bad. The lesson for everyone else is that a vendor's version number describes the vendor's build, not yours. Anyone who self-packages a critical platform owns its dependency tree, and the patch notice will not come to you.

**WordPress core flaw draws 18,500 exploit attempts in 21 hours, before it even has a CVE number**
Source: SecurityWeek | [Link](https://www.securityweek.com/fresh-wordpress-vulnerability-in-attackers-crosshairs/)
WordPress 7.1.4, released 28 October with backports to 4.7, fixes an unauthenticated race condition in the core update process that can lead to code execution through predictable staging paths. Defiant says its first firewall match came nine minutes after deployment, and attackers are polling those paths to hit a seconds-long window. It reports no successful compromises, and rates attack complexity high. Defiant sells Wordfence.
💡 Strategic take: SecurityWeek says exploitation began within hours of the patch, so a release note is now an attack brief. The window here is small, but it opens during your own maintenance, which is the moment most teams stop watching. A vulnerability programme keyed on CVE feeds will not even see this one yet.

**Binance's co-CEO says it was not one engineer's permissions, and about $2.7 billion has been frozen**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/binance-links-63-billion-crypto-theft-to-compromised-devops-laptop/)
Moved since Friday's brief. Co-CEO Richard Teng said the attacker "did not simply use one engineer's permissions" and that it was "not a single 'one-click' action", and that no individual has been held responsible. Attackers reached a service container and submitted unauthorised withdrawals. TRM says stablecoin issuers and Arbitrum's Security Council froze about $2.7 billion, while the attacker raced them through market makers and Aave. Deposits and withdrawals are suspended. Attribution to TraderTraitor remains TRM's and Chainalysis's, not Binance's.
💡 Strategic take: Teng's sentence is the right thing to say about the engineer and an uncomfortable thing to say about the architecture. If the path from one laptop to billions needed several steps, then several controls did not fire. The freezes also show that recovery now depends on third parties willing to act within minutes, a relationship worth arranging before you need it.

**Florida says 4.9 million financial aid applicants had Social Security numbers exposed**
Source: The Record | [Link](https://therecord.media/florida-warns-5-million-affected-financial-aid-site-breach)
The Florida Department of Education said Thursday it learned on 19 October of a breach of the Florida Student Financial Aid website, exposing names, addresses, phone numbers, emails, dates of birth and Social Security numbers. The site is down and FLDOE says the incident is contained; it declined to discuss the attackers. The Record notes California's education department disclosed its own breach a week earlier, and that Florida's motor vehicle agency was breached last month.
💡 Strategic take: A student who filled in one form at seventeen is now in a dataset that will outlast the degree. State education systems collect adult-grade identity data on teenagers and retain it on an agency IT budget. Retention is the control here: the record you deleted cannot appear in the notification letter.

**Revolve tells the SEC hackers took saved card numbers, and Texas filings show at least 127,200 people affected**
Source: The Record | [Link](https://therecord.media/resolve-fashion-retailer-data-breach-payment-cards)
Fashion retailer Revolve (misspelled "Resolve" in The Record's headline) filed an 8-K on Thursday saying attackers used "automated means" to obtain names, contact details, card numbers and expiry dates for customers with saved payment information. It told Texas regulators 127,200 residents there were affected, plus 1,328 in Vermont, and has not given a total. Revolve says there was no operational disruption and it expects no material financial impact.
💡 Strategic take: "Automated means" reading saved cards out of customer accounts sounds a lot like the application doing what it was built to do. The state filings are worth noticing too: the national count is withheld, but the regulators' web pages tell you the floor. If your venue or loyalty platform stores cards, tokenise them, because a saved number is a liability you chose to keep.

**TeamViewer flaw reverses the usual direction: a malicious host can take over the support technician**
Source: SecurityWeek | [Link](https://www.securityweek.com/teamviewer-flaw-could-let-malicious-hosts-take-over-support-technicians-machines/)
CVE-2026-111585 (CVSS 8.8) is an integer overflow in how clients size the screen buffer from dimensions the remote host announces. Remedio says it achieved code execution on the viewer in a default configuration; TeamViewer says exploitation requires connecting to an attacker-controlled or compromised host. Version 15.83 fixes it on Windows, macOS, Linux and Android, and legacy versions are also patched. TeamViewer knows of no exploitation.
💡 Strategic take: Help desks connect to untrusted machines for a living, and their technicians usually hold the keys to everything else. A customer's compromised PC is now a way into your support team. Patch the viewers first, and keep technician workstations off the admin tier.

**Talos details KeyDrift, malware that lifts IPsec keys from inside Cisco SD-WAN controllers**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-exploit-cisco-sd-wan-flaw-to-deploy-custom-keydrift-malware/)
Moved since Thursday's Silent Train item. Cisco Talos says a new cluster, UAT-11473, exploited CVE-2026-20127 (patched in February) against space and satellite services, defence and aerospace technology, and policy research organisations. KeyDrift runs inside SD-WAN control-plane processes and hooks SSL_read to read control traffic after decryption, taking what Talos assesses to be AES-256 IPsec keys. Talos sees overlap with Volt Typhoon's Silent Train network and makes no definitive link.
💡 Strategic take: Steal the keys from the machine that issues them and encryption across the whole WAN stops being a protection. Patching in February does not undo an implant from January, so anyone exposed then needs a compromise assessment and a key rotation. Assume controllers are a target tier of their own.

**Trezor warns of a suspected cellular implant in a wallet sold through the same reseller as the Ledger case**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/trezor-warns-of-suspected-crypto-theft-implant-in-hardware-wallet/)
An Indonesian buyer of a Safe 5 from reseller CryptoBilis reported an extra circuit board with a cellular module and antenna, apparently able to read the recovery phrase from the screen. Trezor has not yet examined the device and calls the capabilities unconfirmed, but tells buyers since March 2026 to move funds. Ledger has confirmed its own implant case, and BleepingComputer cites a Singapore police advisory adding $3.5 million to reported losses. Trezor knows of no thefts from its devices.
💡 Strategic take: Tamper-resistant hardware is a promise made by the factory and kept by every pair of hands after it. A radio inside the case defeats the whole air-gap story at a cost of a few dollars of parts. For anything security-critical, including kiosks and terminals, buy direct and keep the chain of custody on paper.

**Sixteen-year-old UEFI flaw reaches most of the world's servers, but only those booting over the network**
Source: SecurityWeek | [Link](https://www.securityweek.com/in-other-news-anthropic-breach-dispute-uefi-flaw-hits-most-servers-cloudflare-ai-data-leak/)
SecurityWeek, summarising Ars Technica, says CVE-2026-97631 is a critical buffer overflow in TianoCore EDK II, code reaching most server vendors through AMI, Insyde and Phoenix. An attacker on the local network can hang a PXE boot or, knowing the firmware build, run code before Secure Boot takes effect. It was found by Anthropic and Google researchers and went public months before a planned January disclosure after AMI published a fix. Ars Technica's report itself was not read for this brief.
💡 Strategic take: Firmware patches arrive whenever your hardware vendor gets round to them, which makes this a procurement problem as much as an operations one. The practical move is to find out which estates use PXE and fence that network now. A bug that runs before Secure Boot is invisible to everything you bought to watch the machine.

**US officials reportedly suspect Iran in hacks that spoiled food at military commissaries**
Source: SecurityWeek | [Link](https://www.securityweek.com/in-other-news-anthropic-breach-dispute-uefi-flaw-hits-most-servers-cloudflare-ai-data-leak/)
Nextgov reports, citing people familiar with the matter, that officials suspect Iran-linked hackers caused freezer malfunctions at around six base commissaries from late August; at Fort Huachuca a freezer entered defrost mode and heated its contents. Cyber Command and Defense Department law enforcement units are investigating with French authorities, after refrigeration controls were overridden at some 350 French establishments this month. This is reported suspicion, not attribution, and Nextgov's report itself was not read for this brief.
💡 Strategic take: Nobody budgets for a freezer as a national security asset, which is rather the point. Low-cost harassment of physical supply lines costs an attacker almost nothing and costs the victim real stock, so it will spread. Anything with a thermostat and a vendor portal now belongs on the asset register.

## 🤖 AI & Security

**Trojanised Claude Code extension sat on Cursor's marketplace for 11 hours, and nobody has said how it got there**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/malicious-claude-code-extension-on-cursor-planted-ssh-backdoors/)
Version 2.1.313 was available from about 2:27 am to 1:35 pm PT on 29 October, exposing up to 5,000 developers to a loader that adds an SSH key and a reverse tunnel, and sends reconnaissance data including the names of credential environment variables. Phorion says coding errors likely stopped remote access on many Windows and macOS machines where SSH was not already running. Cursor pulled it, shut its marketplace to audit and rotate keys, and says Anthropic did not develop it. Early reports of npm, Open VSX and VS Code Marketplace impact were later ruled out. Check for an "svc-telemetry" SSH key.
💡 Strategic take: A coding agent runs with a developer's keys, so a fake one is a developer with a stranger's intentions. The attacker's own typos are the only reason this was not far worse, and that is no control to rely on. Pin extension versions and gate marketplace updates on developer machines the way you gate production dependencies, and rotate secrets if the key is present.

**A startup founded by ex-DOGE staff says its agents got inside Anthropic in under 12 hours, and Anthropic disputes the scope**
Source: SecurityWeek | [Link](https://www.securityweek.com/in-other-news-anthropic-breach-dispute-uefi-flaw-hits-most-servers-cloudflare-ai-data-leak/)
According to SecurityWeek's summary of a Washington Post report on a draft, Cathedral says its AI agents abused third-party business software to reach meeting transcripts, salary data and Slack discussions about advanced models. Anthropic says only limited sales, HR and IT systems were reached, access was cut the same day, and Claude conversations, source code and weights were unaffected. It accuses Cathedral of breaking its bounty rules. Cathedral sells security services and both sides have an interest in their version; the Post report was not read for this brief.
💡 Strategic take: Set aside who is right about scope. Even on the defender's version, agents went through the SaaS estate, which is where most firms keep their secrets and do the least monitoring. The Slack archive of a company is a better intelligence target than its source code, and very few security programmes treat it that way.

**Cloudflare says its Workers AI served 155 responses containing other customers' prompts**
Source: SecurityWeek | [Link](https://www.securityweek.com/in-other-news-anthropic-breach-dispute-uefi-flaw-hits-most-servers-cloudflare-ai-data-leak/)
Cloudflare says that between 27 August and 9 September, 155 GLM 5.3 responses contained data from other customers' recent requests to the same model, all on prompts over 681,280 tokens. A KV cache smaller than the advertised context window combined with a bug in the SGLang inference engine let over-length requests read leftover data. A Knostic researcher reported it through HackerOne. Cloudflare found no evidence of deliberate exploitation and recommends SGLang 0.5.22 or later. The figures are Cloudflare's own.
💡 Strategic take: For anyone holding customer PII, this is the argument for local models in one number: 155 small leaks from a shared cache nobody thought of as a data store. Hosted inference is a multi-tenant system, and a cache is a shared table with a nicer name. Ask vendors how inference state is isolated between tenants, and if they cannot say, keep regulated data in the building.

**Vendors report AI agents running ransomware lateral movement, on three fronts at once**
Source: SecurityWeek | [Link](https://www.securityweek.com/in-other-news-anthropic-breach-dispute-uefi-flaw-hits-most-servers-cloudflare-ai-data-leak/)
Sophos says the Gold Ogre group behind Aur0ra ransomware used agents orchestrated through Cursor for discovery, credential attacks and lateral movement, bypassing guardrails by framing intrusions as authorised red team work. Cisco Talos details NIK C2, a new AI-integrated command-and-control framework. Symantec and Carbon Black warn of a grey-market relay, seen at a dozen organisations mostly in mainland China, that routes genuine Codex and Claude Code CLIs through third-party servers with sandboxing off. All three sell security products.
💡 Strategic take: "This is an authorised pentest" is the oldest line in social engineering, and it now works on the model. The relay story is the sleeper: staff chasing cheaper tokens may be piping your code through a stranger's server. Check where your developers' AI traffic actually goes, because price-shopping is a shadow IT driver no policy memo will stop.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The thread today is the fix that turned out to be only part of the fix. Ivanti customers who patched on 13 October learned on Friday that the chain had two more links. Alpine Linux updated GitLab and stayed vulnerable, because the bug lived in the Ruby underneath. Disabling Multipath TCP does not close the Linux flaw, and the distributions cannot agree whether it scores 5.9 or 9.8. WordPress shipped a patch that attackers were working from within hours, before the bug had a CVE number to track. Each of these is a team doing the right thing and getting a false sense of completion from it, and AI shortens the distance between that false sense and an exploit that works. The question for a decision-maker this week: when your team says something is patched, what exactly did they check, and did anyone look for what got in before the patch?
