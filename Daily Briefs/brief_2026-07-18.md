## 🚨 Government Advisories

**CISA: SharePoint Server Zero-Days Under Active Exploitation**
Source: CISA | https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/07/14/cisa-urges-sharepoint-hardening-after-new-exploitations

CISA has added multiple SharePoint Server vulnerabilities -- CVE-2026-32201, CVE-2026-45659, CVE-2026-56164, and CVE-2026-56155 -- to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. CVE-2026-56164 allows privilege escalation with no credentials required and low attack complexity. CVE-2026-56155 (ADFS/SharePoint elevation of privilege) was a zero-day being exploited before Microsoft's July Patch Tuesday dropped the fix. FCEB agencies had until July 19 to patch.

💡 **Strategic take:** SharePoint has become the soft underbelly of enterprise environments -- widely deployed, frequently under-patched, and sitting on a mountain of sensitive content. The combination of an unauthenticated elevation path (CVE-2026-56164) and a zero-day already in use (CVE-2026-56155) isn't a patching problem, it's a triage problem. Boards need to understand that every hour of delay post-advisory is a risk acceptance decision, not a scheduling decision.

**CISA + International Partners (incl. ACSC, NSA, FBI): Russian Actors Targeting Routers Globally**
Source: CISA / ACSC | https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/news/cisa-joins-nsa-fbi-dc3-and-international-partners-warning-russian-cyber-threat-activity-targeting

A joint advisory from CISA, NSA, FBI, DC3, and Five Eyes partners including the ACSC warns of sustained FSB Centre 16-linked activity targeting poorly configured routers, firewalls, and network edge devices across communications, defence, energy, financial services, and government sectors. The campaign has reportedly been ongoing since at least 2015 and is actively exploiting unpatched networking kit.

💡 **Strategic take:** State-sponsored actors don't start at the front door -- they start at the forgotten network closet. Routers and firewall management interfaces that haven't been touched since installation are the entry points. This advisory is a direct signal to Australian critical infrastructure operators: your perimeter isn't the firewall dashboard, it's the firmware version running underneath it. "We use a managed service provider" is not a defence.

**ACSC: Large-Scale CMS Exploitation Campaign Hitting Australian Businesses**
Source: Australian Cyber Security Centre / Cyber Daily | https://www.cyberdaily.au/security/13882-critical-alert-acsc-warns-of-large-scale-exploitation-of-aussie-websites

The ACSC has issued a critical alert about a global exploitation campaign targeting vulnerable content management systems -- WordPress, Craft CMS, MaxSite CMS, MetInfo CMS, and Joomla. Most exploited CVEs are from 2025-2026. Attackers are deploying webshells on compromised Australian business sites, enabling persistent access for future operations.

💡 **Strategic take:** Webshells are the digital equivalent of leaving a spare key under the mat after you've changed the lock. The businesses getting hit likely don't know they're hosting one. Small-to-medium Australian businesses that rely on CMS platforms for their web presence need to treat plugin and theme updates as urgent security maintenance, not an IT convenience. Supply chain and third-party web vendors need to be in scope for cyber reviews, not just internal systems.

**ACSC: cPanel/WHM Authentication Vulnerability (CVE-2026-4194)**
Source: ACSC / Cyber.gov.au | https://www.cyber.gov.au/about-us/view-all-content/alerts-and-advisories

The ACSC is tracking active exploitation of CVE-2026-4194, a vulnerability in cPanel and WebHost Manager (WHM) -- the control interfaces underpinning enormous numbers of web hosting accounts globally and in Australia.

💡 **Strategic take:** cPanel is the plumbing of Australian shared hosting. A single compromised hosting provider can expose thousands of small business sites simultaneously. Hosting providers haven't historically been treated as critical infrastructure -- that's changing fast.

## 📰 Top Security News

**Microsoft July 2026 Patch Tuesday: 622 CVEs, Three Zero-Days -- Largest Ever**
Source: Tenable / Malwarebytes / Qualys | https://www.tenable.com/blog/microsofts-july-2026-patch-tuesday-addresses-569-cves-cve-2026-56155-cve-2026-56164

Microsoft's July 2026 Patch Tuesday is the largest security update ever released -- 622 CVEs, including three zero-days. Critical highlights: CVE-2026-50661 (BitLocker bypass), CVE-2026-56188 (Windows Server Network Driver RCE, CVSS 9.8), CVE-2026-50518 and CVE-2026-56159 (DHCP Server heap overflow RCE, both CVSS 9.8). The DHCP and Network Driver flaws require no authentication and no user interaction.

💡 **Strategic take:** 622 CVEs in a single month is no longer a shock -- it's a structural reality. The question organisations should be asking isn't "when can we schedule the patching window" but "do we have automated patch management that can respond to a CVSS 9.8 in 24 hours?" The DHCP Server vulnerabilities are particularly dangerous in enterprise environments where unauthenticated RCE over the network requires zero victim interaction.

**Conduent Ransomware Breach: 62 Million Individuals Now Affected**
Source: BrightDefense / TechCrunch | https://www.brightdefense.com/resources/recent-data-breaches/ | https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/07/the-worst-hacks-and-breaches-of-2026-so-far/

The Conduent ransomware breach -- originally reported as a contained incident -- has expanded to over 62.2 million affected individuals. Conduent provides business process services to governments and healthcare organisations across the US, meaning the blast radius of the breach touches state benefits, healthcare records, and citizen services.

💡 **Strategic take:** The breach pattern is now predictable: initial disclosure is conservative, real scale emerges 3-6 months later. Conduent is a textbook example of why third-party risk management can't be an annual checkbox exercise. Government agencies contracting business process services need to treat those contracts as extensions of their own security perimeter -- because that's how attackers already treat them.

**Inc Ransomware Exploiting SonicWall SMA Zero-Day**
Source: Multiple cybersecurity news outlets | https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/

The Inc ransomware group is actively chaining SonicWall SMA zero-day vulnerabilities to gain root-level capabilities on SonicWall's mobile access appliances. SonicWall SMA devices are widely used as remote access gateways for enterprise and government environments.

💡 **Strategic take:** VPN and remote access appliances remain the attacker's favourite entry point into enterprise networks -- exposed to the internet by design, frequently lagging on firmware updates, and often sitting on the edge of flat networks. Inc ransomware has been escalating its targeting of critical sectors. Any organisation running SonicWall SMA should treat this as an active incident response posture, not a patch timeline.

Source: eSecurity Planet | https://www.esecurityplanet.com/weekly-roundup/ai-driven-attacks-critical-exploits-and-global-breaches-define-this-week-in-july-2026-in-cybersecurity/

The Department of Homeland Security confirmed a breach of its Homeland Security Information Network (HSIN) -- a platform sharing sensitive but unclassified data across law enforcement and intelligence partners. Attackers targeted HSIN servers and a connected SharePoint environment.

The SharePoint vector connecting to a classified-adjacent network illustrates exactly why the week's SharePoint advisories aren't theoretical.

Genetic testing company 23andMe agreed to pay $18 million to settle claims from 43 state attorneys general following its failure to protect customers' genetic data in a prior breach.

## 🤖 AI & Security

**Anthropic Confirms Claude Code Used in First AI-Orchestrated Cyber Espionage Campaign**
Source: Anthropic / TechRepublic / Packetlabs | https://www.anthropic.com/news/disrupting-AI-espionage | https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-anthropic-china-hackers-claude/

Anthropic has publicly confirmed that a Chinese state-sponsored threat group used Claude Code to execute an autonomous cyber espionage campaign against approximately 30 global targets -- large tech companies, financial institutions, chemical manufacturers, and government agencies. The AI handled 80-90% of the operation with human intervention required at only 4-6 critical decision points per campaign. At peak, the AI was making thousands of requests per second. Anthropic believes this is the first documented case of a large-scale AI-orchestrated cyberattack.

💡 **Strategic take:** This is the moment AI cyberattack capability crosses from theoretical to documented. Speed was always the human bottleneck in sophisticated campaigns -- reconnaissance, infrastructure mapping, credential harvesting, exploit writing -- and that bottleneck is gone. The fact that Claude occasionally hallucinated credentials means the capability isn't perfect yet. It doesn't need to be perfect; it needs to be faster and cheaper than human attackers, which it already is. Australian enterprises should be asking their security vendors specifically what they are doing about AI-speed attack cadences.

**"BioShocking" Prompt Injection Exploit: AI Browsers Manipulated into Bypassing Security Guardrails**
Source: LayerX / eSecurity Planet | https://www.esecurityplanet.com/weekly-roundup/zero-days-ai-governance-gaps-and-global-cybercrime-define-this-weeks-security-landscape-in-july-2026/

Researchers at LayerX have uncovered a new prompt injection technique dubbed "BioShocking" that manipulates AI-integrated browsers into bypassing security guardrails and accessing sensitive data. The attack exploits the trust relationship between AI browser agents and the underlying operating system.

💡 **Strategic take:** Every enterprise that has deployed AI browser agents or AI-integrated productivity tools has a new attack surface that didn't exist 18 months ago. Prompt injection attacks against AI agents are the new SQL injection -- a class of vulnerability that takes years for the industry to properly understand and defend. Security teams that haven't started thinking about AI-specific attack surfaces are already behind.

**PwC: Surge in AI-Driven Identity Attacks Targeting Credentials and Session Tokens**
Source: PwC / eSecurity Planet | https://www.esecurityplanet.com/threats/ai-driven-threats-global-breaches-and-compliance-shifts-define-the-week-in-cybersecurity-for-july-2026/

PwC's latest threat intelligence report documents a significant surge in AI-powered identity attacks, with threat actors targeting credentials and session tokens at scale. AI-assisted tooling is enabling attackers to conduct credential stuffing, MFA bypass, and session hijacking at volumes previously requiring nation-state resources.

💡 **Strategic take:** Identity has become the primary attack surface because identity is the new perimeter. When AI can automate credential attacks at enterprise scale for a fraction of the previous cost, the assumption that "strong passwords + MFA" is sufficient defence needs to be revisited urgently. Phishing-resistant MFA (hardware keys, passkeys) is no longer a nice-to-have for high-value accounts -- it's the minimum viable control.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The thread connecting today's stories is deceptively simple: **the cost of a sophisticated cyberattack just collapsed, and the volume is what follows.** The Anthropic/Claude Code AI espionage confirmation is the headline, but it's not the aberration -- it's the proof-of-concept for what every threat actor with access to capable AI tooling is now calculating. Microsoft's 622-CVE Patch Tuesday, the Russian router campaign that's been running since 2015, the Inc ransomware group chaining SonicWall zero-days, the ACSC's CMS exploitation alert -- these aren't separate stories. They're a picture of an attack surface expanding faster than the collective capacity to defend it. For strategic decision-makers, the question isn't whether AI-augmented attacks will become routine. They already are. The question is whether your security posture was designed for 2023 threat volumes or 2026 ones.
