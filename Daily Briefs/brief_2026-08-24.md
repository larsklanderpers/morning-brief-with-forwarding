## 🚨 Government Advisories

### CISA KEV — CVE-2026-68820: Microsoft Windows AFD Use-After-Free (PATCH DEADLINE TODAY)
**Source:** CISA KEV Catalog | [Alert](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/08/11/cisa-adds-three-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)

A use-after-free vulnerability in the Windows Ancillary Function Driver for WinSock is confirmed under active exploitation. Check Point Research has linked activity to the Lazarus Group (North Korea). **CISA's patching deadline for federal agencies is August 25 — today.** This is the single most urgent item in today's brief.

💡 **Strategic take:** Lazarus Group using a kernel-level Windows flaw means this is almost certainly part of a financial or espionage campaign, not opportunistic noise. Any org that hasn't patched since August 11 should treat this as an active incident posture. The 24-hour CISA deadline is a loud signal that threat intelligence is concrete, not precautionary.

### CISA KEV — CVE-2026-65400: Apple macOS Screen Sharing Auth Bypass (CVSS 9.8)
**Source:** CISA KEV | [Alert](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/08/18/cisa-adds-four-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)

💡 **Strategic take:** Three enterprise-critical platforms in one KEV batch — macOS endpoints, SharePoint, and vCenter — is not coincidence. The ACSC is aware of active exploitation of two HIGH-rated (CVSS 8.2) authentication bypass vulnerabilities in N-able N-central, an RMM platform used widely by Australian MSPs. Patches were available from August 1; Hotfix 2 dropped August 6. The ACSC is specifically calling out MSPs and enterprise IT teams to patch as a priority.

This should be a board-level escalation for MSP operators today.

### Snowflake GitHub Actions Workflow Injection Vulnerability
**Source:** SecurityWeek | [CISA Advisories](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories)

A workflow injection vulnerability in Snowflake's `snowflake-connector-net` public repository could be exploited via a crafted GitHub issue to execute commands in the CI/CD pipeline. Disclosed August 17.

💡 **Strategic take:** This is a textbook supply chain risk hiding in plain sight — a public repo's GitHub Actions workflow accepting untrusted input from issue submissions. Any org consuming Snowflake connectors in their pipeline should audit their own dependency chain. More broadly, "malicious GitHub issue" is now a documented attack vector that deserves a line in your threat model.

## 🤖 AI & Security

### CrowdStrike 2026 Threat Hunting Report: AI Is Both Weapon and Target
**Source:** ASIS Online / CrowdStrike | [Report Summary](https://www.asisonline.org/security-management-magazine/latest-news/today-in-security/2026/august/crowdstrike-threat-hunting-report-2026/)

CrowdStrike's 2026 threat hunting report finds AI is simultaneously the most potent offensive tool and the most valuable defensive asset. Attack chains now operate at machine speed — phishing, malware delivery, and lateral movement combined with minimal human input. Deepfake social engineering and AI-driven adaptive malware are reaching operational maturity.

💡 **Strategic take:** "Machine speed" is the key phrase to pull out of this report. Human SOC analysts making decisions on a minutes-to-hours cycle are structurally outpaced when adversaries are iterating in seconds. The implication isn't "buy AI tools" — it's "re-examine every process that still requires a human in the approval loop for containment decisions."

## 📌 Today's Signal

The through-line in today's brief is **the collapse of the trust perimeter**. CVE-2026-68820 shows a nation-state burning a kernel zero-day against Windows infrastructure; the N-able N-central exploitation shows MSPs — the trust intermediaries for thousands of SMBs — being targeted as a force multiplier; the Snowflake GitHub Actions flaw shows CI/CD pipelines accepting malicious input through social channels; and the WEF data shows AI deepfakes eroding the integrity of legal and institutional documents. These aren't separate stories. They're the same story about attackers systematically targeting the mechanisms organisations use to trust each other: patch authorities, IT management platforms, software supply chains, and the document layer. The strategic question for decision-makers is no longer "how do we protect our perimeter?" but "what are the trust relationships we depend on, and how do we verify each one under adversarial conditions?"
