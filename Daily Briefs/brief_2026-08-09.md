## 🚨 Government Advisories

### CISA KEV: Progress Kemp LoadMaster CVE-2026-8037 (CVSS 9.6)
**Source:** CISA | [Advisory](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/08/08/cisa-adds-known-exploited-vulnerability-catalog) | [THN Coverage](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/progress-kemp-loadmaster-flaw-hits-cisa.html)

CISA added CVE-2026-8037 to its KEV catalog on August 8 following 792 reported exploitation attempts. The command injection flaw in Progress Kemp LoadMaster allows unauthenticated attackers to execute arbitrary commands on load balancer appliances via unsanitised input. watchTowr Labs traced the flaw to a function named `escape_quotes()`.

💡 **Strategic take:** Load balancers sit in front of everything — they're trusted, high-throughput, and rarely patched on the same cycle as endpoints. An unauthenticated RCE on a load balancer is a network chokepoint compromise. Any org running LoadMaster should treat this as P0 patching, not routine vulnerability management.

### CISA KEV: N-able N-central CVE-2026-18577 + Hotfix 2 Required
**Source:** CISA | [Alert (Aug 3)](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/08/03/cisa-adds-one-known-exploited-vulnerability-catalog) | [THN Coverage](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/n-central-attackers-reach-managed.html)

N-able confirmed unusual activity in a customer environment on July 31, leading to discovery of a then-zero-day in N-central (CVE-2026-18577, CVSS 8.2). Attackers have now reached managed endpoints and achieved persistence — N-able has issued a mandatory Hotfix 2 that supersedes the earlier patch. CISA also added three more KEVs on August 4: CVE-2026-9198 (IBM Langflow code injection), CVE-2026-18556 (N-central auth bypass variant), and CVE-2026-34486 (Apache Tomcat missing encryption).

💡 **Strategic take:** RMM platforms are the master key to every client environment an MSP manages. When one is compromised and attackers reach managed systems and establish persistence before the vendor issues a patch, you've handed them the entire supply chain. Every MSP running N-central should verify Hotfix 2 is applied — not Hotfix 1 — and audit managed endpoint activity going back to July 31.

## 📰 Top Security News

### Metabase Zero-Day (CVSS 10.0) Exploited in Wild — No CVE Yet
**Source:** The Hacker News | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/metabase-zero-day-exploited-in-wild.html)

A maximum-severity zero-day in Metabase (versions 1.58+) is being actively exploited. An unauthenticated attacker can inject arbitrary SQL into the Metabase application database, gaining administrator access, changing configuration, stealing database credentials, and exfiltrating all accessible data. Metabase Cloud instances have been patched; self-hosted deployments require manual update.

💡 **Strategic take:** Metabase is the BI tool that sits on top of production databases in thousands of data-driven organisations — it's connected to the crown jewels by design. A CVSS 10.0 unauthenticated SQLi-to-admin path on a tool like that isn't just a product vulnerability; it's a data governance failure waiting to surface. Self-hosted deployments are the exposure, and "self-hosted" is exactly what you get when a business unit spins up analytics without central IT involvement.

### CSS Attacks Break Webmail Defenses Across Outlook, Gmail, Yahoo, Proton (Black Hat 2026)
**Source:** The Hacker News | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/new-css-attacks-can-break-webmail.html)

PortSwigger researcher Gareth Heyes presented research at Black Hat USA 2026 demonstrating email content escaping message boundaries to interfere with webmail UI. Proven attack chains cover Outlook/Firefox (spoofs Microsoft sign-in, captures password), Yahoo/AOL (exposes Medium login token), and Gmail/Cowork (exfiltrates Slack token via prompt injection). Public PoCs remain available as of August 8.

💡 **Strategic take:** Email is the identity vector most organisations have been defending for 30 years, and it's still not solved. The interesting escalation here is the chaining: email → webmail UI manipulation → credential theft → lateral movement into SaaS. Defenders focused on email gateways are looking at the wrong layer — the webmail rendering engine is now part of the attack surface.

### Atlassian Rovo Tricked Into Exfiltrating Jira and Confluence Data
**Source:** The Hacker News | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/atlassian-rovo-can-be-tricked-into.html)

Two security firms (PromptArmor and Varonis Threat Labs) independently found that attacker-controlled instructions could make Rovo — Atlassian's AI assistant — collect Jira and Confluence data accessible to a signed-in user and send it to an external server. PromptArmor's route used an uploaded file; Varonis used a malicious URL parameter (rovoChatPrompt). Only one bypass is confirmed closed as of August 8.

💡 **Strategic take:** AI assistants in enterprise tools inherit the access rights of whoever is using them — meaning a single prompt injection attack on Rovo can silently exfiltrate whatever that user can read across your entire Jira and Confluence instance. That's project plans, security assessments, customer data, and M&A documentation. The attack surface here scales with your AI adoption rate, not with the traditional threat surface.

### UNC6671 Vishing Campaign Targets Personal Phones, Drains SaaS Environments
**Source:** The Hacker News | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/unc6671-vishing-attacks-target-personal.html)

Google Threat Intelligence Group and Mandiant attributed a wave of attacks against financial services, private equity, and professional services to UNC6671. The group calls employees on their personal mobile devices posing as IT helpdesk, then directs them to spoofed portals that intercept credentials and MFA tokens. Automated scripts then exfiltrate data from M365 and Okta.

💡 **Strategic take:** Calling employees on personal phones is a deliberate bypass of corporate security tooling — those calls don't route through corporate networks, don't trigger DLP, and aren't logged. The MFA interception is now table-stakes for sophisticated threat actors; the differentiator here is the out-of-band channel that organisations have almost no visibility into. PE and financial services should consider this a current, active threat.

### ~800 Malicious npm Packages Deliver Cross-Platform RAT + Infostealer
**Source:** The Hacker News | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/nearly-800-malicious-npm-packages.html)

A cluster of ~800 packages using AI-generated typosquatted names has been published to the npm registry, delivering WEL1DROPPER — a downloader that identifies the host OS/architecture and fetches a compatible RAT and infostealer payload. Unusually, the packages use a README instructing developers to load them with `require()` rather than lifecycle hooks.

💡 **Strategic take:** AI-generated typosquatting at this scale — 800 packages — represents a qualitative shift in supply chain attack economics. The effort required to create convincing-sounding fake package names has collapsed to near-zero. Any organisation without Software Composition Analysis (SCA) tooling in CI/CD is flying blind through a minefield that is now much denser than it was 12 months ago.

### ClickFix Evolves: macOS Variant Drains Crypto Wallets
**Source:** The Hacker News | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/clickfix-attacks-deliver-macos-stealer.html) | ACSC Alert

A Go-based macOS stealer delivered via ClickFix-style social engineering can drain cryptocurrency accounts, steal browser-stored passwords, and harvest Apple iCloud Keychain data. The campaign also aligns with the ACSC alert tracking ClickFix attacks via compromised WordPress sites targeting Australian networks.

💡 **Strategic take:** ClickFix works because it exploits a gap in security culture: users have been trained to distrust email links but not terminal commands they pasted themselves. The psychological trick — convincing someone they've already taken the unsafe action and must paste this command to fix it — consistently bypasses both technical controls and security awareness training.

### Microsoft 365 AitM Phishing Targeting Payroll and Finance Workflows
**Source:** The Hacker News | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/microsoft-365-aitm-phishing-hijacks.html)

An active AitM campaign (linked to Storm-2755 / "Payroll Pirates") hijacks M365 accounts, identifies employees involved in financial workflows, and collects payroll-related email. Residential proxies disguise sign-ins as consumer traffic; automated scripts maintain compromised sessions on ~8-hour intervals. Targets span healthcare, education, manufacturing, government, and professional services.

💡 **Strategic take:** "Payroll Pirates" is the right framing — this is financially motivated, operationally patient, and specifically hunting for the people who process wire transfers and payroll runs. The 8-hour session maintenance means a single MFA capture is converted into sustained, persistent access. Conditional access policies that don't enforce device compliance or re-authentication windows are not providing the protection organisations assume.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The dominant pattern across this week's threat landscape is the systematic exploitation of trust — and specifically the trust that enterprise software places in authenticated users and the AI systems acting on their behalf. Atlassian Rovo exfiltrates data because it trusts the content it reads. Ruflo's MCP bridge executes commands because it trusts callers on its exposed endpoint. UNC6671 harvests MFA tokens because enterprise portals trust the authentication flow even when it's being intercepted. N-able N-central was trusted with god-mode access to managed endpoints, which made its compromise a supply chain event rather than a single-victim incident. The throughline is that modern enterprise infrastructure is built on layered trust relationships — and attackers are now systematically walking those trust chains rather than breaking through them. Perimeter security, endpoint detection, and even MFA are all being routed around, not defeated. For strategic decision-makers, the question is no longer whether your controls are technically sound in isolation, but whether the trust architecture connecting your systems, your AI tools, and your managed providers has ever been modelled as an attack surface.
