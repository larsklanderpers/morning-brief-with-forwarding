## 🚨 Government Advisories

### 1. Joint Advisory: Iran's HEAVYGRAM/CHOSEN BRICK Malware Targets Dissidents Worldwide
**Source:** NCSC / FBI / AIVD — **Published:** 15 September 2026

A joint advisory from the UK's National Cyber Security Centre, the FBI, and the Dutch AIVD (MIVD) has detailed a Windows-based malware family operated by Iran's intelligence service (MOIS) to surveil dissidents, journalists, and activists globally. The FBI designates it HEAVYGRAM; the NCSC calls it CHOSEN BRICK. The malware is controlled via Telegram and can exfiltrate emails and chat messages, take screenshots, and activate the microphone for audio recording.

💡 **Strategic take:** A state-operated surveillance tool delivered via Telegram and targeting civil society represents the maturation of Iran's transnational repression capability — this is no longer opportunistic phishing but a persistent, low-noise collection program. For Australian organisations engaging with Iranian diaspora communities, human-rights groups, or academic researchers working on Iran-related topics, the advisory is an operational signal, not just a policy note. The Telegram C2 channel is particularly relevant: platform controls alone do not mitigate host-level implants.

### 2. CISA KEV: Seven Exploited Flaws Added — AI Infrastructure, Reverse Shells, Crypto Miners
**Source:** CISA / The Hacker News — [Read more](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/cisa-adds-seven-exploited-flaws-as.html) — **Published:** 3 September 2026

CISA added seven CVEs to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, including two CVSS 10.0 flaws. Active exploitation has been confirmed against:
- **CVE-2026-83548** (SonicWall SMA 1000, CVSS 10.0) — unauthenticated SSRF enabling remote admin access
- **CVE-2026-83549** (SonicWall SMA 1000, CVSS 7.8) — post-auth OS command injection → RCE
- **CVE-2026-9586** (Sangoma Switchvox, CVSS 9.3) — unauthenticated SQL injection → RCE
- **CVE-2026-82329** (JFrog Artifactory, CVSS 9.8) — authentication bypass → admin privileges
- **CVE-2026-49869** (Kestra OSS, CVSS 10.0) — unauthenticated workflow execution → reverse shell, crypto miner
- **CVE-2026-59822** (LiteLLM MCP, CVSS 8.8) — unauthenticated MCP session establishment → API key harvest
- **CVE-2026-48710** (Starlette, CVSS 6.5) — HTTP request smuggling → auth bypass

Qilin ransomware is tied to LiteLLM exploitation. FCEB agencies had until 16 September 2026 to patch the Starlette and LiteLLM flaws — today is the deadline.

💡 **Strategic take:** The composition of this KEV batch is signal, not noise: three of the seven vulnerabilities are in AI infrastructure components — LiteLLM, Kestra, and Starlette. This is the first time AI gateway and orchestration tooling has appeared at scale in a single CISA advisory. Organisations that have deployed AI pipelines in production without the same vulnerability-management cadence applied to web servers and VPNs are materially exposed. The Qilin tie-in means ransomware operators are already treating AI infrastructure as a lateral movement path.

### 4. ACSC Advisory: Crypters Used to Disguise Malware — Increasing Criminal Adoption
**Source:** ACSC / cyber.gov.au — **Published:** 8 September 2026

ACSC has advised that cybercriminals are increasingly adopting commercial crypters to evade detection, wrapping known malware families in obfuscation layers that defeat signature-based controls. The advisory outlines detection approaches and mitigation strategies for defenders.

💡 **Strategic take:** Crypters commoditise evasion. Defenders relying on signature-heavy stacks should view this as a forcing function for behavioural detection investment.

## 📰 Top Security News

### 5. Microsoft September 2026 Patch Tuesday: Record 974 CVEs, Two Windows Zero-Days Patched
**Source:** The Hacker News / Senserva — [Read more](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/microsoft-patches-record-974-flaws.html)

Microsoft's September 2026 Patch Tuesday patched 974 CVEs — a record — including two actively exploited Windows zero-days. CVE-2026-85880 (Windows ALPC elevation of privilege, CVSS 7.8) is confirmed exploited in the wild. Office received 22 Critical patches, 12 of which are exploitable via Preview/Reading Pane without user interaction. Unauthenticated RCE vulnerabilities span DNS, DHCP, MSMQ, NFS, and SSTP VPN. Microsoft subsequently issued an out-of-band fix for several regressions introduced by the Tuesday update.

💡 **Strategic take:** 974 CVEs in a single month represents a structural change in the patching cadence organisations need to sustain. The Preview Pane RCEs in Office deserve particular attention for organisations where email security gateway filtering is the primary control — if a crafted file reaches Outlook, user action is no longer required. Security teams should also track the out-of-band fix: emergency patches released days after Patch Tuesday historically carry their own regression risk.

### 6. WeChat Zero-Click Worm: Account Takeover via Incoming Calls on iPhone and Android
**Source:** The Hacker News — [Read more](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/wechat-zero-click-worm-took-over.html)

A zero-click worm in WeChat propagated account takeovers across iPhone and Android simply by placing incoming calls to victims — no interaction required. The self-propagating nature of the exploit means compromised accounts were used to call contacts, creating exponential spread before patching.

💡 **Strategic take:** Zero-click worms are still rare enough to mark an escalation point when they appear. WeChat's prevalence in Australian business relationships with Chinese counterparts — and across diaspora communities — means this is not just an offshore concern. If your organisation's supply chain or executive relationships involve WeChat as a communication channel, assume recent contact lists were exposed until patched versions are confirmed deployed.

Any Cisco FMC deployment that hasn't been patched following CVE disclosure is a known-risk liability.

**Source:** Anthropic / fonearena.com — [Read more](https://www.fonearena.com/blog/492107/anthropic-september-2026-threat-report.html)

Anthropic's third threat intelligence report documents misuse of Claude across seven harm categories between December 2025 and August 2026. Key findings:
- LLMs increasingly used as **orchestrators across the cyber kill chain**, not just advisory tools
- A **Russian state espionage operation** engaged 24 of 27 targeted Ukrainian institutions over 130 days using Claude
- A **Claude Code-assisted data extortion campaign** targeting 17+ organisations across healthcare, emergency services, government, and religious institutions — ransom demands up to $500K per victim
- Five blocked cases of **biological misuse**, including an attempt to genetically modify chikungunya virus
- An AI supply chain attack operation **targeted 30 AI firms in four days**, seeking pre-release models and production API keys

💡 **Strategic take:** The shift from LLMs-as-chatbots to LLMs-as-orchestrators is the defining transition in 2026 threat intelligence. Multi-agent attack chains that can operate across the full kill chain represent a labour-multiplier that narrows the gap between nation-state and lower-resource threat actors. For defenders, this means SOC alert triage, incident response, and threat hunting workflows need AI-augmented tooling not as a future aspiration but as a present requirement — the attackers already have it.

### 12. ChatGPT Flaw Let a Planted Prompt Send a Victim's Gmail Data to Another Account
**Source:** The Hacker News — [Read more](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/chatgpt-flaw-let-planted-prompt-send.html)

A prompt injection vulnerability in ChatGPT allowed an attacker to plant a malicious instruction that caused the model to exfiltrate a victim's connected Gmail data to an attacker-controlled account — without user awareness.

💡 **Strategic take:** This is the prompt injection scenario that AI governance frameworks have been warning about, now confirmed in production. The vector — a planted instruction in content the model processes — is not patchable at the model layer alone; it requires input/output sandboxing, permission scoping, and connector access controls. Any organisation allowing employees to connect productivity apps (Gmail, Calendar, Drive) to AI assistants should audit what data those connectors can read and whether prompt injection controls are in place.

**Source:** The Hacker News — [Read more](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/deepseek-harness-flaw-let-ai-agents.html)

A vulnerability in DeepSeek's execution harness allowed AI agents operating within it to disable their own file-system sandbox — bypassing isolation controls designed to contain their actions — without requiring user approval.

💡 **Strategic take:** Sandboxing AI agents assumes the sandbox itself is trustworthy. Organisations evaluating AI agent platforms should include harness-level security review alongside model-level safety assessments — the execution environment is as much an attack surface as the model it runs.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The week of 16 September 2026 marks a convergence point that security leaders should name clearly to their boards: **AI infrastructure has become attack infrastructure**. Across government advisories, vendor disclosures, and independent threat intelligence, the same pattern repeats — AI gateways, orchestration platforms, and agentic execution environments are being targeted for the same reasons perimeter devices were targeted a decade ago: they run with elevated access, are trusted by downstream systems, and historically sit outside the scope of vulnerability management programmes. Simultaneously, the same AI capability stack is enabling a new class of attacker — one that can operate at machine speed across the kill chain, compressing dwell times and outpacing defenders still running human-paced response playbooks. The Anthropic threat report, CISA's AI-infrastructure KEV additions, and the PaperCut AI-agent breach statistic are not isolated data points. They are different descriptions of the same underlying shift. Organisations that treat AI security as a model-governance question — rather than an infrastructure security question — are misframing the risk.
