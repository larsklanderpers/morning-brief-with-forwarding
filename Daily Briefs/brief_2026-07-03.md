## 🚨 Government Advisories

**1. CISA KEV: Microsoft SharePoint RCE CVE-2026-45659 — Patch by July 4**
Source: CISA / The Hacker News | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/sharepoint-rce-cve-2026-45659-added-to.html) | [KEV Catalog](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)
CISA added CVE-2026-45659 (CVSS 8.8) to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog following confirmed active exploitation. The flaw is a deserialization-of-untrusted-data bug in SharePoint Server (all editions) allowing authenticated attackers with only Site Member privileges to achieve remote code execution. Microsoft patched it in May 2026; federal agencies must comply by July 4.
💡 **Strategic take:** SharePoint sits at the centre of enterprise collaboration — document libraries, intranets, Teams integrations. An authenticated RCE here is a lateral-movement jackpot, requiring nothing beyond a standard employee account. Any organisation still running unpatched SharePoint after Microsoft released the fix six weeks ago is carrying unacceptable board-level risk. Patch today; don't wait for the long weekend.

**2. CISA KEV: SimpleHelp RMM Auth Bypass CVE-2026-48558 — Deadline Passed July 2**
Source: CISA / Arctic Wolf | [Article](https://arcticwolf.com/resources/blog/cve-2026-48558-critical-authentication-bypass-vulnerability-in-simplehelp-rmm-exploited-for-credential-theft-and-malware-delivery/)
A critical authentication bypass in SimpleHelp RMM (improper OIDC token signature validation) lets unauthenticated attackers forge identity tokens, bypass MFA, and gain technician-level access. Approximately 14,000 SimpleHelp servers are externally exposed; ~1,000 are directly vulnerable. The CISA remediation deadline was July 2 — yesterday.
💡 **Strategic take:** RMM tools are skeleton keys: whoever controls them controls every managed endpoint. The fact that ~1,000 servers sit exposed and patchable tells you MSP patch hygiene is still a sector-wide blind spot. If you use SimpleHelp — or a vendor who does — treat this as a supply-chain risk question, not just a software update.

Start mapping your critical-infrastructure obligations now.

## 📰 Top Security News

**1. Google Disrupts NetNut Residential Proxy Network Spanning 2 Million Devices**
Source: The Hacker News | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/google-disrupts-netnut-residential.html)
Working with the FBI and Lumen, Google's Threat Intelligence Group (GTIG) has significantly degraded NetNut (also tracked as Popa) — a residential proxy network that rented access to over 2 million compromised home devices including smart TVs, streaming boxes, and home routers. The network let paying customers route their traffic through real home IP addresses, masking their origin from security tools.
💡 **Strategic take:** NetNut's business model — monetising compromised consumer devices as anonymisation infrastructure — represents a structural problem no single takedown solves. The demand from nation-state actors and cybercriminals for residential proxies is constant; another network will fill the gap. The deeper issue is that consumer IoT security remains the internet's weakest link, and it's being systematically weaponised.

**2. Anubis Ransomware Exploits Citrix Bleed 2 (CVE-2025-5777) + BYOVD**
Source: The Hacker News / Arctic Wolf | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/ransomware-groups-turn-to-citrix-bleed.html)
Anubis ransomware affiliates (a RaaS rebrand of Sphinx) are exploiting Citrix Bleed 2 for initial access, then using bring-your-own-vulnerable-driver (BYOVD) techniques and supply-chain credential theft to move laterally. Legitimate RMM tools — ScreenConnect, Zoho Assist, MeshAgent, Remotely, UltraVNC — are being abused to blend in with normal IT activity.
💡 **Strategic take:** The abuse of legitimate remote access tools is now table stakes for sophisticated ransomware operators. If your EDR and SIEM aren't baselining which RMM tools are authorised in your environment and alerting on anomalous usage, attackers can operate inside your network using tools your helpdesk uses every day. "Trusted" is no longer synonymous with "safe."

**3. FortiBleed Credential Theft Now Linked to INC and Lynx Ransomware Operations**
Source: The Hacker News / SOCRadar | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/fortibleed-credential-theft-linked-to.html)
The FortiBleed campaign — which last month was confirmed to have harvested credentials from 86,644 FortiGate devices — has now been directly tied to INC and Lynx ransomware deployments. SOCRadar tracked scanning against ~11,250 FortiGate portals across 150+ countries, with confirmed admin-level access on 409 targets and at least 12 ransomware deployments resulting from stolen credentials.
💡 **Strategic take:** FortiBleed is a case study in how credential harvesting becomes ransomware deployment at industrial scale. The weeks-long gap between credential theft and ransomware activation means organisations may already hold compromised credentials without any indicator of active intrusion. If you run FortiGate and haven't rotated admin credentials since May, assume breach and act accordingly.

**4. ToddyCat's Umbrij Malware Abuses OAuth to Silently Read Corporate Gmail**
Source: The Hacker News / Kaspersky | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/toddycat-linked-umbrij-malware-abuses.html)
The Chinese APT ToddyCat has deployed a new malware called Umbrij that uses a technique called Shadow Token via Remote Debug (STRD) to steal OAuth tokens through browser remote debugging ports, then uses those tokens to silently access corporate Gmail via the Google API — no password, no MFA prompt, no visible login event.
💡 **Strategic take:** OAuth token theft is the authentication bypass of 2026. Traditional security controls — password policies, MFA, login alerting — are blind to an attacker holding a valid access token. Organisations relying on Google Workspace for sensitive communications need to be auditing OAuth application grants and monitoring API access patterns, not just authentication logs.

**5. ChocoPoC RAT Targets Vulnerability Researchers via Fake GitHub PoC Repos**
Source: The Hacker News / YesWeHack + Sekoia | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/new-chocopoc-rat-targets-vulnerability.html)
Attackers are embedding a data-stealing trojan (ChocoPoC) inside fake proof-of-concept exploit code on GitHub. The malware hides in a dependency package — not the visible PoC code — allowing it to pass quick code reviews. When executed, it steals saved passwords, browser cookies, and files, and hands attackers a shell. The campaign and its infrastructure were still live as of July 1.
💡 **Strategic take:** Attacking the security researchers and red teamers who are hardening your defences is a high-leverage move. If your security team runs community PoC code in anything other than fully isolated environments, this campaign is a direct threat to your most privileged internal users. Sandbox discipline for PoC testing is non-negotiable.

**6. Unpatched Argo CD Flaw Enables Kubernetes Cluster Takeover**
Source: The Hacker News / Synacktiv | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/unpatched-argo-cd-repo-server-flaw.html)
Synacktiv disclosed an unpatched, unauthenticated RCE vulnerability in Argo CD's repo-server component that can lead to full Kubernetes cluster takeover. The flaw has no CVE and no patch — Synacktiv reported it to maintainers in January 2025, roughly 18 months ago, with no fix released. The attack requires network access to the internal gRPC port.
💡 **Strategic take:** An 18-month-old unpatched critical vulnerability in one of the most widely used Kubernetes deployment tools is a supply-chain risk that goes well beyond the organisations who know they're using Argo CD. Every DevSecOps pipeline that touches Argo CD is exposed. The broader lesson: open-source project patch timelines are opaque, and organisations can't assume CVE absence means safety.

## 🤖 AI & Security

**1. AI Agent Runs First Fully Autonomous Ransomware Attack (JADEPUFFER / Langflow)**
Source: The Hacker News / Sysdig | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/ai-agent-exploits-langflow-rce-to.html)
Sysdig's Threat Research Team documented what it believes is the first ransomware attack executed end-to-end by an AI agent, attributed to a threat actor called JADEPUFFER. A large language model autonomously handled initial access (via CVE-2025-3248, a missing-auth flaw in Langflow), credential theft, lateral movement, and database encryption/wiping — with no human in the loop.
💡 **Strategic take:** The "human in the loop" assumption has just been disproven in a production attack. JADEPUFFER isn't about AI making attacks faster — it's about AI eliminating the skilled labour requirement. If a rented LLM can chain initial access through to data destruction without human direction, the cost and skill floor for damaging attacks just collapsed. Every organisation's incident response plan needs to account for AI-speed attack progression.

**2. Identity Lifecycle Management Wasn't Built for AI Agents**
Source: The Hacker News | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/identity-lifecycle-management.html)
A detailed analysis highlights the structural gap in enterprise identity governance: IGA tools are designed around humans with employment records, managers, and departure dates. AI agents have none of those attributes, yet they're being provisioned with credentials, API keys, and access rights. The result is a blind spot in joiner/mover/leaver processes that existing tooling cannot detect.
💡 **Strategic take:** Every AI agent you've deployed into your enterprise has an identity that's likely ungoverned. No HR system triggered its provisioning. No offboarding process will revoke its access when the project ends. CISOs who haven't started mapping AI agent identities in their IAM systems are building technical debt that will crystallise as a breach.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The overarching theme today is the collapse of trusted channels as a defensive perimeter. JADEPUFFER's AI-automated ransomware proves attacks no longer need skilled human operators. Umbrij steals email access without touching a password or triggering an MFA prompt. Anubis blends into enterprise environments using the same RMM tools your helpdesk runs. ChocoPoC targets the security researchers hardening your systems. And AI agent identities are proliferating through enterprise environments with no governance model to track or revoke them. What connects all of this: attackers have learned that the fastest path through enterprise defences is not to break them — it's to impersonate what's already trusted. The strategic priority for decision-makers right now is not more detection capability; it's a rigorous audit of what your environment already trusts, and why.
