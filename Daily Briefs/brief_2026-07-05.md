## 🚨 Government Advisories

**CISA adds SharePoint RCE (CVE-2026-45659) to KEV after active exploitation**
Source: The Hacker News — https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/sharepoint-rce-cve-2026-45659-added-to.html
A remote code execution flaw in Microsoft SharePoint Server (CVSS 8.8), rooted in deserialization of untrusted data, was added to CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog with a federal patch deadline of July 4. Microsoft quietly patched it in the May cycle but never disclosed it; CISA has now confirmed in-the-wild exploitation.
💡 Strategic take: A patch that ships without a CVE is a patch most defenders never prioritize. The gap between "fixed in code" and "known to the people running the servers" is where adversaries live, and silent fixes quietly transfer risk from the vendor's changelog onto your incident report. Ask your teams how they'd even know a SharePoint box was patched for something Microsoft never announced.

**SimpleHelp RMM (CVE-2026-48558) KEV deadline hits — CVSS 10.0, MSP supply chain**
Source: Threat-Modeling.com Vulnerability Intelligence — https://threat-modeling.com/vulnerability-intelligence-report-july-2-2026/
A maximum-severity flaw in the SimpleHelp remote monitoring and management tool reached its CISA remediation deadline, with a TaskWeaver loader observed being deployed through compromised MSP tooling.
💡 Strategic take: RMM platforms are the skeleton key of the managed-services economy — one compromised tool reaches every downstream client at once. When your supply chain is someone else's admin console, your risk register has to include vendors you've never heard of. Third-party RMM exposure belongs on the board risk map, not just the patch queue.

**ACSC maintains active advisories on Fortinet, WatchGuard Firebox (CVE-2025-14733), and ClickFix campaigns**
Source: Australian Cyber Security Centre — https://www.cyber.gov.au/about-us/view-all-content/alerts-and-advisories
Australia's ACSC continues to flag active exploitation against edge devices — Fortinet firewalls, WatchGuard Firebox appliances — alongside ClickFix social-engineering lures delivered through compromised WordPress sites.
💡 Strategic take: The perimeter appliance you bought to keep attackers out is increasingly the thing they exploit to get in. Edge devices sit outside your EDR's line of sight and rarely get the patch urgency of a workstation, which is exactly why they keep showing up on national-CERT advisories. Treat your firewall's firmware like production code.

## 📰 Top Security News

**DHS confirms hackers breached HSIN intelligence-sharing platform — via SharePoint**
Source: BleepingComputer — https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/dhs-confirms-hackers-breached-hsin-info-sharing-platform/
An unknown actor accessed the Homeland Security Information Network — the unclassified backbone federal, state, local and private partners use to coordinate — sometime between late May and early June. The intrusion hit HSIN servers and a SharePoint system used for inter-agency collaboration. Attribution is still unknown, and the timing coincides with the US hosting 2026 FIFA World Cup matches.
💡 Strategic take: "Unclassified" is not the same as "low-impact" — the aggregation of sensitive coordination data is itself the crown jewel, and the SharePoint vector ties this directly to the CVE above. A breach of the platform everyone uses to share threat intel during a global sporting event is a coordination-layer failure, and those cascade. Watch whether attribution lands on a nation-state; it changes the entire risk calculus.

**Microsoft 365 password-spraying campaign generates 81M+ login attempts in two weeks**
Source: The Hacker News — https://thehackernews.com/
An aggressive, sustained password-spray operation hammered M365 tenants with more than 81 million login attempts over a fortnight.
💡 Strategic take: Volume at this scale isn't about brute-forcing one account — it's a census of your weakest credentials and your detection thresholds. Any tenant still leaning on password-only auth is being enumerated in real time. Conditional access and phishing-resistant MFA aren't hardening projects anymore; they're the price of keeping the lights on.

**Ford and manufacturer Chemco named in fresh ransomware listings (Krybit, Qilin)**
Source: SharkStriker / breach trackers — https://sharkstriker.com/blog/july-2026-data-breaches/
Ford Motor Company appeared on a leak forum tied to the Krybit group, with scope still under investigation; Calgary manufacturer Chemco was hit by Qilin. Early-July listings also named INC_RANSOM, ANUBIS and Bashe victims across transport and municipal targets.
💡 Strategic take: Manufacturing keeps topping the victim charts because OT downtime converts directly into ransom leverage — you can't negotiate with a stopped assembly line. The named-victim leak-site model is as much PR extortion as data theft, so response planning now has to include the comms war, not just the forensics. Assume your incident will be public before you're ready.

## 🤖 AI & Security

**AI named the defining force of the 2026 threat landscape**
Source: Darktrace / IBM / CrowdStrike 2026 reports — https://www.darktrace.com/blog/the-state-of-ai-cybersecurity-2026
Industry data converges on the same picture: ~94% of organizations call AI the biggest force shaping 2026 security, and nearly 90% of CISOs rank AI-driven attacks as a top threat. The concrete edge is "adaptive malware," agentic attack tooling that pivots at machine speed, and deepfake-grade social engineering at scale.
💡 Strategic take: The story has shifted from "AI writes better phishing emails" to "AI agents run the intrusion." When adversaries operate at machine speed, human-in-the-loop response becomes the bottleneck — and the answer isn't removing the human, it's deciding in advance which decisions get automated and which stay gated. Boards should be asking not "do we use AI in security" but "where have we drawn the automation line, and who drew it."

**Weaponized GitHub PoCs deliver ChocoPoC RAT to researchers**
Source: The Hacker News — https://thehackernews.com/
Multiple booby-trapped proof-of-concept exploits on GitHub were found dropping a Python RAT dubbed ChocoPoC, in a campaign apparently aimed at security researchers themselves.
💡 Strategic take: Poisoning the well of public PoC code is a supply-chain attack on the defender community's own workflow — the exact repos analysts pull under deadline pressure. It's a reminder that "trusted because it's on GitHub" is not a security model. Sandbox everything, including the tools you use to study attacks.

## 📌 Today's Signal

Today's stories rhyme around a single uncomfortable theme: **the failures aren't in the exploits, they're in the disclosure and coordination layers**. A SharePoint RCE was patched but never announced — and the same technology then shows up as the vector into DHS's inter-agency intelligence-sharing platform, during a World Cup the US is actively securing. An RMM flaw threatens hundreds of downstream clients through a tool none of them chose. GitHub PoCs poison the defender community's own supply of research. The connective tissue is trust in shared infrastructure — vendor changelogs, government coordination networks, managed-service consoles, public code repos — and every one of those trust relationships is now an attack surface. For decision-makers, the strategic move this week isn't another control on the endpoint; it's mapping the trust you've outsourced. Which patches would you never hear about? Which platforms aggregate your most sensitive coordination? Which vendors hold an admin console into your environment? Those are the questions that separate resilient organizations from the next name on a leak site.
