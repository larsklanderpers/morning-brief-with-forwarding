## 🚨 Government Advisories

**CISA: Ivanti Connect Secure zero-day is being used for credential theft, and Ivanti's own checker may not see it**
Source: CISA | [Link](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/10/13/cisa-urges-patching-newly-exploited-vulnerability-ivanti-connect-secure)
CISA added CVE-2026-104935, an authentication bypass allowing unauthenticated remote command execution on Ivanti Connect Secure (25.1.1.1 and prior, 25.1.2.2 and prior, 22.8R2.4 and prior), to the KEV catalog on 13 October and says attackers are using it for credential theft and data exfiltration. CISA warns the Integrity Checker Tool may miss exploitation because commands run in memory, so confirming compromise needs network and memory forensics; recovery means factory reset, rebuild, and resetting every password, certificate and API key the appliance held. Volexity describes the activity as a chain of Connect Secure zero-days.
💡 Strategic take: Connect Secure has been here before, in January 2024 and January 2025, and each cycle ends the same way: patch, then assume the box leaked everything it touched. When the vendor's own health check is declared unreliable by the regulator, the honest cost of an edge appliance includes a full credential rotation every time it fails. Boards still comparing VPN vendors on licence price are comparing the wrong number.

**ASD's ACSC issues an OT alert off the back of the French supermarket refrigeration attacks**
Source: ASD's ACSC | [Link](https://www.cyber.gov.au/alert/protecting-operational-technology-and-edge-devices)
Moved since yesterday's brief: on 14 October ASD's ACSC published an alert for Australian organisations running internet-connected OT, explicitly naming commercial refrigeration and cooling. It says it has not seen active targeting in Australia, and asks operators to take OT interfaces and edge devices off the direct internet, patch, enforce strong authentication and segmentation, and watch for unusual automated activity.
💡 Strategic take: A regulator writing an alert about fridges is a regulator telling you the contractor-managed kit in your venues is now in scope. For Australian clubs and hospitality, the cool room, the HVAC panel and the gaming-floor environmental controls are usually installed and remotely maintained by someone else, on a connection nobody in IT has inventoried. "Not seen in Australia yet" is the window to find those connections, not a reason to wait.

## 📰 Top Security News

**Microsoft's October Patch Tuesday fixes a record 1,411 flaws, with one zero-day exploited**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/microsoft/microsoft-october-2026-patch-tuesday-fixes-1-411-flaws-3-zero-days/)
BleepingComputer counts 1,411 fixes on the day (70 Critical, 317 remote code execution), excluding 348 more shipped earlier in the month, and ties the record volume to Microsoft using AI to find bugs. The exploited zero-day, CVE-2026-96951, is a Windows Shell Mark of the Web bypass that CISA added to KEV the same day. Windows 11 24H2 Home and Pro and Windows 10 Enterprise LTSB 2016 received their last update.
💡 Strategic take: AI-assisted discovery has turned Patch Tuesday from a monthly chore into a monthly flood, and the bottleneck has moved from finding bugs to absorbing fixes. A team that still tests every patch by hand is now rationing risk by default. The useful question for this month is not "are we patched" but "which of the 1,411 actually touch something we expose", which is exactly the triage CISA's KEV list exists to do.

**OpenAI engineer charged with planting a recording device in an executive conference room**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/artificial-intelligence/openai-engineer-arrested-for-allegedly-planting-spy-device-in-office/)
US prosecutors charged Abdulrahman Alfozan, a Saudi national who joined OpenAI in July 2025, with allegedly hiding a recording device in a San Francisco conference room; OpenAI security found it on 30 September, fired him and referred the case to the FBI. Who, if anyone, he was collecting for has not been stated in the reporting.
💡 Strategic take: The most valuable thing in an AI lab right now is the conversation about what comes next, and it was guarded by the same badge access as the coffee machine. Every firm with a strategy worth stealing should ask when it last swept the rooms where that strategy is said out loud. Insider risk programmes built around data loss tools see files, not microphones.

**Leaked chats show a Russian extortion gang directing US-based "agents" into law firms**
Source: The Record | [Link](https://therecord.media/leaked-chats-show-russian-extortion-gang-sending-agents-to-law-firms)
Chats from August 2025 to September 2026, reviewed by The Record, show members tracking dozens of victims, haggling over multimillion-dollar payments and directing US operatives to gain physical access to law firms, including posing as IT staff. The group claims around $200 million in settlements; The Record notes many of its claimed operations remain unverified.
💡 Strategic take: Extortion crews have worked out that the cheapest way past a firewall is a person in a lanyard. Law firms hold every other industry's secrets and are notoriously thin on physical and vendor controls, which makes them the soft side door into their clients. If you are the client, your outside counsel's visitor policy is now part of your threat model.

**Docker Hub flaw let attackers take over other users' image repositories**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/docker-hub-flaw-let-attackers-impersonate-users-tamper-with-images/)
Docker disclosed CVE-2026-105445: converting an account into an organisation could reattach the original owner's personal access token, letting an attacker push, pull or delete images in the victim's repositories. Docker says it found the issue on 30 September, fixed it within hours, saw no evidence of exploitation, and deactivated affected tokens.
💡 Strategic take: A container registry is a build pipeline's single source of truth, so a token mix-up there is a supply-chain incident waiting for an attacker to notice. Docker's quick fix is the good news; the lesson is that most shops cannot say which images they pulled by tag rather than by digest. Pin by digest and you turn a registry compromise from silent to obvious.

**SAP's October patches include a maximum-severity OAuth2 SSRF in its Cloud SDK for Python**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/sap-warns-of-maximum-severity-oauth2-ssrf-security-flaw/)
SAP fixed 20 flaws this month, headlined by CVE-2026-89111, a server-side request forgery in the Cloud SDK for Python's OAuth2 handling that can be used to steal credentials. SAP's interim mitigation is an allowlist of tenant subdomains; NetWeaver (CVE-2026-89110) and Manufacturing Integration (CVE-2026-89108) also got critical fixes.
💡 Strategic take: SDK flaws travel further than product flaws because they ship inside code SAP never sees. The patch goes to SAP customers; the exposure sits in every in-house integration someone built with that library two years ago. Ask your developers for the dependency list, not your SAP basis team.

## 🤖 AI & Security

**Anthropic: small open models may be months from self-replicating across cheap IoT devices**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/anthropic-warns-self-replicating-ai-could-spread-to-your-smart-devices/)
Anthropic's researchers had small models (Qwen3.5-9B, GLM-5.2) install and configure inference software on off-brand Android TV boxes with unauthenticated ADB exposed, and ran Gemma4-26B-A4B split across 15 boxes. They did not demonstrate a full autonomous worm, but estimate models of 30 billion parameters or fewer could get there by the end of 2026; setup currently takes 28 to 150 hours, which leaves defenders time to detect large model transfers and odd device-to-device traffic.
💡 Strategic take: Read the conditions before the headline: unauthenticated ADB is a door left open, not one picked. The economics are the real point. A botnet that carries its own model needs no command server to take down and no cloud bill to pay, which makes the cheapest device on your guest Wi-Fi a compute node for someone else. The defence is the same boring one as the fridge alert above: nothing unauthenticated on a network you care about.

**Vercel Sandbox flaw lets a rogue agent smuggle data out through an approved server**
Source: The Record | [Link](https://therecord.media/sandbox-vulnerability-gives-rogue-ai-agents-path-out)
AI security startup AIR says a network-filtering flaw in Vercel Sandbox lets an agent send a crafted certificate to an allowlisted server, which then makes a request to an attacker-controlled host, so data leaves without the sandbox ever contacting the unapproved destination. AIR says Vercel has not yet worked out how to patch it; there is no evidence of in-the-wild exploitation. The research comes from a company that sells AI agent security, and Vercel had not commented.
💡 Strategic take: An allowlist answers "who did the agent talk to", not "who did they talk to next". That is the same gap that let yesterday's Anthropic agents use URL shorteners to get round fetch limits: the control checks the first hop and trusts the rest. If an agent can reach anything that will make a request on its behalf, its sandbox is a suggestion.

## 📌 Today's Signal

Almost every story this morning is about trust already granted. A VPN appliance that is supposed to be the front door, compromised in a way its own integrity check cannot see. An approved server that will happily carry a sandboxed agent's data to someone else. An employee with a badge and a conference room. "Agents" posing as IT staff in law firms. A registry token that quietly changed owners. Even the IoT research works only because a device trusted anyone who asked over ADB. The defences that failed were all checking the right thing at the wrong layer: identity at the door, destination at the first hop, files instead of rooms. For a decision-maker in a regulated business the question this week is what you have stopped checking because it is already inside, and the record 1,411-patch month is a reminder that you will not get there by patching alone.
