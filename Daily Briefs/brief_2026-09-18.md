## 🚨 Government Advisories

### CISA: Cyber Decoy Guidance Released
**Source:** CISA | [Using Cyber Decoys to Strengthen Detection and Response](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/using-cyber-decoys-strengthen-detection-and-response)

CISA published new guidance on 16 September urging organisations to deploy fake credentials, decoy databases, inactive admin accounts, honeytokens, and simulated servers inside their environments. The target problem: attackers who avoid malware in favour of legitimate accounts and living-off-the-land (LOTL) techniques are nearly invisible to signature-based detection. Decoys create tripwires that generate alerts the moment an attacker touches them.

💡 **Strategic take:** This is a formal endorsement of deception technology from the U.S. government — a signal that assume-breach postures are now table stakes, not leading practice. The complement-to-Zero-Trust framing matters: CISA is acknowledging that perimeter controls will fail and that post-compromise detection speed is the real metric. Boards asking "are we Zero Trust yet?" need a follow-up: "and what happens when someone's already inside?"

### CISA: Known Exploited Vulnerabilities — Two Added (16 Sep)
**Source:** CISA | [CISA Adds Two Known Exploited Vulnerabilities to Catalog](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/09/16/cisa-adds-two-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)

CISA added two more entries to its KEV Catalog on 16 September based on evidence of active exploitation. Earlier in the month (9 Sep), four were added: CVE-2026-19490 (Citrix NetScaler Authentication Bypass), CVE-2026-87491 (Chromium V8 Out-of-Bounds Write), CVE-2026-20079 (Cisco Firewall Management Center Auth Bypass), and CVE-2025-25249 (Fortinet Multiple Products Heap-based Buffer Overflow).

💡 **Strategic take:** Citrix and Cisco FMC auth bypasses on the same KEV update is a significant pairing — both sit at network perimeters and are prime targets for initial access brokers. If your organisation's patch cadence treats KEV additions as "within 30 days," that window is closing fast on these. Federal agencies must act within days; everyone else should treat KEV entries as de facto breach notifications for vulnerable assets.

### CISA: Ending Weekly Vulnerability Bulletin (28 Sep 2026)
**Source:** CISA | [Bulletins](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/bulletins)

CISA will discontinue the weekly Vulnerability Bulletin at the end of FY26 (28 September) as it transitions from severity-based to risk-based vulnerability prioritisation.

💡 **Strategic take:** The Bulletin's retirement is an acknowledgment that CVSS scores alone don't drive patching decisions — exploitation probability does. The shift aligns with SSVC and EPSS frameworks. If your vulnerability management programme still scores and queues patches by CVSSv3, this is the warning shot: the U.S. government is moving on, and your programme should too.

### ACSC: Active Directory Compromise Detection & Mitigation Guidance
**Source:** ACSC | [cyber.gov.au](https://www.cyber.gov.au/sites/default/files/2026-09/Detecting%20and%20mitigating%20Active%20Directory%20compromises%20(September%202026).pdf)

The Australian Signals Directorate's ACSC released updated PDF guidance on detecting and mitigating Active Directory compromises in September 2026. Earlier alerts this month flagged active exploitation of an Adobe Commerce/Magento critical vulnerability (9 Sep) and increased use of crypters to evade antivirus detection (8 Sep).

💡 **Strategic take:** AD remains the crown jewel of enterprise environments — it's what every attacker pivots through. Fresh ACSC guidance here is timely given the surge in LOTL attacks CISA simultaneously flagged. Australian organisations running hybrid AD environments should treat this as mandatory reading, not optional hardening.

## 📰 Top Security News

### Microsoft September Patch Tuesday: Record 964 CVEs, Two Zero-Days
**Source:** SecurityWeek | [Microsoft Patches Record 974 Vulnerabilities, Including Two Exploited Zero-Days](https://www.securityweek.com/microsoft-patches-record-974-vulnerabilities-including-two-exploited-zero-days/)
Also: [Tenable](https://www.tenable.com/blog/microsofts-september-2026-patch-tuesday-addresses-964-cves-cve-2026-81963-cve-2026-85880) | [Rapid7](https://www.rapid7.com/blog/post/em-patch-tuesday-september-2026/)

Microsoft addressed approximately 964 CVEs in September 2026 Patch Tuesday — the most ever in a single release — including two actively exploited zero-day flaws: CVE-2026-81963 and CVE-2026-85880, both local privilege escalation vulnerabilities. A critical 9.8 CVSS RCE in Windows DNS Server (CVE-2026-69730) allows unauthenticated remote exploitation via crafted packets. Additionally, a researcher published a PoC claiming Microsoft's patch for CVE-2026-69414 (ShieldBreak/Microsoft Defender) is incomplete, with an alternate attack path to SYSTEM-level file reads still unpatched.

💡 **Strategic take:** Nearly a thousand CVEs in one month represents a systemic scaling problem — no enterprise team can triage 964 items on a monthly cycle. The incomplete Defender patch (if the PoC holds) is particularly corrosive: it forces defenders to evaluate whether their primary endpoint protection tool has a known unpatched bypass. DNS Server RCE at 9.8 CVSS should be at the top of every Windows admin's weekend queue.

### First Agentic Ransomware Attack Documented
**Source:** TechCrunch | [Leaks, data breaches, and ransom notes: The worst hacks of 2026 so far](https://techcrunch.com/2026/09/15/the-worst-hacks-and-breaches-of-2026-so-far/)
Also: [BrightDefense](https://www.brightdefense.com/resources/recent-data-breaches/)

Researchers documented what is described as the first agentic ransomware attack: a likely Russian-speaking threat actor deployed hundreds of AI agents to autonomously exploit PaperCut systems, compromising at least 440 systems at 395 organisations across 48 countries. The attack used AI orchestration to chain reconnaissance, exploitation, and lateral movement at a scale and speed not previously seen.

💡 **Strategic take:** Agentic ransomware changes the economics of attack in a way that dwarfs previous automation milestones. Human operators have always been the bottleneck in scaling intrusion campaigns; AI agents remove that constraint. 395 organisations in 48 countries from what appears to be a single campaign is a preview of what mass-casualty cyber events will look like in 2027 and beyond. PaperCut patch status should be verified immediately across any fleet running it.

### FBI Seizes NightmareStresser DDoS-for-Hire Domains
**Source:** BleepingComputer | [bleepingcomputer.com](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/)

The FBI seized the domains operating NightmareStresser, one of the world's longest-running DDoS-for-hire (booter) services, disrupting infrastructure that had been operating for years and enabled attacks by non-technical actors.

💡 **Strategic take:** Booter/stresser takedowns are meaningful but incomplete — the underlying market reconstitutes quickly. What matters here is the signal about FBI prioritisation of cybercrime-as-a-service infrastructure. The more interesting story is what happens to NightmareStresser's customer lists: those are active threat actors with a sudden need for a new supplier.

### Ship Loses Communications for 30 Hours in Cyberattack
**Source:** SecurityWeek | [securityweek.com](https://www.securityweek.com/)

A vessel reportedly lost all communications for 30 hours after hackers gained control over engine-room systems in a maritime cyberattack.

💡 **Strategic take:** Maritime OT security remains one of the softest targets in critical infrastructure. Losing engine-room command doesn't just strand a ship — in busy shipping lanes it's a safety of life at sea (SOLAS) event. This incident pattern should be front of mind for the roughly $14 trillion in goods that transit the world's seas annually.

### BIND 9 Patches 14 Security Flaws
**Source:** BleepingComputer | [bleepingcomputer.com](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/)

The Internet Systems Consortium released BIND 9.20.29 and 9.21.26 on 16 September to address 14 security vulnerabilities in BIND 9, the dominant open-source DNS server software.

💡 **Strategic take:** BIND underpins DNS resolution for an enormous share of internet infrastructure. Fourteen flaws in a single release is a notable batch — anyone running authoritative or recursive BIND resolvers should treat this as urgent given the concurrent Windows DNS Server RCE story above. DNS is the one service you almost certainly can't take offline to patch, which is exactly why it's an attacker favourite.

## 🤖 AI & Security

### OpenAI Publishes Model Misalignment Disclosure Framework
**Source:** Security Week | [securityweek.com](https://www.securityweek.com/)

OpenAI published a framework for disclosing model misalignment, alongside six individual reports documenting specific instances of problematic model behaviour. This represents a novel disclosure format — essentially bug reports for alignment failures rather than code vulnerabilities.

💡 **Strategic take:** OpenAI is creating a disclosure category that doesn't yet have a governance response. Traditional CVE infrastructure, patch management, and vulnerability scanning have no analogue for "the model behaves incorrectly under certain conditions." Enterprises deploying AI in decision-making roles should be watching this framework closely — it may become the equivalent of CVSS scoring for AI risks, and regulators will likely mandate similar disclosures within 12–18 months.

### Agentic AI Now Executing Ransomware Campaigns at Scale
*(See also Top Security News above)*

The documented use of hundreds of AI agents to coordinate a multi-country ransomware campaign is the clearest example yet of what the threat landscape looks like when AI removes human operator constraints from attack campaigns. Previous AI-assisted attacks used AI for individual tasks (phishing, code generation); this represents full-loop agentic orchestration of an entire intrusion lifecycle.

💡 **Strategic take:** The 2026 Cloud Security Alliance survey found 94% of organisations identify AI as the biggest cybersecurity force this year. This attack is the empirical proof. Security teams that haven't modelled what a fully autonomous AI-orchestrated attack looks like against their environment are six months behind the threat.

### Identity Surpassing Ransomware as Primary Threat Vector
**Source:** IBM Think | [Cybersecurity Trends 2026](https://www.ibm.com/think/insights/more-2026-cyberthreat-trends) | Fortinet | [cybersecurity-trends-2026](https://www.fortinet.com/resources/cyberglossary/cybersecurity-trends-2026)

Security researchers and vendors are converging on a consensus: in 2026, identity hijacking has eclipsed ransomware as the primary threat category. Hyper-personalised phishing (50% of AI threat concerns), deepfake voice fraud (40%), and credential harvesting at scale are now the preferred initial access methods. Per IBM: "The biggest data breaches of 2026 will come from the tools companies willingly connect to their own systems."

💡 **Strategic take:** The IBM framing is the sharpest: the breach vector is often the SaaS integrations, OAuth connections, and API keys you handed out without thinking. Third-party identity sprawl — all the apps with "sign in with Google/Microsoft" access — is the shadow attack surface most security teams haven't fully enumerated, let alone defended.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The shape of this week's intelligence is harder to miss than it might first appear: every major story — CISA's decoy guidance, the agentic ransomware campaign, the identity-as-primary-vector trend, the Microsoft Defender incomplete patch — points to the same underlying shift. The attacker has learned to operate inside the defenders' trusted environment, using credentials, legitimate tools, and now autonomous AI agents that move faster than any human SOC can respond. The answer CISA is giving with the decoy guidance is essentially "if you can't stop them entering, make sure every step they take generates a signal." That's a philosophical concession that perimeter security is over — and it's the U.S. government saying it. Strategic decision-makers should be asking not whether they have monitoring, but whether their monitoring is designed to catch attackers who already have valid credentials, are using built-in tools, and are being orchestrated by AI agents working in parallel. Most aren't.
