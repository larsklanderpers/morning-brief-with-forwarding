## 🚨 Government Advisories

**CISA adds four actively-exploited flaws to the KEV catalog** — *CISA / The Hacker News*
[cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)
Four vulnerabilities were added on evidence of active exploitation, led by two perfect-10s: CVE-2026-48282, a path-traversal-to-RCE flaw in Adobe ColdFusion, and CVE-2026-56290, an unauthenticated file-upload RCE in the Joomlack Page Builder. CVE-2026-55255, an authorization bypass in Langflow, rounds out the AI-adjacent entries. Recent KEV additions also flag missing-authentication flaws in Oracle PeopleSoft PeopleTools and Splunk Enterprise.
💡 **Strategic take:** The two 10.0s share a signature — unauthenticated code execution on widely deployed web infrastructure, exactly the "front door left open" that automated scanners monetize first. The Langflow entry is the one to watch: it's the same product family that seeded this year's agentic-ransomware incident, and its presence in KEV signals that AI-orchestration tooling has quietly become production attack surface. Treat any internet-facing ColdFusion or low-code AI builder as compromised-until-proven-patched.

**ACSC warns on Fortinet campaign, ClickFix, and cPanel/WHM exploitation** — *ASD's ACSC*
[cyber.gov.au/alerts-and-advisories](https://www.cyber.gov.au/about-us/view-all-content/alerts-and-advisories)
Australia's cyber agency is tracking a malicious campaign against Fortinet firewalls and VPN gateways, a "ClickFix" social-engineering wave distributing malware through compromised WordPress sites, and active exploitation of CVE-2026-4194 in cPanel/WebHost Manager.
💡 **Strategic take:** The through-line here is the hosting stack — Fortinet edge, WordPress, cPanel — the connective tissue of small and mid-market businesses that rarely have a SOC. ClickFix in particular weaponizes the user's own copy-paste reflex, which no firewall patch will fix. For anyone running downstream suppliers on shared hosting, this is a supply-chain exposure wearing an IT-hygiene costume.

## 📰 Top Security News

**Microsoft 365 device-code phishing campaign revives Storm-2372 playbook** — *The Hacker News*
[thehackernews.com](https://thehackernews.com/)
A device-code phishing operation running from late June into early July used collaboration-themed lures to hijack M365 accounts, with strong overlaps to the Storm-2372 activity Microsoft documented in February 2025.
💡 **Strategic take:** Device-code phishing sidesteps the MFA most organizations consider "done" — it abuses a legitimate OAuth flow rather than stealing a password, so the victim authenticates the attacker's session for them. The recurrence a year later tells you the technique still works because the mitigation is a conditional-access policy nobody enabled. Phishing-resistant auth and locking down device-code grants belong on this quarter's roadmap, not next year's.

**AdaptHealth breach: social engineering into the cloud, patient data stolen** — *The Register*
[theregister.com](https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/07/03/adapthealth-crooks-stole-our-passwords-patient-health-data/5266512)
Attackers talked their way into AdaptHealth's cloud environment through an unwitting third-party contractor, exfiltrating patient data including credentials tied to insurance billing.
💡 **Strategic take:** No zero-day, no malware — just a persuasive human and a contractor with too much access. Healthcare keeps losing this fight because identity trust is extended to vendors without being scoped or monitored. The board-level question isn't "are we patched?" but "who can a smooth-talking stranger become in our environment, and would we notice?"

**Progress Kemp LoadMaster under active attack (CVE-2026-8037, CVSS 9.6)** — *The Hacker News*
[thehackernews.com](https://thehackernews.com/)
An OS command-injection flaw in the Kemp LoadMaster load balancer has seen exploitation attempts since June 29.
💡 **Strategic take:** Load balancers sit in front of everything and are trusted implicitly, which makes command injection here a skeleton key rather than a single lock. This is the same edge-appliance pattern the government advisories keep circling — the network's most privileged, least-monitored devices are where 2026's intrusions begin.

## 🤖 AI & Security

**JADEPUFFER: Sysdig details the first end-to-end agentic ransomware operation** — *Sysdig / BleepingComputer / CyberScoop*
[sysdig.com/blog/jadepuffer-agentic-ransomware-for-automated-database-extortion](https://www.sysdig.com/blog/jadepuffer-agentic-ransomware-for-automated-database-extortion)
Newly published analysis fills in the mechanics behind the incident: JADEPUFFER breached an internet-facing Langflow instance via CVE-2025-3248, then let an LLM-driven agent run reconnaissance, credential theft, lateral movement, privilege escalation, and encryption autonomously — recovering from a failed login to a working fix in 31 seconds. It encrypted 1,342 Nacos configuration items and, tellingly, harvested API keys for OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepSeek, and Gemini as it went.
💡 **Strategic take:** The detail that should reset planning assumptions is the economics: Sysdig notes the agent ran partly on *stolen* model credentials ("LLMjacking"), meaning the skill floor and the cost floor for running ransomware both approach zero simultaneously. We flagged JADEPUFFER as it broke; the follow-up analysis confirms it wasn't a stunt but a repeatable pattern. Defenders should assume the next operator won't be a specialist — it'll be anyone who can rent, or steal, an agent.

**Five Eyes' AI-attack warning came true in weeks, not months** — *Business Standard / CISO Series*
[cisoseries.com](https://cisoseries.com/cybersecurity-news-first-ai-ransomware-adapthealth-suffers-cyberattack-uk-cyber-plan-delayed/)
The joint Five Eyes prediction from late June that AI-powered attacks were "months" away was overtaken by JADEPUFFER within weeks, even as the UK's national cyber plan slipped its timeline.
💡 **Strategic take:** The forecasting error is the story — official bodies are consistently pricing AI acceleration too slowly, while the UK's delayed plan shows policy moving at the opposite tempo. That widening gap between adversary velocity and institutional response is itself a strategic risk decision-makers should be budgeting against.

## 📌 Today's Signal

Yesterday the story was that a machine could run a ransomware attack end to end; today the follow-up reporting tells us what that actually costs — close to nothing. Sysdig's deeper look at JADEPUFFER lands on the uncomfortable part: the agent ran on *stolen* LLM credentials, so the same drop in skill that autonomy provides is now matched by a drop in price. Set that beside the morning's other thread — Langflow in the KEV catalog, a command-injectable load balancer under active attack, ColdFusion and cPanel RCEs, a contractor social-engineered into a healthcare cloud — and the pattern is not a new class of exploit but the industrialization of old ones. The doors haven't changed; the thing knocking on them has gotten cheaper, faster, and tireless. For decision-makers the implication is a shift in where the marginal security dollar goes: away from buying another detection tool and toward compressing the time between "an edge device is exposed" and "we've closed it," because a machine-speed adversary running on someone else's API key will find the gap before the quarterly patch cycle does.
