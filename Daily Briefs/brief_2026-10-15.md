## 🚨 Government Advisories

**CISA adds four exploited flaws to KEV: Palo Alto PAN-OS, Cisco Secure Email Gateway, and two in AhsayCBS backup**
Source: CISA | [Link](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/10/14/cisa-adds-four-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)
On 14 October CISA added CVE-2026-0322 (PAN-OS code injection), CVE-2026-76519 (Cisco SEG path traversal) and CVE-2026-105133 / CVE-2026-105134 (AhsayCBS improper authentication and command injection) to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, citing evidence of active exploitation. BleepingComputer reports the federal remediation deadline for the PAN-OS flaw is 17 October.
💡 Strategic take: Read the list as a map of where attackers are standing: the firewall, the mail filter and the backup server. Those are the three boxes every incident-response plan assumes are on your side, and a compromised backup console turns a ransomware event from a bad week into a negotiation. If AhsayCBS or a white-labelled version of it sits anywhere in your MSP chain, ask the question today, not at renewal.

**No new ACSC alert since Wednesday's OT and refrigeration alert**
Source: Australian Signals Directorate's ACSC | [Link](https://www.cyber.gov.au/alert/protecting-operational-technology-and-edge-devices)
The ACSC's alerts page shows the 14 October OT and edge-device alert, covered in yesterday's brief, as its most recent item; the 9 October joint advisory on Chinese state-linked data theft precedes it.
💡 Strategic take: Nothing has moved on the Australian side overnight, which makes this the morning to actually action Wednesday's alert rather than file it. Clubs running cellar cooling, kitchen refrigeration or building management on vendor remote access are squarely in its scope.

## 📰 Top Security News

**Cisco patches another exploited Secure Email Gateway zero-day that gives unauthenticated attackers root**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/cisco-patches-actively-exploited-secure-email-gateway-zero-day/)
CVE-2026-76519 is a relative path traversal triggered through attachment handling on SEG appliances with File Reputation enabled and Content Scanner Tools older than 25.3.0.5525, letting a remote attacker run commands as root. Cisco has published indicators of compromise; BleepingComputer describes it as "another" exploited SEG zero-day.
💡 Strategic take: The email gateway is the one appliance whose whole job is to open hostile files from strangers, so a parser bug there is a front door with a welcome mat. The repeat is the story: when the same control fails twice in a short window, the board question shifts from "are we patched" to "how long would we take to notice if it happened again".

**Palo Alto Networks confirms PAN-OS management-interface RCE exploited in the wild**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/palo-alto-networks-warns-of-pan-os-rce-flaw-exploited-in-attacks/)
CVE-2026-0322 is a high-severity flaw in the PAN-OS management interface that lets an authenticated attacker with network access execute code as root. Palo Alto says it has seen limited exploitation, offers no workaround, and tells customers to restrict the management interface to trusted internal IPs.
💡 Strategic take: "Authenticated" and "management interface" sound reassuring until you remember how many firewall consoles are still reachable from the internet and how many admin credentials are already for sale. This is a hygiene bill coming due: the fix that matters is the network rule that should have been there before the patch existed.

**Microsoft, Japan's police and Health-ISAC take down three Chinese-language phishing platforms**
Source: The Record | [Link](https://therecord.media/microsoft-takes-down-3-major-chinese-phishing-platforms)
Microsoft obtained a US court order to seize domains behind YAM Store, CoGUI and Iron Man, platforms it says impersonated more than 75,000 organisations; it disabled over 20,000 YAM Store accounts and blocked more than 100,000 impersonation domains. The Record puts the message volume at more than 750 million; BleepingComputer's account of the same operation says more than one billion, so treat the headline number as unsettled.
💡 Strategic take: Phishing is now a franchise with a storefront, a template catalogue (Iron Man reportedly ships over 2,300 lure pages) and a support desk, which is why a takedown is a speed bump, not an ending. The useful signal for defenders is the device-code phishing angle BleepingComputer reports: if your tenant still allows device-code sign-in for users who never need it, that is a setting, not a training problem.

**FBI raids North Korean IT-worker laptop farms in Texas; two US nationals arrested, 14 charged**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/fbi-raids-dprk-it-worker-laptop-farms-in-texas-us-nationals-arrested/)
The Justice Department says it seized nearly 187 laptops used to let North Korean operatives pose as US-based remote workers. A related Ohio indictment covers a scheme that reached about 70 US companies and generated around $3 million for the DPRK, per the DoJ as reported.
💡 Strategic take: Every one of those laptops was shipped by a hiring manager who believed they had onboarded a real person. Identity verification at hire is now a security control, and it belongs to HR and IT jointly; a remote engineer you have never seen on an unscheduled video call is a risk you have chosen, not one you have mitigated.

**UK Legal Aid Agency says stolen data reaches back to 1978**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/uk-legal-aid-agency-hackers-stole-records-dating-back-to-1978/)
In an October update on the April 2025 breach, the LAA said it has confirmed about 250,000 stolen records predate 2007, mostly from the 1990s and 2000s, with the earliest verified record from 1978.
💡 Strategic take: Records from 1978 were not breached because anyone needed them; they were breached because nobody deleted them. Under the Australian Privacy Act and GDPR alike, data you are not obliged to keep is pure liability, and retention schedules are the cheapest breach-cost reduction available to any CTO.

**Poland's PM says the spyware vendor had access to data harvested under the previous government**
Source: The Record | [Link](https://therecord.media/spyware-manufacturer-had-access-to-data-harvested-by-previous-polish-government)
Donald Tusk, presenting an investigation he ordered, said the Pegasus operation run by the Central Anti-Corruption Bureau relied on infrastructure under the manufacturer's control, and called it one of the biggest scandals since the fall of communism. The Record reports more than 500 people were targeted.
💡 Strategic take: This is the sovereignty argument in its purest form: a state bought a surveillance capability and, on the PM's account, did not control where the data went. Swap "spyware" for "AI model hosted offshore" and the governance question for any regulated business is identical: who else can see what your tool sees?

**Ledger asks sellers across seven Asian markets to pull its wallets from shelves**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/ledger-asks-sellers-across-asia-to-halt-sales-after-supply-chain-attack/)
Moved since Tuesday's brief: Ledger has widened its response from the Malaysian reseller implant to a sales halt in Indonesia, Singapore, Thailand, the Philippines, Vietnam, Malaysia and Hong Kong while it investigates device tampering.
💡 Strategic take: A one-reseller incident has become a regional distribution freeze, which tells you Ledger cannot yet prove where the tampering stops. For any hardware security purchase, the procurement channel is part of the threat model: buy direct or accept that someone in the middle had the box before you did.

**Meta says attackers used an employee's stolen credentials to take code and personal data from GitHub repositories**
Source: SecurityWeek | [Link](https://www.securityweek.com/meta-says-hackers-took-code-personal-data-of-1500-in-github-breach/)
Meta says an outside group accessed some of its GitHub repositories; about 99% of what was taken was product source code, and the remainder was personal data mistakenly committed to the repositories, affecting roughly 1,500 people, mostly US employees, who are being offered credit monitoring.
💡 Strategic take: The breach path is ordinary (one stolen credential) and the damage multiplier is ordinary too: personal data sitting in a code repository where it never belonged. Secret and PII scanning on every commit is cheap; explaining to 1,500 employees why their data was in source control is not.

## 🤖 AI & Security

**Booz Allen: frontier AI models reached an industrial control network from the perimeter in about 16 minutes**
Source: SecurityWeek | [Link](https://www.securityweek.com/ai-agents-navigate-unfamiliar-ot-systems-and-move-equipment-in-minutes-booz-allen-finds/)
In Booz Allen's isolated OT lab, unnamed frontier models met every test objective across eight scenarios, in one case moving from perimeter compromise to actions inside the control network in just over 16 minutes, and in another locating a robotic arm, mapping its safety zones and moving it. Booz Allen says no single safeguard consistently stopped the models. Booz Allen sells cyber and OT security services, and the models and scenarios are not named in the coverage.
💡 Strategic take: Put this next to this week's French supermarket refrigeration attacks and the ACSC alert that followed: the cheap part of an OT attack used to be getting in, and the expensive part was understanding an unfamiliar plant. If an agent now does the understanding in minutes, the skills moat around industrial systems is gone, and segmentation plus human-in-the-loop on physical actions is what remains. Discount the vendor framing, keep the timing.

**Harmonic Security raises $50 million to police employee AI use**
Source: SecurityWeek | [Link](https://www.securityweek.com/harmonic-security-raises-50-million-for-workforce-ai-protection/)
The startup will expand engineering and go-to-market teams in the US and UK, per SecurityWeek.
💡 Strategic take: Capital follows the gap, and the gap here is data leaving through staff prompts into tools the company never approved. For a business holding member PII, that is a privacy-law problem before it is a security one, and a policy plus a sanctioned, local option usually beats another monitoring layer on cost.

## 🗣️ Community Pulse

⚠️ **Reddit lane failed: r/netsec and r/cybersecurity not reachable this run**
Source: Reddit | [Link](https://www.reddit.com/r/netsec/)
On this run (15 October), WebFetch refused both hot.json fetches ("unable to fetch from www.reddit.com"), and the site-restricted WebSearch returned only third-party pages about the subreddits, no posts. Third consecutive day; this is a tool failure, not a quiet community.
💡 Strategic take: Three dark days is a pattern; the skill needs a different route (Chrome, or the subreddits' RSS feeds) wired in rather than a fourth retry.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The overnight list reads like an inventory of the things organisations trust by default: the firewall console, the email filter, the backup server, the remote hire, the hardware wallet bought from a reseller, the code repository. Each was compromised not through novelty but through a place where trust was assumed and never re-checked, and Booz Allen's OT lab result says the time attackers need to exploit that assumption is collapsing. The strategic question for this quarter is not "what new tool do we buy" but "which of our trusted components have we never independently verified", and that audit costs less than any one of these incidents.
