## 🚨 Government Advisories

### CISA Emergency Directive: F5 BIG-IP APM Zero-Day — Patch by Friday
**Source:** CISA / BleepingComputer | [Read more](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/f5-warns-of-big-ip-apm-remote-code-execution-zero-day-exploited-in-attacks/)

CISA has ordered all federal agencies to patch CVE-2026-94127 — a critical remote code execution zero-day in F5 BIG-IP APM — by this Friday, after confirming it is actively exploited in the wild. The flaw affects deployments configured as OAuth Authorization Servers. Over 14,700 BIG-IP APM instances are publicly exposed online. F5 has released patches; an iRule workaround is available for those unable to patch immediately.

💡 **Strategic take:** CISA emergency directives carry the implicit weight of "assume breach" for any federal contractor or supply-chain partner still running unpatched BIG-IP. For private-sector organisations: the 14,700+ exposed instances mean this is an active hunting ground for threat actors today. If your network access broker or VPN infrastructure runs BIG-IP APM, this is a board-level conversation, not a ticket in the patch queue.

### CISA KEV Update: Seven Exploited Vulnerabilities Added (September 2)
**Source:** CISA | [Read more](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/09/02/cisa-adds-seven-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)

CISA added seven vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog on September 2, with a further one added September 4. The September Patch Tuesday cycle has since surfaced an additional 972 CVEs from Microsoft alone, including 114 rated Critical and 20 described as **wormable** — capable of autonomous lateral movement with no user interaction. Two Windows privilege escalation bugs (CVE-2026-81963, CVE-2026-85880) are confirmed under active exploitation.

💡 **Strategic take:** The pace at which the KEV catalog is growing in September is not incidental — AI-assisted vulnerability discovery is being cited as a driver of the 20-wormable-bug anomaly in this month's Microsoft release. We are entering an era where the attack surface is expanding faster than traditional patch cadences can absorb. Organisations that have not automated vulnerability prioritisation tied to the KEV catalog are structurally behind.

**Source:** BleepingComputer | [Read more](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/shinyhunters-claims-fbi-hack-data-theft-in-peoplesoft-zero-day-breach/)

The ShinyHunters threat group claims to have exfiltrated 2–3TB of FBI data — including PII, health records, and internal HR data from FBI employees, job applicants, and Criminal Justice services — by exploiting an alleged Oracle PeopleSoft zero-day. The group defaced FBIjobs.gov and has indicated it is actively using the same vulnerability against Fortune 500 companies. The FBI confirmed an investigation but has not verified the breach. 💡 **Strategic take:** Whether or not this breach is fully verified, the operational message is unambiguous: legacy enterprise HR platforms are now primary targets for state-adjacent threat actors. Oracle PeopleSoft is widely deployed across government and large enterprise. If you haven't audited your PeopleSoft exposure this quarter, today is a reasonable day to start — and the Fortune 500 targeting claim should sharpen that urgency considerably.

### Check Point Security Management Server Zero-Day Exploited Since July
**Source:** The Hacker News | [Read more](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/check-point-warns-of-management-server.html)

CVE-2026-93616, a path traversal vulnerability in Check Point's Security Management Server, was exploited in real-world attacks as far back as July before a patch was released on September 22. The flaw allowed unauthenticated script execution against the management plane — the architectural layer supposed to control security policy across an organisation's entire network.

💡 **Strategic take:** The July-to-September disclosure gap is the story here. Security management infrastructure has become the highest-value target precisely because compromising it gives attackers policy-level access. Organisations need to treat their security control planes with the same — or greater — isolation discipline as production databases. A breached management server doesn't just expose one asset; it hands over the keys to the entire estate.

### VeloCloud Orchestrator: CVSS 10.0 Flaw Under Active Exploitation
**Source:** The Hacker News | [Read more](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/new-cvss-100-velocloud-orchestrator.html)

CVE-2026-93952 in VMware's VeloCloud Orchestrator (on-premises deployments) carries a perfect CVSS 10.0 score. Unauthenticated remote attackers can invoke privileged internal functions via certificate-based authentication pathways. Active exploitation is confirmed. Patches are available for some release trains as of September 22; organisations on unsupported versions face urgent upgrade decisions.

Any organisation running on-premises VeloCloud should treat this as an emergency this week.

### Rogue MFA Providers Can Intercept Credentials — New Attack Research
**Source:** BleepingComputer | [Read more](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/rogue-external-mfa-providers-can-steal-passwords-during-logins/)

Researchers have demonstrated that threat actors with privileged network access can register malicious external MFA providers that silently intercept credentials during the authentication flow — essentially creating a trusted-looking man-in-the-middle within the identity stack. The attack undermines assumptions about MFA as a final line of defence.

💡 **Strategic take:** This research matters beyond the technical proof-of-concept. It signals that MFA, long treated as the non-negotiable baseline control, is becoming a target in its own right as attackers move further up the identity stack. The implication for enterprise security architectures: identity provider governance — including who can register MFA providers and under what conditions — needs the same rigour as privileged access management.

**Source:** The Hacker News | [Read more](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/wordpress-issues-patch-for-critical.html)

WordPress released version 7.1.2 to address CVE-2026-87902 (CVSS 9.2), which allows unauthenticated attackers to load external PHP files and potentially execute arbitrary code on certain server configurations. The vulnerability affects all WordPress installations back to version 4.7 — a substantial portion of the global web.

💡 **Strategic take:** The scope of this vulnerability — reaching back to WordPress 4.7 — reflects the compounding challenge of long-tail unpatched deployments. Millions of sites run outdated WordPress versions, often managed by third parties or abandoned post-build. For organisations with large web estates or digital marketing properties, this is a reminder that content management platforms are attack surface, not just productivity tools.

## 🤖 AI & Security

### Microsoft Dismantles EvilTokens: AI-Powered Device-Code Phishing Platform
**Source:** The Hacker News | [Read more](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/microsoft-takes-down-eviltokens-device.html)

Microsoft's Digital Crimes Unit dismantled EvilTokens, a criminal phishing-as-a-service platform that used AI to conduct device-code phishing attacks, compromising approximately 12,000 email accounts. Two individuals were arrested in connection with the operation. EvilTokens automated the creation and delivery of convincing phishing flows, substantially lowering the skill floor for conducting token-theft attacks at scale.

💡 **Strategic take:** The commoditisation of AI-assisted phishing infrastructure is the accelerant that security teams have been warning about. EvilTokens isn't notable because of its sophistication — it's notable because it packaged that sophistication into a service that non-technical criminals could operate. Every takedown of a platform like this is followed by forks and successors. Conditional access policies, phishing-resistant MFA (FIDO2, not TOTP), and user awareness programmes calibrated to AI-generated lures are the operational response stack.

### Critical Vulnerability in Bifrost AI Gateway (CVE-2026-90898, CVSS 9.8)
**Source:** The Hacker News | [Read more](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/critical-bifrost-ai-gateway-flaw-lets.html)

CVE-2026-90898 in Bifrost, an open-source AI gateway and API management platform, allows unauthenticated attackers to execute arbitrary commands via the management API. The flaw (CVSS 9.8) affects all versions prior to 2.1.0 and is particularly dangerous because authentication is disabled by default in many deployments. Bifrost is used to route and manage traffic to LLM APIs including OpenAI and Anthropic.

💡 **Strategic take:** AI gateway infrastructure is the new application delivery controller — and it's receiving scrutiny commensurate with its newness, not its criticality. An unauthenticated RCE in a component that sits between your enterprise and your LLM providers means that an attacker doesn't just own a box; they potentially intercept or manipulate every prompt and response flowing through it. Security teams onboarding AI infrastructure need to apply the same hardening discipline as they would to a reverse proxy or WAF — which means authentication must not be optional by default.

### CSA Research: 73% of Security Leaders Say AI Threats Already Materially Impacting Orgs
**Source:** Cloud Security Alliance | [Read more](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/blog/2026/04/02/the-state-of-ai-cybersecurity-2026-unveiling-insights-from-over-1-500-security-leaders)

The CSA's State of AI Cybersecurity 2026 report (surveying 1,500+ security leaders) found that 73% report AI-powered threats are already having significant organisational impact, while 92% say they are being forced to materially upgrade defences in response. Only 14% of organisations currently permit AI to execute remediation autonomously — the majority maintain human-in-the-loop models. Notably, only 23% are monitoring AI agents for threats.

💡 **Strategic take:** The 23% monitoring figure is the number that should drive boardroom conversation. Organisations are rapidly deploying AI agents while monitoring them at the same coverage rates they applied to shadow IT five years ago — which is to say, barely. The asymmetry between deployment speed and security visibility is where the next generation of material breaches will originate. Governance frameworks for AI agents need to be a 2026 deliverable, not a 2027 initiative.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The defining pattern of this week's threat landscape is the weaponisation of critical infrastructure and foundational security controls — not peripheral applications, but the very mechanisms organisations rely on to maintain visibility and access control. Attackers are targeting Check Point management servers, F5 access proxies, VeloCloud SD-WAN orchestrators, and AI API gateways: all components that, when compromised, yield not just a foothold but policy-level or network-wide control. Simultaneously, AI is industrialising attacker capabilities — from EvilTokens' phishing-as-a-service to the AI-driven discovery of 20 wormable Windows vulnerabilities in a single patch cycle. The strategic question for decision-makers is no longer "are we resilient enough?" but "have we stress-tested the security infrastructure itself?" When the attack surface is your security stack, traditional defence-in-depth assumptions need revisiting at the architecture level.
