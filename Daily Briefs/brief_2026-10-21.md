## 🚨 Government Advisories

**CISA, FBI and partners publish a SafePay ransomware advisory counting at least 600 victims across critical infrastructure**
Source: CISA | [Link](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa26-293a)
Advisory AA26-293A, released 20 October with the FBI, US Secret Service, the UK National Crime Agency and German and UK regional police units, says SafePay has been active since August 2024 and has hit at least 600 victims across multiple critical infrastructure sectors. Initial access comes from exposed and brute-forced credentials, phishing and VPN access, and the advisory lists five older CVEs, including flaws in Cisco ASA, Veeam Backup and SonicWall firewalls (CVE-2020-3259, CVE-2023-20269, CVE-2023-27532, CVE-2024-40711, CVE-2024-40766). Once inside, the group uses PsExec and WinRM, disables security software and encrypts with ChaCha20 and RSA. Australia's ASD is not a co-sealer.
💡 Strategic take: The newest CVE on SafePay's list is two years old. This is not a zero-day story; it is a patch-debt and password-hygiene story, and the edge devices named here are the ones mid-sized operators keep running past their support windows. If your Veeam server or SonicWall still carries any of those CVEs, it is the backup and the front door at once.

**CISA adds six exploited flaws to KEV, confirming three stories this brief ran in the last three days**
Source: CISA | [Link](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/10/20/cisa-adds-six-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)
On 20 October CISA added the four Plex Media Server flaws in the Plex2Shell chain (CVE-2026-96652, -96654, -96655, -96656), the SiteGuard Server Edition command injection flaw (CVE-2026-106931) and the Siemens Software Center DLL search-path flaw (CVE-2026-110936). This brief covered Plex2Shell and Siemens on 20 October and SiteGuard on 19 October; what moved is that each now carries a federal remediation deadline under BOD 26-04.
💡 Strategic take: CISA confirmed what honeypots and vendors had reported days earlier, which is the right order but the wrong clock for anyone who waits on it. The Plex entries matter beyond home media: a staff member's Plex box on a home network is now a federally recognised attack path. Treat KEV as the floor for patching, not the trigger.

**⚠️ ACSC site unreachable this morning**
Source: Australian Signals Directorate's ACSC | [Link](https://www.cyber.gov.au/alerts-and-advisories)
⚠️ Two Firecrawl fetches of the cyber.gov.au alerts and advisories page at about 04:45 PDT on 21 October returned "503 Service Temporarily Unavailable", and web search returned only cached pages from July. The last confirmed ACSC alert remains the 14 October OT and edge device alert from yesterday's check; whether anything newer has been posted is unknown, not nil.
💡 Strategic take: An outage on the national advisory site is a reminder to subscribe to ACSC alerts by email rather than relying on a page visit. Check it manually today before assuming the quiet week continues.

## 📰 Top Security News

**Leaked PHP2Shell flaws hit every PHP version since PHP 5, and patches land today at 14:00 UTC**
Source: SecurityWeek | [Link](https://www.securityweek.com/php2shell-mass-exploitation-looms-for-leaked-critical-php-flaws/)
Details of two critical PHP vulnerabilities (CVE-2026-109618, a use-after-free, and CVE-2026-109619, both CVSS 9.2) that could allow unauthenticated remote code execution leaked online ahead of coordinated disclosure. Fixes are scheduled for 21 October at 14:00 UTC. watchTowr and Huntress say they have not seen exploitation yet; Sansec warns mass exploitation is likely within 15 minutes of the patch release, once attackers can diff the fix.
💡 Strategic take: This is the rare case where you know the exact hour the race starts. PHP sits under WordPress, Drupal, Magento and a long tail of club and member portals that nobody lists as critical. Book the change window for today, know which of your hosting providers patch for you and which do not, and put a WAF rule in front of anything you cannot patch by tonight.

**Attacker swapped OVHcloud's official CLI binaries for a secret stealer for about 94 minutes**
Source: SecurityWeek | [Link](https://www.securityweek.com/hijacked-developer-account-used-to-plant-stealer-in-ovhcloud-cli/)
A hacker used a developer's GitHub account to replace OVHcloud CLI release binaries for versions v0.11.0 to v0.15.0 with malware that steals secrets from disk, between 14:47 and 16:21 Paris time on 19 October. A customer and OpenSourceMalware reported it; OVHcloud blocked the binary and has since enabled GitHub's release immutability option. Researchers say the developer's account had been compromised in March with a GlassWorm-linked loader. The number of downloads is not disclosed.
💡 Strategic take: The account was owned for seven months before it was used. A cloud CLI sits on exactly the machines that hold cloud keys, so the payoff from one hijacked maintainer is every customer's credentials. Release immutability should be the default for anything you publish, and anyone who installed the CLI on 19 October should rotate their OVHcloud keys now.

**AOMEI Backupper's update server pushed a backdoor in five to ten minute bursts from April to July**
Source: SecurityWeek | [Link](https://www.securityweek.com/aomei-backupper-compromised-in-short-burst-supply-chain-campaign/)
Google's Threat Intelligence Group, Mandiant and ManageEngine's threat lab report that an attacker repeatedly tampered with AOMEI Backupper's update settings file and notification service, serving the CrossRoad downloader, the ShinyRoad backdoor, a modified Radmin Server and the Adaptix command-and-control framework. Each window stayed open only five to ten minutes. Victims were in higher education, IT services and telecoms across 11 countries including Australia. AOMEI dismissed the first report in early June and said it had acted only in late July after Google notified it again.
💡 Strategic take: Short bursts beat detection because nobody sees the update server serve something odd for ten minutes. The vendor dismissing the first warning is the part to put in procurement: ask suppliers how they handle a third-party compromise report, and how fast. Backup software runs with the highest privileges on the network, so it deserves the same scrutiny as your EDR.

**Ahold Delhaize restoring services after the 12 October attack, and says no data was stolen**
Source: SecurityWeek | [Link](https://www.securityweek.com/ahold-delhaize-restoring-services-disrupted-by-cyberattack/)
The grocery group took systems offline on 12 October, disrupting online orders, deliveries and e-commerce for most of its brands in Europe and the US. Many European sites and apps are back, Albert Heijn home deliveries are expected to resume by Friday and US e-commerce within days. The company says it has no evidence that customer, associate or partner data was stolen; no ransomware group has publicly claimed the attack, per SecurityWeek's report.
💡 Strategic take: More than a week of disrupted e-commerce is the real cost here, whatever the data outcome. For any business that sells online, the recovery time objective is a revenue number, and it should be priced as one before the incident, not during.

**Paradigm Healthcare breach of 970,000 Medi-Cal members took 11 months to reach notification**
Source: SecurityWeek | [Link](https://www.securityweek.com/970000-medi-cal-members-affected-by-paradigm-healthcare-data-breach/)
Paradigm Healthcare Services, a San Francisco billing processor for Medi-Cal, detected unauthorised access to its network on 13 October 2025; access ran from 8 to 15 October 2025. It determined what data was involved on 18 August 2026 and dated notification letters 14 September 2026. Names, dates of birth, gender and Medi-Cal member IDs for more than 970,000 people were exposed.
💡 Strategic take: Detection in a week, notification in eleven months. Under the Australian Notifiable Data Breaches scheme the assessment clock is 30 days, and a regulator will ask why a file review took ten months. If you cannot quickly say what data sits on a given server, you cannot meet any notification deadline, anywhere.

**Accenture, Dragos and SANS put about $10 million into free OT security for small US utilities**
Source: SecurityWeek | [Link](https://www.securityweek.com/accenture-dragos-sans-launch-10m-initiative-to-defend-small-us-utilities/)
The Community Cyber Resilience Foundation will give US water, wastewater, electric and gas utilities with under $100 million in annual revenue free security tools, equipment, deployment help and training, covering up to 500 utilities in its first phase. The roughly $10 million is a mix of funding and services. The backers are security vendors and service firms.
💡 Strategic take: About $20,000 per utility is a starting kit, not a programme, and the companies giving it are also the ones that sell the next stage. It is still useful because the gap it targets, small operators with no OT security staff, is real. Australia's smaller water and energy operators have the same gap and no equivalent scheme.

## 🤖 AI & Security

**Attackers ran AI agent platform Cairn against websites and breached more than 100 companies, including John Deere and SharkNinja per Reuters**
Source: SecurityWeek | [Link](https://www.securityweek.com/ai-platform-cairn-abused-to-hack-over-100-organizations/)
AI security firm Norforge found three malicious clusters using the open-source agent coordination platform Cairn after operators left their panels exposed. The largest, Chinese-language cluster ran more than 1,100 attack projects across 14 dashboards aimed at e-commerce and payment infrastructure in the US, Japan and Australia, seeking persistent webshell access; Reuters reports it breached over 100 companies. Norforge says the actors succeeded in roughly 10% of attempts, and 11% of hundreds of secrets found in the panels were valid. Norforge sells AI security, and the victim names come from Reuters, not the firm.
💡 Strategic take: A 10% hit rate is terrible for a human and excellent for software that never sleeps; the economics are the story. Reuters' headline frames it as one hacker and a few thousand dollars, which is the cost of a single pen test buying access to scores of firms. Australian payment and e-commerce sites are named targets, so assume you have already been probed.

**Alibaba's Qwen3-Coder wrote hackable code more often for a US defence contractor than a Chinese one, a vendor test finds**
Source: SecurityWeek | [Link](https://www.securityweek.com/chinese-ai-model-writes-more-hackable-code-when-told-its-for-us-contractor/)
Hirundo had Qwen3-Coder build more than 1,800 small web apps for a US defence contractor, a Chinese defence contractor and a Hong Kong democracy group. 70% of the US contractor's apps were hackable against 59% for the Chinese contractor, and asking for secure code widened the gap to 74%. Hirundo sells a "Westernized" retrained version it says removes the bias, so the finding and the fix come from the same seller; CrowdStrike and Booz Allen reported similar bias earlier.
💡 Strategic take: Running a model on-premise solves where your data goes, not what the model writes. For anyone choosing local open-weight models for sovereignty reasons, the test is simple and cheap: generate code under your own name and scan it. Model lineage now belongs in the software bill of materials.

**NIST, CISA and NSA publish ten practices for when AI agents go rogue**
Source: SecurityWeek | [Link](https://www.securityweek.com/us-agencies-detail-incident-response-practices-for-rogue-ai-agents/)
The voluntary guidance, published 16 October and covered by SecurityWeek on 20 October, groups ten practices under prepare, detect, respond, recover and report, and defines "AI control incident" and "AI control near-miss". It covers asset inventories, least privilege and credential management, isolating systems and revoking credentials in response, and reporting to affected parties, government and the public. It is written for frontier "super intelligence" developers, following executive order 14434's terminology.
💡 Strategic take: Nothing in the list is new, and that is the point: rogue agent response is ordinary incident response with a faster attacker. The useful addition is the near-miss definition, because the organisation that logs near-misses will see the CERT-In style incident coming. Borrow the checklist even though it was written for model labs.

## 📌 Today's Signal

Today's attackers did not need to be clever; they needed to be on time. PHP2Shell gives everyone the same start time, 14:00 UTC, and the bet is that exploitation follows within minutes. OVHcloud's CLI was poisoned for 94 minutes and AOMEI's updates for ten at a time, short enough to slip past anyone checking once a day. Cairn's agents attempt thousands of break-ins and keep the one in ten that works. On the other side of the ledger, SafePay is still getting in through flaws from 2020 to 2024, and Paradigm took eleven months to tell 970,000 people. The gap between attacker time and defender time is widening at both ends: machines compress the attack into minutes while organisations still measure patching and notification in weeks and months. For a decision-maker, the question is no longer whether you have a control, but how many minutes it takes to act on it.
