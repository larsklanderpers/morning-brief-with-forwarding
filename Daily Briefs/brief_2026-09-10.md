## 🚨 Government Advisories

### NSA, CISA & FBI Name Six Chinese AI Companies in Mass Distillation Alert
**Source:** CISA Advisory AA26-251A | [Northeast Times](https://northeasttimes.com/2026/09/09/nsa-cisa-and-fbi-name-six-chinese-ai-companies-in-mass-distillation-alert/) | [Executive Gov](https://www.executivegov.com/articles/nsa-fbi-cisa-warn-china-ai-distillation-attacks)

A joint advisory issued 8 September names DeepSeek, Moonshot AI, Alibaba, MiniMax, StepFun and Z.AI as having systematically extracted capabilities from Claude, GPT, Gemini and Grok at industrial scale since late 2024 — using fraudulent accounts, bulk subscriptions and proxy routing to evade detection. The agencies say the activity almost certainly had Chinese government backing, and that it is not just commercial theft: it directly strengthens China's military and cyberattack capabilities. Notably, Moonshot AI's Kimi K3 is specifically linked to distillation from Claude Fable; Kimi K2 to GPT-4o.

💡 **Strategic take:** The advisory arrives 14 days before Xi Jinping's Washington visit, and ahead of mid-September US-China AI safety talks — the diplomatic timing is not accidental. What makes this advisory different from prior IP-theft warnings is the explicit claim that model distillation is a weapons-development pathway, not just commercial free-riding. Boards need to understand that their AI provider relationships now sit inside an active intelligence competition, and AI API access controls — rate limits, anomalous-use detection, ToS enforcement — are the new export controls.

## 📰 Top Security News

### September 2026 Patch Tuesday: Record Patch Count, 2 Zero-Days, SigRed Successor
**Source:** [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/09/september-2026-patch-tuesday-zero-days-sigred-successor/) | 9 Sep 2026

Microsoft shipped a record-breaking patch load this month, including fixes for two actively exploited zero-days:

- **CVE-2026-81963** (Windows Update Stack) — privilege escalation to SYSTEM, reported by MSTIC; believed to be used in malware/ransomware delivery chains in combination with code-execution bugs.
- **CVE-2026-85880** (Windows ALPC) — second privilege-escalation-to-SYSTEM zero-day, reported by Proofpoint.
- **CVE-2026-69730** (Windows DNS) — described as "the spiritual successor to SigRed"; remote unauthenticated code execution, wormable potential across most Windows versions.
- **CVE-2026-69676** (Kerberos) — authentication bypass leading to RCE on domain controllers; one phished workstation account is sufficient to reach DC-level compromise. Microsoft rates this "Exploitation More Likely."
- **CVE-2026-55007** (Exchange Server) — RCE via malicious Visio attachment in email.
- Anonymous researcher Nightmare Eclipse published "ShieldCrash," a bypass for the already-patched CVE-2026-69414 (Microsoft Defender), hours after Tuesday's release.

💡 **Strategic take:** The DNS wormable flaw is the one to treat as a fire drill. SigRed (2020) was patched before widespread exploitation but sat in vulnerable estate for years; organisations that have not learned automated patch deployment cadences since then are structurally exposed. The Kerberos flaw is the quiet priority — domain-compromise from a single phished user is a board-level risk metric, not a patching footnote. Apply the cumulative update; don't triage these individually.

Browser update enforcement, not patch schedules, is the operative control here.

### Nearly 1 in 10 Exposed LiteLLM Gateways Accepted the Default "sk-1234" Admin Key
**Source:** [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/nearly-1-in-10-exposed-litellm-gateways.html) | 10 Sep 2026

Wiz scanned 3,074 publicly exposed LiteLLM API gateway instances and found 294 of them — roughly 9.6% — accepted the example admin key ("sk-1234") that ships in the default configuration. A successful authentication exposes all underlying AI provider API keys routed through the gateway and, in tested cases, granted cloud IAM access to connected infrastructure.

💡 **Strategic take:** This is the AI-infrastructure equivalent of finding "admin/admin" still set on network devices — except the blast radius includes billing exposure across every AI provider the gateway proxies. LiteLLM is widely used in enterprise RAG and agent deployments where teams move fast to stand up internal AI access layers. Security teams need an inventory pass across AI middleware with the same rigour they'd apply to exposed management consoles: default credentials are not a user-error story, they are a deployment-process story.

### Veradigm Patient Records Held for Ransom; Leak Deadline Friday
**Source:** [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/veradigm-discloses-patient-data-breach-after-gentlemen-gang-claims-attack/) | 9 Sep 2026

Veradigm has disclosed a patient data breach after The Gentlemen ransomware group claimed responsibility, asserting it holds 3.5 million records including full names, SSNs, addresses and contact details. The gang has set a leak deadline of Friday, 11 September. The Gentlemen list more than 800 victims across 86 countries; opportunistic access availability appears to be the selection criterion rather than sector targeting.

💡 **Strategic take:** Healthcare remains the highest-value ransomware target not because of technical vulnerability but because operational pressure — patient safety, regulatory timelines, reputational exposure — compresses the negotiation window. The Gentlemen's 800-victim breadth across 86 countries is the more strategically significant data point: this is not a targeted operation, it is industrialised credential-to-ransom arbitrage at scale.

## 🤖 AI & Security

### AI Now Weaponises Vulnerabilities Within 48 Hours — CrowdStrike & IBM Data
**Source:** [Tech Insider](https://tech-insider.org/ai-cyberattacks-48-hour-exploit-crowdstrike-2026/) | 10 Sep 2026

CrowdStrike's 2026 Threat Hunting Report (released 3 August, data from Jan–June 2026) and IBM's 2026 Cost of a Data Breach report (released 29 July) together constitute the clearest quantified picture of AI-accelerated offense to date:

| Metric | Figure |
|---|---|
| Vulnerabilities exploited within 48hrs of public PoC | **88%** |
| Average eCrime breakout time | **29 minutes** (fastest observed: 27 seconds) |
| Increase in AI-enabled adversary attacks YoY | **89%** |
| Increase in cloud-conscious eCrime activity | **171%** |
| AI-enabled breaches as share of all malicious breaches | **1 in 4** (up 56% YoY) |
| Average cost of AI-enabled breach | **$6.04M** (~$1M above non-AI average) |
| Shadow AI incidents as share of AI-related breaches | **43%** |

LLMjacking — attackers stealing cloud credentials to run workloads against an organisation's own AI model deployments — is now a documented attack class, with one case involving ~200,000 API requests in 2 minutes.

💡 **Strategic take:** The 48-hour exploitation window is not a future risk projection; it is the documented median reality for the first half of 2026. Vulnerability management programmes built on weekly scan cadences are structurally misaligned with this timeline. Any CVE with a public PoC needs same-day triage against internal asset inventory. The shadow AI figure — 43% of AI-related breaches — is the more actionable board lever: most organisations still cannot tell you every AI tool their workforce is using.

### NCSC Warns: Shadow AI Is Now a Documented Security Risk Category
**Source:** [NCSC Blog](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/blogs/the-hidden-risks-of-shadow-ai) | [Infosecurity Magazine](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/ncsc-warns-shadow-ai-security-risks/) | 7 Sep 2026

The UK's National Cyber Security Centre published guidance on shadow AI — unapproved AI tools used by staff — explicitly calling it a security risk category rather than a governance or productivity concern. The guidance frames the problem as understanding *why* staff adopt unauthorised tools, not just blocking them.

💡 **Strategic take:** The NCSC is one of the more technically credible national cyber agencies; when it publishes on an emerging risk category, the framing tends to precede regulatory requirement. Shadow AI now has the same documentation footprint that BYOD had in 2013 — within 18 months of similar advisories, BYOD became a formal compliance category. CISOs who have not started an AI tool inventory should treat this advisory as the clock starting.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The dominant thread across today's brief is not any single incident or patch — it is the compression of time. The NSA/CISA/FBI distillation advisory and the CrowdStrike/IBM data are two expressions of the same structural shift: AI has not just changed what attackers can do, it has changed how quickly they can do it. Capabilities that used to require nation-state resources (systematic model capability extraction, sub-30-minute network breakout, 48-hour PoC-to-exploit) are now documented in financially motivated criminal campaigns. Strategic decision-makers need to retire the mental model in which AI cyberattacks are an advanced, future-state concern. The data published this week is retrospective — it describes January to June 2026. The second half of the year will be worse.
