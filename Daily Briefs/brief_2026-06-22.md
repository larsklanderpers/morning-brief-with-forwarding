## 🚨 Government Advisories

### FortiBleed: Widespread Credential Exposure Affecting Fortinet Firewalls & VPN Gateways
**Sources:** [CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories) | [ACSC](https://www.cyber.gov.au/about-us/view-all-content/Reported-widespread-credential-exposure-affecting-Fortinet-Firewalls-and-VPN-Gateways)

CISA and the ASD's ACSC have both issued urgent advisories on "FortiBleed" — an active campaign by Russian-speaking threat actors targeting internet-exposed Fortinet FortiGate and VPN appliances via compromised credentials. 86,644 devices confirmed compromised as of 19 June. Generic admin accounts (35%) and built-in Fortinet system accounts (28.3%) are the primary attack vectors. Both agencies are directing all organisations to **immediately rotate all admin and VPN credentials** and enforce MFA.

💡 **Strategic take:** This isn't a novel exploit — it's credential hygiene failure at industrial scale. The fact that nearly 90,000 devices are compromised using built-in and generic accounts points to a systemic governance failure across the vendor's customer base. For boards and CISOs: if you're running Fortinet appliances and can't answer "who has admin credentials and when were they last rotated," that's your top action item this morning. The dual CISA-ACSC advisory signals Five Eyes-level concern — treat this as an active incident, not a patch advisory.

### CISA Adds BerriAI LiteLLM to Known Exploited Vulnerabilities — Due Date: Today
**Source:** [CISA KEV Catalog](https://www.cisa.gov/known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)

CVE-2026-42271 affecting BerriAI LiteLLM (a widely used open-source LLM proxy/router) has been added to CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog with a remediation due date of **22 June 2026**. Federal agencies are required to patch or mitigate today. The vulnerability affects a third-party library used broadly across AI infrastructure stacks.

💡 **Strategic take:** The addition of an AI infrastructure component to the KEV catalog is a leading indicator of where threat actors are focusing. As organisations rush to build LLM-powered applications on top of open-source orchestration layers, they're also inheriting the attack surface of those layers. Security teams that treat AI infrastructure as "someone else's problem" are building on sand — the attack surface of AI deployments needs the same patching discipline as any other production system.

## 📰 Top Security News

### North Korean Sapphire Sleet Poisons 140+ npm Packages in Mastra AI Supply Chain Attack
**Source:** [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/) | [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/)

Microsoft has attributed a sophisticated supply chain attack on the Mastra AI framework to North Korean hacking group Sapphire Sleet. More than 140 npm packages were compromised, potentially exposing any downstream project or developer that installed them. The campaign aligns with DPRK's persistent targeting of developer ecosystems for financial gain and intelligence collection.

💡 **Strategic take:** Supply chain attacks on AI tooling are the new frontier for nation-state actors. The Mastra AI ecosystem — popular with developers building agentic AI applications — represents exactly the kind of high-value, rapidly adopted tooling that attackers know organisations haven't had time to properly vet. Any organisation that has recently adopted open-source AI frameworks should treat their dependency graph as an attack surface and audit it accordingly. SBOM practices aren't optional anymore.

### GitHub Breach: ~3,800 Internal Repositories Allegedly Exfiltrated
**Source:** Community intelligence via r/netsec, multiple outlets

GitHub is investigating a breach linked to a compromised employee device. The threat group TeamPCP claims to have exfiltrated approximately 3,800+ internal repositories and is attempting to sell the data. GitHub has not yet confirmed the full scope.

💡 **Strategic take:** A breach of this scale at one of the world's most critical software development platforms is a systemic risk event, not just a corporate incident. Internal repositories at a platform like GitHub may contain security tooling, internal APIs, and infrastructure configurations. Until the scope is confirmed, any organisation that relies on GitHub's internal tooling or GitHub Actions should review their own exposure. The pattern — compromised employee device as initial access — remains the most common and most preventable attack path.

### Microsoft June 2026 Patch Tuesday: 206 Vulnerabilities, 3 Zero-Days
**Source:** [Zero Day Initiative](https://www.zerodayinitiative.com/blog/2026/6/9/the-june-2026-security-update-review) | [CrowdStrike](https://www.crowdstrike.com/en-us/blog/patch-tuesday-analysis-june-2026/)

- **CVE-2026-45657** — Windows Kernel RCE, CVSS 9.8, unauthenticated remote exploitation
- **CVE-2026-47291** — Windows HTTP Protocol Stack RCE via crafted packets
- **CVE-2026-42985** — Remote Desktop Client heap buffer overflow RCE

💡 **Strategic take:** 571 CVEs in a single month is not a statistical anomaly — it reflects AI-assisted vulnerability discovery dramatically accelerating research output. Defenders are facing a patching rate that no manual process can sustain. Organisations without automated patch prioritisation tied to live exploit intelligence are structurally unable to keep pace. The Windows Kernel CVSS 9.8 unauthenticated RCE should be your emergency patch for the week.

Higher education institutions remain chronically under-resourced for their threat exposure. Oracle PeopleSoft customers in particular need to treat this as an active incident: if you haven't patched CVE-2026-35273 yet, assume breach posture and investigate.

## 🤖 AI & Security

### NCSC UK: AI-Generated Code Is a Security Timebomb if Left Unchecked
**Source:** [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/ai-news/britain-cyber-ai-code-security-disasters/) | [NCSC](https://www.ncsc.gov.uk/collection/guidelines-secure-ai-system-development/guidelines)

Britain's National Cyber Security Centre warned on 22 June that the shift to AI-assisted software development ("vibe coding") poses serious hidden security risks when teams trust AI output without proper scrutiny. The NCSC distinguishes between low-risk use cases (acceptable) and critical systems (requires thorough review, testing, and auditing). Key concern: AI-generated code can introduce vulnerabilities and create systems that are difficult to audit or maintain.

💡 **Strategic take:** The NCSC's framing is deliberately measured — this isn't a blanket ban, it's a risk-stratification argument. But the underlying concern is acute: organisations are deploying AI-generated code at pace into production systems that their security teams don't fully understand. The "move fast" culture of AI adoption is colliding with the "move carefully" imperative of security. CISOs need a clear policy on AI-assisted development that distinguishes between internal tooling and customer-facing critical infrastructure — and that policy needs teeth.

### Cloud Security Alliance: 92% of Security Leaders Concerned About AI Agents
**Source:** [Cloud Security Alliance](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/blog/2026/05/27/state-of-ai-cybersecurity-2026-92-of-security-professionals-concerned-about-the-impact-of-ai-agents) | [Darktrace State of AI Cybersecurity 2026](https://www.darktrace.com/blog/the-state-of-ai-cybersecurity-2026)

The CSA's *State of AI Cybersecurity 2026* report (1,500+ security leaders surveyed) finds 92% are concerned about AI agents across the workforce. Darktrace's companion report notes 67.3% of analysed threat actors used AI for initial reconnaissance and malware writing, with 6.5% using it for complex lateral movement tasks.

💡 **Strategic take:** The asymmetry is stark — defenders are anxious about AI agents while attackers are already deploying them. The 6.5% using AI for lateral movement is the number to watch: as that percentage grows, dwell time will compress and the window for detection and response will shrink further. Organisations still measuring security maturity by perimeter controls are measuring the wrong thing.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The through-line in today's brief is **the industrialisation of cyber offense**. FortiBleed isn't sophisticated — it's 87,000 devices compromised via bad credential hygiene, exploited at industrial scale. ShinyHunters isn't targeting one victim — it's running parallel ransomware campaigns, exploiting ERP zero-days, and exfiltrating universities simultaneously. The Gentlemen RaaS is packaging EDR bypass tools as a subscription service for affiliates. And DPRK's Sapphire Sleet is poisoning the AI developer tooling ecosystem with supply chain attacks designed to scale. The common thread: attackers have automated, scaled, and commoditised capabilities that most defenders are still responding to manually. The organisations that will weather 2026 are the ones that have matched that automation with their own — in patching, in detection, in identity governance. Everyone else is playing defence with last decade's playbook.
