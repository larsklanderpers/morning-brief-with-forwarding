## 🚨 Government Advisories

### CISA: Defending Against Active Threat to Siemens S7 Series PLCs
**Source:** CISA / DoD | [Joint Advisory PDF](https://media.defense.gov/2026/Aug/18/2003983494/-1/-1/1/CSA_ACTIVE_THREAT_TO_SIEMENS_S7_SERIES_PLCS.PDF)

US cybersecurity agencies have issued a joint advisory warning that threat actors are using AI-generated scripts to actively exploit Siemens S7 Series programmable logic controllers in US critical infrastructure. The advisory details active intrusion techniques targeting OT environments.

💡 **Strategic take:** This is the clearest confirmation yet that AI has crossed from the phishing desk into the OT environment — attackers are now scripting ICS exploits at machine speed. For any organisation running industrial control systems, the question is no longer whether OT-specific threat intelligence is a priority; it's how fast the gap between IT and OT security practices can be closed. Board-level conversations about critical infrastructure resilience need to start now, not after the first incident.

### CISA KEV: MLflow SSRF Added (August 19)
**Source:** CISA | [Alert](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/08/19/cisa-adds-one-known-exploited-vulnerability-catalog)

CISA added CVE-2026-64849, an MLflow Server-Side Request Forgery vulnerability, to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on August 19. This follows four additions on August 18: Microsoft IKE double-free (CVE-2026-33824), SharePoint weak authentication (CVE-2026-55040), VMware vCenter path traversal (CVE-2026-59310), and Apple macOS improper authentication (CVE-2026-65400).

💡 **Strategic take:** MLflow landing in the KEV catalog is the tell: AI/ML infrastructure is now a primary attack surface, not a secondary concern. Organisations that spun up MLflow environments quickly during the generative AI build-out often skipped the security hygiene applied to production systems. The Monday question to any CISO should be: where does MLflow sit in your network, and is it patched?

### ACSC: N-able N-central Authentication Bypass — Actively Exploited in Australia
**Source:** ACSC / Australian Cyber Security Magazine | [Report](https://australiancybersecuritymagazine.com.au/acsc-warns-of-active-exploitation-targeting-n-able-n-central-in-australia/)

The Australian Cyber Security Centre has issued a high alert confirming active exploitation in Australia of CVE-2026-18556 and CVE-2026-18577 — authentication bypass vulnerabilities (CVSS 8.2) in N-able N-central, a remote monitoring and management platform widely used by MSPs. Patches were released August 1; Hotfix 2 followed August 6. Upgrade is the priority action.

💡 **Strategic take:** N-able N-central sits at the crown of the MSP trust model — it authenticates *into* client environments. An auth bypass here isn't a single-organisation breach; it's a skeleton key for the MSP's entire customer estate. Australian SMBs relying on MSPs should be asking their provider this week: are you patched, and have you reviewed your N-central exposure? Regulators and insurers are watching MSP security posture more closely in 2026 than ever before.

### CISA #StopRansomware: Gunra Ransomware
**Source:** CISA | [Advisory](https://media.defense.gov/2026/Aug/10/2003976697/-1/-1/0/CSA_STOPRANSOMWARE_GUNRA_RANSOMWARE.PDF)

CISA released a joint advisory on Gunra ransomware, a double-extortion variant that emerged in April 2025 and has been escalating through 2026. The advisory covers TTPs, indicators of compromise, and mitigations.

💡 **Strategic take:** Double-extortion is now the baseline, not the exception. Gunra's emergence-to-advisory timeline shows threat actors are iterating faster than defenders can document — by the time a formal advisory ships, most victims are already six months downstream. Organisations without offline, immutable backups and tested recovery playbooks are, at this point, accepting ransomware risk as a business decision.

## 📰 Top Security News

### Microsoft August 2026 Patch Tuesday: 421 CVEs, One Exploited Zero-Day
**Source:** SecurityWeek | [Article](https://www.securityweek.com/august-2026-patch-tuesday-microsoft-fixes-421-cves-one-exploited-zero-day/)

Microsoft patched 421 vulnerabilities in August 2026 Patch Tuesday, including 62 critical. The actively exploited zero-day is CVE-2026-68820, a use-after-free in the Windows Ancillary Function Driver for WinSock that allows privilege escalation to SYSTEM. Three CVEs carry CVSS 9.8: CVE-2026-62878 (Windows DNS Server RCE, wormable), CVE-2026-62893 (Deployment Services TFTP RCE), and CVE-2026-62815 (Microsoft QUIC RCE).

💡 **Strategic take:** A wormable DNS Server RCE at CVSS 9.8 deserves immediate attention regardless of where you are in your patch cycle — CVE-2026-62878 requires no authentication and no user interaction. Any organisation with Windows DNS Server internet-exposed is in acute risk. The volume (421 CVEs in one month) also reinforces the structural problem: the patch economics are broken, and triage capability has become as important as remediation speed.

### Slovakia Discovers Russian Backdoor in NERO R-ONE Speedometers
**Source:** IT Security News | [Summary](https://www.itsecuritynews.info/it-security-news-daily-summary-2026-08-19)

Slovak authorities confirmed on August 19 that a Russian-origin backdoor was discovered in NERO R-ONE speedometers. The incident represents a continued pattern of hardware-level supply chain interference in European infrastructure products.

💡 **Strategic take:** Hardware backdoors in speed measurement and road infrastructure equipment sit in the ungoverned space between physical security, transportation regulation, and cybersecurity. This isn't a novel vector — it's a known risk that most governments have not operationalised detection for. The significance is less the specific device and more the confirmation that state actors continue to invest in supply-chain access for physical-world systems.

### CEVA Logistics Hit by Ransomware; Eight European Warehouses Disrupted
**Source:** Cybernews / Bleeping Computer

CEVA Logistics suffered a ransomware attack disrupting contract logistics operations at eight European warehouses, causing shipment delays across multiple customers. This adds to the Medusa ransomware group's record of targeting over 500 critical infrastructure organisations.

💡 **Strategic take:** Logistics is the operational backbone of manufacturing, retail, and healthcare supply chains. A ransomware hit on a major 3PL isn't just an IT incident — it cascades into inventory shortfalls, SLA breaches, and in healthcare contexts, potential patient harm. Organisations assessing third-party risk need to include logistics partners alongside technology vendors, a gap that most TPRM programs haven't closed.

Heights Finance confirmed unauthorised access to a third-party cloud system containing customer records, affecting over 1.2 million individuals across multiple US state filings. Data location must follow data liability, not procurement convenience.

### StubMaker: Typosquatting Campaign Targets RubyGems Users
**Source:** IT Security News / OpenSourceMalware | [Summary](https://www.itsecuritynews.info/it-security-news-daily-summary-2026-08-19)

Security researchers discovered a typosquatting campaign targeting RubyGems users deploying a Windows-based information stealer, tracked as StubMaker. The campaign was identified August 15 and is consistent with ongoing supply-chain targeting of open-source package registries.

Organisations relying on manual dependency audits are operating on hope. Software composition analysis in CI/CD pipelines is the floor, not an upgrade.

## 🤖 AI & Security

### AI Agents Can Now Infect Each Other: Anthropic/EPFL Worm Research
**Source:** Preprint (Aug 10, 2026) | referenced in IT Security News

Researchers from Anthropic and EPFL demonstrated that self-propagating payloads can spread from one AI agent to another through system prompt files, functioning as an AI-native worm. The work was released as a preprint on August 10, 2026.

💡 **Strategic take:** This is the first credible demonstration that multi-agent AI architectures have an intrinsic propagation risk — not just from compromised models, but from the trust relationships between agents themselves. As enterprises accelerate agentic AI deployment, the threat model must include prompt injection as a worm vector. Security teams need to be involved in agentic AI architecture design before deployment, not in incident response after.

### State of AI Cybersecurity 2026: Identity Is the New Ransomware
**Source:** Cloud Security Alliance | [Report](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/blog/2026/04/02/the-state-of-ai-cybersecurity-2026-unveiling-insights-from-over-1-500-security-leaders)

The CSA's State of AI Cybersecurity 2026 report (1,500+ security leaders) identifies hyper-personalized phishing (50%), automated exploit chaining (45%), and adaptive malware (40%) as top concerns. Identity is emerging as the primary battleground, eclipsing ransomware. 77% of organisations now use GenAI in their security stack; 67% have deployed agentic AI for autonomous operations.

💡 **Strategic take:** The parity point has arrived: defenders are automating at roughly the same rate as attackers, which means the advantage goes to whichever side executes operationally, not technologically. Organisations that treat AI security tools as a procurement exercise rather than a capability-building one are buying headcount efficiency without buying resilience.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The dominant thread across today's intelligence is the deliberate expansion of attack surface into infrastructure that most organisations have not yet brought under consistent security governance: OT/ICS environments, ML infrastructure, MSP management platforms, and logistics networks. The CISA advisory on Siemens PLCs and the EPFL agent-worm research share a common implication — AI is no longer accelerating known attack categories, it's enabling new ones. The Siemens advisory is particularly significant because it marks the first government-confirmed use of AI-generated scripts in active ICS exploitation, not a proof-of-concept or red team exercise. Decision-makers who have been treating OT security as a deferred IT problem and ML infrastructure as exempt from the same controls applied to production systems should treat today's briefing as the deadline for revising that position.
