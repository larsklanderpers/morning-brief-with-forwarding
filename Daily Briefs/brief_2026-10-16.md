## 🚨 Government Advisories

**CISA adds the Atlassian Data Center file-read flaw to KEV, ten days after exploitation began**
Source: CISA | [Link](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/10/15/cisa-adds-one-known-exploited-vulnerability-catalog)
On 15 October CISA added CVE-2026-21589, listed as an Atlassian "Files or Directories Accessible to External Parties" vulnerability across multiple products, to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog. This brief covered the flaw on 9 October, when watchTowr reported in-the-wild exploitation hours after a public proof of concept; what moved is the federal listing, which starts a remediation clock for US agencies.
💡 Strategic take: KEV is a lagging indicator by design, and this one lagged about ten days behind the honeypots. If your patch policy for self-hosted Jira and Confluence waits for CISA to say "exploited", you have built a process that guarantees you patch after the attackers arrive. Use KEV to audit what you missed, not to decide what to fix first.

**No new ACSC alert overnight; the 14 October OT alert is still the latest**
Source: Australian Signals Directorate's ACSC | [Link](https://www.cyber.gov.au/alert/protecting-operational-technology-and-edge-devices)
The ACSC alerts and advisories page shows the 14 October alert on operational technology and edge devices, including refrigeration and cooling, as its newest item, followed by the 9 October joint advisory on Chinese state-linked data theft.
💡 Strategic take: Two quiet days on the Australian side is not an all-clear, it is the window to work Wednesday's alert into a vendor-access review. Any club or venue whose cellar, kitchen or HVAC contractor has standing remote access should know by Monday who holds those credentials.

## 📰 Top Security News

**Cyberattack on contractor BAE Systems takes down the US federal bankruptcy courts' notice system**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/bae-systems-cyberattack-disrupts-us-federal-court-operations/)
The Administrative Office of the US Courts said on Thursday that all access to the Bankruptcy Noticing Center systems and websites BAE operates for the judiciary, and the related noticing process, is unavailable after a cyberattack on BAE. The AO says BAE "is actively investigating this incident and working to restore operations"; BAE did not immediately comment, and no ransomware group or data theft claim has been reported so far.
💡 Strategic take: The court did not get hacked, its outsourced post room did, and the court still cannot send notices. That is the shape of most modern outages: your resilience is capped by the weakest contractor running a function you stopped thinking of as yours. The board question is not "is BAE secure" but "which of our statutory obligations does a single vendor carry, and what is the manual fallback".

**BeyondTrust patches Remote Support zero-day exploited since late August, attributed to a China-nexus actor**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/beyondtrust-patches-zero-day-exploited-in-attacks-since-august/)
CVE-2026-102764 is an authentication bypass in BeyondTrust Remote Support 26.2.3 and earlier, spotted in attacks by Mandiant and Google Threat Intelligence Group, who link it to a suspected China-nexus espionage actor they track as Armory Castle (formerly UNC5221); CrowdStrike tracks the same actor as Warp Panda. BeyondTrust patched SaaS customers on 8 October; self-hosted installations without automatic updates must be patched manually.
💡 Strategic take: Remote support tools are a master key you hand to strangers on purpose, which is why espionage crews keep queuing at that door. Seven weeks of quiet exploitation before disclosure means patching closes the hole but says nothing about who already walked through it. If you run self-hosted BeyondTrust, treat this as a hunt, not a change ticket.

**Attackers exploit Collabora flaw to drop webshells on self-hosted Nextcloud servers**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-exploit-collabora-rce-flaw-to-backdoor-nextcloud-servers/)
CVE-2026-110447 is an unauthenticated OS command injection in the Collabora Online Built-in CODE Server app (richdocumentscode) for Nextcloud before 26.4.303, triggered through a request to its proxy.php endpoint. Administrator reports describe PHP webshells used to hunt for password databases, password-manager artifacts and cryptocurrency wallets; the number of affected servers is not stated.
💡 Strategic take: Self-hosted Nextcloud is often the sovereignty answer: keep the files onshore, off the hyperscalers. The trade is that you also own the patch cadence of every plug-in someone enabled once for a demo. Sovereignty without an inventory is just a server nobody is watching.

**Citizen Lab and Confiant: ad platform delivered Candiru spyware zero-click through rendered adverts**
Source: The Record | [Link](https://therecord.media/spyware-citizen-lab-advertising)
Parallel investigations by the Citizen Lab and ad-security firm Confiant say a firm called Discover-Tech, through a platform named Controlium, let spyware clients "deanonymize, fingerprint and automatically infect devices the moment an advertisement is rendered". Targets reportedly included at least 45 Al Jazeera journalists and networks at NATO, the US Department of Defense, Estonia's RIA, the IAEA and the International Criminal Court. Confiant sells ad-quality and malvertising protection, so one of the two investigators has a commercial stake in the finding; the Citizen Lab does not.
💡 Strategic take: Programmatic advertising is a global system for running a stranger's code on your screen within milliseconds, sold by the impression. That makes it the most efficient targeting infrastructure ever built, and someone has noticed it works for implants too. For high-risk staff, ad blocking on managed devices has quietly moved from preference to control.

**Proxyjacking campaign hijacks NAS and video recorders in 34 countries to resell victims' bandwidth**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-hijack-d-link-ubiquiti-devices-to-resell-your-stolen-bandwidth/)
GreyNoise reports 143 compromised devices across 34 countries, broken into via old flaws including CVE-2024-3272 and CVE-2024-3273 in D-Link ShareCenter NAS, Log4Shell (CVE-2021-44228) in Ubiquiti UniFi Video NVR and CVE-2025-31324 in SAP NetWeaver, with Hikvision and Dahua recorders also targeted. The bandwidth is monetised through the commercial residential proxy network Pawns.app.
💡 Strategic take: The device count is small; the economics are the story. A five-year-old bug on a forgotten camera recorder now earns rent by the gigabyte, and the traffic leaving through your IP will be someone else's credential stuffing. End-of-life gear on the edge is not dormant risk, it is a revenue stream for someone else.

**Suspected Kazakhstan-linked espionage campaign hits governments across nine former Soviet states**
Source: The Record | [Link](https://therecord.media/suspected-kazakhstan-phishing-central-asia-espionage)
Cisco Talos (tracking UAT-6275) and Recorded Future's Insikt Group (tracking TAG-193) describe a campaign running since January 2025 against government institutions in nine countries including Russia, Belarus, Armenia, Uzbekistan and Tajikistan, using phishing from already-compromised government mailboxes, GitHub-hosted payloads and the WinRAR flaw CVE-2025-8088. Attribution to Kazakhstan is at medium confidence. The Record is published by Recorded Future, one of the two research sources.
💡 Strategic take: Mid-tier states now run the same playbook as the big four: compromise one ministry's mailbox, then phish its neighbours from a trusted sender. The defensive lesson travels: mail from a known government domain is a claim of identity, not proof of it. Expect more regional actors, not fewer, as the tooling gets cheaper.

## 🤖 AI & Security

**Google rolls out on-device and cloud AI scam detection for Android messages and calls**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/android-gets-ai-defenses-against-messaging-scams-fake-calls/)
Google announced Gemini-backed scam detection in Google Messages, with complex conversations analysed in its encrypted Private AI Compute cloud environment and link checks done on-device by Gemini Nano. Android will also block apps from silently enabling call forwarding and warn before manually entered forwarding codes take effect, and its verified bank calls feature, which can end calls that fail verification, is expanding to more institutions including Revolut, Itaú Unibanco and Banco do Brasil.
💡 Strategic take: Note where the processing lives: the simple checks run on the handset, the hard ones go to Google's cloud, under a privacy promise rather than a locality guarantee. For regulated businesses the call-forwarding block is the quiet win, because SIM and forwarding abuse is how one-time passcodes get stolen. The verified-calls model, where the bank proves itself to the phone, is the pattern Australian banks and clubs should be asking their telcos about.

**Two vendor-sponsored posts in two days argue AI pentesting is ready, each selling the answer**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/is-ai-pentesting-as-good-as-a-human-pentester/)
On 15 October BleepingComputer ran a sponsored piece by Intruder comparing AI and human pentesters, a day after a sponsored Picus Security post on the blind spots of agentic pentesting. Both conclude that AI agents find real vulnerabilities but miss business context and coverage, and both position their own product as the fix.
💡 Strategic take: Yesterday's Booz Allen claim of frontier models reaching an ICS network in about 16 minutes was a vendor's research; today's balancing view is also a vendor's marketing. Nobody independent is publishing the comparison yet, and that gap is the story. Until someone without a product to sell runs the test, buy AI pentesting as a supplement priced like one, not as a replacement for the human report your auditor actually reads.

## 📌 Today's Signal

Every material story this morning is about a function someone outsourced and then stopped watching. The bankruptcy courts lost their notices because a contractor was hit; BeyondTrust and Collabora are tools organisations invite in to do a job, and attackers rode them in for weeks; programmatic advertising turned out to be a delivery network for spyware; forgotten recorders are earning rent for proxy brokers. Even the AI pentesting debate is being argued by the people selling the tool. For a decision-maker, the question is less "are we patched" and more "which functions have we handed to someone else, and who on our side would notice if that someone was compromised tomorrow". That list, with a named owner per line, is the most useful security document most organisations do not have.
