# 🛡️ Cybersecurity Morning Brief

## 🚨 Government Advisories

**CISA adds the Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator zero-day to its exploited list, two days after the patch**
Source: CISA | [Link](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/10/23/cisa-adds-one-known-exploited-vulnerability-catalog)
On 23 October CISA added CVE-2026-114625, a SQL injection flaw in Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator, to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on evidence of active exploitation. This moves Friday's item: Arista's patch for its fourth exploited VeloCloud zero-day of the year is now a federal remediation obligation under BOD 26-04.
💡 Strategic take: Four exploited zero-days in one SD-WAN orchestrator in a year is no longer bad luck. That is a product category telling you something. If your branch network is run from a single on-premise console, treat it like the domain controller it effectively is: segment it, log it, and price in the cost of a forced migration.

**CISA flags an exploited command-injection flaw in Collabora Online, the engine behind many self-hosted office suites**
Source: CISA | [Link](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/10/23/cisa-adds-one-known-exploited-vulnerability-catalog-0)
CISA's second 23 October KEV addition is CVE-2026-110447, an OS command injection vulnerability in Collabora Online's built-in CODE server. Collabora is the document editor commonly embedded in Nextcloud and other self-hosted collaboration stacks.
💡 Strategic take: This one lands on exactly the organisations that left SaaS office suites for sovereignty reasons. Bringing documents in-house is a sound privacy decision, but it also brings the patch duty in-house. Sovereignty without a patch cadence is just a different place to be breached.

**No new ACSC alert since Friday's Fortinet notice**
Source: Australian Cyber Security Centre | [Link](https://www.cyber.gov.au/alerts-and-advisories)
A live check of the ACSC alerts page shows the most recent item is still the 23 October critical Fortinet alert, followed by the 21 October PHP alert. Nothing new was published over the weekend.
💡 Strategic take: Both open alerts are internet-facing classes of kit that attackers scan on weekends. The Fortinet technical detail is held until 10 November, so the window between patch-now and here-is-how is the one to close.

## 📰 Top Security News

**PHP2Shell exploitation attempts are everywhere, confirmed compromises are not, and 41 million servers are still unpatched**
Source: The Record | [Link](https://therecord.media/php-php2shell-exploitation-attempts-pocs)
This moves the week's lead story. Dozens of experts told Recorded Future News that none of the observed attempts had succeeded as of Friday, that most public proof-of-concept exploits only work in lab conditions, and that ASLR is stopping mass exploitation. Sophos retracted an earlier report of successful exploitation, and Google's John Hultquist said exploitation "may have already begun in a limited fashion". VulnCheck says it has built a working ASLR bypass but has not released it. Censys counted more than 41 million internet-facing PHP services on affected versions and about 1 million on fixed releases as of 22 October.
💡 Strategic take: A 40-to-1 unpatched ratio three days after a CISA take-it-offline notice is the real headline. The quiet is a lab constraint, not a defence: someone is already scanning and building target lists, so when a reliable exploit leaks the lists are ready. Use the borrowed time to patch, not to argue about whether "exploitation" means attempts or success.

**eMudhra quits the global certificate business after its CA keys turned out to be the numbers 4, 56 and 115**
Source: The Record | [Link](https://therecord.media/indian-tech-company-announces-withdrawal-certificate-business-incident)
This moves Thursday's item. eMudhra, one of the only Indian root CAs trusted by major browsers, said it is withdrawing from SSL/TLS after researcher Scott Helme showed that private keys on eight intermediates were trivially weak. The company says the flaw came from one step of an April 2025 key ceremony and survived two independent audits for 18 months. Google says it is "closely investigating" eMudhra's remaining roots in the Chrome store. The revoked intermediates were not due to expire until April 2040.
💡 Strategic take: Two clean audits and a private key equal to four. That is the clearest argument this year that a certificate of compliance is not a control. The long tail is the cost: embedded devices, old containers and vendor appliances carry their own trust bundles, and most organisations have no inventory of where those live.

**Quest Diagnostics discloses unauthorised access to the lab platform clinicians use to order tests and read results**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/quest-diagnostics-confirms-data-breach-exposed-patient-records/)
Quest told the SEC it learned on 21 October of unauthorised access to Quanum LSM, the online system physicians and hospital staff use to place orders and look up results, and that some patient records were exposed. The data types are not yet known, the system is offline, customers have been moved to paper requisitions, and Quest has paused two other client systems as a precaution. No group has claimed the attack. Quest says it serves one in three US adults each year.
💡 Strategic take: Healthcare keeps getting breached through the portal it hands to partners, not the core lab system. Any business that gives third parties a login into customer data, which includes every club and casino loyalty platform, should assume that partner-facing door is the one being tried first.

**APT41-linked BlackX bootkit beat Secure Boot on fully patched Windows because nobody flipped the manual revocation**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/blackx-uefi-bootkit-exploits-baton-drop-flaw-to-bypass-secure-boot/)
Kaspersky reports a UEFI bootkit used against Central Asian diplomatic agencies earlier this year, attributed with medium confidence to APT41. It exploits Baton Drop (CVE-2022-21894), the 2022 flaw BlackLotus used. Every victim was fully patched, but the fix requires admins to manually revoke the "Windows Production PCA 2011" certificate, and none had. Kaspersky warns that the certificate's 19 October 2026 expiry does not stop vulnerable binaries loading unless it is blocklisted.
💡 Strategic take: "Fully patched" is a status your dashboard reports, not a fact about your boot chain. Any fix that needs a manual second step is, in practice, a fix most of the estate never received. Ask your team one question on Monday: have we applied the Baton Drop revocations, and how would we know?

**HPE patches 72 ArubaOS flaws, eight of them maximum severity**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hpe-patches-eight-max-severity-rce-and-auth-bypass-aos-flaws/)
Hewlett Packard Enterprise released updates for 72 vulnerabilities in the Aruba Operating System, including eight maximum-severity authentication bypass and remote code execution issues. No exploitation was reported at publication.
💡 Strategic take: Network operating systems are now on the same patch clock as web stacks, and this week alone that is PHP, Fortinet, VeloCloud and now Aruba. The constraint is no longer finding the bugs; it is maintenance windows on kit that runs the building.

**Windows Server 2016 stops getting security updates in January**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/microsoft/microsoft-windows-server-2016-reaches-end-of-support-in-january/)
Microsoft reminded customers that Windows Server 2016 reaches end of support in January 2027, after which it receives no security updates.
💡 Strategic take: Ten weeks is not a migration window for anything running a line-of-business app. The decision now is economic: budget the move, or budget the extended security update fees, but do not let it default to neither.

**Researchers remotely shut off an abandoned Popcorn Time fork's updater that exposed millions of PCs to code execution**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/abandoned-popcorn-time-app-exposed-millions-of-device-to-rce-attacks/)
Researchers disabled a vulnerable updater in Time4Popcorn, an abandoned fork of the Popcorn Time BitTorrent client, closing a flaw that could have let attackers run code with elevated privileges on millions of computers.
💡 Strategic take: Abandonware with a live update channel is a standing backdoor waiting for whoever registers the expired domain. The same pattern sits inside enterprise estates as forgotten agents and dead vendor tools, and an asset inventory that cannot list them cannot remove them.

## 🤖 AI & Security

**Microsoft ties the ConnectWise supply-chain attack to a Chinese state group running ARTEX AI agents, at 2,900 sign-ins a minute**
Source: The Record | [Link](https://therecord.media/china-cyber-connectwise-ai)
Missed on Wednesday and worth catching up. Microsoft attributes the ConnectWise Cloud Backup compromise to Storm-0940, which used ARTEX, an open-source agentic penetration-testing framework, to abuse already-consented apps into Microsoft 365 tenants across government, defence, finance and energy. In several cases the group went from targeting to privileged access in under three hours. Microsoft counted 2,900 sign-in attempts per minute and more than 111,000 failed logons on 11 October alone. ARTEX's developer says it was built for authorised testing and has taken it closed source.
💡 Strategic take: This is the agentic attack model with a named state actor and a supply-chain victim attached. Three hours from first touch to privileged cloud persistence is faster than most SOCs can triage a ticket. The control that matters is not detection speed; it is what third-party apps already hold consent in your tenant and whether you could revoke them in an hour.

**Teams will start reading conversation context for phishing from November, and Microsoft says it will not keep the messages**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/microsoft/microsoft-teams-to-get-ai-powered-context-aware-phishing-detection/)
Microsoft says Teams will roll out AI-powered, context-aware phishing detection from early November, aimed at external first-contact chats and switched on with no admin action. Microsoft states the content is used only for security and is not retained for model training or other uses. General availability is expected in December.
💡 Strategic take: Defaults matter: this switches on AI analysis of external chats in every tenant without a decision being made. For regulated Australian and EU businesses the question is not whether it works but whether the privacy impact assessment covers it, and right now the only assurance on retention is the vendor's own statement.

## 📌 Today's Signal

Today's thread is the gap between "patched" and "safe". BlackX beat Secure Boot on fully patched machines because the fix needed a manual step nobody took. eMudhra's keys passed two audits while one of them was the number four. PHP2Shell has 41 million exposed servers against one million fixed, held back only by a lab constraint that one researcher has already broken. And Microsoft's ConnectWise write-up shows an agent-driven state group going from first touch to privileged cloud access in three hours, through apps that already had consent. Every one of these is a status report that said green while the real control was absent. For a decision-maker, the question this week is simple to ask and hard to answer: which of our "done" controls have we actually verified, and which ones only report as done?
