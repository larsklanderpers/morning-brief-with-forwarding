## 🚨 Government Advisories

### CISA — Lantronix EDS5000 Critical Flaw Actively Exploited (Patch Deadline: Today)
**Source:** CISA | [Alert](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/06/25/cisa-adds-two-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)

CVE-2025-67038 (CVSS 9.8) — a code injection flaw in the HTTP RPC module of Lantronix EDS5000 Series devices — allows unauthenticated attackers to inject OS commands executed as root. FCEB agencies had until today to apply patches. Disclosed by Forescout Research Vedere Labs in April 2026.

💡 **Strategic take:** A CVSS 9.8 in a network-edge serial device is about as bad as it gets. Lantronix appliances sit in OT/IT convergence zones — power, manufacturing, utilities — and are frequently overlooked in patching cycles. If you haven't scanned for these in your environment, do it today. Attackers will have these fingerprinted and queued before the advisory clears the news cycle.

### CISA KEV Update — Cisco Unified CM SSRF & Ubiquiti UniFi
**Source:** CISA | [Jun 25 Alert](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/06/25/cisa-adds-two-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog) | [Jun 23 Alert](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/06/23/cisa-adds-four-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)

CISA added CVE-2026-20230 (Cisco Unified CM SSRF, CVSS 8.6) to the KEV catalog on June 25 after active exploitation was confirmed. Attackers are using crafted HTTP requests to write files that enable privilege escalation to root. Separately, CVE-2026-34910 (Ubiquiti UniFi OS improper input validation) was added June 23.

💡 **Strategic take:** The Cisco Unified CM flaw is particularly dangerous because it targets communications infrastructure — phone systems and collaboration platforms that enterprises consider "stable" and rarely patch aggressively. Post-exploitation root access on a UCM cluster gives attackers a listening post inside every voice and video interaction. Treat this like a data-tier vulnerability.

### ACSC — ClickFix Campaign Targeting Australian Networks via WordPress
**Source:** Australian Cyber Security Centre | [cyber.gov.au](https://www.cyber.gov.au/about-us/view-all-content/alerts-and-advisories)

Threat actors are targeting Australian organisations with ClickFix social engineering delivered through compromised WordPress sites. The technique tricks users into executing malware by presenting fake browser update prompts requiring manual clipboard-based installation steps — bypassing automated defences. ACSC has published indicators and mitigations.

💡 **Strategic take:** ClickFix is a masterclass in weaponising user trust in "helpful" prompts. It bypasses email filtering, endpoint AV, and even sandboxing because the user is the execution engine. Effective defence here is purely behavioural — and that requires security awareness training that keeps pace with social engineering innovation, not last year's phishing modules.

## 📰 Top Security News

### Amadey & StealC Malware Network Disrupted — 27M Credentials Recovered
**Source:** The Hacker News | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/06/amadey-and-stealc-malware-network.html)

Europol, Bitdefender, ESET, and Microsoft dismantled the infrastructure behind Amadey and StealC — two of the most widely deployed infostealer platforms in the criminal ecosystem. 326 servers and 142 domains taken down. Over 27 million stolen credentials recovered, and $47M in cryptocurrency identified and restricted. Follows a separate SocGholish disruption that cleaned ~15,000 infected WordPress sites.

💡 **Strategic take:** Consecutive takedowns at this scale signal a maturation in public-private threat intelligence cooperation. But operational disruption is not eradication — Amadey and StealC have been "taken down" before, and reconstitution timelines for these platforms are shrinking. The 27 million recovered credentials should be treated as confirmed-compromised and force-reset, not celebrated and shelved.

### Cordyceps — CI/CD Flaw Exposes 300+ GitHub Repos Including Microsoft, Google, Apache
**Source:** The Hacker News | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/06/cordyceps-cicd-flaws-expose-300-github.html)

Novee Security has identified a critical, unauthenticated GitHub Actions workflow flaw (codenamed Cordyceps) that allows any free GitHub account to forge approvals, push code, or steal credentials in over 300 major repositories. Named after the parasitic fungus for its ability to hijack host behaviour. Repositories at Microsoft, Google, Apache, and Cloudflare are confirmed fully exploitable.

💡 **Strategic take:** This is a supply-chain attack surface hiding in plain sight. The flaw doesn't require org membership — any internet user can exploit it. The downstream blast radius of a compromised Apache or Cloudflare repo is effectively unbounded. Engineering teams need to audit their Actions workflow permissions today, specifically the `pull_request_target` trigger pattern that's at the core of this class of exploit.

### Chrome Ad Blocker (10M+ Installs) Contains Dormant Script Injection Capability
**Source:** The Hacker News | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/06/chrome-ad-blocker-with-10m-installs.html)

Island security researchers found that "Adblock for YouTube" (10M+ installs, Featured badge on Chrome Web Store) contains server-side-activatable arbitrary JavaScript execution — no extension update, no store review required. The capability can be triggered silently by a single server-side config change across every user simultaneously.

💡 **Strategic take:** The Chrome Web Store's "Featured" badge is meaningless as a security signal — this has now been demonstrated multiple times. Browser extensions are unmanaged endpoints for most enterprises, and one server-side config flip at an extension vendor is equivalent to a zero-day pushed to millions of browsers. Enterprise browser management and extension allowlisting are no longer optional postures.

**Source:** The Hacker News | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/06/cisco-catalyst-sd-wan-zero-day-cve-2026.html)

Mandiant confirmed an unknown threat actor exploited CVE-2026-20245 (Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN, CVSS 7.8) at least two months before Cisco publicly disclosed it. The actor used extensive anti-forensic techniques — selectively deleting and restoring configuration files — suggesting a sophisticated, persistent campaign with dwell-time as a primary objective.

This is the definition of a strategic position. Patching after disclosure doesn't undo pre-positioning. Assume breach posture on Cisco SD-WAN environments and hunt for lateral movement artifacts dating back to April.

## 🤖 AI & Security

### Gaslight — North Korean macOS Malware Uses Prompt Injection to Defeat AI Analysis
**Source:** The Hacker News | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/06/new-gaslight-macos-malware-uses-prompt.html)

SentinelOne researcher Phil Stokes disclosed "Gaslight" — a Rust-based macOS implant attributed with high confidence to North Korea that embeds fabricated system-failure messages designed to trick LLM-assisted triage agents into refusing or aborting malware analysis. Uses Telegram bot API for C2. The malware "attacks the agent's perception, rather than the sandbox it runs in."

💡 **Strategic take:** This is the first documented case of adversarial prompt injection deployed as an evasion technique against AI-assisted SOC tooling — not a proof of concept, but a live weapon. As AI co-pilots become standard in security operations, attackers will increasingly embed adversarial payloads targeting the AI layer, not just the analyst. AI-assisted triage tools need adversarial robustness testing now, before the capability proliferates.

### Self-Replicating AI Worm Built on Local Open-Weight Models — Researchers
**Source:** The Hacker News | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/06/researchers-build-self-replicating-ai.html)

Researchers demonstrated a self-replicating AI worm that operates entirely on local, open-weight models — requiring no cloud API or centralised infrastructure. The worm propagates autonomously, uses AI to adapt its behaviour, and cannot be blocked by cutting off access to commercial AI providers.

💡 **Strategic take:** The significance here is the "local, open-weight" qualifier. Air-gapped detection and perimeter controls on commercial AI APIs are now insufficient as a containment strategy. A self-replicating AI worm that runs on commodity hardware and open models is an offline-capable, self-improving threat. This moves the threat model from "controlled AI misuse" to "autonomous adversarial AI in the wild."

### "Dawn of the Apex Agentic Adversary" — AI Compresses Discovery-to-Weaponisation Window
**Source:** The Hacker News | [Article](https://thehackernews.com/2026/06/dawn-of-apex-agentic-adversary.html)

A Hacker News analysis argues that frontier agentic AI models have ended the era of "human-speed threats." Agentic AI can now autonomously test vulnerabilities, compress the CVE-to-exploit window from weeks to hours, and conduct multi-step attack chains with minimal human input. The article notes organisations have "handed AI the keys to the deepest layers of their infrastructure."

💡 **Strategic take:** The patch cycle cadence that has governed enterprise vulnerability management for 20 years is no longer viable. If AI can weaponise a CVE within hours of disclosure, a 30-day remediation SLA is functionally the same as having no remediation process at all. Continuous exposure management, runtime isolation, and assume-breach architecture are the only postures that scale to agentic adversaries.

## 📌 Today's Signal

**The threat surface has fundamentally shifted from perimeter to trust — and trust itself is being weaponised at every layer.** Today's brief reveals a single coherent adversarial logic: attack where defenders expect safety. A Chrome extension with a Featured badge. A CI/CD workflow at Microsoft and Google. An AI triage tool's LLM context window. A cPanel admin portal. An SD-WAN fabric sitting two months compromised before anyone noticed. The Gaslight macOS malware — designed to fool the AI analyst, not the sandbox — is a signal flare for what comes next: adversaries who have studied how defenders deploy AI and are engineering their payloads against it. Meanwhile, state actors are explicitly "prepositioning" in critical infrastructure. Decision-makers who still think of cybersecurity as a compliance function are operating with a map from a different decade. The question for boards and CISOs this week isn't "are we patched?" — it's "what are we trusting that we shouldn't be, and what does an attacker do the moment that trust is exploited?"
