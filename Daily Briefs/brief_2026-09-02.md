## 🚨 Government Advisories

### #StopRansomware: Gunra Ransomware — CISA/FBI/NSA/USSS/DC3 (AA26-222A)
**Source:** [CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa26-222a) | **Date:** 10 August 2026 (still active)

Joint advisory from CISA, FBI, NSA, US Secret Service, and South Korea's National Police Agency. Gunra is a double-extortion ransomware-as-a-service variant derived from leaked Conti source code, targeting healthcare, financial services, government, and professional services globally. Initial access via FortiOS vulnerabilities CVE-2024-55591 and CVE-2025-24472; ransom demanded via Tor portal with a dedicated leak site as leverage.

💡 **Strategic take:** Gunra is Conti's DNA in new clothes, with the added risk of a structured RaaS affiliate ecosystem that dramatically lowers the barrier to entry. The fact that a US-Korea joint advisory is still live tells you the actor is still at volume. Healthcare and financial services boards need a current answerability plan for data exfiltration extortion — not just ransomware recovery.

### ACSC: Active Exploitation of N-Central (CVE-2026-18556, CVE-2026-18577)
**Source:** [Cyber.gov.au](https://www.cyber.gov.au/about-us/view-all-content/alerts-and-advisories/active-exploitation-of-remote-monitoring-and-management-platform-within-australia)

Authentication bypass vulnerabilities in SolarWinds N-Central are being actively exploited in Australia. Patches were released 1 August 2026; Hotfix 2 followed 6 August. RMM platforms are high-value targets because compromise gives attackers a foothold across every managed client.

💡 **Strategic take:** RMM exploitation is the supply-chain attack that doesn't look like a supply-chain attack — it lands inside the management layer, not the production layer. Every MSP and enterprise running N-Central that hasn't applied the August patches is effectively holding the door open. Verify patch status today.

**Source:** [Cyber.gov.au](https://www.cyber.gov.au/about-us/view-all-content/alerts-and-advisories/active-exploitation-of-a-software-development-platform-within-australia)

ACSC has observed active exploitation of a vulnerability in TeamCity On-Premises servers within Australia. CI/CD pipeline compromise enables injection of malicious code into software builds at source.

💡 **Strategic take:** TeamCity exploitation is the quiet kill shot — attackers don't exfiltrate data, they subvert the assembly line. If you haven't restricted network access to your CI/CD infrastructure, that is today's priority.

## 📰 Top Security News

### PaperCut Zero-Days Escalate to Active Intrusions — Federal Patch Deadline 14 September
**Source:** [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/papercut-exploitation-escalates-to-active-intrusions/) | [eSentire](https://www.esentire.com/security-advisories/apercut-discloses-zero-day-vulnerabilities-cve-2026-82078-and-cve-2026-81578) | [Rapid7](https://www.rapid7.com/blog/post/etr-papercut-ng-mf-critical-zero-day-exploited-in-the-wild/)

CVE-2026-82078 (CVSS 9.4) and CVE-2026-81578 (CVSS 8.8) in PaperCut NG/MF are being chained to achieve unauthenticated remote code execution. Disclosure was 27 August; by week's end, threat actors had moved from reconnaissance to hands-on-keyboard intrusion. A third emergency patch shipped 1 September. ShadowServer estimates >1,000 internet-exposed PaperCut instances. Federal agencies must remediate by 14 September.

💡 **Strategic take:** The PaperCut pattern — disclosure to active intrusion in under a week — is the new normal. Print management software in corporate and education environments rarely gets the same patch urgency as edge devices, which is exactly why attackers are in. If you run PaperCut and it touches the internet, treat this as an emergency response, not a maintenance window.

### Cloud Platforms Weaponised for Phishing Campaigns
**Source:** Cybernews

Cybercriminals are routing phishing infrastructure through trusted cloud platforms — Azure, Google Firebase, Google Cloud Storage, AWS, and Cloudflare — to bypass domain-reputation filters. The use of legitimate CDNs gives attackers clean TLS certificates and trusted origin IPs.

💡 **Strategic take:** The perimeter check that used to catch phishing — "is this a known-bad domain?" — is broken when the URL is `storage.googleapis.com`. Security awareness training needs to teach employees that a legitimate-looking URL is not safety evidence. Email gateway rules should flag cloud storage links in unsolicited messages, not just traditional phishing indicators.

## 🤖 AI & Security

### State of AI Cybersecurity 2026: Hyper-Personalised Phishing Now Top Concern
**Source:** [Kiteworks AI Cybersecurity 2026 Trends Report](https://www.kiteworks.com/cybersecurity-risk-management/ai-cybersecurity-2026-trends-report/) | [Fortinet Cybersecurity Trends 2026](https://www.fortinet.com/resources/cyberglossary/cybersecurity-trends-2026)

The State of AI Cybersecurity 2026 report ranks hyper-personalised phishing as the top concern (50% of respondents), ahead of automated vulnerability scanning and exploit chaining (45%), adaptive malware (40%), and deepfake voice fraud (40%). Gartner projects global infosec spend at $240B in 2026, a 12.5% year-on-year increase. The World Economic Forum reports 94% of organisations say AI is the biggest cybersecurity force shaping 2026.

💡 **Strategic take:** The $240B figure reflects a market responding to machine-speed attacks with machine-speed defence — but the asymmetry still favours attackers, who only need to get it right once. The hyper-personalised phishing concern is well-founded: AI removes the tell-tale markers (poor grammar, generic greetings) that human recipients have been trained to spot. Identity verification at the protocol level — not awareness training — is becoming the necessary response.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The through-line across today's brief is **speed asymmetry**: attackers are moving from disclosure to exploitation in days (PaperCut, CVE-2026-82329), while defenders are still operating on maintenance-window cycles. This is being compressed further by AI-assisted attack tooling that automates scanning, personalises phishing, and chains vulnerabilities without the dwell time that historically gave defenders a window. Simultaneously, the attack surface is expanding upward into the management layer — RMM platforms, CI/CD pipelines, cloud storage infrastructure — where compromise is harder to detect and the blast radius is multiplied across downstream clients or applications. Decision-makers need to benchmark their patch velocity against real-world exploitation timelines, not vendor SLAs. The organisations getting hit are not outliers; they are the ones whose patching cycles are measured in weeks when the exploitation window is now measured in days.
