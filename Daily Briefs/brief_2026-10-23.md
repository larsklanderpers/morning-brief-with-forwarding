# 🛡️ Cybersecurity Morning Brief

## 🚨 Government Advisories

**ACSC tells every Fortinet customer to patch now, while the details wait until 10 November**
Source: Australian Cyber Security Centre | [Link](https://www.cyber.gov.au/alert/critical-vulnerabilities-in-fortinet-products)
The ACSC's 23 October alert says Fortinet has announced critical vulnerabilities in multiple versions of FortiOS, FortiManager, FortiAnalyzer, FortiMail and FortiAuthenticator. The agency has no details beyond Fortinet's public advice and says Fortinet expects to release more on 10 November 2026. FortiSASE was updated automatically, but FortiManager may need manual updates. The ACSC is not aware of active exploitation in Australia. Fortinet's own 21 October post calls this a hardening release and says the fixes came out of a frontier AI-assisted security testing program; that framing is the vendor's.
💡 Strategic take: This is a patch-first, explain-later release, and it asks boards to trust the vendor for three weeks. That is a reasonable request when the alternative is publishing a map for attackers, but it means your change board cannot wait for a CVE score before acting. If you cannot patch this week, do what Fortinet itself suggests: take the management interfaces off the internet and put MFA on every admin account.

**CISA and Canada's Cyber Centre publish guidance on running out of AI tokens**
Source: CISA | [Link](https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/managing-ai-token-consumption-operational-resilience)
On 22 October, CISA, the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security and partners released joint guidance on managing AI token consumption. It warns that unmanaged usage can bring unpredictable costs, degraded performance, token exhaustion and "denial-of-wallet" attacks. It tells critical infrastructure operators to identify mission-critical functions that depend on third-party AI services and plan for running with less of them during outages or high demand. CISA's last new alert remains the 21 October PHP alert.
💡 Strategic take: Governments now treat your AI budget as an availability risk, not just a finance line. If a fraud check or a customer service flow stops when the monthly token quota runs out, that is an outage with a billing cause. Put a ceiling, an alert and a fallback mode on every AI dependency that a customer can feel.

## 📰 Top Security News

**Amazon says attackers were probing PHP2Shell four days before the patch shipped**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/amazon-says-php2shell-exploitation-began-4-days-before-patches/)
Moved since yesterday: Amazon Threat Intelligence says the first exploitation attempts hit its honeypots on Saturday 17 October, while the flaws were still under embargo, from a commercial anonymisation network. AWS CISO CJ Moses says the flaws are "now being broadly exploited in the wild", though it is unclear how many attacks succeed. Dataminr's TRACE team reported the same day that it had seen only scanning and non-working exploits, with no known compromises. The PHP Foundation says details leaked online two days before disclosure. VirusTotal now tracks more than 5.1 million exposed PHP hosts.
💡 Strategic take: Two credible teams read the same week differently, and that gap is the point: nobody can tell you yet how bad this is. What Amazon has shown is that the disclosure date is not the start date. Any incident review of your PHP estate should look back to at least 17 October, not 21 October.

**Arista patches its fourth exploited VeloCloud zero-day of the year**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/arista-patches-new-velocloud-orchestrator-zero-day-exploited-in-attacks/)
CVE-2026-114625 is a critical SQL injection in on-premises VeloCloud Orchestrator that lets any authenticated user gain operator privileges and act as a local "superuser operator". Arista says it is being exploited and has named two attacker IP addresses to block. Hosted deployments are already patched. Arista has now fixed exploited zero-days in this product family in May, July, September and October.
💡 Strategic take: The orchestrator is the console that configures every branch's network at once, so one compromised login there is worth a hundred at the edge. Four exploited bugs in six months is a pattern, not bad luck. If you run it on-premises, ask whether the hosted version, which Arista patched for you, is now the safer choice.

**Capcom says 11.7 million CAPCOM ID accounts were exposed**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/capcom-confirms-data-breach-affecting-117-million-accounts/)
Capcom confirmed on 22 October that attackers breached its CAPCOM ID membership service and took email addresses, account creation dates, login counts and last-login IP addresses for more than 11.7 million accounts. For 196 accounts the attackers changed passwords to lock owners out. Capcom says other passwords and card data were not exposed, and it reported the breach to Japan's Personal Information Protection Commission on 21 October, two days after detection. It has not said how the attackers got in.
💡 Strategic take: Gaming loyalty systems hold exactly this profile: an email, a login history and an IP, which is everything a phisher needs to write a convincing "your account" email. Capcom's two-day path from detection to regulator is the benchmark an Australian Privacy Act notification will be measured against. For a club or casino member database, rehearse that timeline now, before you need it.

**Lazarus wraps its malware delivery in NIST's post-quantum encryption**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/lazarus-hackers-use-post-quantum-cryptography-for-malware-delivery/)
Kaspersky says a North Korean campaign it calls FlashHole, attributed to the Lazarus Group, uses ML-KEM-1024, the NIST-standardised post-quantum key exchange, to protect payloads sent from its command servers. The campaign began in July 2025 and hit at least three South Korean companies in software, construction and telecoms equipment. Kaspersky says most of the code runs in memory to avoid detection.
💡 Strategic take: The attackers adopted post-quantum cryptography before most enterprises finished their inventory of where it is needed. The practical lesson is not about quantum computers; it is that defenders who rely on inspecting network traffic are losing visibility. Detection has to move to the endpoint and to behaviour, because the wire will increasingly be unreadable.

## 🤖 AI & Security

**LiteLLM's release pipeline was hijacked to ship a credential stealer to AI gateways**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/litellm-release-pipeline-hacked-to-distribute-info-stealer-malware/)
LiteLLM, an open-source AI gateway with a reported 92 million monthly PyPI downloads, says its release pipeline was compromised on 21 October. Version 1.107.0 was live on PyPI for about 1.7 hours and on Docker Hub for about 2.5 hours. The malware ran at every Python start-up and took cloud, Kubernetes, AI provider, Git and CI credentials, SSH keys, .env files and browser data. Security firms flagged it within six minutes. The project was also hit in March via a compromised scanner in its CI pipeline. The clean recovery version is 1.106.2.
💡 Strategic take: An AI gateway is a keyring by design: it holds the API keys for every model provider you use, so whoever owns its install step owns all of them. Six-minute detection did not help anyone who pulled "latest" in that window. Pin versions by hash for anything that sits between your apps and your models, and treat a gateway compromise as a full credential rotation, not a package update.

**Railway tells all 4.8 million users to rotate secrets in an attack it links to AI inference providers**
Source: BleepingComputer | [Link](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/railway-warns-devs-to-rotate-secrets-after-potential-breach/)
Cloud host Railway says an unauthorised third party may have accessed encrypted customer environment variables and, in some cases, obtained the decrypted values. Railway says the incident was part of a series of attacks targeting AI inference providers, which it notified directly. It paused all deployments, and as of 22 October builds were restored only for Pro and Enterprise workspaces. Railway says only a subset of users may be affected but asks everyone to rotate.
💡 Strategic take: This is the fourth hosting platform to be breached as a whole in a week, after OVHcloud's CLI, Zeabur and Onamae, and this one names AI keys as the target. Environment variables were never designed as a vault; they are the sticky note under the keyboard of modern deployment. Move model and database credentials into a secrets manager with short-lived tokens, so that the next platform breach yields keys that have already expired.

## 🗣️ Community Pulse

**Reddit lane failed for an eighth morning**
Source: Reddit | [Link](https://www.reddit.com/r/netsec/)
⚠️ The r/netsec and r/cybersecurity hot.json fetches were both refused on 23 October ("Claude Code is unable to fetch from www.reddit.com"), the same host-level block as 16 to 22 October. The site:reddit.com WebSearch fallback again returned only third-party pages about the subreddits.
💡 Strategic take: Eight identical failures say the lane needs a decision, not another retry. The options are an RSS route, a different community source such as Hacker News, or dropping the section.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The thread through today's stories is the keyring. LiteLLM's stealer went for the AI provider keys a gateway exists to hold. Railway's attacker went for environment variables and named AI inference providers as the target. Capcom lost the login profiles phishers use to steal the next set of keys. Meanwhile the clock on vulnerabilities keeps running ahead of the calendar: PHP2Shell was being probed four days before its patch, Fortinet is asking for patches three weeks before it explains them, and Arista is on its fourth exploited zero-day of the year. For a decision-maker the question is shifting from "are we patched?" to "if every secret in our deployment platform leaked tonight, how many of them would still work tomorrow?" Short-lived credentials are now cheaper than the incident.
