## 🚨 Government Advisories

### CISA/FBI/NSA/DoD Joint Advisory: #StopRansomware — Gunra Ransomware
**Source:** CISA | **Date:** 10 August 2026
**URL:** https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa26-222a

A six-agency joint advisory (CISA, FBI, DoD Cyber Crime Center, NSA, U.S. Secret Service, and South Korea's National Police Agency) warns that Gunra ransomware — a double-extortion RaaS operation derived from leaked Conti source code — is actively targeting healthcare, financial services, critical manufacturing, transportation, government facilities, utilities, academia, and retail. Initial access comes via CVE-2024-5559 and CVE-2025-24472 in internet-facing devices. Gunra demands ransom through a Tor-based portal and threatens to publish stolen data on a dedicated leak site.

💡 **Strategic take:** A North American and South Korean intelligence coalition doesn't issue a joint advisory lightly. The Conti lineage matters: Conti's tradecraft is well-documented and its playbooks are still being recycled years later. Organisations running unpatched VPN gateways or RDP infrastructure are providing the on-ramp — CISA's core recommendation (patch internet-facing systems, segment networks) is the same advice it's been giving for three years, which is itself a signal that execution remains the industry's structural failure.

### CISA KEV Additions — Three New Actively Exploited CVEs
**Source:** CISA | **Date:** 11 August 2026
**URL:** https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/08/11/cisa-adds-three-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog

CISA added three CVEs to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog: **CVE-2026-20349** (Cisco Secure Firewall ASA/FTD heap inspection flaw enabling unauthenticated DoS), **CVE-2026-68820** (Windows Ancillary Function Driver for WinSock use-after-free allowing SYSTEM privilege escalation — see Patch Tuesday below), and **CVE-2026-72898** (Metabase SQL injection). Federal agencies have mandatory 21-day remediation windows; the private sector should treat KEV additions as a risk escalation signal.

💡 **Strategic take:** The Metabase SQLi entry is the one to watch for smaller organisations. Metabase is widely deployed for business intelligence dashboards, often on-premises and frequently under-patched. SQL injection through a BI tool is a high-yield lateral movement opportunity — it's often directly adjacent to sensitive data stores. If your organisation runs Metabase and it's not on the patch list this week, it should be.

## 📰 Top Security News

### Microsoft August 2026 Patch Tuesday: 421 CVEs, Zero-Day Exploited by Lazarus Group
**Source:** SecurityWeek / BleepingComputer / The Hacker News | **Date:** 12 August 2026
**URLs:**
- https://www.securityweek.com/august-2026-patch-tuesday-microsoft-fixes-421-cves-one-exploited-zero-day/
- https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/microsoft/microsoft-august-2026-patch-tuesday-fixes-400-flaws-3-zero-days/
- https://thehackernews.com/2026/08/microsoft-patches-398-flaws-including.html

Microsoft patched 421 CVEs, including 62 critical. The actively exploited zero-day, **CVE-2026-68820**, is a use-after-free bug in the Windows WinSock kernel driver (afd.sys) that enables SYSTEM-level privilege escalation. Check Point Research attributes exploitation to North Korea's **Lazarus Group** as part of its ongoing **Operation Dream Job** campaign — which uses fake employment offers to compromise targets. Also notable: **CVE-2026-62878** is a wormable, unauthenticated Windows DNS Server RCE (stack-based buffer overflow) requiring no user interaction.

💡 **Strategic take:** Lazarus burning a kernel zero-day in Operation Dream Job means they assessed the espionage or disruption value of their current targets as worth the exposure. Nation-state groups don't spend zero-days for commodity attacks. Organisations in defence, financial services, or crypto (historically Lazarus targets) should treat this patch as P0 even with the 5-day elapsed time since Patch Tuesday. The wormable DNS RCE is the other fire: a network-propagating vulnerability hitting DNS infrastructure can collapse name resolution across a domain in minutes.

### Ransomware Attacks on Large Enterprises Up 74% QoQ — Multiple August Incidents
**Source:** TechCrunch / SharkStriker / PKWARE | **Date:** Ongoing August 2026
**URLs:**
- https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/07/the-worst-hacks-and-breaches-of-2026-so-far/
- https://sharkstriker.com/blog/august-2026-data-breaches/

Ransomware attacks against billion-dollar companies surged 74% quarter-over-quarter in H1 2026. August incidents include: **Questal** (ShinyHunters claimed 21 million Salesforce records + 147 GB corporate data), **ProHealth Medical Group** (Krybit claimed 114 GB of health data), and **Hyundai Motor Türkiye** (CRPx0 claimed 1.5 GB of recruitment and HR data). Earlier, Coca-Cola subsidiary Fairlife was forced to temporarily suspend U.S. production operations following a breach.

💡 **Strategic take:** The 74% QoQ spike is being driven by RaaS maturity — lower barrier to entry, better tooling, and a deep bench of affiliates. The Salesforce data point at Questal is a reminder that SaaS platforms aren't architecturally immune to breach; the data lives there even if the infrastructure doesn't. The Fairlife/production shutdown story is the template regulators are increasingly citing for OT/IT convergence risk: ransomware that takes physical operations offline is a different liability category than a data breach.

### Cisco ASA/FTD SSL VPN Vulnerability Actively Exploited
**Source:** Multiple | **Date:** August 2026
**URL:** https://www.vicisecurity.com/blog/august-2026-security-alert-critical-flaws-active-exploit/

**CVE-2026-20349** in Cisco Secure Firewall ASA and FTD allows unauthenticated remote attackers to restart vulnerable devices, producing denial-of-service conditions. CISA has added it to the KEV catalog, and exploitation is confirmed in the wild. Also actively exploited: **Progress LoadMaster CVE-2026-8037** (command injection, added to KEV in early August), **JetBrains TeamCity CVE-2026-63077** (deserialization), and **N-able N-central CVE-2026-18577** (authentication bypass).

💡 **Strategic take:** Cisco ASA/FTD is perimeter infrastructure for tens of thousands of enterprises. A DoS against a firewall or VPN concentrator is not just a nuisance — it's a potential forcing function to disable security controls or trigger a failover to a less-secure path. The cluster of RMM/MSP-adjacent vulnerabilities (N-able, Progress LoadMaster) continues a trend where attackers are going after the tools that managed service providers use to manage their clients — one compromise, many victims.

## 🤖 AI & Security

### Identity Is Eclipsing Ransomware as the Primary Battleground — CSA Survey
**Source:** Cloud Security Alliance / Fortinet / Cynet | **Date:** 2026
**URLs:**
- https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/blog/2026/04/02/the-state-of-ai-cybersecurity-2026-unveiling-insights-from-over-1-500-security-leaders
- https://www.fortinet.com/resources/cyberglossary/cybersecurity-trends-2026

A CSA survey of 1,500+ security leaders identifies AI-powered hyper-personalised phishing (50%), automated vulnerability scanning and exploit chaining (45%), adaptive malware (40%), and deepfake voice/video fraud (40%) as the top AI-enabled threats. The World Economic Forum reports 94% of organisations now regard AI as the dominant force shaping their threat environment. The strategic shift: attackers are orchestrating full attack chains — reconnaissance through exfiltration — with minimal human involvement via AI-as-a-service platforms.

💡 **Strategic take:** The "identity eclipsing ransomware" framing is worth unpacking. Ransomware was always a monetisation endpoint; the identity theft is the access mechanism. What's changed is that AI is making identity-based initial access fast, cheap, and scalable enough that it's now the primary investment for threat actors — and the primary exposure for defenders. Organisations still measuring security by "did we stop the malware" are measuring the wrong thing.

## 📌 Today's Signal

This week's cybersecurity landscape reflects a maturing threat economy that has absorbed AI capabilities faster than the defence side. Three separate threat streams converged: a nation-state group (Lazarus) burning a kernel zero-day in a social engineering campaign, a ransomware ecosystem that has industrialised to the point of 74% QoQ growth against large enterprises, and an AI-enabled phishing and identity threat that is dismantling the human-judgment layer that security architectures have long relied on as a backstop. The through-line is operational leverage — attackers are achieving more impact per unit of effort than at any prior point, while defenders are still largely operating point-solution security programmes designed for a slower, less automated threat. The CISA advisory count and Patch Tuesday volume are not independent — they reflect the same dynamic: the attack surface is expanding faster than the industry's collective capacity to close it.
