## 🚨 Government Advisories

### 1. ACSC CRITICAL: Large-Scale CMS Exploitation Campaign Targeting Australian Businesses
**Source:** ASD's Australian Cyber Security Centre | **Date:** 9 July 2026 (active)
**URL:** https://www.cyber.gov.au/about-us/view-all-content/alerts-and-advisories/large-scale-exploitation-campaign-targeting-website-content-management-systems-cms

Malicious actors are actively scanning and compromising websites running WordPress, Joomla, Craft CMS, and other platforms via 17+ identified CVEs. Attackers deploy webshells for persistent access, enabling credential theft, lateral movement, and malware delivery. Many small-to-medium Australian businesses have already been impacted. The ACSC explicitly links this campaign's scale to AI-accelerated exploitation, citing the Five Eyes joint statement on AI collapsing the time between vulnerability disclosure and exploitation.

💡 **Strategic take:** This isn't a novel attack — it's industrial automation applied to a known playbook. The ACSC is flagging it at CRITICAL severity because the volume is unprecedented, not because the technique is new. Every organisation running a public-facing CMS on unpatched plugins is a target right now. The question boards should be asking their IT leads isn't "have we been scanned?" — it's "how quickly could we detect a webshell that's already there?"

### 2. CISA/FBI/NSA: Iranian State Actors Targeting US Critical Infrastructure ICS Devices
**Source:** CISA Advisory AA26-097A | **Date:** July 2026
**URL:** https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories/aa26-097a

A coordinated campaign by Iranian state-sponsored threat actors is targeting internet-exposed industrial control systems manufactured by Siemens, Schneider Electric, and Rockwell Automation. Six US federal agencies (FBI, CISA, NSA, EPA, DOE, US Cyber Command) issued a joint advisory. The focus is on internet-exposed PLCs in water, energy, and critical manufacturing sectors.

💡 **Strategic take:** The update to this advisory matters as much as the original — agencies don't refresh joint advisories unless the threat is actively evolving. Internet-exposed ICS devices remain the most consequential unpatched attack surface in critical infrastructure: they're often running vendor firmware that can't be patched quickly, in environments where taking a system offline carries its own operational risk. Water utilities and energy operators need to treat this as a fire drill, not a compliance checkbox.

## 📰 Top Security News

### 4. Origin Energy Data Breach: 900,000 Australian Customer Accounts Compromised
**Source:** ABC News, Nine.com.au, Origin Energy | **Date:** 28 July 2026
**URL:** https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-07-28/origin-energy-data-breach-900k-customers-impacted/106961804

Origin Energy confirmed on 28 July that approximately 900,000 customers had their data accessed and exfiltrated following an incident first detected on 22 July. Compromised data includes names, addresses, dates of birth, phone numbers, account information, and partial payment details (last four digits of credit cards or BSB/account numbers). Origin notified authorities and is providing customer support.

💡 **Strategic take:** This is the most significant Australian data breach of 2026 so far. A utility company holding billing and identity data for nearly a million customers is exactly the target profile ransomware groups and nation-state actors have been building toward — the data is useful for both fraud and social engineering at scale. The 6-day gap between detection (22 July) and public disclosure (28 July) will come under scrutiny given Australia's mandatory breach notification obligations.

### 5. Operation BlueDash: Phishing Campaign Deploys Dual RMM Access via Fake Teams Update
**Source:** The Hacker News, CybersecurityNews, Cyberpress | **Date:** 27–28 July 2026
**URL:** https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/operation-bluedash-deploys-level-rmm.html

ZeroBEC researchers uncovered an active phishing campaign using a fake Microsoft Teams "secure document" lure. Victims are directed to a counterfeit Microsoft Store page and tricked into downloading `supportdev.exe`, an Inno Setup loader that silently installs Level RMM (using an attacker-controlled API key) and then attempts a parallel ScreenConnect deployment — giving attackers two independent remote access channels. The dual-channel approach ensures persistence even if one tool is detected and blocked.

💡 **Strategic take:** The sophistication here isn't technical — it's operational. Two independent RMM channels means blocking one doesn't evict the attacker; it just reduces their options. The use of legitimate, signed remote admin software is also deliberate: it bypasses AV, blends with IT operations, and creates a detection blind spot in most SIEM configurations. Organisations that don't audit which RMM tools are in use on their endpoints are flying blind.

## 🤖 AI & Security

### 9. Hermes AI Agent Used in Unattended "YOLO" Mode for Post-Exploitation at Thai Finance Ministry
**Source:** BleepingComputer, The Hacker News, Security Affairs | **Date:** 24–25 July 2026
**URL:** https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hermes-ai-agent-used-to-automate-attack-on-thai-finance-ministry/

A threat actor ran the open-source Hermes AI agent in unattended "YOLO" mode (approval prompts disabled) to automate post-exploitation activity during an alleged breach of Thailand's Ministry of Finance. The attacker left logs exposed on an open server — revealing the AI workflow — and had a Hades implant staged for deployment. This appears to be one of the first confirmed in-the-wild uses of an agentic AI tool for autonomous post-breach operations.

💡 **Strategic take:** "YOLO mode" — running an AI agent without human approval steps — is exactly what makes agentic AI operationally attractive to attackers. The attacker doesn't need to be online; the agent works autonomously. Defenders, by contrast, still need a human in the loop to approve most response actions. That asymmetry is going to define the next phase of incident response architecture. The exposed logs are a gift for researchers, but the next operator won't make that mistake.

### 11. Is AI the Biggest Cyber Threat of 2026? Expert Survey Says Yes
**Source:** Tech.co | **Date:** 29 July 2026
**URL:** https://tech.co/news/state-of-cybersecurity-2026

A fresh expert survey finds AI has overtaken ransomware as the top-cited cyber threat for 2026, with particular concern around AI-accelerated vulnerability discovery, AI-powered social engineering, and autonomous attack tooling. 💡 **Strategic take:** The semantic shift from "AI-assisted" to "AI-autonomous" is the thing to watch. The Unit 42 finding on agentic ransomware managing its own extortion stages is the clearest indicator yet that this isn't a future risk — it's a present one.

## 📌 Today's Signal

Today's stories don't just coexist — they describe a single, accelerating dynamic: the human-speed constraint on attackers is being removed. The ACSC's CMS campaign runs at AI-enabled scan-and-exploit velocity. The Hermes AI agent post-exploits a government network while the attacker sleeps. Operation BlueDash deploys dual RMM channels to ensure persistence without ongoing human involvement. Ransomware groups are running agentic extortion workflows. Meanwhile, defenders are still patching quarterly, escalating through change management committees, and waiting for human approval before responding. The asymmetry isn't new, but AI is widening it fast — and the Five Eyes chiefs saying so publicly is the clearest signal yet that this has moved from theoretical risk to operational reality. Decision-makers who haven't restructured their security programs around the assumption of AI-enabled attackers are already behind.
