## 🚨 Government Advisories

### CISA KEV Update: Cisco ISE & Acronis Backup (Sep 16)
**Source:** CISA | [Alert](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/09/16/cisa-adds-two-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog)

CISA added two vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on September 16: **CVE-2026-76460** (Cisco Identity Services Engine) and **CVE-2026-87886** (Acronis Backup). Both are confirmed exploited in the wild. Federal agencies are required to patch by their CISA deadline.

💡 **Strategic take:** Cisco ISE is the network access control backbone for many enterprise and government environments: exploitation means adversaries may be staging for lateral movement or policy bypass at scale. Acronis Backup is a high-value target because it holds recovery data; ransomware actors specifically seek to compromise or corrupt backup systems before detonating to eliminate the victim's walk-away option.

### Joint Advisory: Iranian MOIS HEAVYGRAM/CHOSEN BRICK Spyware (Sep 15)
**Source:** NCSC (UK) / FBI / AIVD (Netherlands) | [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/iranian-hackers-use-telegram-controlled.html) | [TechNadu](https://www.technadu.com/iran-linked-hackers-deploy-chosen-brick-spyware-against-activists-joint-ncsc-fbi-aivd-advisory-reveals/637163/)

A five-nation joint advisory exposed CHOSEN BRICK (NCSC designation) / HEAVYGRAM (FBI designation), a Windows surveillance malware deployed by Iran's Ministry of Intelligence and Security (MOIS) against dissidents, journalists, and activists. Active since at least 2023, delivered via social engineering on WhatsApp and Telegram: disguised as AI apps, antivirus tools, and even fabricated MRI scan results. Once installed, it exfiltrates email and chat content, takes screenshots, and activates the microphone.

💡 **Strategic take:** Nation-state actors are now weaponising the AI-app distribution channel: victims are primed to install software labelled as AI tools precisely because everyone is looking for them. The Telegram C2 mechanism is notable: using a legitimate platform as the command channel makes attribution harder and egress filtering useless unless you're willing to block Telegram entirely. For organisations hosting at-risk communities (journalists, advocacy groups, diaspora networks), this advisory is a direct threat model update.

**Source:** Truesec / CISA | [Truesec](https://www.truesec.com/hub/blog/critical-cisco-secure-email-gateway-vulnerability-exploited-in-the-wild) | [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/cisa-adds-seven-exploited-flaws-as.html)

A critical SQL injection vulnerability (CVE-2026-76461) in Cisco AsyncOS for Secure Email Gateway is being actively exploited. Cisco confirmed exploitation in the wild this month. This follows a broader batch of 16 Cisco security advisories dropped in three days.

💡 **Strategic take:** Email gateway compromises are entry points for everything downstream: BEC, credential harvest, supply chain attacks. Organisations that process sensitive communications through Cisco SEG need to treat this as a P0 patch, not a Patch Tuesday item. The velocity of Cisco advisories this month (16 in three days) suggests either a coordinated disclosure or a serious internal audit finding.

### Microsoft September Patch Tuesday: A Record 974 CVEs, Two Exploited Zero-Days
**Source:** Tenable / BleepingComputer | [Tenable](https://www.tenable.com/blog/microsofts-september-2026-patch-tuesday-addresses-964-cves-cve-2026-81963-cve-2026-85880) | [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/microsoft/microsoft-september-2026-patch-tuesday-fixes-966-flaws-2-zero-days/)

Microsoft's September 2026 Patch Tuesday (8 Sep) is the largest on record. Counts differ by method: Tenable says 964 CVEs, BleepingComputer 966, SecurityWeek and The Cyber Express 974, the spread depending on whether external and Chromium bugs are counted. Two zero-days were exploited in the wild: CVE-2026-85880, a heap buffer overflow in Windows ALPC, and CVE-2026-81963, a link-following flaw in the Windows Update Stack. Reporting also counts 20 wormable bugs in the same release.

💡 **Strategic take:** The number is the story only if you let it be. A 974-CVE release is unrunnable as a single change window, so the organisations that cope will be the ones that already rank by exploitation status rather than by severity score: two zero-days and 20 wormable bugs are the actual work, and the rest is scheduling. Any shop still treating Patch Tuesday as one monolithic event has a process that breaks at exactly the volume attackers now count on.

### Azure AD B2C CVSS 10.0 Authorization Bypass Was Fixed Cloud-Side, No Customer Action
**Source:** Microsoft early security update, 3 Sep | [OpenCVE CVE-2026-83711](https://app.opencve.io/cve/CVE-2026-83711)

CVE-2026-83711, an authorization bypass in Azure AD B2C via a user-controlled key (CWE-639), carries a CVSS 10.0 and is exploitable over the network with no authentication. It was not part of Patch Tuesday: Microsoft shipped it in an early security update on 3 September, and reporting describes it as exclusively cloud-side, mitigated server-side, requiring no customer action. Note for the record: an earlier cut of this brief folded this flaw into the 8 Sep Patch Tuesday item and implied it was outstanding. It is not.

💡 **Strategic take:** The interesting part is not the 10.0, it is that a perfect-score identity bypass in a platform underpinning countless B2C sign-in flows was opened and closed without any tenant doing a thing. That is the bargain of managed identity: the provider patches faster than you could, and in exchange you get no telemetry on what happened to your own authentication surface. Anyone relying on B2C for customer PII should be asking Microsoft what the exposure window was and whether tenant-side logs would show abuse, because "no action required" is not the same as "nothing happened".

## 🤖 AI & Security

### AI Agent Swarm Compromises 440 PaperCut Servers Across 395 Organisations
**Source:** GreyNoise / Help Net Security / The Hacker News | [GreyNoise](https://www.greynoise.io/blog/ai-orchestrated-campaign-against-papercut-ng-mf) | [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/09/11/ai-agents-papercut-ng-mf-attack-campaign/) | [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/papercut-attacker-uses-hundreds-of-ai.html)

A likely Russian-speaking threat actor used hundreds of AI agents (OpenAI Codex as the harness, a DeepSeek model, and public offensive tooling) to exploit CVE-2026-81578 (authentication bypass) and CVE-2026-82078 (unsafe reflection RCE) in PaperCut NG/MF. The campaign compromised at least 440 instances across 395 organisations in 48 countries. GreyNoise reports 11 organisations breached within 26 seconds of launch, first RCE under four hours from a blank workspace, and one US high school taken from initial access to domain admin in seven minutes. On depth of impact: 280 victims had credentials harvested and 147 had OS or domain secrets pulled, but domain admin was achieved against only 12 organisations. GreyNoise says the DeepSeek model was chosen because it lacks the content-safety restrictions US frontier models apply to offensive security queries.

💡 **Strategic take:** Read the two halves of this against each other. Breadth was automated and near-instant, but depth was not: 12 domain-admin outcomes out of 395 targets is the signal that the agents were excellent at the commodity opening and still mediocre at the bit requiring judgement. That gap is the defensive budget, and it is closing. The procurement detail is the one to carry to a board: the crew selected its model on the basis of which vendor would answer the question, which makes safety tuning a market variable rather than an industry floor.

### Identity Eclipses Ransomware as Top Threat Vector in 2026
**Source:** Cloud Security Alliance / BCG | [CSA](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/blog/2026/04/02/the-state-of-ai-cybersecurity-2026-unveiling-insights-from-over-1-500-security-leaders) | [BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2026/cybersecurity-spending-ai-threat-trends)

The State of AI Cybersecurity 2026 report (1,500+ security leaders) identifies hyper-personalised phishing (50%) and automated vulnerability exploitation (45%) as the top AI-enabled threat concerns. Industry consensus: identity hijacking at scale has surpassed ransomware as the primary battleground. Cyber spending rose 12% in 2025; 89% of companies still reported AI-enabled attacks.

💡 **Strategic take:** Spending more on legacy controls while adversaries shift to AI-native attack chains is a classic "fighting the last war" scenario. The identity-as-primary-vector shift means MFA is table stakes, not a solution: the attack now lives inside the authenticated session. Boards asking "are we spending enough on cybersecurity" are asking the wrong question; the right question is "are we spending it on the right layer?"

## 📌 Today's Signal

Today's pattern is **volume as a weapon**. Microsoft shipped a record 974 CVEs in one Tuesday. CISA added two more exploited flaws the same week. A crew pointed hundreds of agents at PaperCut and breached 11 organisations in 26 seconds. Iranian MOIS is running its spyware through the app category everybody is currently downloading without much thought. Each of these works by overwhelming a queue that was sized for human throughput: the patch queue, the triage queue, the "is this app legitimate" judgement. The honest read of the PaperCut numbers is that automation has conquered the commodity opening and not yet the part that needs judgement, since only 12 of 395 targets reached domain admin. That gap is the whole defensive opportunity, and it is the thing to spend on: not more alerts, but fewer decisions that a human has to make at speed. For a decision-maker, the question is not "can we patch faster" but "which of our queues has no ranking function", because an unranked queue at 974 items is indistinguishable from no queue at all.
