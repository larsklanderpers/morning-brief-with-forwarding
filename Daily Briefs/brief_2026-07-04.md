## 🚨 Government Advisories

### CISA: SharePoint RCE CVE-2026-45659 Added to KEV
**Source:** The Hacker News / CISA | [Read →](https://thehackernews.com/2026/07/sharepoint-rce-cve-2026-45659-added-to.html)

CISA added CVE-2026-45659 — a Microsoft SharePoint Server remote code execution flaw (CVSS 8.8) — to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog after confirming active exploitation in the wild. The patch deadline for FCEB agencies was today, July 4. Notably, Microsoft patched this during the May cycle but failed to disclose it publicly for weeks.

💡 **Strategic take:** The fact that Microsoft patched silently and CISA had to force disclosure is a reminder that vendor transparency has limits. Organisations relying solely on vendor comms for patch prioritisation are flying blind. An internally-owned vulnerability intelligence function isn't a nice-to-have — it's a risk management necessity. For Australian entities, this SharePoint exposure deserves immediate attention given how widely it is deployed in government and enterprise environments.

### CISA/FBI: Joint PSA on Messaging App Phishing (Signal, WhatsApp)
**Source:** CISA / FBI | [Read →](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories)

An updated joint public service announcement warns of Russian-nexus threat actors impersonating tech support staff to steal Signal and WhatsApp recovery keys from officials, journalists, and NGO staff. The DOJ has offered a $10 million reward for information leading to the actors responsible.

💡 **Strategic take:** Recovery key theft is a surgical attack — it gives adversaries persistent access without triggering MFA. Secure messaging platforms are only as strong as their account recovery flows. Organisations protecting sensitive populations (journalists, government liaisons, NGOs) need to be counselling their people on this specifically, not just issuing blanket "use Signal" advice.

### ACSC: ClickFix Social Engineering via Compromised WordPress Sites
**Source:** Australian Cyber Security Centre | [Read →](https://www.cyber.gov.au/about-us/view-all-content/alerts-and-advisories)

ASD's ACSC has issued an advisory on the ClickFix technique — a social engineering lure embedded in compromised WordPress sites that tricks users into executing PowerShell commands to "fix" a fake display error, delivering malware. A separate advisory covers active exploitation of a cPanel/WHM vulnerability (CVE-2026-4194).

💡 **Strategic take:** ClickFix is clever because it exploits user obedience — people do what their screen tells them to do. Traditional security awareness training doesn't cover "never copy-paste from a website into a terminal," and it probably should. For Australian organisations, compromised hosting infrastructure is the delivery mechanism; their own website vendors may be the weak link.

## 📰 Top Security News

### DHS HSIN Breach — Sensitive Info-Sharing Platform Compromised
**Source:** BleepingComputer / Security Boulevard | [Read →](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/dhs-confirms-hackers-breached-hsin-info-sharing-platform/)

DHS has confirmed that an unknown threat actor breached the Homeland Security Information Network (HSIN), a platform used by federal, state, local, and private-sector security partners to share sensitive (but unclassified) threat intelligence. The intrusion is believed to have occurred between late May and early June and attribution remains open.

💡 **Strategic take:** HSIN is the connective tissue between federal threat intelligence and the broader US security community. If adversaries had read access for weeks, they could map who is watching what — a counterintelligence windfall. The incident raises uncomfortable questions about the security posture of the platforms we build to coordinate security. Australia has analogous information-sharing platforms that should be reviewing their monitoring maturity in light of this.

### KDDI Breach — 14.2 Million Email Accounts Across Six ISPs
**Source:** Security Affairs | [Read →](https://securityaffairs.com/194387/data-breach/kddi-data-breach-impacts-up-to-14-2-million-email-accounts-at-six-isps.html)

Japan's KDDI disclosed that a vulnerability in shared third-party infrastructure exposed email credentials (addresses and passwords) for approximately 14.2 million customers across six ISPs. Japan's communications ministry has required KDDI to report by July 6.

💡 **Strategic take:** One vulnerability, one piece of shared infrastructure, six ISPs, 14 million accounts. Shared infrastructure risk is the silent multiplier in breach impact — a supplier's patch cadence becomes your exposure. For telcos and ISPs operating in Australia, this is a direct reminder that third-party software in the delivery chain is as much a security question as it is an operational one.

### BlueHammer Ransomware Exploiting Microsoft Defender Flaw
**Source:** SecurityWeek | [Read →](https://www.securityweek.com/bluehammer-vulnerability-exploited-in-ransomware-attacks/)

CVE-2026-33825 ("BlueHammer"), a Microsoft Defender local privilege escalation flaw disclosed publicly before Microsoft had a fix, is now actively used in ransomware attacks. CISA has added it to KEV. A second Defender zero-day — "RoguePlanet," a TOCTOU race condition enabling SYSTEM shell access — was confirmed by Microsoft this week alongside BlueHammer.

💡 **Strategic take:** Two Defender zero-days in the same week is not a coincidence — it signals that Windows' built-in security layer is under active research by adversarial actors. Organisations that treat Defender as a sufficient endpoint security posture should be uncomfortable right now. Defence-in-depth and detection-over-prevention are the appropriate response; patch both flaws immediately and review LPE detection rules.

### INC_RANSOM Active — Hitting Logistics and Local Government
**Source:** Breach Sense / CM Alliance | [Read →](https://www.breachsense.com/breaches/)

INC_RANSOM claimed multiple victims this week including the City of Acworth (Georgia), a logistics firm, and an industrial manufacturing company. The group continues to run an affiliate model targeting organisations with limited SOC capability.

💡 **Strategic take:** INC_RANSOM deliberately targets the under-resourced middle — local government and mid-market industrials. These entities tend to lack both the budget for enterprise detection tools and the staffing to respond effectively. For state and local government in Australia, this is a direct threat profile match. The affiliate model means attack TTPs are inconsistent and harder to detect via signature-based controls.

## 🤖 AI & Security

### Agentic AI Attacks Become the Defining Threat Vector of Mid-2026
**Source:** eSecurity Planet / CrowdStrike / Hacker News | [Read →](https://www.esecurityplanet.com/threats/ai-driven-threats-global-breaches-and-compliance-shifts-define-the-week-in-cybersecurity-for-july-2026/)

July 2026 is characterised by agentic AI attacks — autonomous threat agents that can pivot, adapt, and persist without human direction. 82.6% of analysed phishing emails now show AI involvement. 85% of defence industrial base firms expect AI-powered attacks and deepfake social engineering to affect them within two years, while only 28% feel confident in detecting nation-state threats. PwC identifies AI-driven identity attacks — targeting credentials and session tokens — as the fastest-growing threat vector.

💡 **Strategic take:** The gap between what attackers can automate and what defenders can verify is widening every quarter. Organisations still running manual phishing triage are already behind. The immediate practical implication: phishing-resistant MFA, continuous session monitoring, and zero trust access need to be non-negotiable in 2026 security plans — not aspirational roadmap items.

### Russian Hackers Targeting Signal and WhatsApp Recovery Keys
**Source:** DOJ / CISA | [Read →](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/cybersecurity-advisories)

A Russian threat actor has been systematically impersonating tech support staff to extract Signal and WhatsApp recovery keys from high-value targets — officials, journalists, and NGO workers. The DOJ's $10M reward signal reflects the severity of the targeting and breadth of impact.

💡 **Strategic take:** Nation-state actors have identified secure messaging as a priority target — not through cryptographic attack but through social manipulation of account recovery. The security of these platforms is a human problem, not a technical one. Threat awareness training for journalists and advocacy organisations is an underfunded but high-ROI intervention.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The through-line in today's brief is **trust infrastructure under attack** — and not in a metaphorical sense. Adversaries breached the platform the US government uses to share threat intelligence (HSIN). They're stealing the recovery keys for the apps security-conscious people use for private comms. They're hiding inside the antivirus software that's supposed to stop them. Each of these is an attack on the assumption that the layer underneath you is trustworthy. For decision-makers, the practical takeaway is stark: zero trust is not an architecture pattern, it's an operating philosophy. Assume the platform beneath you has been compromised, and design your detection and response posture accordingly. Patch BlueHammer and RoguePlanet today. Review your SharePoint estate now. And ask yourself what your organisation's equivalent of HSIN is — and whether you'd know if it was reading your traffic.
