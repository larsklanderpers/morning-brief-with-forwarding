Lars Klander has spent thirty years building software for environments where the rules are not optional. Gaming, banking, and defense.

He has worked for and run companies delivering products whose job is to integrate with somebody else's, inside heavily regulated environments, without breaking either. As an independent software vendor he delivered proprietary gaming management infrastructure to four of the five largest gaming companies in the world, and software he helped create is installed in more than 150 gaming entities across the United States, Australia and Macau. The work has spanned systems engineering, software design and architecture, programming, and network security.

He has written numerous books on programming, translated into more than 25 languages, and a book on network and cyber security that went through two editions.

For the last decade he has been CTO of Player Elite, a Sydney-based software company delivering a stack of CRM, business intelligence, communications, customer journey and mobile applications, and now AI-driven tools, to gaming operators across Australia and more recently the United States. That has meant building and operating AI agents inside a regulated business, and living with the consequences when the controls around them do not hold. He now writes daily on AI governance and control design for an audience of CISOs, regulators and decision-makers.

The useful part of that history is the range. He has worked the operations end and the governance end of his own business, and has spent twenty years inside the regulatory obligations of his customers' businesses. It means he can discuss the same problem at the level of the code and at the level of the board.
