## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**The non-coding orchestrator**
Source: X | [Link](https://x.com/bruno_apps/status/2106928856737210542)
The setup that shipped had one agent whose whole job was to split work, write tasks, review output, hold the pending list and gate releases, with the human as final approver. That is the Orchestrator and Conductor archetypes in one seat, plus a rule worth stealing: "the reviewer is never the author".
💡 Constructive read: This is a project manager's job description with agents as the team. People who already run work without doing it themselves are closer to this role than most engineers.

**The definer of done**
Source: X | [Link](https://x.com/emollick/status/2108652762904793360)
Mollick's question (what is progress when output is cheap?) points at a role every team now needs: whoever sets the bar for what is worth producing at all. Maps to Editor plus Risk Steward.
💡 Constructive read: Cheap volume makes judgment the bottleneck. The person who can say "no, that is not the goal" stops being a blocker and starts being the point.

**The engineer pointed at new ideas**
Source: Y Combinator / YouTube | [Link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ofxAtQhV-xA)
Patel's "more engineers, not fewer" rests on AI unlocking new work rather than shrinking existing work. In archetype terms, that is headcount moving from Maintainer toward Prototyper and Scout.
💡 Constructive read: The same engineer, a different brief. The career move is to be the one who brings the new idea, not the one guarding the old backlog.

Source: YourStory, reporting Naukri JobSpeak (September 2026) | [Link](https://yourstory.com/2026/10/white-collar-hiring-grows-2-led-by-aiml-sector-in-sep-report)
Per the Naukri JobSpeak index as reported, India's white-collar hiring grew 2% year on year in September, AI/ML roles grew 20%, and IT and software services hiring fell 4%; demand grew fastest for professionals with 8 to 16 years' experience. 💡 Constructive read: Reshuffle, not collapse: the money is moving between kinds of tech work. The experience skew is the honest caution, since it sits alongside the entry-ladder question this thread keeps open.

**Pew: 34 of 37 countries expect AI to destroy more jobs than it creates**
Source: Pew Research Center (17 Sep 2026) | [Link](https://www.pewresearch.org/global/2026/09/17/globally-more-people-expect-ai-to-cause-job-loss-than-growth)
A 42,151-person survey fielded 8 Feb to 13 May 2026. Australia and South Korea are highest at 76% expecting job losses, the US at 71%; under-35s are now the most worried, a reversal from five years ago. Three weeks old but not previously in this brief, and it resurfaced on AI Chat Daily's careers beat this week.
💡 Constructive read: Expectation is not observed displacement, and the labour data has so far been calmer than the mood. But a generation that expects the ladder to vanish will under-invest in climbing it, which is a problem employers can fix by showing the rungs.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The week's quiet thread is that cheap output has moved the bottleneck to judgment and arrangement. NLW reads the launches as models becoming swappable parts where the harness is the lock-in; a solo founder found that 44 named agents did nothing until one orchestrator wrote no code and kept asking whether the work shipped the product; Mollick asks what progress even means when PowerPoint and code are a hundred times cheaper; and Cisco's product chief expects to hire more engineers because AI opens new work rather than finishing old work. For someone adapting rather than despairing, the move is to claim the judgment seat on one piece of work you already own: write down what "done" means for it, split it into fronts, and be the reviewer who is never the author. That is the Editor, the Orchestrator and the Conductor in one habit, and none of it needs permission.
