## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

Today's clearest archetype signals, mapped to the architecture-of-work lens:

| New Role | Salary Signal | Architecture-of-Work Mapping |
|---|---|---|
| **AI Orchestrator** | $145K–$158K avg; $190K+ in enterprise | Conductor / Orchestrator — routes work between agents and humans |
| **Forward-Deployed Engineer** | Competitive; high-variance | Builder + Scout — scopes, builds, ships, measures adoption |
| **AI FinOps Specialist** | Fast-growing; "Token Tax" managers | Risk Steward — governs inference spend as a real cost center |
| **Agent-Fleet Orchestrator** | Senior engineering comp | Conductor — routing, resource allocation, conflict resolution at scale |
| **Context-Supply-Chain Manager** | Emerging | Maintainer — version control for RAG data; keeps the knowledge graph fresh |

**PwC 2026 AI Jobs Barometer finding** (published, 27 territories, 1B+ job ads):
The labor market is splitting into two tracks. "Professionalised" roles — where AI automates routine tasks and human judgment is amplified — are growing 2× faster and paying 42% more than "democratised" roles where AI makes the role easier for non-experts. The roles adding AI skills are pulling away from those that aren't. Wage premium for AI-skilled workers: 62% (up from 57% last year).

💡 Constructive read: The division isn't technical vs. non-technical. It's judgment-amplified vs. routine-commoditised. A radiologist using AI is in the first category. An IT service manager using AI to make tickets easier is in the second. The architecture-of-work model explains why: Conductor, Risk Steward, and Scout roles are professionalising. Pure Sweeper roles are commoditising. You want to be in both, but the second shouldn't be all you are.

**The IBM reversal** (Aug 3, Kelly Services): IBM cut HR roles to AI, then tripled US entry-level hiring plans when the AI stumbled on the 6% of complex/ethical cases. Commonwealth Bank reversed AI voice-bot layoffs after the bot increased call volume. 32% of US hiring managers who eliminated a role for AI had to rehire for the same role. The lesson isn't "AI doesn't work" — it's "AI needs a human for the finish line definition and the edge cases." That's a Risk Steward and an Editor, not a replacement.
