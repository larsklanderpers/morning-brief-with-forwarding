## 🎙️ Podcasts & Newsletters

**AI Daily Brief — "The Job Positions of the AI Future"** (July 5) · Three key points: (1) product-facing archetypes (prototyper/builder/sweeper/grower/maintainer) get the attention, but the *outward*-facing ones (editor/scout/evangelist/orchestrator/conductor/risk steward) were the missing half; (2) the meta-shift is everyone moving from *doing* a job to *managing agents* that do it; (3) the durable play is becoming the maker for your function.

**Ethan Mollick — "The Twilight of the Chatbots"** (One Useful Thing) · [oneusefulthing.org](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/) · Mollick's frame: we're moving from non-experts using chatbots to fill gaps → experts using *agents* to get work done. His Opus 4.7 example ran 14 hours solo and produced what he estimates as weeks of engineering. The kicker from a Claude Code study: the *more* domain experience someone had, the more they got out of the tool.
💡 Constructive read: The panic assumed AI flattens expertise. Mollick's data says it *levers* it. The leverage goes to whoever can define the job precisely and check the output — which is exactly what deep experience buys you. Your expertise didn't become worthless; it became the steering wheel.

## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

Three concrete titles the market is actually hiring for right now, each mapping cleanly onto the architecture-of-work lens:

- **Agent Orchestration Specialist** → the **Orchestrator/Conductor** archetype made into a job. Designs, deploys, and supervises teams of agents; sets guardrails and monitors performance. Called out (Eightfold, McKinsey/Box/LinkedIn's "20 agentic jobs") as the most critical hire of 2026.
- **Forward-Deployed Engineer (FDE)** → **Prototyper + Evangelist** fused. Embeds inside a customer to build and tune agentic workflows on their stack. Palantir-origin, now hired by OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, EY; listings up 800%+ in 2025.
- **AI FinOps Specialist** → the **Risk Steward** wearing a green eyeshade. Answers the unglamorous but decisive question: is this AI system making money or just burning tokens? Per State of FinOps 2026, 98% of practices now manage AI spend.

The signal: the archetypes NLW sketched aren't theoretical. The market is already minting job titles for them — and the fastest-growing ones are the *outward-facing* archetypes (orchestrator, evangelist, steward), the half everyone under-weighted.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)
The displacement camp is walking things back. Altman now says he's "delighted to be wrong"; Amodei has softened toward automation *expanding* the work people do. The Yale Budget Lab still finds no clear AI fingerprint on unemployment through early 2026 — occupational mix isn't shifting the way the apocalypse thesis predicted. The honest counterweight: finance and information-sector payrolls are down ~28K/month in 2026, and global tech layoffs crossed 115K by May, with some firms citing AI (and some, per Altman, "AI-washing" unrelated cuts). Real, but not the rout that was forecast.

## 📌 Today's Signal
The community conversation converged on one uncomfortable-but-empowering fact this cycle: **the companies using AI hardest are hiring more, not less.** Stack that against Mollick's finding that agents reward domain expertise and NLW's archetype map, and the actionable read is clear — the winners aren't resisting the tools or being replaced by them; they're becoming the *maker for their function*, the person who redesigns how the work gets done and orchestrates the agents that do it. The ExperiencedDevs thread is the necessary caution: outsource the keystrokes, never the judgment. If you're trying to adapt rather than despair, the move this week is concrete — pick the one workflow you own, rebuild it around a small team of agents, and make yourself the orchestrator. That's not a job you're waiting to be given; it's one you can start today.
