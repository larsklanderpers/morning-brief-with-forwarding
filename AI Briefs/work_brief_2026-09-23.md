## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)** - 982 open roles, one tracker (Plank), September 2026
One tracker's title-matched count, not a market total. Job-board counts for this title are notoriously soft: a broad OR-match for the same role was killed on 2026-08-23 after Indeed's own count dropped roughly 70x when restricted to the actual title. 982 is the conservative end, and that is why it is the number printed here.
In 2026 the FDE title has become a common route for getting AI that works in a demo to work *inside an actual company*. It no longer names one coherent thing: it covers DevOps, enablement, custom solutions, data integration, and agent building. That's not a problem; it's the shape of what "Builder + Orchestrator" looks like when companies are hiring for it rather than naming it.
*Architecture-of-work fit: **Builder / Orchestrator***

**Algorithm Auditor** - emerging AI assurance role, growth rate not sourced here
Acts as QC for AI decisions - drift detection, fact-check loops, compliance certification to ensure AI decisions are traceable and fair. Starting to appear in financial services, healthcare, and insurance.
*Architecture-of-work fit: **Risk Steward***

**AI FinOps Sr. Engineer** - appearing in enterprise job boards (Cargill, Microsoft, others)
Token economics, unit cost models, cost guardrails for AI/ML workloads. The "who pays for all these agent calls?" question now has a job title. This is FinOps for the silicon workforce - tracking spend on AI inference the way DevOps tracked compute costs a decade ago.
*Architecture-of-work fit: **Sweeper / Maintainer** (keeping the system economically sustainable)*

**AI Workforce Manager** - new people-management archetype
Coordinate blended human-AI teams, onboard and supervise agents at work. The "who manages the bots" role is real and it's hiring. Not a DevOps role - a management role for people who understand both operational context and agent behavior.
*Architecture-of-work fit: **Conductor***

**Agent Supervisor / Customer Success Orchestration Manager** - operational hybrids
Bridges operations and technology; handles escalation paths when agents fail and owns the handoff protocols between AI and human. Customer-facing, not backend.
*Architecture-of-work fit: **Orchestrator / Editor***

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

Only Altman conceded: "my intuitions were just off," at the Commonwealth Bank conference with Matt Comyn, 26 May 2026. Amodei did not walk his warning back, he re-framed it as a Jevons argument. The sequence is worth printing without the causal claim that usually gets attached to it: the statements came on 5 and 26 May, the IPO filings on 1 and 8 June. Filings after statements is a sequence, not a motive.

A16z called the doom framing "unhelpful marketing, bad economics and worse history." Treat that as a position, not a confirmation: it is a synthesis of four papers, one of which is the Yale Budget Lab work usually cited beside it, written by a firm with direct portfolio interest in the conclusion. The Budget Lab's own September CPS update is the better citation and it holds: exposure, usage, occupational and industry dissimilarity all flat, with no clear AI footprint in the labour data ([Budget Lab](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/evaluating-impact-ai-labor-market-september-cps-update)).

The firm-level picture is the other half. Layoffs.fyi counts 128,536 tech employees cut at 299 companies from 1 Jan to 10 Sep 2026, which has already passed all of 2025 (123,941 at 269 companies), not "on pace to." Challenger is a separate tracker on a separate basis and should never be blended with it: 155,126 tech cuts through August, and AI named in 116,175 US cuts across all sectors, its top stated reason this year. Meta and Snap cited AI explicitly; Amazon framed its cuts as bureaucracy and culture with AI as enabler, which is AI-adjacent, not "citing AI." The disruption is real at the firm level and still not visible in aggregate labour data, which is the whole tension rather than a contradiction to resolve.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The AIDB "Job Positions of the AI Future" episode is the convergence point for everything this brief tracks. The archetypes framework - Prototyper, Builder, Sweeper, Maintainer, Scout, Editor, Grower, Evangelist, Orchestrator, Conductor, Risk Steward - finally gives practitioners a vocabulary for what's actually happening to work that isn't reducible to "replaced" or "safe." The Microsoft data (58% producing work they couldn't a year ago) and the emerging job titles (FDE, Algorithm Auditor, AI Workforce Manager, AI FinOps) are the same story told in job postings rather than podcast episodes. The thread heading into Q4: not whether AI changes work, but *which archetype do you want to become*, and who in your function is going to be the Maker who figures it out first. Every function still has that seat open.
