## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**AI Orchestrator / Agent Orchestration Specialist** — The most concrete new title emerging across job boards.
- 739 listings in February 2026 (Lightcast data), a **1,294% increase** year over year
- Average salary: ~$103K; experienced: up to $175K
- Key insight: **domain expertise preferred over coding skills** — operations, project management, and HR backgrounds actively sought over pure technical profiles
- Multiple sources (Eightfold, ServiceNow, IT Brew) calling this the most important hire of 2026
- Maps to: **Orchestrator + Conductor** archetypes in the framework

**AI Agent Manager** — Formalized as a job title by HBR in February 2026. Appears on Salesforce and other major company job boards. Distinct from "AI Engineer": the agent manager doesn't build the agent, they govern its behavior, set permissions, and own outcomes.
- Maps to: **Risk Steward** archetype

**Forward-Deployed Engineer** — In-demand title blending customer-facing domain work with technical deployment capability. Figma CEO Dylan Field noted in October 2025 that job titles were merging — "everyone is becoming a product builder."
- Maps to: **Builder + Grower** archetypes

**Vercel's @v agent model (live case study):**
Vercel has deployed three internal production agents: a lead processing agent (automated sales qualification, saving hundreds of days of manual work); an anti-abuse agent (59% faster content moderation); a data analyst agent (automated SQL for BI). Every human at Vercel now works in relation to one or more agents.
- This is what **Conductor** looks like in practice: you're not replacing judgment, you're directing a team of agents that operate at a pace and scale no human team could.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

The most important development in the displacement debate: **Altman and Amodei both walked it back.** The Yale Budget Lab tracker (updated through March 2026) shows no meaningful change in employment for AI-exposed occupational groups since ChatGPT launched. Altman said he's "delighted to be wrong." Amodei now suggests AI may expand the work people do rather than shrink it. This doesn't mean displacement isn't coming — it means the timeline and mechanism are less clear than the 2025 predictions implied. The honest read: AI is boosting productivity faster than it's eliminating headcount, for now. The structural question of what happens when productivity gains don't translate into new hiring remains open.
