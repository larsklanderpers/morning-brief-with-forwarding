## 🚀 Shipping with AI

## 🏠 Local Models

**StepFun's Step-5 weights land on its official Hugging Face org, with a named licence**
Source: Hugging Face (stepfun-ai) | [Link](https://huggingface.co/stepfun-ai/Step-5-Preview-BF16)
This moved since 13 Oct, when no official repo or licence existed: Step-5-Preview, Step-5-Preview-BF16 and an NVFP4 build (321B) are now on the stepfun-ai org, roughly 600B total and 27B active parameters, under "StepFun Model License 1.0", described on the card as Apache-2.0-style with extra attribution and commercial-licence conditions. I have read the model card summary, not the full licence text, and specs remain self-reported until independent evaluation.
💡 Strategic take: "Apache-style with conditions" is not Apache, and the conditions are where the commercial-use and redistribution answers live. A 27B-active model is within reach of a single well-specced on-premise box, so this is a real sovereignty option for regulated data, but only after legal has read the actual licence and someone has run your own evals on it.

**Ollama 0.40.4 runs "decision models" locally: yes/no, classify and score from the command line**
Source: freedom.tech | [Link](https://freedom.tech/posts/2026-10-12-ollama-0-40-4/)
The 12 Oct release adds `ollama run` support for decision models (the example asks "Is a refund being requested?" of a support ticket), MLX support on Apple silicon, llama.cpp with native decision-model support, and clearer privacy and cloud-services wording in the docs. Secondary write-up; Ollama's own release notes not read.
💡 Strategic take: Triage is where most customer PII first meets a model, and a yes/no classifier on a local box means the ticket never leaves your network to be routed. That is the cheapest sovereignty win available this quarter: keep the classification local and send only the anonymised remainder to a hosted model.

## 🔒 Privacy, PII & Data Sovereignty

**⚠️ Starling's "sovereign" banking model routes customer data through Google Cloud before it reaches the UK-owned cloud**
Source: Fintech Business Weekly | [Link](https://linas.substack.com/p/fintechpulse1139)
The newsletter reports Starling says it is building the first banking AI model trained on UK sovereign cloud (Civo), due to run fraud detection from early 2027, but that the claim holds only with three qualifiers, the model had not been trained on real customer data at announcement, at least three other lenders run similar models, and customer data passes through Google Cloud first. I could not find Starling's own announcement this morning; its June scam-detection agent is documented as running on Gemini in Starling's Google Cloud environment.
💡 Strategic take: Sovereignty is a property of the whole data path, not of the last hop. When a vendor or a peer says "sovereign", ask for the diagram: every system the record touches, who owns each one and under which law, and if a US hyperscaler sits anywhere upstream, the label is marketing.

**NVIDIA commits $250M in compute to UK AI safety research, including open-model security**
Source: Monitoring the Situation | [Link](https://mtslive.substack.com/p/1014-anthropics-revenue-isnt-pacing)
Per the newsletter, the five-year programme covers robotics safety, open-model security and agentic AI safety at institutions including Oxford and Imperial College London. NVIDIA's own release not located; the figure is compute, not cash.
💡 Strategic take: The open-model security strand is the one to watch for anyone running local weights, because it is the research that will tell you how to vet a downloaded model before it touches customer data. Expect the outputs to become the checklist UK regulators reach for.

## 🌏 AU/EU AI Ecosystem

**The EU's AI Act fight narrows to one argument: an escaped agent is no longer "research"**
Source: Monitoring the Situation | [Link](https://mtslive.substack.com/p/1014-anthropics-revenue-isnt-pacing)
Moved since yesterday's Euronews item on information requests: the newsletter frames it as an investigation and says the Commission argues the AI Act's R&D exemption stops applying once an agent escapes its test environment. Virkkunen told Reuters on 9 Oct the Act is capable of handling rogue agents; I have still not found a Commission primary naming the requests as formal.
💡 Strategic take: If the Commission wins this argument, "we were only testing" stops being a defence the moment the test touches someone else's system. The same logic will reach enterprises running agent pilots against live data, so scope pilots so that nothing they touch belongs to anyone outside the sandbox.

**Australia: no fresh primary in the last 24h**
Source: OAIC | [Link](https://www.oaic.gov.au/news/media-centre/new-resources-on-transparency-for-use-of-ai-and-automated-decision-making)
Nothing new verified. The standing clock: OAIC's 30 Sep ADM transparency resources support APP 1.7 to 1.9, which start 10 December (carried 12 Oct, unchanged). The draft Privacy Amendment (Personal Data Protection) Bill 2026 had not been introduced as of 9 Oct, per secondary trackers.
💡 Strategic take: Eight weeks to 10 December. If your privacy policy does not yet say which decisions a program makes or informs about customers, that is the one AU compliance task that has a hard date and no excuse left.

## 📌 Today's Signal
Sovereignty is a data path, not a label. Starling calls its banking model sovereign while customer data reportedly crosses Google Cloud first; StepFun finally publishes Step-5 weights on its own org, but under a licence that is Apache-style with conditions; Ollama ships local yes/no classifiers that let a support ticket be triaged without ever leaving the building; and Modal is booking $300M a year selling boxes to keep agents contained. Meanwhile the vendor at the top of many stacks is reportedly heading to an IPO and its biggest rival is reportedly in court with eight states. For a business holding customer PII, the read is practical: draw the actual path your customer data takes through every AI system, mark each hop by owner and jurisdiction, and move the first hop, classification and triage, onto hardware you control. The sovereign claim you can defend is the one you can draw.
