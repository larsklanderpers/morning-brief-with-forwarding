## 🔬 Frontier Labs

**Meta ships Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, with memory that persists across conversations**
Source: Dataconomy | [Link](https://dataconomy.com/2026/10/13/meta-muse-wonder-full-ai-models/)
Meta released two models on 12 Oct: Muse Wonder through the Meta Model API and Muse Code at $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output, and Muse Wonder Full for Muse Code subscribers only, not via the API. Both add Active Memory (context carried across weeks or months of conversations) and Active Focus (keep working, recover from errors, check with the user before important actions); Monitoring the Situation reports Wonder Full benchmarks just under Claude Opus 5.5, a vendor-sourced comparison.
💡 Strategic take: The headline is the memory, not the benchmark. A model that remembers "important personal context" for months is a new personal-data store, and nobody has yet published where it lives, how long it is kept or how a customer asks for it to be erased. Before any staff member pastes customer detail into it, those three answers belong in writing.

**Zuckerberg rejects calls for an AI slowdown on the back of Wonder Full**
Source: Monitoring the Situation | [Link](https://mtslive.substack.com/p/1012-muse-wonder)
Per the newsletter's 12 Oct summary, Zuckerberg said advanced AI can be made safe through engineering safeguards, that labs are responsible for safety before release, and that Meta will keep investing towards "personal superintelligence." I have not read the primary remarks.
💡 Strategic take: "We will make it safe ourselves" is the position Brad Smith argued against a day earlier when he asked for incident reporting as a legal duty. Two of the largest vendors now disagree in public on whether self-assessment is enough, which tells a buyer not to treat either one's assurances as a control.

**White House moves three senior CAISSI testing officials out of Commerce**
Source: Monitoring the Situation | [Link](https://mtslive.substack.com/p/1012-muse-wonder)
The administration is moving three unnamed officials from the Center for AI Standards and Safety Innovation at Commerce into roles reporting directly to the White House, after a run of leadership turnover and resourcing doubts at the body. This is one newsletter's summary; I could not find the underlying report.
💡 Strategic take: Pre-release model testing in the US is drifting from an agency to the President's office in the same month the UK channel was frozen and then restored. Testing that sits closer to politics can be switched on and off faster, so treat any government "evaluated" stamp as a point-in-time fact, not a standing assurance.

**Microsoft says Maia 300 becomes its primary inference chip from 2027, per one newsletter**
Source: Monitoring the Situation | [Link](https://mtslive.substack.com/p/1012-muse-wonder)
The newsletter reports Microsoft released its third-generation Maia 300, built for long-context and long-running agent work, and will make it the primary inference chip for its products from 2027 and use it for internal model training. I could not find Microsoft's own announcement; earlier reporting (The Information, August) put TSMC talks at more than 300,000 units for 2027.
💡 Strategic take: When the cloud owns the chip, the model and the data centre, its inference price stops tracking anyone's market but its own. That is a lock-in risk worth pricing now, because the exit cost from a vertically integrated stack only rises.

## 🚀 Shipping with AI

**AI labs are buying dead startups' Slack, email and code to train agents**
Source: AI Chat Daily | [Link](https://www.aichatdaily.com/ai-business/ai-labs-buying-dead-startups-data-train-models)
SimpleClosure says its Asset Hub has brokered more than 150 deals since April and expects to return over $10M to shuttered startups by year-end, with 47% of its shutdown deals now including code or data; one founder reports about $25,000 for a codebase. The report says Google outbid Mercor in August for about 100M emails, 500M Teams messages and 176,000 employee records from Spirit Airlines' bankruptcy for $10M; the piece credits MIT Technology Review and Bloomberg for parts and I have not seen those primaries. Sellers say PII is scrubbed; an attorney quoted calls the data "legally radioactive," and there is no US federal law banning the sales.
💡 Strategic take: Every vendor you hand customer data to is one insolvency away from being a seller of it. Read the termination and insolvency clauses in your SaaS and AI contracts this quarter: what happens to your data, and your customers', if the vendor folds and a liquidator starts taking bids.

**Grok Bot moves to Claude Opus 5.5 underneath, and can now read bank data and send email**
Source: Fintech Business Weekly | [Link](https://linas.substack.com/p/how-to-hire-a-grok-bot)
The newsletter reports the model behind Grok Bot has changed twice in six weeks and is now moving to Anthropic's Claude Opus 5.5, and that Bots can join Slack, propose work, read bank data and send email from a dedicated address; it cites Bloomberg for 418,000 weekly users by mid-September.
💡 Strategic take: The model under your agent changed twice in six weeks without a contract change. For anything touching customer data, that is a change of sub-processor, and your privacy notice and vendor register should say so; if the vendor will not tell you when it swaps, that is your answer on whether to use it.

## 🏠 Local Models

**Google ships EmbeddingGemma 2 at 740M parameters**
Source: Google AI for Developers | [Link](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/releases)
Google's Gemma release page lists EmbeddingGemma 2 in a 740M parameter size, released 6 Oct. It follows Perplexity's pplx-embed-v2 (10 Oct brief) as the second small open embedding model in a week.
💡 Strategic take: Embeddings are where your documents get turned into searchable vectors, which makes them the step where PII most often leaks to a third-party API. A sub-billion-parameter open model means that step can run on a box you own, and the retrieval index never has to leave your network.

**Step-5 weights still due Thursday, still no licence**
Source: Forkast | [Link](https://forkast.news/step-5-ships-in-four-days-and-its-license-is-still-missing/)

💡 Strategic take: Watch Thursday for the licence, not the weights. Without one, "open" is a download link, not permission.

## 🔒 Privacy, PII & Data Sovereignty

**⚠️ Ukrainian strikes reportedly take out all three YandexGPT supercomputers, and Russian payments with them**
Source: AI Chat Daily | [Link](https://www.aichatdaily.com/ai-news/ukrainian-drones-knock-out-three-yandex-data-centers)
Per the report, drone strikes on 8, 9 and 11 Oct damaged Yandex facilities in Sasovo, Kaluga (63 MW) and Vladimir, reportedly disrupting payments, search, mail, ride-hailing, delivery and maps; an unnamed expert cited by Kommersant puts replacement at up to six months. Every figure is secondary, attributed to the Moscow Times, Kommersant and Ars Technica; hardware damage is unquantified.
💡 Strategic take: Sovereignty arguments usually stop at "keep the data onshore." This is the other half: concentrate it onshore in three buildings and it fails as one. Residency and resilience are separate controls, and an AI workload holding customer data needs both a home and a second home.

**EU AI Act high-risk duties are live; the GDPR "legitimate interest for AI" fight is not settled**
Source: Tech Policy Press | [Link](https://www.techpolicy.press/eu-set-the-global-standard-on-privacy-and-ai-now-its-pulling-back/)
Secondary sources agree the AI Act's high-risk obligations, including Article 10 data governance, applied from 2 August 2026, while the Digital Omnibus proposal to let AI training rely on legitimate interest under GDPR Article 6(1)(f) remains under negotiation. One source claims a separate AI omnibus entered into force on 27 July; I could not confirm that on EUR-Lex, so treat it as unverified.
💡 Strategic take: Nothing here is a primary text, and it should not be cited to a board as one. The safe working position for a deployer is unchanged: Article 10 documentation is yours even if the model is someone else's, and GDPR still applies in full until the Omnibus is actually adopted.

## 🌏 AU/EU AI Ecosystem

**Australian AI suppliers ask Parliament for sovereign procurement targets**
Source: MLex | [Link](https://www.mlex.com/mlex/artificial-intelligence/articles/2534890)
Australian AI executives told a parliamentary inquiry (reported 7 Oct) that government reliance on overseas models, limited contract access and uneven tax treatment hold back local suppliers; Vault Systems said "public AI investment trails other countries." They warned Australia risks capturing mostly the low-value end, such as data-centre construction.
💡 Strategic take: Name the commercial interest: these are suppliers asking for a preference in public spending. That does not make them wrong, and it is the first concrete form of the sovereign-AI argument the banks raised last week without a primary. A procurement target is a policy you can price; a "national strategy" is not.

**AWS publishes a Digital Sovereignty Lens for its Well-Architected Framework**

💡 Strategic take: A hyperscaler writing the sovereignty checklist is grading its own homework, and portability will be the weakest chapter by design. Use the questions, supply your own answers, and score the exit section yourself.

## 📌 Today's Signal

Today's thread is that customer data is outliving the promises made when it was collected. Meta's new models remember users for months; failed startups sell their Slack and email to labs; a bankrupt airline's staff messages went to the highest bidder; and Grok Bot's underlying model changed twice in six weeks without its users signing anything. Meanwhile the Yandex strikes show what concentration does to data that never left home. For a business with PII obligations, the question is no longer only where the data sits today but who can end up holding it tomorrow: through a vendor's memory feature, a vendor's insolvency, or a silent model swap. The controls are contractual and boring: insolvency and deletion clauses, a sub-processor register that names the model, and a second site. Write them before you need them.
