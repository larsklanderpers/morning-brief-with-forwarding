## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

The official walkback is now complete: both Sam Altman and Dario Amodei have publicly reversed their 2025 AI jobs apocalypse predictions (Fortune, May 2026). Amodei, who warned last year that 10–20% unemployment was plausible, now says automation may *expand* the work people do. The Yale Budget Lab found no meaningful change in unemployment or occupational mix through March 2026 for workers in high-AI-exposure jobs. Brookings reaches a similar conclusion.

The macro numbers aren't moving — but the sector-specific picture isn't uniform. Companies in crypto and fintech have cited AI explicitly in recent layoff announcements. And both Mollick and NLW flag that "rolling disruption" is the right frame: each capability threshold unlocks new use cases overnight, and the macro stats are a lagging indicator of a leading-edge phenomenon. The Software Factory exists. The question is how fast it generalizes.
