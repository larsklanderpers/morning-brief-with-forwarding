## 🐦 The Conversation on X

**@emollick** (11h ago)
"Model + harness. We have barely begun to understand the best ways to do harness engineering. A huge amount of untapped potential even without models getting better (but models are getting better)"
— Responding to news that GPT-5.6 Sol hit SoTA on ARC-AGI-3 via two configuration changes. Mollick's point: the configuration layer — how you wrap and constrain a model — has barely been explored relative to its ceiling, and it's where most non-engineers can participate.

**Why it matters to work:** Harness engineering is emerging as the practitioner's domain — not model training (too technical) or pure prompting (too shallow), but the systems that govern what models do and how you check their work. This is where the Orchestrator and Editor archetypes from the architecture-of-work model live.

## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**Source: Forbes / Sandy Carter, Jun 2, 2026** — "The 20 New Agentic AI Jobs Box, McKinsey, And LinkedIn All See Coming"
https://www.forbes.com/sites/sandycarter/2026/06/02/the-20-new-agentic-ai-jobs-box-mckinsey-and-linkedin-all-see-coming/

LinkedIn: AI has already added **1.3 million new roles** (AI Engineer, Forward-Deployed Engineer, Data Annotator) plus 600K AI-enabled data center jobs. **AI Engineer is the single fastest-growing US job title for the second year running.** McKinsey frames the destination as the "agentic organization" — generalists become orchestrators, entirely new roles emerge to supervise, coach, and govern agents. WEF projects 170M new roles by 2030 vs 92M displaced: net gain of 78M.

Key roles mapped to the architecture-of-work archetypes:

| Role | Archetype |
|------|-----------|
| **Forward Deployed Engineer** (Palantir-originated; OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, EY all hiring) | Builder / Prototyper |
| **Context Engineer** (feeds model right info at right moment; memory + retrieval) | Builder |
| **AI Evals Engineer** (catches hallucinations, regressions, bias before deployment) | Risk Steward |
| **Agent Supervisor** (orchestrates hybrid human-agent workforce; KPMG: put agents on the org chart) | Conductor / Orchestrator |
| **Decision Engineer** (McKinsey's T-shaped expert: where humans and AI split the work) | Editor / Risk Steward |
| **AI Enablement Lead** (adoption programs, usage tracking — McKinsey: "agent coach") | Evangelist / Grower |
| **Conversation Designer** (voice/tone/fallback of the agent — writing discipline, not coding) | Editor |
| **Human-Agent Workforce Lead** (new HR function: manages agent performance like human counterparts) | Conductor |
| **AI Auditor & Assurance Lead** (ISO 42001 verification; pivot for risk/compliance pros) | Risk Steward |
| **AI-Augmented Sales & GTM Specialist** (McKinsey: largest new category; frontline sellers + agents) | Sweeper / Grower |

**Notable:** McKinsey is explicit that *the largest new category is not engineers — it's AI-augmented frontline workers in sales, service, HR, and operations*. Ten of the 20 roles require no code. Box CEO Aaron Levie describes positions at his company that "did not exist two years ago" across engineering, IT, and go-to-market.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

**Altman and Amodei both walked back their apocalypse predictions** (Fortune, May 26, 2026):
Altman told Commonwealth Bank's CEO he was "pretty wrong" about AI's economic impact — a reversal from his June 2025 warnings that entry-level white-collar roles were at serious risk. Amodei, who once claimed AI could eliminate 50% of white-collar jobs, now says automation may actually *expand* the work people do. Goldman Sachs CEO David Solomon has consistently argued the panic was overblown, pointing to a century of American economic history.

This isn't the same as "nothing is changing." The structural shift is real — the two-speed labor market (skills-who-adapted vs. skills-who-didn't) is already visible. What's being walked back is the sudden-cliff version of disruption.
