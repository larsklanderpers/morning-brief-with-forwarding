## 🔒 Privacy, PII & Data Sovereignty

**⚠️ Australia's automated decision-making disclosure deadline: 10 December 2026**
Source: LeadComply / FlowWorks / PADISO
URLs: https://leadcomply.com.au/privacy-act-ai-disclosure-requirements-what-australian-businesses-must-do-by-december-2026/ | https://flowworks.com.au/blog/ai-privacy-act-2026-changes

From 10 December 2026, Australian Privacy Act-covered organisations must disclose in their privacy policies where personal information is used in automated processes to make decisions significantly affecting individuals (new APP 1.7–1.9). OAIC formal guidance is expected September 2026. Penalties for non-compliance: up to AUD 50 million, three times the benefit obtained, or 30% of adjusted turnover — whichever is greatest. Data breach notifications hit 1,205 in 2025 (8% year-on-year rise).

💡 **Strategic take:** Four months out and most organisations haven't mapped which of their AI systems make or substantially assist decisions affecting individuals. The first step is an inventory — you cannot write the policy disclosure until you know what's running. September OAIC guidance will be the last piece of the puzzle before December enforcement begins. If you're waiting for the guidance before starting the inventory, you'll run out of time.

**EU AI Act high-risk obligations now enforceable (2 August 2026)**
Source: Responsible AI Labs / NeuralTrust / GDPR Register
URLs: https://responsibleailabs.ai/knowledge-hub/articles/eu-ai-act-august-2026-compliance | https://neuraltrust.ai/blog/data-sovereignty-requirements-eu-ai-act

Article 10 (data governance) and Article 50 (GPAI enforcement powers) took effect 2 August 2026. Maximum fines reach 7% of global annual turnover — exceeding GDPR's 4% cap. 78% of organisations have not taken meaningful compliance steps; over 50% lack a basic AI inventory.

⚠️ **Critical distinction:** Data residency (where data sits) ≠ data sovereignty (who controls it legally). The US CLOUD Act means data stored in Frankfurt by a US-headquartered provider is still subject to US legal compulsion. Many "EU data centre" commitments deliver residency, not sovereignty.

💡 **Strategic take:** The gap between GDPR and EU AI Act compliance is not a checkbox — it's a new class of documentation (Article 10 data governance, FRIA alongside DPIA) and a new audit trail. For Australian firms with EU customers or EU operations, the August 2 date is not theoretical. The 78% non-compliance figure suggests enforcement actions will land before year-end.

**⚠️ APRA-regulated entities: AI processing sovereignty is the unresolved gap**
Source: Lorikeet / Brightlume AI / Sunburnt AI
URLs: https://www.lorikeetcx.ai/articles/ai-support-australian-data-residency-guide | https://sunburntai.com.au/news/microsoft-australia-data-residency-not-sovereignty

CPS 234 and CPS 230 (effective July 2025) extend information security and operational risk obligations to material AI vendors. The regulatory expectation: understand where your data flows, including AI inference processing. The practical problem: many AI providers claim "Australian hosting" but route inference through Singapore or US regions during peak loads. APRA is expected to release updated CPS 230 guidance on cloud risk that will directly affect banks, insurers, and superannuation funds.

💡 **Strategic take:** "We use an Australian data centre" is not a defensible answer under CPS 230 if inference is being routed offshore during high-load periods. APRA-regulated entities need to contractually lock inference locality, not just storage — and verify it. The updated guidance when it arrives will likely make this explicit.
