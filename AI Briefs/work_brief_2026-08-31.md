## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**Agent Orchestrator** — growing fast across tech and enterprise. Sits at the intersection of AI engineering and DevOps. Responsible for coordinating multiple specialised AI agents in production. Maps to: **Conductor** archetype.
Source: https://amquesteducation.com/blog/ai-agent-orchestrator/

**AI FinOps Specialist** — addresses the rising operational costs of AI systems. Asking: is the AI system actually generating returns, or just consuming resources? Maps to: **Risk Steward** / **Grower** archetype boundary.
Source: https://www.herohunt.ai/blog/fastest-growing-ai-roles-in-2026-data-and-rankings/

**Forward-Deployed Engineer** — evaluates new AI models in customer environments; acts as internal automation engineer rewiring how the company works. One of 1.3M roles AI has *added* over the past three years (per search data). Maps to: **Builder** + **Scout** archetype blend.

Source: https://analyticsindiamag.com/ai-trends/top-7-new-job-roles-created-by-ai-in-2025/

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

Both Sam Altman and Dario Amodei have walked back their "jobs apocalypse" predictions in 2026. Altman says he was "pretty wrong" about AI's economic impact; Amodei now argues automation may expand the work people do rather than eliminate it. The Yale Budget Lab has found no statistically significant changes in occupational mix or unemployment duration in high-AI-exposure jobs since ChatGPT launched in late 2022. Both companies also filed confidential IPO paperwork in May 2026, which has prompted some scepticism about the timing of the softer tone — though the labour data is the labour data.

The honest read: displacement is real at the task level and in hiring freezes; it is not (yet) visible in aggregate employment statistics. The structural shift is underway; the macro signal is lagging.

Sources: https://fortune.com/2026/05/26/sam-altman-dario-amodei-walking-back-ai-jobs-apocalypse-prophecies-ipo/ · https://aiweekly.co/alerts/altman-and-amodei-retreat-from-ai-jobs-wipeout-warnings

## 📌 Today's Signal

The community is converging on a practical consensus that was missing eighteen months ago: the question is no longer "will AI take my job" but "which part of my job do I keep, and how do I build the part that replaces the rest?" Nate Herk's 28K-view course in 8 hours, Mollick's "coworker not chatbot" reframe, and the PwC finding that professionalised roles are growing at 2x the rate of democratised ones are all pointing the same direction — toward the Builder and Conductor archetypes as the high-ground positions. The people figuring this out in public, right now, on YouTube and Substack, are not waiting for their employer to define the new role. They are defining it themselves.
