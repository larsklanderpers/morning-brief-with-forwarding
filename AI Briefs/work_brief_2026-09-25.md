**@emollick (Ethan Mollick)** — Three threads worth tracking:

## 🎙️ Podcasts & Newsletters

**AI Daily Brief — "The Job Positions of the AI Future"** (NLW, ~Sep 22, 2026)
URL: https://www.thefuturist.co/the-job-positions-of-the-ai-future-5/
This is the anchor episode for this brief's thread. Key points:
1. The architecture-of-work model — Prototyper, Builder, Sweeper, Maintainer, Scout, Editor, Grower, Evangelist, Orchestrator, Conductor, Risk Steward — frames emerging archetypes that cut across job titles rather than replacing them. Built on Boris Cherny's Claude Code role taxonomy.
2. Orchestration ("managing a team of agents") is framed as a **baseline skill everyone acquires**, not a new department — meaning it's analogous to learning Excel in 1995, not learning to be a VP of Excel.
3. The biggest opportunity is for people in every function to become the internal "maker" — the person who translates AI capability into operational reality for their team, before anyone above them figures out it's possible.

**Ethan Mollick — "The Overhang"** (One Useful Thing, Sep 18, 2026)
URL: https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/the-overhang
This is the most substantive piece from any newsletter lane this week. Core thesis: GPT-6 Astra and Fable 5.1 are *already* capable of transformative impact across large sections of the economy — the capability overhang between what these models can do and what most organizations are actually using them for is massive. Pre-orders for Mollick's new book *Co-Existence* (out Oct 20).

The four human advantages Mollick identifies for the AI era:
- **Deep knowledge** — domain expertise lets you spot where AI is wrong, and recent Anthropic research suggests experts extract both better and *more* work from AI
- **Wide knowledge** — knowing what to ask for (e.g., "stop adding eyebrows to the headers") determines what the AI produces
- **Taste** — when making is cheap and fast, the scarce resource is knowing which output to keep
- **Agency** — willingness to test the boundaries of what's possible before anyone tells you it's allowed

💡 *This maps directly onto the architecture-of-work model: deep knowledge → Scout/Risk Steward; wide knowledge → Editor/Orchestrator; taste → Editor/Conductor; agency → Prototyper. Worth threading explicitly in a piece.*

## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**AI Agent Orchestrator / Orchestration Specialist** — Being called "the most important job of 2026" (Eightfold.ai, ServiceNow). Cross-functional role: redesigns how work moves, decides when to deploy autonomous agents vs. hand off to humans. Maps to: **Orchestrator + Conductor** archetypes.
Source: https://eightfold.ai/blog/most-important-job-2026/

**Multi-Agent Systems Analyst** — Emerging as the monitoring/quality layer for agent pipelines in production. Tracks agent drift, conflicts, and failures across concurrent workflows. Maps to: **Sweeper + Risk Steward** archetypes.

**Human-Agent Interaction Designer** — UX layer for the agentic workplace. As Mollick notes, "we don't have a vocabulary" for this yet, which means the role is creating itself. Maps to: **Builder + Editor** archetypes.

**AI FinOps Specialist** — Token budgets, inference costs, and ROI measurement for AI deployments. The CFO of the agent stack. Maps to: **Maintainer + Risk Steward** archetypes.

**Algorithm Auditor** — Regulatory compliance and bias testing for deployed AI systems. Growing with regulatory pressure, especially in EU AI Act enforcement. Maps to: **Risk Steward** archetype.

**Forward-Deployed Engineer** — Already established at Palantir; spreading to other AI-native companies. Sits between the product and the customer, adapts AI systems in the field. Maps to: **Builder + Scout** archetypes.

**The salary premium reality check:** 20–40% premium for AI proficiency across industries (WEF / multiple sources). ~28% figure circulating on practitioner YouTube. This isn't theoretical — it's in job postings now.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

The National (Sep 22, 2026) ran "How AI could wipe out one in five white-collar jobs in four years" — sourcing Anthropic's *Economic Scenarios for Transformative AI* report. Amodei's ~50% entry-level white-collar prediction remains in circulation.

Actual displacement data remains much lower than prediction: 38,279 layoffs explicitly linked to AI in 2026 YTD; modeling puts true AI-foregone positions at 200K–300K (0.13–0.20% of US nonfarm employment). The gap between the headline number and the measured number is wide — which is either a lag, or a measurement problem, or both.

WEF projection: 170M new roles created by 2030, 92M displaced — net +78M. AI/ML specialists remain among the three fastest-growing occupations globally.

The Amodei/Altman/a16z debate is less about *whether* displacement happens and more about the pace and the policy response. Nothing new broke this week to resolve that.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The community is converging on a useful distinction this week: **the capability overhang is not a threat, it's a timing problem**. Mollick's "The Overhang" makes it explicit — the models that exist *today* are already sufficient to transform large parts of the economy, but most organizations haven't figured out how to use them. The people who close that gap — who bring deep knowledge, wide knowledge, taste, and agency to their domain and translate AI capability into operational reality — are the Prototypers, Scouts, and Orchestrators the architecture-of-work model describes. The new roles emerging (Orchestration Specialist, Human-Agent Interaction Designer, AI FinOps) aren't roles that someone gets handed. They're roles that someone *claims* by being the first person in the building who knows what's possible. The window is open. It won't be open forever.
