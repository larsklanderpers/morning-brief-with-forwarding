## 🔒 Privacy, PII & Data Sovereignty

**APRA CPS 230 now in force — AI vendors are material service providers** — https://www.trillet.ai/blogs/voice-ai-data-residency-requirements-by-region
Effective July 1, 2026, CPS 230 requires regulated financial institutions to formally register AI service providers, maintain continuity plans, and hold enforceable contractual accountability.
💡 Strategic take: This quietly converts every "we'll just call the OpenAI API" decision inside an Australian bank or insurer into a documented, board-visible vendor dependency. Providers that can't demonstrate onshore processing residency — not just storage residency — will start failing procurement.

**Privacy Act automated-decision-making obligations bite from 10 December 2026** — OAIC — https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-guidance-for-organisations-and-government-agencies/guidance-on-privacy-and-the-use-of-commercially-available-ai-products
APP entities must disclose what personal information feeds substantially automated decisions. OAIC's formal ADM transparency guidance is expected by September, following the May–June consultation. ([Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/australia/australias-privacy-regulator-releases-new-guidance-on-artificial-intelligence))
💡 Strategic take: Five months out. Any Australian business using AI in decisions that touch customers needs an inventory of which PII flows into which model — and most can't produce one today. The businesses running local models have a structural head start: the data-flow map is one box.

**EU AI Act fully applicable in three weeks (2 August)** — https://www.gdprregister.eu/regulations/eu-ai-act-compliance/
High-risk system obligations become enforceable, penalties up to 7% of global turnover — above GDPR's ceiling. On-premise deployment sidesteps GDPR Articles 44–49 transfer analysis entirely. ([IOMETE](https://iomete.com/resources/blog/data-sovereignty-compliance-2026-dora-ai-act), [local AI guide](https://dev.to/aiengineeringat/running-ai-locally-in-2026-a-gdpr-compliant-guide-oml))
💡 Strategic take: The compliance-cost asymmetry is becoming the strongest commercial argument for local AI: Articles 28, 35 and 44–49 mostly evaporate when prompts never leave your infrastructure. Vendors selling "sovereign cloud" will blur storage vs. processing residency — that distinction is where the risk hides.

⚠️ **Flag:** No fresh AI-processed-PII breach in the last 24h, but the MeetingTV/Palo Alto lawsuit (below) is a live example of AI-generated output causing concrete commercial harm — worth watching as a liability precedent.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The through-line today is that **sovereignty has moved from talking point to procurement criterion, and the infrastructure is arriving to meet it**. APRA CPS 230 took effect this month and makes AI vendors formally registered material service providers; the EU AI Act becomes fully enforceable in three weeks; Australia's ADM transparency rules land in December. On the supply side, the answer is materialising fast: GLM-5.2 under MIT within striking distance of frontier capability, Ollama funded with $88M, open weights inside GitHub Copilot, and Mistral building €4B of European compute. Meanwhile the June Fable 5 export ban — resolved but not forgotten — did more to sell multi-model and local-first strategies than any vendor pitch ever could. For a decision-maker holding customer PII, the strategic read: the capability cost of running AI you control has collapsed to near zero at the same moment the regulatory cost of AI you don't control is being written into enforceable rules. The window where "we'll sort out data flows later" was defensible is closing on a published schedule.
