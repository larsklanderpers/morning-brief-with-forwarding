**Developer job market — the real-time picture:**
The ground truth from dev communities is bifurcated and worth keeping distinct:
- Entry-level developer roles: down ~25% YoY, with Stanford's Digital Economy study showing a 20% employment decline for developers aged 22–25 since 2022. Practitioners on Blind are actively discussing it: "stop asking how to get an AI job — start doing AI work in the job you have."
- Senior/specialist roles: AI specialization up 49% in job postings; companies are prioritizing "strong engineers whose output amplifies with AI."
- The practitioner advice circulating is consistent: don't seek a new AI job, turn your current role into one.

**Broad community signal (r/singularity, r/artificial):**
The dominant thread framing in AI community spaces remains displacement anxiety at the consumer layer vs. expanding opportunity framing in practitioner spaces. Experienced engineers in community forums are reporting that AI is making senior devs significantly more productive (consistent with Microsoft's 80% "producing work I couldn't before" figure for frontier users) while entry-level pipelines shrink.

## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) — 🔥 hottest concrete role right now**
- Embedded with customers to make AI products work in messy real-world environments; mid-level engineers earning $300–400k
- Job postings up 1,165% YoY (per Mercor); Palantir, OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Mistral all hiring heavily
- Architecture-of-work map: this is the **Builder + Conductor** combination — someone who builds the workflow *and* orchestrates it in production inside a client org
- URL: https://www.mercor.com/resources/experts/new-artificial-intelligence-job-opportunities/

**AI FinOps / AI Token Economist**
- Roles focused on token economics, cost visibility, and financial accountability of AI workloads ("Lead, AI FinOps & Consumption Governance" actively being hired at Cargill and others)
- Architecture-of-work map: **Risk Steward** role — the person who keeps the agentic org from burning compute budget at scale
- As inference scales, this role scales with it. Not speculative.

**Algorithm Auditor / AI Systems Auditor**
- Quality-control and compliance role triggered by EU AI Act rollout; companies now required to maintain human oversight and bias testing
- Prompt Engineer as a title is collapsing; the skill is being absorbed into Algorithm Auditor, LLM Quality Analyst, and AI Pipeline Engineer
- Architecture-of-work map: **Editor + Risk Steward** — someone who reads the system's outputs against the intent and catches drift
- URL: https://www.solidaitech.com/2026/04/prompt-engineer-job-dead-ai-careers.html

**AI Prompting as a general fluency (not a job title)**
- Andrew Ng's new course "AI Prompting for Everyone" signals the commoditization phase: prompting is becoming a literacy, not a specialty. The value has moved to the architecture layer above it.
- Architecture-of-work map: this is the baseline the Orchestrator and Conductor sit on top of.

**Agent Workflow Designer / Agentic Architect**
- Emerging role: designing agent task graphs, managing tool access and permissions, handling error recovery when agents go wrong
- Architecture-of-work map: **Orchestrator** — pure version of the role NLW is describing
- WEF projects 170M new roles by 2030 vs. 92M displaced = net +78M, with AI/ML specialists among the three fastest-growing globally

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

The macro labor data doesn't yet show what the C-suite rhetoric implies. BLS data through March 2026 shows no significant AI-related shifts in occupation mix or unemployment duration. Altman admitted in May 2026 he was wrong about the pace of entry-level white-collar job loss: "I'm delighted to be wrong about this." Amodei hasn't fully backed down — telling Axios at a September event that "the first step toward solving these problems is being honest with the population that they exist." 

The honest picture: displacement is real in specific pockets (entry-level dev, certain content roles), but the catastrophe timeline has slipped. The leading indicator isn't mass layoffs — it's the 25% drop in entry-level hiring and the near-total absorption of the "prompt engineer" job title into more senior compound roles.

## 📌 Today's Signal

Today the community conversation crystallized something it's been circling for months: the job question isn't "which titles survive?" — it's "which *mode* of work are you in?" NLW named the archetypes today and named the role everyone should be gunning for in *their* org: the Maker, the person in any function who maps what AI-enabled work actually looks like for their team. That's not a job description — it's a move. Andrew Ng is teaching the fluency layer. Matthew Berman is demonstrating overnight agent workflows from his desk. The practitioners who are winning right now aren't waiting for a new job category to open; they're reshaping the job they have. The architecture-of-work frame gives you the vocabulary to describe what you're building — the Prototyper who ships fast, the Orchestrator who runs the agents, the Risk Steward who keeps it from going sideways — and the reframe from "will AI take my job" to "which archetype am I becoming" is the most actionable thing you can hand someone today.
