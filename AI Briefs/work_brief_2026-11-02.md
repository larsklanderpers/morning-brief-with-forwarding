## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**The AI support engineer: "less of the doer but more of the checker"**
Source: Business Standard | [Link](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/traditional-entry-level-engineering-roles-have-collapsed-due-to-ai-ltm-126110100668_1.html)
Gururaj Deshpande, chief delivery officer at Indian IT services firm LTM, told Business Standard (1 Nov) that L0 to L2 entry roles have collapsed in its delivery model: "Any new deal that we bid for, we never put a L0 role." The new roles he names are AI support engineers, AI architects and "tokenomic experts", with humans supervising and verifying machine output. The same interview says LTM still expects to hire at least 6,000 fresh graduates this year, as cheaper AI-native engineers. Company's own account; it is pitching outcome-based pricing to clients at the same time.
💡 Constructive read: The bottom rung was not removed, it was swapped for a different rung. The first job is now checking rather than typing, so the graduate who can explain why a piece of generated code is wrong walks in ahead of the one who can only write it.

**The field solution specialist: engineers who define the problem and direct the agents**
Source: AI Chat Daily | [Link](https://www.aichatdaily.com/ai-business/hd-hyundai-robotics-says-ai-agents-palantir-work)
HD Hyundai Robotics says agents from its Palantir work, live since August, cut average engineering task time from 21.5 to 7.9 days, about 63%, and it expects robot programming costs to fall by more than half. Agents analyse existing robot programs and flag improvements; on-site engineers refine the changes, and the company plans to retrain them as "field solution specialists" who define problems and use AI to implement fixes. Figures disclosed 1 Nov after a keynote at Palantir's own event; Palantir is the vendor, and the report was read only via AI Chat Daily.
💡 Constructive read: A foreman who used to lay every brick now marks where the wall goes. That is the Conductor archetype on a factory floor, and the skill being kept is the one the agent lacks: years of knowing why a robot program that looks fine will fail on the line.

**The tokenomics expert: someone who owns the cost of the work, not just the work**
Source: Business Standard | [Link](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/traditional-entry-level-engineering-roles-have-collapsed-due-to-ai-ltm-126110100668_1.html)
Among LTM's new roles is a "tokenomic expert", named but not described in the interview. Read alongside its push from time-and-materials toward fixed-price and outcome-based contracts, the role appears to be about pricing and controlling model spend on a deal; that reading is this brief's inference.
💡 Constructive read: Once you are paid for the result rather than the hours, every wasted token comes out of your margin. This is the Risk Steward with a spreadsheet, and anyone who can tell a team what an agent workflow costs per outcome has a job description nobody wrote yet.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

**Mollick and Imas: put the productivity line at January 2026, not at ChatGPT**
Source: X | [Link](https://x.com/emollick/status/2116987671939875319)
Responding to DeepMind economist Alex Imas, Mollick wrote (1 Nov, about 34K views) that GPT-4 could do four minutes of independent work at a 50% success rate, current models can do multiple weeks, and January 2026 is a reasonable cut-off for when agentic work began, so large productivity effects before then would mostly be confined to coding. In a follow-up he adds that GPT-4 alone would still have had a large impact, but it needed slower process change. An argument from two commentators, not a measurement.
💡 Constructive read: Judging the car by the first year of the horseless carriage misses the road-building. If the line is January 2026, the labour studies showing little effect so far have mostly been measuring the chatbot era, so watch the 2026 data rather than treating a quiet past as proof of a quiet future.

## 📌 Today's Signal

Monday's thread is the checker. LTM says the first job in IT services is now verifying machine output rather than producing it, and still plans to hire 6,000 graduates into it. HD Hyundai is retraining field engineers to define problems and direct agents. GDPx, the hardest benchmark yet for long professional work, says the best agent finishes about one task in eight and gets roughly half-way on the legal ones. Put together, that is not a picture of work disappearing; it is a picture of work splitting into the part the agent drafts and the part a person with judgment finishes, prices and signs. Every figure today is self-reported by a company or a benchmark builder with something to sell, so hold them loosely. Through the architecture-of-work lens, the Builder is giving ground to the Editor and the Conductor. The Monday action: pick one task you hand to AI, and write down the three checks you run before you trust the result, because that list is the job description of the role that is growing.
