## 🐦 The Conversation on X

Claude in Chrome was connected. The standing searches returned crypto job spam, old posts and Marvel fan accounts, so the signal came from Mollick and from one long founder post.

**@jsnnsa (Jacob Sansbury, Spawn) - "How I Built the Most AI-Pilled Company in the World" (28 Oct, about 995K views)**
Source: X | [Link](https://x.com/jsnnsa/status/2115623382721126826)
The founder of the 10-person game-creation startup Spawn says it spends 30 times payroll on tokens ($3.1M last month) and that permanent named agents, each owning a function from the engine to finance, shipped 77,000 substantial changes in 30 days, with 1,055 of 123,548 changes reverted over the year. All figures are his own and unaudited, and "substantial" is his definition. Simon Willison tried the product briefly and calls the piece fascinating, but doubts the method suits higher-stakes software. Sansbury says irreversible actions such as payments stay behind hard-coded gates, and says Fable is the only model that works for this, which is one customer's experience of one product.
💡 Constructive read: Ants never hold a stand-up; they leave a trail and the next ant reads it. Spawn's lesson is less about the bill than the method: find the thing your work is waiting on, ask whether it exists only because people needed it, and remove it. A game with a 30-second rollback can take that risk, so copy the question, not the appetite.

**@emollick (Ethan Mollick) - personal agents still hide their working (31 Oct, about 39K views)**
Source: X | [Link](https://x.com/emollick/status/2116321075164500438)
Quoting an article on giving OpenAI's Dot responsibilities, Mollick calls it good advice but also a sign of the interface problem with personal agents: you cannot easily tell what they are working on, what they know about you or what they will do for you. In a follow-up he adds that control is welcome, especially in a personal assistant.
💡 Constructive read: Nobody hands a new assistant the keys without a list of what they are doing today. Until the products catch up, keep that list yourself: a one-page note of what your agent owns, what it knows and what it may do unasked.

**@emollick (Ethan Mollick) - OpenAI's own products keep going obsolete (30 Oct, about 43K views)**
Source: X | [Link](https://x.com/emollick/status/2116282885112205365)
Mollick lists short OpenAI product lives (Sora 6 months, Agent Builder 8, Group Chat 8, Atlas 9) and says most became obsolete within months, so even OpenAI misjudges the exponential. His numbers, from a quoted thread; not independently checked here.
💡 Constructive read: You do not plaster the walls of a house still being framed. Sansbury says each new model turns last quarter's workaround into a constraint, and Mollick's list shows that holds for the labs too, so put your effort into the judgment and the data, and hold the tooling loosely.

## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**The paratrooper: an engineer who owns no system**
Source: X | [Link](https://x.com/jsnnsa/status/2115623382721126826)
Sansbury says Spawn's engineers no longer own and deeply know one system; they drop into whatever needs them, get familiar fast and unblock the agents. He says the team spends much of its time making games in the product to feel what is wrong. One company's account of itself, with no evidence it generalises. The label is his.
💡 Constructive read: The old specialist was the only person who knew where the pipes ran; the paratrooper's skill is reading a strange building in ten minutes. Through the architecture-of-work lens this blends Sweeper and Conductor, and the way to practise is simple: volunteer for the unfamiliar system instead of the one you already know.

**The precedent keeper: turning one person's taste into a rulebook agents can read**
Source: X | [Link](https://x.com/jsnnsa/status/2115623382721126826)
After waking to 700 questions from the agents, Spawn turned two years of recorded calls into principles per person and built AI judges. In their first eight days from 6 Oct they returned over 11,000 verdicts: 7,296 decided from record, 2,213 sent back and 1,863 held for a human, with Sansbury ruling 27 times. Daily changes went from about 1,000 to 7,250 on 10 Oct, a single-day peak he reports, and he does not isolate the judges as the only cause. Humans correct the judges and they read the correction next time. The role name is this brief's inference; no employer is known to post it.
💡 Constructive read: Common law is what you get when judges write down why, so the next judge need not ask. This is the Editor and Risk Steward archetypes working as one seat, and you can start it cheaply: log your next twenty decisions with a one-line reason, because that log is the training data for your own judgment.

**The entropy source: the person who asks for 100x and deletes the old work**
Source: X | [Link](https://x.com/jsnnsa/status/2115623382721126826)
Sansbury calls the second new human job asking models for what they would not suggest. He says they compound small gains but rarely stop and rethink: he halted one that spent two days and $100,000 on 1 to 3% improvements, and scrapped six months of work covering about 70% of the engine. He says Spawn 6 came from a human prompt about loading a world a thousand times the size of GTA on a phone in a second.
💡 Constructive read: A river cuts a deeper channel forever and never decides to jump to the next valley. That is the Scout's job, and it pays to be the person in the room who asks what the plan would look like at a hundred times the scale, then who gets to throw away the thing everyone is polishing.

**The AI-native law firm hiring from the wreck of a big one**
Source: The Information | [Link](https://www.theinformation.com/articles/legal-ai-startup-bids-assets-fallen-law-firm-weil-gotshal)
Covered as a business story in today's AI brief; the work angle is the staffing. Crosby, an AI-enhanced firm of about 50 lawyers, reportedly submitted a letter of intent for Weil Gotshal's brand and internal data and hopes to hire some of its 1,200 lawyers, per AIDB's account of The Information, which was not readable here. The Times blamed departures and a failed merger for Weil's fall, not AI. Gen Legal's CEO says his company passed, arguing client data is not buyable and the talent has left, and that it is interviewing Weil attorneys directly.
💡 Constructive read: When the department store closes, the best buyers still know how to buy. Both bidders are making the same bet in different ways: the valuable lawyer in an AI firm is still a lawyer, now with the engineers next door.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

**"AI rebounding": a survey says some laid-off workers are being asked back**
Source: CNBC | [Link](https://www.cnbc.com/make-it/2026/10/30/ai-rebounding-employees.html)
Nearly 1 in 5 of more than 1,000 US workers surveyed said they had been laid off or replaced by AI and then asked to return, according to SHL's report (June to July 2026, 5% margin of error). CNBC adds Orgvue's March finding that 32% of organisations that cut staff for AI have had to rehire, and cites Ford and Amazon. SHL sells talent tools and Orgvue sells workforce planning, so both have a commercial interest, and these are self-reports, not hiring data.
💡 Constructive read: Pull the old hand off the job and you find out what was in their head, not in the manual. Context is the asset; if you are the one who knows why the process works the way it does, that is your negotiating position.

**The Netherlands targets 25,000 fewer civil servants, but the AI plan does not exist yet**
Source: Rijksoverheid | [Link](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/actueel/nieuws/2026/10/26/kabinet-werkt-aan-slagvaardige-overheid-eenvoudiger-slimmer-en-kleiner)
The Dutch government's 26 Oct statement estimates a reduction of about 25,000 FTE over several years, with savings of €900M in 2027 rising to €3.5B from 2035; police and defence are exempt. AI gets one line: targets and savings plans for its use come in Q1 2027. Headlines such as "replacing 25,000 workers with AI" go beyond that text. Reuters says about 20%, while 25,000 of the roughly 160,000 FTE cited elsewhere is closer to 16%.
💡 Constructive read: The budget was set first and the method will follow, which is the order to watch for everywhere. When a headline says AI took a role, ask whether the saving or the capability came first.

**Two big job-cut numbers with no AI cited, and a quiet calendar**
Source: Reuters | [Link](https://www.reuters.com/business/world-at-work/virgin-media-o2-cut-thousands-jobs-sky-news-reports-2026-10-31/)
Sky News reports, via Reuters on 31 Oct, that up to 4,000 of Virgin Media O2's 16,000 staff could be affected amid a debt and cost push; the company declined to comment and neither report mentions AI. Separately, Reuters reported on 30 Oct that Singapore's Q3 layoffs reached 4,800, the highest since Q4 2020, attributed to reorganisations, while total employment grew by 27,700. Proton's re-interviews (4 to 11 Nov) and Microsoft's exit decisions (early December) have not moved.
💡 Constructive read: Not every storm is the one on the forecast. Keeping the non-AI causes in view makes for a better map of your own risk than a single story of machines taking over.

## 📌 Today's Signal

All week the question has been how to stay in charge of the work, and today Spawn offered the most extreme answer yet: stop doing the work and write down how you decide. Treat its numbers as a founder's unaudited claims about a game, where a broken change costs little and rolls back in 30 seconds, and Willison's doubt about higher stakes is the right reflex. But the pattern survives the caveats. The humans left at Spawn do three things: drop into unfamiliar problems, turn their taste into precedent, and ask for what the agents would never propose. That lines up with yesterday's DeepMind essay on coordination, and with CNBC's rehiring story, where the thing companies missed was the knowledge in people's heads. Through the architecture-of-work lens, the Editor is no longer checking output; the Editor is writing the case law. The weekend action: pick one decision you make repeatedly, write the rule and the three exceptions you actually use, and see whether an agent can apply it to last month's cases.
