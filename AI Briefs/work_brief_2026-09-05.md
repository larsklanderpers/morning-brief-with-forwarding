## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**Algorithm Auditor** — Fastest-growing AI ethics title; barely existed two years ago. Job: make sure AI decisions are accurate, fair, and regulatory-compliant. Documents how models reach decisions in terms a regulator will accept.
→ Architecture lens: **Risk Steward** — the archetype that exists to hold the line between what the system can do and what it's allowed to do.

**AI Workforce Manager** — Emerging across 28% of manager job postings. Decides which tasks go to people vs. AI, keeps agents within ethical and legal bounds, manages blended human-agent teams.
→ Architecture lens: **Orchestrator** — at scale, this role is not a manager of people; it's a manager of work, wherever that work runs.

**Forward-Deployed Engineer** — Writing integration code on-site at enterprise clients; combining software development with domain understanding and direct end-user collaboration. In-demand for getting enterprise customers into AI production.
→ Architecture lens: **Builder + Scout** hybrid — building the thing while reading the room.

**AI Job Title Proliferation note:** A CTO guide published Apr 2026 calls the naming situation "the worst naming disaster since DevOps." The archetypes framework is more durable than the titles — the WEF projects 170M new roles and 92M displaced by 2030, but those numbers describe different people doing different flavours of the same underlying archetypes.

**Wage premium signal:** Workers who pair domain expertise with AI fluency command up to 56% wage premium over peers. This is the quantified version of "become the maker for your function."

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

The most significant recent development: Altman and Amodei both walked back their AI-jobs-apocalypse predictions in May 2026 (Amodei had called for 10–20% unemployment within five years; Altman had warned of an "AI jobs apocalypse"). Multiple outlets noted the timing — both companies are eyeing blockbuster IPOs, and threatening to destroy the workforce is not a good institutional-investor pitch. The Yale Budget Lab's May study found no meaningful change in unemployment or occupational mix in high-AI-exposure jobs through March 2026. Tech layoffs hit 115K through May, with companies citing AI — but Yale found no causal labour-market signal yet. The data and the narrative are diverging. ## 📌 Today's Signal

Saturday's community conversation has a quiet centre of gravity: **agency is the new leverage point, not capability**. Mollick's Twilight Factory piece is still driving discussion five days after publication because it answers a question practitioners actually face: not "will AI take my job" but "how do I design the relationship between me and the agents I run?" The thread is converging on a single claim: **the architecture-of-work archetypes are not a future state, they are already the hiring spec** — companies just haven't named them that way yet.
