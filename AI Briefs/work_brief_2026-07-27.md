## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)** — Palantir's invention, now one of LinkedIn's top three fastest-growing titles. Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and EY all hiring. The FDE sits between product and customer, turning AI capability into working deployments in-situ. Maps cleanly to **Builder + Conductor**: they build for the org they're sitting inside, not for an abstract spec.

**Agent Supervisor / Human-Agent Workforce Lead** — McKinsey's "M-shaped supervisor": the person orchestrating a hybrid team of humans and agents. Not an engineer role. McKinsey is explicit that this category will *outnumber* technical AI roles. Maps to **Orchestrator/Conductor**.

**AgentOps Engineer** — real-time monitoring, evaluation, and reliability for agent systems. The site-reliability function for autonomous agents. Maps to **Maintainer + Risk Steward** in the architecture-of-work model.

**AI Auditor / Assurance Lead** — natural pivot for risk, audit, and compliance professionals. Every industry with regulatory exposure (healthcare, legal, financial services) is creating this role. Maps to **Risk Steward**.

**AI Consultant / Strategist** — LinkedIn's number two fastest-growing role behind AI Engineer, and far less technical. Essentially the **Scout + Evangelist**: reads the landscape, translates AI capability into business cases, sells the move internally.

**Scale numbers (LinkedIn, Jan 2026):** AI has already added 1.3 million new roles. WEF projects 170M new roles created by 2030 vs. 92M displaced — net +78M. McKinsey frames the destination as the "agentic organisation."

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

Altman and Amodei have both walked back their most alarming predictions. Altman (May 2026): *"I now think I understand more about why [entry-level job elimination] hasn't happened, and I'm obviously grateful — my intuitions were just off."* The Yale Budget Lab finds no significant AI-related labour changes through March 2026. Goldman Sachs, however, counts 16,000 US jobs/month being cut with AI cited as a factor, with Gen Z and entry-level workers absorbing the brunt. Brynjolfsson (Stanford) flags a 13% relative employment decline in early-career roles in high-AI-exposure jobs. The Japan Times (Jul 17) adds the understated angle: even if job *counts* hold up, AI may be draining the *meaningful* work from roles that remain — reducing human agency without reducing headcount. The honest read: the aggregate numbers are less alarming than the headlines, but the distribution is uneven and entry-level workers are absorbing disproportionate pressure.

## 📌 Today's Signal

Two things converged this week: Mollick's "capability overhang" framing on AIDB and Netflix CPTO Elizabeth Stone's call for generalists over specialists. Separately, they're just takes. Together, they're the same argument — the bottleneck isn't what AI can do, it's the width of the gap between what's possible and what most organisations are actually deploying. The architecture-of-work model is a map for closing that gap deliberately: you don't become a different *person*, you identify which archetypes fit your temperament and lean into the one that compounds. The empowering read from today: nobody who's paying attention is still predicting mass overnight displacement. The debate has moved to *distribution and meaning* — and that's a debate worth contributing to, because the frame you choose shapes the outcome.
