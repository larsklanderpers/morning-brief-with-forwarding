## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)** — The standout labor market signal of the week.
Source: https://getperspective.ai/blog/2026-fde-hiring-trends-what-1000-job-posts-reveal
FDE postings grew >1,000% year-over-year into 2026. Key data from analysis of ~1,000 live job posts:
- Comp bands: $300K–$550K (mid-senior); $1M+ at staff/principal level at frontier labs
- Top hirers: Palantir (highest single-company volume), OpenAI, Anthropic, Google (now hiring *hundreds* for Fortune 500 deployment), Databricks, Scale AI
- Fastest growth: vertical AI startups (Harvey, Sierra, Decagon, Cresta, Hebbia)
- Skill shift: LLM integration is table stakes; "customer discovery" (structured stakeholder interviews, decomposing ambiguous business problems) is now listed in >70% of postings and is the differentiator for senior bands
- The headline: the FDE is "a research role wearing an engineering badge" — the ability to find the real problem before building is what separates the $300K band from the $1M one.
- 41% of AI engineers now spend >30% of their time customer-facing (Stack Overflow 2026), up from 12% in 2023.

**Architecture-of-work mapping:** The FDE role is a Prototyper + Builder + Scout + Orchestrator hybrid. The customer-discovery component is exactly the Scout archetype: going into the field to understand what problem is actually worth solving before the Builders and Sweepers do their work.

**AI Agent Orchestration Specialist** — Named "most important job of 2026" by Eightfold AI
Source: https://eightfold.ai/blog/most-important-job-2026/
Core competencies: AI systems fluency, workflow process engineering, domain-to-agent knowledge transfer, change leadership, governance design. Typically comes from business analysis, process improvement, or technical PM roles — not from coding backgrounds. This is the Orchestrator + Conductor archetype made explicit as a hiring category.

**AI Training/Annotation roles** — Morphing, not disappearing
The work is shifting from data labeling to model evaluation, red-teaming, and judgment calls on edge cases. Classic transition: volume commodity work is automating away; high-judgment calibration work is expanding. Risk Steward archetype taking shape.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

Altman walked back his job-apocalypse predictions in May 2026: *"I'm delighted to be wrong about that."* Amodei's 2025 prediction of 50% entry-level white-collar elimination in 5 years has not materialized in early 2026 data — productivity gains are running ahead of unemployment spikes. Carnegie Endowment frames the current landscape as three camps: Alarmed (Amodei's original position, Bernie Sanders' office), Patient (Gary Marcus: "most white-collar jobs aren't going anywhere that soon"), and Excited (net job creation view). Ground truth from practitioners: layoffs and AI buildout are happening *simultaneously* at the same companies, which the macro data smooths over. Neither camp fully captures the org-chart-level chaos.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The week's sharpest frame comes from Mollick's Summer 2026 guide, amplified by NLW: there are now two types of AI users, and the gap between them is compounding faster than anyone's onboarding program can close. One group is having chatbot conversations. The other is running agent pipelines that execute hours of real work while they sleep. The FDE hiring data makes the same point in labor market terms — the bottleneck isn't model capability anymore, it's deployment, and the people who close that gap are neither pure coders nor pure domain experts but something new: embedded practitioners who know how to ask the right question before building anything. That's the Scout-Orchestrator hybrid. That's the architecture-of-work model made concrete. The actionable read: the summer window is real. The projects NLW is pushing in AI Summer Adventure — context architecture, microbusiness builds, agent loops — are the same skills showing up in six-figure comp bands six months later.
