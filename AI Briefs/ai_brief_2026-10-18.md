## 🔬 Frontier Labs

**Anthropic publishes its own workforce data: agents logged 6 hours per human hour by August, and junior technical hires fell from 27% to 9%**
Source: Anthropic | [Link](https://anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic)
Four Anthropic Institute researchers used HR records, agent telemetry and interview scorecards: commits per engineer reached 5.1x their December 2025 level, and the junior share of technical hires dropped from 27% to 9% while non-technical junior hiring held near a quarter. The report also finds human attention is now the bottleneck: engineering and research staff would need 424 hours a week to read what their agents write. These are Anthropic's own figures about Anthropic, not independently verified, and the authors flag the selection problem themselves.
💡 Strategic take: The number to steal is not the 6x, it is the 424 hours. Every agent output nobody reads is an unreviewed decision, and in a business that touches customer PII an unreviewed decision is a liability you have chosen not to look at. Measure your reading load before you measure your agent hours.

**OpenAI's GPT-6.1 Sol reportedly carries a 500K-token context by default in ChatGPT Work and Codex**
Source: Why Try AI (Sunday Rundown #161) | [Link](https://www.whytryai.com/p/sunday-rundown-161-ai-voices)
Per one newsletter's weekly round-up; I could not reach OpenAI's own release note this run. The same round-up notes Microsoft has finished pre-training MAI-Base-2 for its own products.
💡 Strategic take: A half-million-token default means whole customer files now fit in one prompt by accident, not by design. Context length is a data-minimisation question before it is a capability one: the bigger the window, the more PII a careless paste carries offshore.

## 🏠 Local Models

**NVIDIA ships Nemotron 3.5 Bolt 74B-A9B, built to run in 24GB with native 2-bit quantisation**
Source: HPCwire | [Link](https://www.hpcwire.com/aiwire/2026/10/15/nvidia-expands-nemotron-family-with-highest-efficiency-super-vl-and-bolt-models/)
Released 15 Oct alongside the 120B Super VL; a 4GB Bolt E4B is scheduled for 23 Oct. Coverage says weights, datasets and recipes are open and one analysis names the licence as OpenMDW-1.1, but early reporting notes the "fully open" claim had not been checked against the files. Benchmark rankings are NVIDIA's own.
💡 Strategic take: A 74B-class model on a single 24GB card is the price point where "keep it on-premise" stops being a capital request and becomes a line item. Check the licence file yourself before legal signs off; "open" in a press release and "open" in a licence are two different documents.

**Liquid AI releases LFM2.5-24B-A2B, an on-device reasoning model for private agents**
Source: LLM Reference | [Link](https://www.llmreference.com/model/lfm-2.5-24b-a2b)
Released 13 Oct per secondary sources: a 24B mixture-of-experts with about 2B active parameters and a 128K context, with open weights for base and post-trained checkpoints. Liquid's claim of parity with Qwen3.6-35B-A3B on five of eight agentic benchmarks is vendor-reported and no third party has evaluated it yet.
💡 Strategic take: Two active billion parameters is laptop territory. The interesting test for a regulated business is not the benchmark, it is whether a model this small can do the triage step on customer data so only the redacted remainder ever leaves the building.

## 🔒 Privacy, PII & Data Sovereignty

**⚠️ Meta walks back commercial terms that lawyers said let it scrape any business account's website for AI training**
Source: AI Chat Daily | [Link](https://www.aichatdaily.com/ai-security/meta-walks-back-terms-appeared-authorize-scraping-publisher)
Nieman Lab first reported that terms due 30 Oct let Meta use content from a business account's domains to "train" its products, with no opt-out and a reference to "test credentials"; per The Verge, Meta has now removed the language and says the crawling terms only covered ads and business products. Meta has said throughout that its crawling did not change.
💡 Strategic take: This is the clause pattern to hunt for in every vendor renewal: a data grant buried in a "consolidation" of terms, accepted by continued use. It was caught because journalists read it. Assume nobody is reading yours, and put AI-training carve-outs in your own paper rather than relying on the vendor's.

**OAIC transparency guidance for the 10 December automated-decision duties is reportedly out**
Source: Bright Law | [Link](https://www.brightlaw.com.au/privacy-regulation-update-october-2026/)
Moved since 16 Oct, when the guidance status was unclear: a law-firm update says the OAIC has published its guidance and a flow chart for deciding whether APP 1.3 disclosure applies. I could not confirm the guidance on oaic.gov.au this run, so treat it as secondary until checked.
💡 Strategic take: If the guidance is out, the excuse for waiting has gone with it. Clubs and gaming operators running automated player-risk or eligibility scoring should be mapping which of those decisions "significantly affect" a patron now, because that list is what the privacy policy has to say in seven weeks.

## 📌 Today's Signal
The day's thread is that the question has shifted from what models can do to who reads what they do. Anthropic's own data says its staff would need hundreds of hours a week to read their agents' output; Meta's terms tried to take data through a clause almost nobody reads; GPT-6.1's default window makes whole customer files easy to send by accident; and NVIDIA and Liquid keep pushing capable models down onto hardware a business can own. For a decision-maker balancing AI capability against PII obligations, the strategic read is that unread output and unread terms are the same risk in two places. The controls that hold are the ones that do not depend on a person reading everything: models that run where the data already lives, gateways that enforce where data may go, and contract language you wrote yourself.
