## 🐦 The Conversation on X

**@emollick (Ethan Mollick)** — Agent guide going wide (Forbes, Jul 31).
Key take: Pay the $20 for a premium model. Give an agent a real task from your real life. Ask for changes rather than accept/reject. The bigger shift he's tracking: free tools work for low-stakes tasks; the expert use case now dominates over the non-expert gap-fill use case. "A shift from non-experts using chatbots to fill gaps, to experts using agents to get work done."
💡 Constructive read: The entry point isn't AI literacy — it's just starting. Pick a high-stakes real task, use the right tool, iterate. That's the whole playbook.

**Boris Cherny (Anthropic / Claude Code)** — Original X thread spawning widespread community discussion:
Five archetypes: Prototyper · Builder · Sweeper · Grower · Maintainer — mapped to product-team temperament, not job title. "Maybe product roles of the future will look more like this, and less like the domain-specific roles of today?"
NLW extended this to the outward-facing half of the org: Editor, Scout, Evangelist, Orchestrator, Conductor, Risk Steward.
💡 Constructive read: The thread's real lesson isn't the five names. It's that role identity is decoupling from discipline identity. A Grower isn't a PM or a marketer — they're a Grower, wherever that skill is needed. That's a fundamentally more portable career posture.

**Discussion thread:** Kun Chen (Meta/Microsoft alum) pushed back — archetypes let people "pick one and never question themselves again." Cherny replied: "Roles often change over time/project." Healthy friction on the idea that the archetype framework could become the new silo.

## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**Forward Deployed Orchestrator™ (FDO)** — Capita, 2026
https://www.capita.com/news-and-insights/news/2026/Capita-launches-forward-deployed-orchestrator-turn-agentic-ai-measurable-value
AI-fluent process and change expert embedded directly in client operations. Runs AI-enabled processes *live* in the environment, continuously improving performance, owns a measurable outcome. Distinct from the Forward Deployed Engineer (who builds and integrates). The FDO ensures sustained results in practice.
→ Architecture of work mapping: **Conductor + Risk Steward**. This role is the real-world embodiment of the Conductor archetype — managing a live ensemble of agents toward a business outcome rather than a technical deliverable.
💡 The fact that Capita formalized this with a trademarked title is the signal: enterprise is now naming and hiring for roles that were informal last year.

**Agent Auditor / Agent Trainer / Agent Boss** — emerging from agentic deployment at scale
https://dev.to/sidonnie_hinton_042e3478f/five-open-ai-agent-roles-that-show-where-the-work-is-moving-in-2026-5f8d
As enterprises move from pilots to scaled deployments, the "someone has to watch the agents" role is crystallizing. Agent Auditors evaluate accuracy, transparency, and vulnerability. Agent Trainers shape agent behavior through data and feedback. Agent Boss = essentially the Conductor archetype — routes tasks to the right agent, owns the output.
→ Architecture of work mapping: **Auditor = Risk Steward; Trainer = Builder/Sweeper hybrid; Boss = Conductor**

**AI FinOps Practitioner** — State of FinOps 2026: 98% of FinOps practices now manage AI spend. New specialist role emerging at the intersection of cost governance and agentic deployment oversight.
→ Architecture of work mapping: **Risk Steward** — the accountability function for AI's resource footprint.

**"Become the Maker" — Boris Cherny 5 archetypes going mainstream**
The community uptake of the Cherny framework this week is itself the signal: practitioners are looking for new *identity frames*, not just new job titles. The archetype model is catching because it maps to what people actually *do* rather than what department they sit in.
→ Watch for this to accelerate as organizations start writing JDs in archetype language rather than legacy title language.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

**Altman and Amodei walked back the apocalypse** (May 2026, Fortune/Memeburn). Both admitted earlier predictions were too extreme. Altman: "We've been roughly right on technological predictions and pretty wrong on the social and economic implications." Yale Budget Lab: no meaningful change in unemployment rates for AI-exposed workers since ChatGPT's launch. The office job market is soft, not in freefall.

**The honest version of the displacement story** (Aaron Levie via AIDB Aug 3): verifiable domains — math, code, cybersecurity — *are* seeing real automation pressure, faster than others, precisely *because* success is measurable. The unverifiable domains (legal, sales, finance, marketing) will transform at the **process level**, not through simple headcount substitution. Value builds at the applied AI layer. That's the nuanced version the debate actually needs.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The community conversation today is converging on a useful reframe: the question was never "which jobs survive?" — it was always "which *tasks* are verifiable, and therefore automatable first?" Levie's formulation (verifiable = automated first) maps directly onto the architecture-of-work lens. Sweeper and Builder work is automatable when it's well-specified; Conductor and Risk Steward work is not, because the output is judgment, not a testable artifact. The practitioner signal backs this up: people aren't fleeing these conversations — they're looking for *positioning moves*. The Cherny archetypes are spreading because they give practitioners a vocabulary to locate themselves on the map. For anyone adapting right now, the move is to identify which archetype fits your actual way of working and double into it — because the organizations formalizing these roles (Capita's FDO is the clearest example this week) are starting to hire for them explicitly.
