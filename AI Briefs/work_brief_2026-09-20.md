## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**Agent Supervisor / Agent QA Lead / AI Ops Manager** — Emerging job titles being hired for at enterprises running agents in production. These are *Conductor* and *Risk Steward* roles, exactly — the humans who set what success looks like and catch what agents miss.

**"Agentic lifecycle owner"** — Salesforce Ben's framing for the new defining credential in the Salesforce ecosystem: "In 2026, the Salesforce professional is defined not by which Cloud they know, but by which part of the agentic lifecycle they can own." Lifecycle stages map almost exactly to the archetype model: Prototyper (design), Builder (deploy), Maintainer (monitor), Risk Steward (govern).

**Salesforce Named Agents (Sep 11 launch at Dreamforce)** — Casey (customer service), Paige (IT/HR), Carter, Hunter (sales), Marshall, Piper, Fin — each with a specific business job. The naming move matters organizationally: it signals that agents aren't software you configure, they're *workers you manage*. The Conductor archetype becomes the organizational baseline, not a power-user edge.
URL: https://enterprisedna.co/resources/news/salesforce-agentforce-job-ready-agents-dreamforce-2026/

**AI FinOps / Algorithm Auditor / Forward-Deployed Engineer** — Emerging titles surfaced in practitioner job boards this week. FinOps for AI = the cost discipline that makes agents economically viable (pure Maintainer/Risk Steward blend). Algorithm Auditor = Editor archetype institutionalized in compliance. Forward-deployed engineer = Builder who lives at the client, a role that predates AI but is multiplying.

**"The $50/month AI employee" manager** — Not a formal job title yet, but the practitioner framing is converging on this: humans who manage portfolios of agents the way a team lead manages contractors. Orchestrator archetype, becoming table stakes in sales, operations, and marketing functions first.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

Amodei and Altman both walked back their "AI eliminates 50% of white-collar jobs" predictions in May 2026, with Altman saying "the displacement I feared simply hasn't materialized." Amodei's September 12 essay "We Must Pace the Frontier" shifted the concern axis to *safety and verification speed*, not labor displacement — a notable pivot.

BCG's June 2026 press release is the live datapoint to watch: "AI Is Reshaping Jobs Faster Than Companies Are Reshaping Work." The gap between technical capability and organizational adaptation is widening. Their April report finding: 50-55% of US jobs will be *reshaped* (not eliminated) over 2-3 years; 10-15% face elimination within five years. BCG's warning to over-cutters is direct: "companies that cut beyond what AI can actually deliver will see productivity drop, institutional knowledge disappear, and talent walk out."

## 📌 Today's Signal

The community narrative this week has its center of gravity in one phrase: **"manage the agents."** Three parallel things converged — Salesforce naming their agents and giving them job titles, NLW running back-to-back episodes on personal agents and "opportunity AI," and Mollick's July piece on the chatbot-to-agent transition still setting the terms. The shape-of-work story is no longer "what will AI eliminate" but "who learns to direct agents first." The architecture-of-work frame is no longer speculative — it's what Salesforce is selling to enterprise, what IBM is training engineers on, and what practitioners are discovering in their own workflows. The actionable read for someone trying to adapt: pick one archetype from the model (Conductor is the safest starting point — managing a small agent team in your actual function), build one repeatable workflow around it in the next 30 days, and own that as your proof point. The people in these communities who are energized right now are not the ones worrying about the $50/month AI employee. They're the ones *becoming* the manager of that employee.
