## 🔬 Frontier Labs

**Anthropic's newer draft S-1 shows $16.3 billion first-half revenue, an $80.7 billion net loss and cloud partners refusing it extra capacity**
Source: AI Chat Daily, citing the Wall Street Journal | [Link](https://www.aichatdaily.com/ai-business/anthropic-draft-ipo-filing-shows-16-3b-first-half)
Per the WSJ, which obtained a newer draft of the prospectus on 20 October: revenue of $16.3 billion in H1 2026 against $1.1 billion a year earlier, a net loss of $80.7 billion of which about $72 billion is a non-cash charge on convertible financing instruments, nearly $9 billion of compute spend in six months (more than all of 2025), and long-term compute commitments above $600 billion. Two customers made up 20% of first-half revenue. Linas's Newsletter adds that valuation talk has slipped from $2 trillion to about $1.5 trillion ahead of a November roadshow. I have not seen the draft itself; this is reporting on a leaked, unfiled document.
💡 Strategic take: Strip out the accounting charge and the number that matters to a buyer is the one about capacity: a vendor whose own clouds are rationing it is a vendor whose rate limits and price list can move under you. Two customers at 20% of revenue also tells you who gets served first when capacity is short. If Claude sits inside a customer-facing workflow, write a second model into the architecture now, while it is a design choice rather than an outage response.

## 🚀 Shipping with AI

**ChatGPT adds Locked Chats behind a PIN or biometrics, but nothing is newly encrypted and a forgotten PIN deletes everything**
Source: AI Chat Daily | [Link](https://www.aichatdaily.com/ai-tools/openai-adds-locked-chats-chatgpt-pin-biometric-protection)
Rolling out from 20 October across most account types except ChatGPT for Healthcare. Locked conversations move to their own section with separate memory. Per the report, locking does not add encryption, OpenAI keeps its standard logs, Temp Chats and group chats cannot be locked, and resetting a forgotten PIN permanently deletes everything behind it.
💡 Strategic take: This is a curtain, not a safe: it hides a conversation from someone holding your unlocked phone and does nothing about the provider's copy. Expect staff to read "locked" as "private" and paste more into it, so say so in your acceptable-use guidance before they do. The deletion-on-reset behaviour is the more interesting detail for records teams: a user-controlled self-destruct sits awkwardly next to any retention or legal-hold duty.

**OpenAI adds 12-hour prompt caching to the Responses API: writes cost 2.5x input, reads 0.1x**
Source: AI Chat Daily | [Link](https://www.aichatdaily.com/ai-tools/openai-adds-12-hour-prompt-caching-responses-api-gpt-5)
Live from 20 October for GPT-5.6 and later, minimum 1,024 tokens. A 12-hour cache write costs 2.5 times the uncached input rate (against 1.25 times for the standard 30-minute cache); cached reads bill at 0.1 times, or 0.05 times on GPT-6.1 Sol.
💡 Strategic take: Long caching is a cost lever and a data-residency question at the same time: whatever sits in that cache is your prompt context held on the provider's side for half a day. For a long system prompt with no customer data in it, take the discount. For prompts that carry customer records, ask where the cache lives and whether it falls under the same retention terms as the request, before the finance team switches it on for you.

## 🔒 Privacy, PII & Data Sovereignty

**⚠️ New York City proposes a rule forcing businesses to disclose AI chatbots and offer an easy path to a human, with fines from $525**
Source: AI Chat Daily | [Link](https://www.aichatdaily.com/ai-security/new-york-city-proposes-rule-forcing-businesses-offer)
The Department of Consumer and Worker Protection proposed rules on 20 October requiring businesses to disclose chatbot use, provide a simple route to a human agent and not mislead customers about support availability, with civil penalties starting at $525 per violation and a 30-day comment period. Per the report, it would make New York the first US municipality to enforce such rules. I have not read the DCWP's proposed rule text; this is a secondary report.
💡 Strategic take: This is a customer-service rule, but it encodes the principle every privacy regime is converging on: the business owns what its bot does and cannot use the bot to avoid a human. Anyone running a member-facing assistant should already be able to answer two questions in a sentence: how does a customer reach a person, and how do they know they were talking to a machine. If either answer is "it depends", fix the flow before a regulator in your own market copies New York.

**OAIC automated-decision guidance: law firms now read it as reaching human decisions made on AI summaries**
Source: Allens | [Link](https://www.allens.com.au/insights-news/insights/2026/10/mandatory-automated-decision-making-disclosure-requirements-are-coming-key-takeaways-from-the-oaic-guidance/)
The OAIC's APP 1 guidance (confirmed on the regulator's site in the 19 October brief, dated 30 September) supports the APP 1.7 to 1.9 disclosure duties that start 10 December 2026. What has moved is the commentary: Allens and other firms read the guidance as a broad reading of "automated decision", potentially capturing cases where a human makes the final call based on AI-generated summaries or recommendations. That reading is the firms' interpretation; I have not checked it against the guidance text in this run.
💡 Strategic take: If that reading holds, "a human signs off" stops being a safe harbour, and every workflow where staff decide on the back of an AI summary needs a line in the privacy policy by 10 December. Pair it with the Behavioral Capital piece below on how summaries frame decisions: the regulator and the researchers are describing the same risk from two ends. Start the inventory with any summary that feeds a decision about a member's account, eligibility or exclusion.

## 📌 Today's Signal
Today's thread is who holds the record. Anthropic's draft prospectus shows a vendor whose own clouds are rationing it, which means your workloads sit at the end of somebody else's capacity queue. OpenAI's Locked Chats and 12-hour caching both move more of your context into the provider's keeping while sounding like privacy or savings features. New York and the OAIC are converging on the opposite principle: the business owns what its automation does, a human must be reachable, and a human deciding on an AI summary may still count as automated. And the best governance writing of the week says the AI register is the wrong control because it only knows what people tell it. For a decision-maker holding customer PII, the strategic read is to govern by evidence you control: credentials you issue, logs you keep, summaries you can trace back to source, and inference you can move. Anything held only on the vendor's side is a promise, and this week showed how fast vendor circumstances change.
