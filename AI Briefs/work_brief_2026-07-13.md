## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

Both Altman and Amodei have publicly walked back their apocalypse framing — Altman says he was "pretty wrong" about near-term displacement; Amodei now suggests automation may expand the work people do ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/26/sam-altman-dario-amodei-walking-back-ai-jobs-apocalypse-prophecies-ipo/), [Time](https://time.com/article/2026/05/26/sam-altman-ai-job-losses-openAI-/)). The skeptics' walk-back coincides with IPO season; keep that grain of salt handy.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The conversation has quietly moved from *which jobs survive* to *which people thrive* — and the answer arriving from every lane is the same: the ones who keep choosing the uncomfortable, ambitious version of their own job. AIDB's thriving episode names the mechanism (volition, need for cognition), Mollick's METR chart names the trajectory (plan for the exponential, not the expert consensus line), and the hiring data names the destination (orchestrators, forward-deployed builders, AI FinOps stewards — all roles that assume agents as colleagues). For a reader trying to adapt: the moat isn't a credential, it's the habit of assigning work to machines and reserving the struggle for yourself. That's the architecture-of-work frame paying rent — the archetypes were never job titles, they were postures, and posture is trainable.
