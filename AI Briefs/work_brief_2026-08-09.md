## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**Forward-Deployed Engineer** — Job postings up 800% in 2026. Comp reaching $450K+. Role: embedding at customer sites to make AI implementations actually work in context. Architecture-of-work mapping: *Builder + Scout + Editor* combined — this is the person who bridges the model-to-operation gap. The fastest-growing job in AI right now is not in a lab.

**Agent Orchestrator** — McKinsey's framing: generalists become orchestrators of AI agents; new roles emerge to supervise, coach, and govern them. The Orchestrator is the new senior engineer role — rarely touches implementation, focused on translating messy business requirements into explicit, bounded contexts agents can't misunderstand. Architecture-of-work mapping: *Orchestrator + Conductor*.

**Algorithm Auditor** — Reads agent-generated code with deep skepticism; enforces strict boundaries, demands rigorous unit tests, traces edge cases manually. Architecture-of-work mapping: *Risk Steward*. The demand signal: as the StrongDM model (humans review, don't write) spreads, the auditor becomes a critical path role, not an optional governance function.

**AI FinOps Specialist** — Manages the financial side of AI infrastructure: ensuring every token, request, and model interaction creates more value than it costs. AIDB's August 2 episode framed this as *cost-per-accepted-task*, not cost-per-token. Architecture-of-work mapping: *Maintainer + Risk Steward*. Fastest-growing finance-adjacent AI job in 2026.

**Frontier Professional** (Microsoft WTI framing) — Not a job title but an archetype: advanced AI users who integrate AI into *how they think*, not just what they produce. The distinguishing behaviour is treating AI as a thinking partner for strategy, not just an execution tool. Architecture-of-work mapping: cuts across all archetypes — this is the baseline disposition the whole model assumes.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

The displacement data is genuinely mixed. Goldman Sachs (April 2026) counts 16,000 net U.S. jobs eliminated by AI per month — real, not hypothetical. Stanford/ADP data shows employment among 22–25-year-olds in AI-exposed occupations has fallen ~13% since ChatGPT's launch. Yale Budget Lab (February 2026) finds no macro-level evidence of large-scale disruption *yet* — unemployment duration for AI-exposed jobs is unchanged. The most honest read: displacement is real and uneven (young workers, high-exposure occupations), macro effects have not yet materialized, and the IBM rehiring story (32% already rehired from AI-displacement layoffs, 55% of leaders calling those decisions a mistake) suggests the first wave of reactionary displacement is already self-correcting.
