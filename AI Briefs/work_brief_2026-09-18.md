## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**AI FinOps Specialist** - One of the fastest-growing AI finance roles right now: managing AI compute costs as a dedicated function. Maps to **Maintainer** (keeping the machine economically viable) and **Risk Steward** (cost is a form of risk).

**Agent-Fleet Orchestrator** - Emerging in job postings and practitioner descriptions: managing autonomous AI agents across workflows, not writing the agents themselves. Pure **Conductor** role - the clearest real-world instantiation of the archetype.

**AI Evaluation Writer / "Human in the Loop" Validator** - Companies (especially post-Salesforce Qoa launch) now explicitly hiring for this: writing eval sets, validating model outputs before they reach customers. Maps to **Editor** archetype - quality control as a job.

**Agent Product Manager** - Distinct from traditional PM: translating business needs into agent requirements, designing the workflows agents execute. **Orchestrator** + **Scout** hybrid.

**AI Operations Engineer (AIOps)** - Monitoring and optimizing deployed AI systems. Closest to **Sweeper** (keeping pipelines clean) + **Maintainer** (keeping them running).

**"Judgement Model" Product Specialist** - Nascent, but the AIDB "judgement models" discussion implies a role: someone who decides *which* decisions get routed to a cheap probability model vs. escalated to a human. That's an **Editor** operating at the system-design level.

Most of these roles didn't exist as explicit titles 18 months ago. All of them map cleanly to the architecture-of-work archetypes - which suggests the framework is predictive, not just descriptive.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

**Altman and Amodei walking it back (May 2026):** Both CEOs who predicted 50% white-collar job elimination now say they were wrong - Altman admitted "I thought there would have been more impact on entry-level white-collar jobs being eliminated by now." The revision matters: it's the *architects of the technology* recalibrating, not skeptics dismissing it.

**Yale Budget Lab:** As of late 2025, no widespread measurable displacement in U.S. labor market data since ChatGPT's debut. The share of workers in different jobs hasn't changed significantly.

**The honest reconciliation:** ~188K AI-attributed layoff announcements since 2023 (Challenger tracking); tech layoffs through May 2026 already approaching full-year 2025 levels. The macro data looks stable; the micro experience for specific white-collar functions (entry-level coding, content, some legal/finance tasks) is genuinely harder. Skills in AI-exposed roles changing 66% faster is the real displacement - not mass unemployment, but accelerated obsolescence without active reskilling.

## 📌 Today's Signal

This week's community conversation isn't about whether AI will change work - it's about what *kind* of change and who's ahead of it. The "Opportunity AI" frame from NLW and the Microsoft Work Trend finding that AI expands human agency are converging on the same point from different angles: the people winning right now are those who've shifted from *doing* to *directing* - running agents, packaging judgment, evaluating outputs. The architecture-of-work archetypes (Orchestrator, Conductor, Editor, Risk Steward) aren't arriving as new departments; they're arriving as new *modes* inside existing roles. The actionable read for a reader today: pick one archetype you're not yet practicing in your current job and find the smallest possible experiment to start. That's what "become the maker for your function" looks like in week one.
