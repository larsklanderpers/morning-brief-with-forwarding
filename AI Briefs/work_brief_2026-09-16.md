**Ethan Mollick — "Agency and Agents" (One Useful Thing, recent)**
URL: https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/agency-and-agents
Mollick flips the standard framing: instead of asking when humans should ask AI for help, he asks when AI should ask humans. His "twilight factory" model — agents do most work, but proactively loop in humans at four specific decision points (spending money, contacting outsiders, accessing sensitive material, taking unauthorized actions) — is the clearest practitioner framework for human-agent collaboration yet published. Critically: legal, HR, and other non-tech functions are adopting agents at nearly the same rate as technical teams.
💡 **Constructive read:** The four handoff rules are the thing. Every function that wants to run agents needs a version of this governance layer — and whoever designs it for their team becomes the Risk Steward in the archetype model.

**Mollick — "Real AI Agents and Real Work"**
URL: https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/real-ai-agents-and-real-work

💡 **Constructive read:** Management intuition is suddenly a technical skill.

## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

The most concrete new-role signals from today's sweep:

**Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)**
Source: Multiple (IIT, Hashnode, Plank, KORE1) · URL: https://jobsbyculture.com/blog/forward-deployed-engineer-boom-2026
982 live postings across 462 companies. 800% spike in job listings since January. Total comp: $200K–$630K+. The FDE sits inside customer teams to build and ship AI systems in days. AWS has embedded thousands of them.
*Archetype map:* Builder + Orchestrator. The FDE is the first role that makes "become the maker for your function" a named job title.
💡 **Constructive read:** This role is the most direct path from "I have domain expertise" to "I deploy AI for people who need it." It didn't exist two years ago.

**AI Agent Orchestrator / Orchestration Specialist**
Source: Eightfold.ai, ServiceNow, Arion Research · URL: https://eightfold.ai/blog/most-important-job-2026/
Named "2026's job of the year" (Eightfold). Redesigns how work moves, aligns fragmented workflows across people and AI, and decides when to deploy autonomous agents vs. hand off to humans. Microsoft's 2026 Work Trend Index finds 82% of executives expect agents in their workforce within 18 months; only 23% feel confident about integration — the gap is the Orchestrator's job.
*Archetype map:* Orchestrator + Conductor. The new middle management layer — not a department, a capability every function needs.
💡 **Constructive read:** "Managing a team of agents" is not a metaphor. It's an org chart.

**AI FinOps Analyst / LLM FinOps Lead**
Source: buildmvpfast.com, FinOps.org · URL: https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/ai-finops-function-token-budget-org-chart-2026
Fastest-growing area inside Cloud FinOps. Owns the question: is the AI system making money, or just consuming resources? Titles include "AI Cost Engineer," "LLM FinOps Lead," and "ML Platform Cost Owner."
*Archetype map:* Risk Steward. The economic accountability role — every enterprise AI deployment eventually needs one.
💡 **Constructive read:** Token budgets are the new headcount budgets. The person who understands both finance and inference will be very well paid.

**Algorithm Auditor**
Source: Indeed (615 live postings), Singularity Experts · URL: https://singularity-experts.com/en/blog/algorithm-auditor-a-booming-job
Evaluates AI systems for fairness, accuracy, transparency, and regulatory compliance. 615 postings on Indeed as of August 2026.
*Archetype map:* Risk Steward + Editor. The independent check on what agents are actually doing.
💡 **Constructive read:** As AI decisions touch hiring, lending, and healthcare, the auditor is whoever the regulator calls first. That person should exist inside, not outside, the company.

**Vector Database Engineer**
Source: Mercor, Gloat · URL: https://www.mercor.com/resources/experts/new-artificial-intelligence-job-opportunities/
Demand outstripping supply 4:1. Salaries approaching $300K. Manages the knowledge stores that RAG systems draw from.
*Archetype map:* Builder + Maintainer. The infrastructure role that every knowledge-intensive AI deployment requires.

**"Frontier Professionals" (Microsoft's emerging archetype)**
Source: Microsoft 2026 Work Trend Index · URL: https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/agents-human-agency-and-the-opportunity-for-every-organization
Microsoft's label for the 16% of AI users who use agents for multi-step workflows, routinely redesign how they work, and share AI standards across their teams. 58% of this group produces work they couldn't have produced a year ago; organizational factors drive 2x more AI impact than individual effort.
*Archetype map:* Conductor. These are the people setting the tempo for everyone else.
💡 **Constructive read:** 16% is a beachhead, not a ceiling. The fastest-moving companies are structured so Frontier Professionals mentor everyone else — and that diffusion is itself a new function.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

The AI-jobs-apocalypse headline has largely collapsed under its own weight. In May 2026, both Sam Altman ("I'm delighted to be wrong about this") and Dario Amodei walked back their catastrophist predictions — Amodei's reframe being the most clarifying: "If you automate 90% of the job, then everyone does the 10% of the job... the 10% kind of expands to be 100% of what people do." Yale Budget Lab found no meaningful unemployment change through March 2026 for workers in high-AI-exposure jobs; the white-collar market is soft, not in freefall. WEF projects 170M jobs created and 92M displaced by 2030 — net positive, unevenly distributed. The honest read: displacement is real, transition is real, and it's moving slower and less uniformly than either camp predicted.

Sources: Fortune (May 2026), Yale Budget Lab, WEF Future of Jobs 2025.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The community is quietly making the same move Mollick made on X last week: it stopped asking whether AI reshapes work and started asking *how* to govern the reshape. Practitioners want operating agreements, handoff rules, and accountability structures — the governance layer that makes agent deployment safe enough to ship. That's the Orchestrator and Risk Steward archetypes coming alive in real orgs, not as new departments but as embedded capabilities claimed by whoever moves first. The thread direction: role-design as the new competitive advantage. Not "do you use AI" but "how do you decide when the agent asks for help?"
