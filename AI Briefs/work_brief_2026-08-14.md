## 🎙️ Podcasts & Newsletters

**The AI Daily Brief — "The New Jobs AI Will Create"** (May 2026, Spotify)
The foundational episode for this thread. NLW's argument: AI doesn't simply eliminate work, it expands the volume of useful work the economy can support — cheaper services, broader access, continuous availability, and personalized delivery all create new demand that didn't exist when the service was expensive. This is the Jevons Paradox applied to knowledge work.

**Ethan Mollick — "The Shape of the Thing"** (One Useful Thing, March 2026)
Key framing: we've entered "the era of managing AIs, rather than working with them" — a new phase that began in late 2025 with Claude Code, Codex, and OpenClaw. Mollick's StrongDM case study (Software Factory: code written, tested, and shipped without human authorship) shows radical organizational experimentation is already live. His close: "uncertainty is not the same as helplessness." The window to shape how AI gets used at work is open now, but won't last indefinitely.

**Ethan Mollick — "Real AI Agents and Real Work"** (One Useful Thing, September 2025)
The actionable model: delegate to AI first, correct if needed, do it yourself only if that fails. Expert estimate: 40% faster, 60% cheaper. The failure mode to avoid — the "17 PowerPoints" problem — using AI to do more of the same work rather than better work. Worth re-surfacing in a Diversions piece.

## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**AI FinOps Specialist** — fastest-growing AI finance role. One question: is the AI system making money, or consuming resources? Maps cleanly to the **Risk Steward** archetype. The title is new; the job is old (unit economics analyst), now applied to token spend.

**Algorithm Auditor** — procurement mandates now frequently require bias testing and auditing before deploying AI systems. Demand is coming from compliance departments, not AI teams. Maps to **Risk Steward** and **Editor** archetypes — reviewing AI output for structural fairness, not just factual accuracy.

**Forward-Deployed Engineer** — AI has added 1.3M+ new roles; Forward-Deployed Engineer is one of the fastest-growing. A hybrid of customer success and technical implementation: deployed into client environments to make AI systems actually work, not just theoretically work. Pure **Builder** archetype.

**Agent Orchestrator / AI Workforce Manager** — McKinsey's "agentic organization" framing names this explicitly: generalists who become supervisors of AI agent teams. Allie K. Miller is the clearest practitioner case study. The Greg Isenberg video is the best 29K-view explainer of what this looks like in practice.

**AI Change Manager** — surfacing in enterprise HR as a distinct role: not technical, but the person who manages the human side of AI adoption. Exists because 91% of data leaders cite "cultural challenges" as the primary obstacle to becoming data-driven. Maps to the **Evangelist** archetype operationalized inside an org.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

The Altman/Amodei apocalypse predictions have both been walked back as of May 2026. Altman: "I thought there would have been more impact on entry-level white-collar jobs being eliminated by now than has actually happened." Amodei (who previously warned of 50% white-collar elimination) has shifted to an augmentation frame. a16z called the AI jobs apocalypse narrative "unhelpful marketing, bad economics and worse history." Yale Budget Lab: no meaningful change in unemployment rates for AI-exposed workers since ChatGPT launched. The near-term reality is transition and compression, not mass elimination — enterprise customers are discovering AI tools sometimes cost more than the humans they were supposed to replace. The disruption is real and ongoing; the timeline is slower than the headlines.
