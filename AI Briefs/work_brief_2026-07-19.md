## 🎙️ Podcasts & Newsletters

### AI Daily Brief — "The Job Positions of the AI Future"
**Episode: Sunday, July 5, 2026** · Host: Nathaniel Whittemore (NLW)
https://aidailybrief.ai/e/2026-07-05

This is the anchor episode for this entire thread and the richest single piece of content this week. Key points:

1. **The meta-shift:** Everyone is now exploring "how much of what I do can be outsourced to agents, and what of the actual substance of my role remains?" NLW frames this as the underlying question of the current moment — not layoffs, but role archaeology.

2. **Boris Cherny's five product-facing archetypes** (source: creator of Claude Code):
   - **Prototyper** — eliminates the endless-discussion phase; takes ideas to tangible reality fast
   - **Builder** — turns prototypes into production-grade work; different mindset, often different person
   - **Sweeper** — optimises at a DNA level; not cleanup, but the "optimise everything" temperament
   - **Grower** — takes the built product outward to find PMF; the first role that faces people, not just artifact
   - **Maintainer** — sustains mature systems; entirely different disposition from builders; persists into agentic era

3. **NLW's six outward-facing additions:**
   - **Editor** — selects which cheap prototypes actually deserve to be built; taste + judgment; the new bottleneck when ideas are abundant
   - **Scout** — aggregates real-world signal and distils it back in; NLW notes this role may often be *played by an agent*
   - **Evangelist** — gets the market to adopt the builder's worldview; owns the internal/external intersection
   - **Orchestrator** — manages agent fleets and seams; a baseline skill everyone acquires *plus* a critical org layer for large deployments
   - **Conductor** — manages teams of agents so their outputs stay coherent; the specialist version of orchestration
   - **Risk Steward** — anticipates what could derail the project several steps ahead; an *accelerator*, not a bottleneck

4. **The cross-function mapping is the money:** In sales, the prototyper tests pitches, the builder makes them repeatable, the sweeper prunes bad segments, the grower owns deal velocity. In marketing, the scout reads the audience, the editor selects the best stories. Back office concentrates on Maintainers, Risk Stewards, Sweepers. The template applies everywhere.

5. **NLW's practical advice:** "Become the maker for your function." The back-office person who builds a small tool for a specific exception instead of filing a ticket — that's prototyping. It puts you at the centre of the change reshaping the job.

## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**AI Agent Orchestration Specialist**
Named by Eightfold.ai as the most important hire of 2026. Source: Microsoft Work Trend Index — 82% of executives expect AI agents in their workforce within 18 months, but only 23% feel confident about integrating them. This is the gap this role fills: systems choreography, governance architecture, cultural calibration. Maps to *Orchestrator + Conductor + Risk Steward* in the AIDB framework.
https://eightfold.ai/blog/most-important-job-2026/

**Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)**
Listings rose 800%+ in 2025; agentic AI job postings overall up 280% YoY (Stanford 2026 AI Index). The FDE sits at the intersection of frontier AI research and customer-facing deployment — embedding with enterprise clients to deploy, tune, and integrate models into production. Total comp at mid-senior level: $350K–$550K. Maps to *Builder + Sweeper + Risk Steward.*
https://hashnode.com/blog/a-complete-2026-guide-to-the-forward-deployed-engineer

**AI Orchestrator (Upwork's framing)**
Upwork's Future Workforce Index 2026 (July 14) introduces the "AI Orchestrator" as the emerging role that "connects AI tools to domain expertise, applies human judgment, and turns AI-enabled execution into business outcomes." Crucial finding: complex AI-augmented work is earning 45% more YoY, while simple AI execution (generative creative production) has declined 13% in per-contract earnings even as volume grew 90%. The lesson: orchestration + domain expertise = premium. Execution alone = commoditised.
https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/14/3326964/0/en/Upwork-s-Future-Workforce-Index-2026-How-AI-is-Redefining-the-Value-of-Work-as-Skilled-Freelancing-Accelerates.html

**The Maker-in-Function**
Not a new title — a new *posture* within existing titles. From the AIDB episode: the person in finance, HR, or ops who learns to build small tools for their own workflow instead of filing a ticket. Prototyping + domain knowledge. This is NLW's main practical frame for non-technical workers wanting to future-proof.

**AI SEO Specialist**
Emerging fast as AI search traffic surged 527% in one year and people increasingly bypass Google for AI-native answers. Someone who understands how AI systems surface and synthesise content — distinct from traditional SEO. Maps to *Scout + Grower.*

**LinkedIn's #1 fastest-growing job title in the US, 2026:** AI Engineer (+143% YoY). The technical counterpart to all the archetype work — demand for the people who actually build and run the systems everyone else will orchestrate.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

The displacement picture is real and uneven. Goldman Sachs estimates AI eliminates ~25,000 jobs/month while creating ~9,000 — a net -16,000/month. Workers aged 22-30 are experiencing displacement at nearly 3x the rate of workers 40-55; entry-level is the first casualty. Q1 2026 tech layoffs ran 52K–80K with ~50% attributed to AI.

The counter-evidence is also real: Ramp's firm-level study (June 30, 21,599 US firms tracked) found heavy AI adopters grew headcount 10% and entry-level headcount 12% following adoption. The WEF projects 170M jobs created vs. 92M displaced by 2030 — net gain. But the jobs being destroyed and created are not the same jobs, don't require the same skills, and aren't in the same geographies. The split isn't optimist vs. pessimist — it's between people acquiring the new archetypes and people waiting for the old roles to stabilise.

## 📌 Today's Signal

This week's community conversation has quietly moved past the displacement debate and into a more granular question: *which archetype are you, and which function do you serve it in?* The AIDB July 5 episode gives the most operationally useful framework on offer — eleven archetypes that map to temperament more than title, applicable from sales to back-office to finance. The Upwork data lands the economic proof: domain expertise + orchestration = 45% earnings premium; AI execution alone = commoditised. Mollick's taste-and-judgment thread closes the loop — as instructions get mass-generated and agents proliferate, the Editor is the scarcest resource. For someone trying to adapt rather than despair, the immediate move is clear: figure out which archetype fits your disposition, pick the highest-leverage function in your org that lacks it, and start making something — anything — in that role. The architecture of work is being drafted right now, and the people who show up as builders rather than spectators will write their own position description.
