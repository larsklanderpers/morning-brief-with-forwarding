## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**The agent supervisor: SK Telecom gives every in-house agent an employee ID and a named human boss**
Source: AI Chat Daily | [Link](https://www.aichatdaily.com/ai-business/sk-telecom-gives-ai-agents-employee-ids-human)
Single-source, not yet checked against SK Telecom's own text: AI Chat Daily reports the telco's first AI Governance Report (26 Oct) gives in-house agents employee IDs and a named human supervisor each, with high-autonomy agents answering to a team leader or executive. Agents are reviewed on task completion and can be kept, improved, suspended or retired, in a rollout to 2028. The ID scheme dates to June's AX Innovation 2.0.
💡 Constructive read: Every agent with a badge needs a manager, and that manager is a person. This is the Orchestrator and Risk Steward archetypes written into an HR process: the new job is not building the agent, it is owning its performance review, and the people who learn to run that review well will be the ones the agent workforce reports to.

## 📌 Today's Signal

A quiet Monday morning, but the two signals that did land fit together. SK Telecom is writing the agent workforce into its HR process, with IDs, supervisors and performance reviews that end in retirement for agents that do not earn their keep, and Goldman's numbers say three in four US workplaces have not started yet. Mollick's weekend advice is the bridge between them: push every task until you find where the tool stops, because that boundary is where the supervisory work lives. Through the architecture-of-work lens, the growth role this week is the person who can say what an agent is for, measure whether it delivered, and switch it off when it does not. The Monday action: pick one agent or automation your team already runs and write down who owns it and how you would know it was failing.
