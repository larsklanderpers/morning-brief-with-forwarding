## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**Agent Orchestrator** — the fastest-growing enterprise title of 2026. Translates business requirements into explicit agent contexts; rarely touches implementation; designs which microservices agents can access. Roughly 60% of new enterprise software projects now include an agentic component. **Archetype map: Conductor + Orchestrator.**

**AI FinOps Specialist** — answers the question the line manager never used to have to ask: is the AI system actually making money or just consuming resources? Governance/economics role that didn't exist 18 months ago. **Archetype map: Risk Steward.**

**Algorithm Auditor** — quality control for AI decisions: catches reasoning errors, prevents hallucinations from propagating, documents how systems operate. A business necessity, not an optional compliance role. **Archetype map: Risk Steward + Sweeper.**

**Forward-Deployed Engineer** — spans orchestration, behavior design, safety, multimodal delivery, and live platform deployment. The Builder who ships to real users in the field and owns what happens when it hits reality. **Archetype map: Builder + Orchestrator.**

🔑 **The headline signal:** Forbes, McKinsey, and LinkedIn jointly identified 20 new agentic job categories in mid-2026. The breakdown: non-technical roles will **outnumber** technical ones, and most require no code. The Conductor archetype is for everyone — not just engineers.

By the numbers: 1.3M new roles added as of Jan 2026. WEF projects 170M new jobs by 2030; 92M displaced; net +78M. Workers with AI skills command wage premiums up to 56%.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

Both Altman and Amodei softened apocalypse warnings in May 2026, ahead of IPOs. Altman: "I thought there would have been more impact on entry-level white-collar jobs by now than has actually happened." a16z labels the apocalypse narrative "unhelpful marketing, bad economics and worse history." Yale Budget Lab backs the skeptics: no meaningful change in unemployment for AI-exposed workers since ChatGPT launched in late 2022. The counterweight: tech layoffs through May 2026 hit 115K (Meta, Amazon, Snap all cite AI), already approaching 2025's full-year total of 124K — and AIDB is now naming "AI washing" as a distinct phenomenon, companies using AI-as-narrative as cover for financial restructuring. The macro numbers are intact; the narrative is being gamed. That distinction matters.

## 📌 Today's Signal

AIDB naming "AI washing" as an identifiable phenomenon is the tell: the community has matured past the binary and into specifics. Which companies are genuinely transforming? Which are laundering a layoff? Practitioners logging 5,000 hours of real automation, open models commoditizing the stack, non-technical orchestration roles proliferating faster than engineering ones — the architecture of work is decomposing exactly as the model predicted. The question is no longer whether the archetypes are real. It's whether your organization is building toward them, or using the language to do something cheaper and older. For readers trying to adapt: the tell is in the specifics. An org that can name *which* tasks are moving to agents, and *which* judgment calls stay human, is building. One that just says "we're using AI" is washing.
