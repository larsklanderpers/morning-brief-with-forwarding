## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**Forward-Deployed Engineer (FDE)** — Embedded inside a customer organization to build and tune agentic workflows. Job postings up 800%; salaries reaching $450K+. Andrew Ng called it one of "two hot new career paths" right now (the other: AI Engineer). In the architecture-of-work frame, this is the **Orchestrator** gone customer-facing — the person who doesn't just build agents but translates business context into agent instructions at the point of use.

**AI Workforce Manager** — Decides which tasks go to humans and which to AI; keeps agents within ethical and legal bounds; troubleshoots cross-system agent failures. A new management layer that didn't exist 18 months ago. Maps to **Conductor** in the work framework (coordinating the human-agent mix, not doing individual tasks).

**Agent Supervisor** — Positioned at critical handoff points rather than reviewing every action. Handles ambiguity, risk, escalation, and decisions where human context is load-bearing. Deloitte's September report highlights this as the highest-value human role in agent-heavy workflows. Closest to the **Risk Steward** archetype.

**AI FinOps Specialist** — Decides when to route to frontier models vs. cheaper ones; optimizes token spend; works with architects to eliminate recursive inefficiency. Procurement mandates are creating this role at enterprise scale. The **Maintainer** archetype applied to cost infrastructure.

**Algorithm Auditor / AI Governance Specialist** — Procurement now frequently requires auditing and bias testing before enterprise AI deployment. New public policy + domain expertise hybrid. The **Editor** archetype at system level rather than document level.

**Agent Behavior Coach** — Tunes AI agents to reflect brand voice, business values, and ethical guardrails. Emerging in marketing and customer operations. A **Grower** function: not building the agent, but calibrating how it performs over time.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

Both Altman and Amodei walked back peak "jobs apocalypse" rhetoric in May 2026. Altman: "I thought there would have been more impact on entry-level white-collar jobs than has actually happened." The Yale Budget Lab found no meaningful unemployment change through March 2026 even in high-AI-exposure jobs. Amodei hasn't fully backed down — told Axios in September: "the first step toward solving these problems is being honest with the population that these problems exist." The current honest read: displacement is happening unevenly and with a lag, not in the wave the 2025 headlines predicted. Real story is role *transformation*, not elimination — at least so far.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The week opens with personal AI agents officially crossing into consumer mainstream — Meta's Muse was the test, the App Store chart was the answer. What that means for work is that the "orchestration is a team skill" thesis just got compressed: people who aren't thinking about agent handoffs at home will encounter them at work sooner than expected. Meanwhile, Deloitte's September data quantifies the readiness gap precisely: 150,000 agents per Fortune 500 company by 2028 is the Gartner forecast, and only 5% of organizations say they're ready. That gap is not a warning sign — it's a map. The architecture-of-work frame is the right lens here: companies don't need to solve "agent strategy," they need someone in each function who can act as Conductor. Right now, that person is almost always self-appointed. The week's constructive read is that the role exists whether or not anyone has the title yet.
