## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**AI Agent Orchestration Specialist** — Multiple sources (Eightfold.ai, ServiceNow, Aquent) now calling this "the most important job of 2026." Not a technical role — it's cross-functional: maps workflows for human-agent collaboration, transfers expertise from humans to agents, manages trust and adoption, designs governance for agent behavior.
→ *Architecture-of-work lens:* This is the **Conductor** archetype at org scale. Healthy orgs will have one per function, not one per IT department.

**AI Work Architect + AI Steward** — WEF identifies these two as the emerging human-oversight roles: the Architect designs how AI should be used; the Steward monitors outputs and outcomes. Clean decomposition of what used to be called "AI governance."
→ *Architecture-of-work lens:* Architect = **Orchestrator**; Steward = **Risk Steward**.

**AI FinOps** — Starting to appear in job postings as a real role title (not just a practice). As token spend becomes a budget line, someone has to own the economics.
→ *Architecture-of-work lens:* **Maintainer** function applied to infrastructure cost, not product quality.

**Decision Engineer** — WEF / Harvard Business Review frame: designs the workflows where humans and AI work together, specifically around who decides what and when to escalate. Distinct from an AI architect — this is workflow, not system design.
→ *Architecture-of-work lens:* Overlaps **Orchestrator** and **Editor** depending on where the decision sits in the flow.

**The "Botsitter" problem** (AIDB Jun 26): Glean / Work AI Institute named "bot sitting" — 6.4 hours/week of hidden labor supervising agents — as the role eating AI's productivity dividend. The constructive read: whoever eliminates botsitting in their function becomes the most valuable person in it.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

**McKinsey (Sep 29):** 11 million Americans — ~7% of the workforce — may need to change occupations by 2035 because of AI. The annual rate of occupation-switching may triple. Low-income workers 8× more likely to be affected than high-income. The framing from McKinsey's own authors: the challenge is helping people transition, not an apocalypse. · [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-29/ai-may-force-11-million-workers-into-new-jobs-mckinsey-says) · [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/09/29/ai-jobs-roles-mckinsey)

**Amodei vs Altman (May 2026, still the reference point):** Amodei warned of a "white-collar bloodbath" (50% of entry-level office jobs); Altman said he was "pretty wrong" about AI's economic impact and walked back his earlier warnings. Neither is a reliable forecaster here — both have institutional interests in their predictions. · [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/26/sam-altman-dario-amodei-walking-back-ai-jobs-apocalypse-prophecies-ipo/)

**a16z:** "Don't believe the hype" — the lump-of-labor fallacy underlies most job-loss predictions. Three independent labor datasets show no significant unemployment rise for AI-exposed workers through late 2025.

**Data center point:** Workers with AI skills command 62% higher wages; up to 118% in some sectors. The wage premium story is the quiet counter-narrative to the displacement story.
