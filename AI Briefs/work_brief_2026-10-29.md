## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**Pipeline-in-a-Person: one scientist running several drug programs at once**
Source: AI Chat Daily | [Link](https://www.aichatdaily.com/ai-business/anthropic-buys-one-person-biotech-mithril-medicine-starts-own)
The deal ran in yesterday's AI brief as a business story; the work angle is the operating model. Per Anthropic's account as reported, Mithril founder Cody Tranbarger used Claude to coordinate contracts, logistics and experiments across 11 external labs, with a few part-time consultants, and took one candidate from idea to promising mouse results in under 18 months. He calls the model "Pipeline-in-a-Person" and says one reason he joined Anthropic is to learn how many programs one person can run at once. The Column Group is reportedly setting up Pleiades to run a growing number of drug programs with a small team on Claude. All preclinical; nothing tested in humans.
💡 Constructive read: The general contractor never laid a brick; he knew which crew to call and in what order. Through the architecture-of-work lens this is the Conductor archetype made into a whole company, and the scarce skill is not the chemistry the labs already have but the judgment to sequence eleven of them.

## 📌 Today's Signal

Yesterday's lesson was that experts quietly hand their judgment calls to the agent; today's community answer is to build the system so it cannot happen quietly. Herk's Roast Room makes the AI argue with itself before he decides, Medin's checklist starts with what the agent knows rather than what it can do, and Mithril shows the end state: one scientist conducting eleven labs, where the job is the sequencing and the calls, not the pipetting. Mollick's point lands in the same place from the other end: when the cost of a first attempt collapses, the human value moves to choosing which bets are worth the expensive test. Through the architecture-of-work lens, the Conductor is no longer a senior title; it is the default shape of a solo practitioner with good tools. The Thursday action: take one recurring decision you make, and add a step where an agent has to argue the opposite case before you sign.
