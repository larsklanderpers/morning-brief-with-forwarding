## 🔬 Frontier Labs

**OpenAI cuts its round from $30B to $20B and its valuation from $1.4T to $1.25T, and the first close may come in lower still**
Source: AI Chat Daily, citing the Financial Times | [Link](https://www.aichatdaily.com/ai-business/openai-cuts-funding-round-target-10b-20b)
Investors had until 30 October to commit, and some reduced their allocations, according to people familiar with the process. OpenAI had wanted the round closed before Anthropic's expected mid-November listing, because the two labs draw on a similar pool of investors. OpenAI did not respond to a request for comment, and final terms are still being worked out.
💡 Strategic take: A third off the raise in the final days of the book is what a market saying "show me" sounds like. Nothing changes in your tenancy on Monday, but a supplier that needs capital behaves differently from one that has it: pricing moves, terms tighten, and data becomes an asset to be monetised. This is the week to read the change-of-control and insolvency clauses in your AI contracts, while the counterparty is still flush enough to negotiate.

Inference prices have been a subsidy as much as a market price. Build your 2027 AI budget on a scenario where they rise, because the cheap tier is the first thing a stretched supplier reprices.

## 🔒 Privacy, PII & Data Sovereignty

**⚠️ An AI law firm bids for Weil Gotshal's brand and "unspecified internal data" as the 95-year-old firm prepares to dissolve**
Source: AI Chat Daily | [Link](https://www.aichatdaily.com/ai-business/ai-law-firm-crosby-bids-weil-gotshal-brand)
Crosby, valued at a reported $400M, has bid for the trademark, brand and internal data and wants to hire some of Weil's roughly 1,200 lawyers; terms are undisclosed and the bid may not succeed. Per The AI Daily Brief, rival Gen Legal looked at the deal and passed, with CEO Ryan Walker arguing the data belongs to the clients; he is a competitor, so note the interest. Sources differ on the process: The AI Daily Brief says Weil filed for Chapter 11, while AI Chat Daily reports a coming dissolution vote and a possible Chapter 7, so treat the legal route as unsettled.
💡 Strategic take: A law firm's archive used to be worth nothing once the partners left. It is now training data for agents, and that changes what happens to a client's files when an adviser fails. Ask every professional adviser who holds your sensitive material what their engagement letter says about insolvency. Then ask the same of every AI vendor, because the file you thought was confidential may be an asset on someone's sale schedule.

**⚠️ UK CMA opens a Competition Act case into Meta's terms that would let it use publisher website content in AI features, after a Norwegian court temporarily blocked them**
Source: AI Chat Daily | [Link](https://www.aichatdaily.com/ai-security/uk-cma-investigates-meta-over-terms-letting-use)
The CMA will test whether Meta holds a dominant UK position and whether the terms are an abuse; Meta had planned the change for 20 November, has put it on hold, and says there are no grounds for an investigation. The probe covers advertisers as well as publishers. The CMA's own case page was not found in this run, so this rests on reporting that quotes chief executive Sarah Cardell.
💡 Strategic take: If you run ads on Meta, the terms you click through may reach content on your own website. Competition law and a court challenge, not privacy law, are what paused this one, which is worth noting for any Australian firm waiting on the Privacy Act to do that job. Read platform terms at each renewal with one question in mind: what did this just give them that I did not mean to give?

**OpenAI, Google, Microsoft and Anthropic back one robots.txt token to opt out of AI training, and it does not touch what they already hold**
Source: AI Chat Daily | [Link](https://www.aichatdaily.com/ai-security/openai-google-microsoft-anthropic-back-one-robots-txt)
The token, __usage_ai-training, comes from an individual Internet-Draft filed on 30 October by Anthropic's Kevin Kelley, which has no formal IETF standing and expires on 3 May 2027; the primary draft was not found in this run. Anthropic has started, OpenAI says coming months, Google says 2027, and Microsoft's preferences take effect on 1 February 2027. It is a preference, not an access control, and providers that have not implemented it can ignore it. Critic Nate Hake says disputes remain, including an exemption for user-initiated actions.
💡 Strategic take: A "please don't" sign is progress, and it is written by the people it asks. Add the token to your public sites now, because it costs nothing, but keep the provider-specific rules and do not list it as a control. Anything already collected stays collected, which makes this a door closed after the furniture has left.

## 📌 Today's Signal
Data outlives the company that holds it, and this week's markets turned that from a thought experiment into a planning question. A 95-year-old law firm is winding down and an AI firm wants its internal data. Character.ai's transcripts sit under a privacy policy that allows disclosure in a sale, due within weeks. OpenAI cut its raise by a third, Alphabet lost $500B in a day, and Meta paused terms reaching further into businesses' own content only after a court and a regulator stepped in. Meanwhile the labs offered a polite opt-out that does nothing for what they already hold. For a business with PII obligations, the usual AI questions (is it accurate, is it secure, where is it hosted) all assume a supplier that stays solvent and keeps its promises. Add a fourth question: when this company is sold, refinanced or wound up, what happens to what we gave it? The firms that can answer will have written it into the contract while they still had leverage, and most of them will have sent less data in the first place.
