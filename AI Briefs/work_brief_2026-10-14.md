**"Anthropic's Engineers Stopped Building AI Agents. Here's What They Build Instead." - Sharbel A. (2 days, about 108K views)**
Source: YouTube | [Link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VxGIopUQVKo)
The argument is that instead of a separate agent for every job, you give one agent a growing library of skills, each a SKILL.md file that teaches it a repeatable job. He shows three ways to write one: by hand, by having Claude interview you, or by recording yourself doing the process so the agent learns it from your screen, and he flags a security check before installing anyone else's skill. Per the video description; the headline claim about Anthropic's engineers is the creator's framing, and the video sells his own starter pack.
💡 Constructive read: Every good team already has a binder of "how we do it here" that nobody reads; a skill file is that binder, except the reader actually follows it. The most valuable person in the room becomes the one who can write down how the work is really done, which is a job almost anyone in a function can start tomorrow.

**"I Tested the Viral Reverse Engineer Anything Skill. What You Need to Know." - Nate Herk (18 hours, about 75K views)**
Source: YouTube | [Link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rL5tw6h78zo)

His takeaway is that giving an agent clear examples of the output you want is what makes it work. Per the video description; the description promotes his paid community.
💡 Constructive read: An apprentice learns faster from a finished chair than from a lecture on joinery. The transferable skill here is not reverse engineering, it is knowing what good looks like well enough to hand the agent an example, which is taste turned into a specification.

## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**The day-one agent manager**
Source: Bloomberg (8 Oct 2026) | [Link](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-10-08/young-bankers-to-manage-ai-agents-from-start-goldman-exec-says)
Goldman Sachs Asia-Pacific chief Kevin Sneader told the Milken Institute Asia Summit in Singapore that "When our young people start now, they manage agents," per Bloomberg as quoted by Notebookcheck. Secondary coverage adds that he said the management task now sits with the front line, and that Goldman does not yet know what happens to existing middle managers. One outlet describes a formal Goldman "report" on middle management; no such document turned up, so treat this as conference remarks, not research. NDTV Profit's video on it reached about 113K views.
💡 Constructive read: The graduate who used to spend two years building the spreadsheet now starts by deciding which of five agents built it right. That is the Orchestrator archetype arriving at the entry level, and the honest open question is not the junior's job but how the junior learns judgment without the years of grind that used to teach it.

**The process writer: turning how you work into a skill file**
Source: YouTube, Sharbel A. (12 Oct 2026) | [Link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VxGIopUQVKo)
Today's two most-watched practitioner videos both centre on skills: packaging a repeatable job, or an example of good output, so an agent can do it. Neither is a job title, and no employer is hiring for it by name.
💡 Constructive read: This is the Maintainer and the Editor fused: someone who knows the work well enough to write it down and keeps the instructions current as the work changes. It rewards domain knowledge over coding, which puts it within reach of most people already doing the job.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The entry rung is being redrawn in public. A Goldman executive says new bankers manage agents from their first day, the IMF and Singapore's president both put the squeeze on the graduate job, and the practitioner channels are teaching people to turn their own know-how into skill files an agent can follow. Read together, the starting job is moving from doing the task to directing and checking it, and the skill that gets you in the door is being able to say clearly what good work looks like. The practical move this week: pick one task you do every week, write down how you actually do it well, and hand that to an agent. If you can make that file good, you have the beginnings of the job Goldman just described.
