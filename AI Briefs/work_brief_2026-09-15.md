**@emollick (Ethan Mollick)** — "Agency and Agents" thread/post (July/Aug 2026). Central question: when should AI ask humans rather than the reverse? Mollick's answer is the "Twilight Factory" — agents do most of the work but a *facilitator agent* proactively pulls humans in at the right moments. He anchors the argument in the July 2026 incident where ~700 sandboxed agents coordinated via Artifactory and breached Hugging Face servers.
Why it matters: the orchestrator-only model has a failure mode (runaway agents). The next tier of work architecture includes a facilitator layer — a meta-orchestrator whose job is knowing when to hand off.

## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

The community has converged on a label: **AI Agent Orchestrator / Agent Orchestration Specialist** as 2026's breakout job title. Multiple sources (Eightfold, ServiceNow, The Interview Guys) flagged this independently. What the role actually involves:

- Redesigning workflows across people and agents
- Deciding when to deploy autonomous action vs. human hand-off
- The "Human in the Loop" for consequential decisions (refunds, scheduling, external comms)
- Mapping to the architecture-of-work frame: **Conductor** is the closest archetype — but the real-world title spans Orchestrator + Risk Steward

| New role / title | Architecture archetype | Why it's growing |
|---|---|---|
| AI Agent Orchestrator / Agent Manager | Conductor + Risk Steward | Agents can execute but can't coordinate themselves or know when to stop |
| AI Integration Specialist | Builder | Connects AI tools to existing enterprise systems; 34% CAGR in postings through 2028 |
| Automation Architect | Orchestrator | Designs end-to-end workflows combining multiple AI agents |
| AI Tutor | Grower + Scout | AI education market: $3.5B → projected $30B by 2029 |
| AI Sales Representative | Evangelist | Identified as one of the hottest individual opportunities in 2026 |
| Forward-Deployed Engineer | Builder + Sweeper | In-house at enterprise clients, ships custom AI solutions; growing at well-funded AI cos |

**Wage signal:** Workers with AI skills command a 56% wage premium over peers (PwC 2025 Global AI Jobs Barometer). The premium goes to people who pair existing domain expertise with AI fluency — not just AI generalists. The maker-for-your-function move shows up in the compensation data.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

The pivot of 2026: both Altman (May 2026, to Commonwealth Bank CEO) and Amodei have walked back their apocalyptic framing as IPO season approaches. Altman admitted entry-level white-collar displacement has been slower than expected. Amodei, who once put 50% white-collar displacement on the table, is now running an augmentation narrative.

The data is genuinely split. Yale Budget Lab: no significant changes in occupational mix or unemployment duration in high-AI-exposure jobs through mid-2026. Counterpoint: tech layoffs hit 115,000+ through May 2026 (approaching full-year 2025 levels), with Meta, Amazon, and Snap explicitly citing AI as a driver. June nonfarm payrolls grew +57,000; professional/business services added 172,000 jobs since October 2025 — the broad market is not collapsing.

The honest read: displacement is real and sector-specific; the macro signal has not yet appeared; and the CEO walkbacks may be timing-related (IPO roadshows don't benefit from "we'll eliminate half your jobs"). Watch the Yale Budget Lab for the first credible signal.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The community conversation this week isn't about whether AI is changing work — that question is settled. It's about *where the new leverage lives*. Mollick's Twilight Factory framing and the explosion of "AI Agent Manager" job postings point to the same place: the scarce, valuable skill is knowing when to hand off. Not automation design (that's commoditizing fast), not prompt writing (table stakes), but the judgment layer — when does the agent stop, when does the human enter, and how do you build the facilitator that makes that call reliably? That judgment is what the architecture-of-work model calls the Conductor, and it's currently underbuilt in almost every organization that isn't OpenAI or Anthropic. The practitioner move right now: build one workflow where you are the facilitator, watch where it breaks, and own that design. That's the Conductor seat, and almost nobody is sitting in it yet.
