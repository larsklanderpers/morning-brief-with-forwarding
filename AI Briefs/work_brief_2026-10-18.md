## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

The AI brief covered the headline numbers from Anthropic's 16 Oct employment report this morning (6x agent hours, junior technical hires 27% to 9%). This section takes the roles angle. Anthropic sells the agents it is measuring, and the data is its own; there is no outside audit.

**The manager of Claudes: meta-work doubles**
Source: Anthropic, "How employment is changing at Anthropic" (16 Oct 2026) | [Link](https://www.anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic)
Meta-work (writing specs for multi-agent runs, having one agent review another, building dashboards and monitors for agent pipelines) rose from 10% of the average employee's interactive Claude sessions in fall 2025 to 22% in spring 2026, and standing instructions for agents grew from a few hundred to 8,500 in a year. In an April survey, 20% of employees described their role shifting from doing the work to managing Claudes, and the report says even non-technical staff would need over half their week just to read their agents' output.
💡 Constructive read: This is the Orchestrator archetype stepping out of the slide deck and into an HR dataset. The bottleneck is a person's reading time, so the job that grows is the one that decides what does not need reading: write the standing instruction once, build the monitor, and spend your attention where judgment is actually needed.

**Writers become prompt and eval writers; assistants become chiefs of staff**
Source: Anthropic, "How employment is changing at Anthropic" (16 Oct 2026) | [Link](https://www.anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic)
A product content team that wrote Claude.ai's greetings built a context graph that let agents write the copy, then rotated into agent prompting and writing model evals; its manager says writers who used language to drive human behaviour now use it to drive Claude's. An executive assistant automated scheduling and triage and moved to "chief of staff" work, a lawyer now writes frameworks that clients' agents can discover, and staff with a year's tenure were 50% more likely to move departments each month in 2026 than in 2025.
💡 Constructive read: The skill did not die, it changed audience: a writer who can move a reader can move a model, and a good assistant was always half a chief of staff. That is the Editor and the Conductor showing up in content and admin roles, and the 50% jump in internal moves says redeployment, not exit, was the common path here.

**Hiring for judgment over implementation**
Source: Anthropic, "How employment is changing at Anthropic" (16 Oct 2026) | [Link](https://www.anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic)
Coding of thousands of interview scorecards shows mentions of candidates' creative AI use roughly tripled for technical roles and quadrupled for non-technical roles between 2024 and 2026. For technical candidates the biggest gains were in comments on autonomy and judgment, while domain expertise shrank as a share of assessment; one manager wants people who are "fungible across domains" because models stay jagged.
💡 Constructive read: The interview is moving from "can you do the task" to "can you decide which task matters and own the result", which is the Risk Steward and Conductor brief written into a scorecard. Anyone preparing for a role can act on that today: bring one story about something you built with agents and one about a call you made that the agents could not.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

**Anthropic's own data on juniors, and its own caveat**
Source: Anthropic, "How employment is changing at Anthropic" (16 Oct 2026) | [Link](https://www.anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic)
The junior share of technical employees fell from 21% to 14% since early 2025, and the report says reading the pattern across the economy "could risk worker displacement". Its authors add that none of the company's measures (shared stock ownership, an early warning system for at-risk roles, internal redeployment) address the slowdown in junior hiring, and call for retraining and income-support policy alongside employer action.
💡 Constructive read: A firm publishing the soft spot in its own data is rarer than the data itself. The honest read for anyone early in a career is that the first rung is narrowing at exactly the AI-heaviest employers, so the way in is proof of judgment and a portfolio of agent work, not a quieter version of the old entry-level job.

## 📌 Today's Signal

A quiet Sunday feed, with one dense document at its centre. Anthropic's report is the first employer dataset to show the orchestration shift as numbers rather than anecdotes: meta-work doubled, standing instructions went from hundreds to thousands, writers turned into eval authors, assistants into chiefs of staff, and hiring managers started scoring judgment over execution. CampusX's roadmap says the same thing from the training side, and Mollick's services question says it from the citizen's side: when everyone has an agent, the scarce thing is the person who designs how the work, or the queue, should flow. The constructive move this week is small and concrete: list where you spend time reading agent output, write one standing instruction that removes a chunk of it, and keep a running note of the calls you made that the agents could not. That note is the evidence the next interview is now looking for, and it is the Conductor's job description in your own handwriting.
