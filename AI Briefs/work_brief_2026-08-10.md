## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**AI Orchestrator / Agent Boss** — The term is standardizing. McKinsey, Microsoft, LinkedIn, and Box all using it. Definition coalescing around: someone who designs multi-agent workflows, selects which agents handle which tasks, sets success criteria, and reviews outputs. *Architecture-of-work lens: this is the Conductor + Orchestrator archetypes, now a named job market category.*

**Forward-Deployed Engineer (FDE)** — Growing fast in the enterprise AI space. Works at the boundary between an AI company's platform and a customer's operational environment — part implementation engineer, part domain expert, part translator. ~1.3M new AI-related roles created since 2024; FDE is among the fastest-growing title strings on LinkedIn. *Architecture lens: Scout + Builder, externally facing.*

**AI Work Architect / AI Steward** — Two emerging roles flagged by the WEF that may become dedicated positions or embedded responsibilities. The Work Architect designs how humans and agents divide labor within a team or function; the Steward handles ongoing governance, error-review, and course-correction. *Architecture lens: Conductor (Work Architect) and Risk Steward (AI Steward) — the two roles that ensure agents are doing the right work, not just work.*

**Cost-per-Accepted-Task Analyst (no standard title yet)** — Emerging inside enterprise AI ops teams: someone who measures the business value of agent outputs rather than the cost of tokens consumed. Framed by AIDB's token episode as the critical missing role in most early AI deployments. *Architecture lens: Risk Steward meets Maintainer — the person who keeps the economics honest.*

**Forbes / McKinsey / Box / LinkedIn (Jun 2026):** Collectively flagging 20 new agentic AI job categories, including AI FinOps, Algorithm Auditor, Agent Trainer, and Human-AI Collaboration Lead. [Forbes piece](https://www.forbes.com/sites/sandycarter/2026/06/02/the-20-new-agentic-ai-jobs-box-mckinsey-and-linkedin-all-see-coming/).

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

The alarm-vs-reassurance cycle is settling into data. **Yale Budget Lab** (March 2026): no meaningful change in unemployment rates for AI-exposed workers since ChatGPT launched in Nov 2022 — occupational mix and unemployment duration are statistically flat. **Goldman Sachs** (May 2026) revised net job losses down 30% from earlier estimates. **Altman and Amodei** both walked back apocalypse predictions — Altman: "pretty wrong on the social and economic implications." **a16z** characterizing the jobs-apocalypse narrative as "unhelpful marketing, bad economics and worse history." Counter-signal: entry-level roles in AI-exposed occupations are being rewritten to require judgment skills previously reserved for experienced workers — compressing the on-ramp for early-career people. That's a real structural shift even if headline unemployment isn't moving. WEF projects net +12M jobs from AI adoption through 2030; 170M new roles vs. 92M displaced.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The community conversation has crossed a threshold this week: the question is no longer "will this happen?" — it's "what's the fastest path to being the person who shapes how it happens in my function?" The archetype vocabulary (Orchestrator, Conductor, Risk Steward, Builder) is giving practitioners a map that job titles never could, because it describes *what you do* rather than *what you're called*. The displacement debate is losing oxygen not because the concern was wrong but because the practitioners who absorbed it fastest have already moved on to building the next thing. The architecture-of-work frame is the answer to the anxiety: identify which archetypes you're already performing well, figure out which one is next, and become the maker for that function before the function comes looking for one.
