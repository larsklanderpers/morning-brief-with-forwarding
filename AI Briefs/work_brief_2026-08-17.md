## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)** — 729% demand growth in 12 months per Indeed data; job postings in Jan 2026 were 19× prior year. Role: builds production-grade solutions at the client boundary, after the deal is signed. 2026 skill bar has shifted to agentic orchestration (LangGraph, CrewAI), evaluation frameworks, and AI observability on top of RAG and fine-tuning. Salary: $47K–$113K.
→ Architecture lens: **Builder + Orchestrator** — this is the Prototyper who stayed in the field.

**AI Agent Orchestration Specialist** — Eightfold calls it "the most important job of 2026." Typically comes from business analysis, process improvement, or TPM backgrounds — not engineering. Coordinates multi-agent systems across the org.
→ Architecture lens: **Conductor** — this is exactly the role the framework named.

**AI FinOps & Consumption Governance** — Autodesk and others are hiring "Lead, AI FinOps & Consumption Governance" roles now. Owns the financial discipline behind AI spending — GPU budgets, inference cost allocation, consumption accountability.
→ Architecture lens: **Risk Steward** — the person who makes sure the factory doesn't burn money while running.

**AI Auditor / Algorithmic Auditor** — Average US salary $72K as of June 2026. Evaluates AI models for bias, ethical risk, security, and regulatory compliance. Also appearing as: AI Governance Auditor, AI Ethics Auditor, AI Assurance Specialist.
→ Architecture lens: **Risk Steward** — the governance half, distinct from the financial half above.

**Forbes / Box / McKinsey / LinkedIn consensus (Jun 2026):** 20 new agentic AI jobs confirmed as real and actively hiring across major employers. The list includes AI Coach, AI Strategist, AI Compliance Manager — all governance and translation roles that require understanding both the capability and the organization.
→ Architecture lens: **Scout + Evangelist** for the translation roles; **Risk Steward** for compliance.

**Allie K. Miller's "AI chief of staff + 6 directors" model** — The most concrete practitioner demonstration this week of what the Conductor archetype looks like in practice: one human Conductor, a named AI chief of staff, function-specific AI directors. The org chart is real; the headcount is artificial.
→ This is the "everyone becomes a manager of a team of agents" thesis, fully embodied.

**By the numbers (WEF Future of Jobs Report 2025):** 170M new jobs created vs. 92M displaced by 2030 — net +78M. Caveat that belongs in every citation of this stat: those numbers describe different people in different places. The workers losing roles are not automatically the ones filling the new ones. The transition cost is real even if the net is positive.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

The position of the major figures has settled into a triangle: Altman (May 2026, Sydney) said he was "pretty wrong" about the apocalypse, now pegging AI at "30-40% of tasks" eventually — a significant retreat. Amodei is still warning that up to half of entry-level white-collar jobs could dissolve within five years and unemployment could reach 10-20%. a16z's counter is the sharpest: "unhelpful marketing, bad economics and worse history," citing Yale Budget Lab data showing no meaningful unemployment change for AI-exposed workers since ChatGPT launched. The on-the-ground ambiguity: tech layoffs through May 2026 hit 115,000, already approaching all of 2025's 124,000, with Meta, Amazon, and Snap citing AI as a driver. Task-level automation is real even where job-level displacement isn't — yet.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The Allie K. Miller conversation is the signal of the week: a practitioner who built a 34-agent org chart, named her agents, and is now publishing the operating manual. What's new isn't the technology — it's the organizational imagination. The community is moving from "AI can do tasks" to "AI can hold a role," and the people getting ahead are the ones who are designing the role first and letting the AI fill it, rather than delegating tasks and hoping for coherence. The architecture-of-work model has a real-world proof point now: the Conductor isn't a future job title, it's what Allie K. Miller is doing on a Monday morning.
