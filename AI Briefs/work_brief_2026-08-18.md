## 🐦 The Conversation on X

**@emollick (Ethan Mollick)** — "On Working with Wizards" · [x.com/emollick](https://x.com/emollick/status/1966244598726152261)
Key line: *"the way that we work with AI is shifting from being collaborators who shape the process to being supplicants who receive the output."* Maps the de-skilling risk: each time you hand work to an AI wizard without staying in the loop, you lose a chance to develop the judgment needed to evaluate the output. Partial counter-tactic: use multiple AI systems to critique each other's work (GPT-4 catching Claude's errors and vice versa).
💡 **Why it matters:** Mollick is naming the trap precisely. The answer isn't to use less AI — it's to stay in the decisions that shape the output, not just receive it.

**@emollick** — on anti-slop agent instructions · [x.com/emollick](https://x.com/emollick/status/2077226285919531191)
*"Reading one of the viral 'anti-slop' markdown files for agents and realizing it is basically 100% AI written and will basically hyperstition your outputs into mega-slop. You need to either have a sense of taste or hire someone with one."*
💡 **Why it matters:** Taste is the new scarce skill. Not prompt engineering, not model selection — the ability to tell the difference between good output and convincing slop. That's what stays irreplaceable.

## 🎙️ Podcasts & Newsletters

**The AI Daily Brief — Aug 16: "The New Problems AI Is Creating (And How People Are Solving Them)"**
*NLW · ~30 min · [aidailybrief.ai/e/2026-08-16](https://aidailybrief.ai/e/2026-08-16)*

Three points most load-bearing for this thread:

1. **De-skilling is the undertracked risk.** BCG paper "The Tragedy of the Cognitive Commons": checking AI output requires deep expertise; deep expertise comes from grunt work; AI eats grunt work first. Each company eliminating junior roles acts rationally; collectively, professions lose the capacity to catch AI's mistakes. NLW's counter-question: *is grunt work a law of nature, or just how expertise has always happened — and are there other ways to build it?*

2. **KPMG: companies are 2× more likely to invest in new tech than in their people.** The payoff data for doing both: 37% of leaders who increased workforce investment reported 20%+ revenue growth over three years, vs. 25% overall. Most companies are betting the wrong way.

3. **The CEO question has quietly shifted.** From "which model?" to "own the harness" (BCG Global Chair Rich Lesser's framing) — the enterprise cortex of IP, data, business rules, and codified process knowledge that can plug any model in. The organizations building this layer are building something defensible; the ones chasing the best model on the current benchmark are not.

## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**Forward Deployed Engineer** → Scout + Builder + Orchestrator archetypes combined in one role
729% YoY growth in postings. 5,330 openings on Indeed by April 2026. $170K–$200K+, with an additional 15–25% premium over equivalent ML engineering roles. Forbes Aug 11: banks specifically hunting FDEs to solve the "AI Budget Hangover" — the gap between what the models can do and what the workflows actually deliver in production. The FDE is the person who bridges that gap end-to-end.

**Finance-as-Builder** → Prototyper archetype spreading function by function
OpenAI research this week: 40% of finance professionals' AI use now falls outside traditional finance work; 22% involves engineering-adjacent tasks. OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar's team is building custom dashboards and live tools with ChatGPT and Codex. The Excel → live tool transition is happening function by function, not in one big transformation. This is exactly what "become the maker for your function" looks like in the wild.

**Agent Orchestration Specialist** → Conductor/Orchestrator archetypes, most acute demand
Named "the most important job of 2026" by Eightfold.ai. Supply of practitioners with real orchestration experience remains vanishingly small relative to demand. The premium is here — not in prompt writing or model selection, but in knowing how to design, direct, and quality-control a team of agents.

**The "Frontier Professional"** → Microsoft's name for the advanced-use archetype
From the 2026 Work Trend Index: top-decile AI users who report producing work impossible a year ago (80% say so vs. 58% overall). The distinguishing move: they define clear *intent* — desired outcome and quality bar — rather than just prompting for output. Human agency expands as AI handles execution; the constraint is whether the organization is built to support what these people can now do.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

EY (via AIDB Aug 16) made the historical calibration case: productivity revolutions don't appear on quarterly dashboards. Steam engine took a century, electricity 50 years, computers a decade. The gains are real; the timeline for broad diffusion is measured in years, not quarters.

The "AI caused the layoffs" framing is collapsing. NLW: "A complete and utter crock" — pushed by lab leaders (some now walking it back) and executives who needed a market-acceptable reason. The counter-signal: companies re-hiring people laid off for AI reasons; every such story puts a dagger further into the excuse.

The de-skilling/expertise-pipeline question remains the live, unresolved debate. Nobody has a clean answer to "who checks AI's homework in 15 years?" Worth watching whether new models for expertise development emerge before the junior-hire pipeline runs dry.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The dominant community conversation this week isn't about whether AI reshapes work — that debate is over. It's about the second-order problems: how to manage token costs as a capital allocation question, how to preserve the expertise pipeline when AI eats the grunt work, and how to keep human judgment in the loop when AI output is increasingly opaque. The fact that NLW can run a 30-minute episode on *how companies are already solving these problems in public* is itself the constructive signal. We're in the "how" era now. The architecture-of-work frame makes the constructive read concrete: if every function is producing Frontier Professionals who build their own tools and orchestrate their own agents, the question stops being "which jobs survive AI" and becomes "which *people* make the move from executing tasks to designing how tasks get executed." That move is available to anyone, in any function — and the playbooks are now being written in public.
