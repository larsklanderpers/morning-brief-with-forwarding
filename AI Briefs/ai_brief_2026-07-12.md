## 🔒 Privacy, PII & Data Sovereignty

**Countdown: Australian ADM transparency obligations hit 10 December 2026** — Landers & Rogers / LeadComply — https://www.landers.com.au/legal-insights-news/australian-privacy-law-update-what-app-entities-need-to-know-in-2026
New APP 1.7–1.9 obligations require privacy policies to disclose what personal information feeds substantially automated decisions and which decisions are made significantly by computer programs. OAIC formal guidance expected by September; consultation closed 15 June.
💡 Strategic take: Five months out, and most Australian businesses using AI in customer-facing decisions haven't mapped which decisions are "substantially automated." The mapping exercise is the hard part — the policy update is trivial once you know. Start with the inventory now, not in November.

**EU AI Act becomes fully applicable August 2 — three weeks away** — Alation / GDPR Register — https://www.alation.com/blog/eu-ai-act-compliance-guide/
Full applicability lands August 2, 2026, though the Digital Omnibus may postpone Annex III high-risk obligations to December 2027. GDPR enforcement hit ~€2.3B in 2025 alone, up 38% YoY; AI Act penalties top out at €35M or 7% of global turnover.
💡 Strategic take: The Omnibus delay talk is lulling companies into treating August 2 as soft. It isn't — prohibited practices, GPAI transparency, and literacy obligations bite regardless of what happens to Annex III. And the dual DPIA+FRIA burden makes on-premise architectures genuinely cheaper to assess.

**APRA CPS 230 now live (July 1): AI vendors are material service providers** — Lorikeet / Brightlume — https://brightlume.ai/blog/data-residency-ai-workloads-australian-compliance-sovereign-deployment
CPS 230 classifies AI vendors serving regulated financial institutions as material service providers, requiring service-provider registers, continuity planning, and resilience testing. Key 2026 distinction: storage residency vs processing residency — the LLM layer often sits offshore even when the database doesn't.
💡 Strategic take: ⚠️ The "processing residency" gap is where most Australian AI deployments quietly fail compliance — data stored onshore, inference offshore. Anyone selling AI into AU financial services needs an onshore-inference answer; anyone buying should be asking for one.

**Structural assurance over contractual promises — the three-level LLM data model** — LinkedIn (see Social Intelligence)
A cyber risk practitioner's framing gaining traction: Level 1 default vendor retention, Level 2 Zero-Data-Retention contracts, Level 3 structural assurance (private routing, gateway redaction, confidential compute).
💡 Strategic take: "Contracts promise, architecture proves" is exactly the right frame for PII-heavy businesses. ZDR clauses are floors, not ceilings — the only assurance tier that survives a vendor's bad day is the one where sensitive data never leaves your perimeter.

That's harder to navigate, not easier. The businesses that treat the December ADM deadline as their forcing function will be ahead of the pack.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The week's stories all point at one structural shift: **assurance is migrating from contracts to architecture**. The Fable 5 export-control episode proved vendor promises are subordinate to government directives; Meta's pivot to paid APIs proved "open" access can be repriced; and APRA's CPS 230 processing-residency distinction proved regulators now look past where data is stored to where it is computed. Meanwhile the compliant alternative got dramatically better — MIT-licensed models near frontier capability, 90% faster local inference, sovereign European models arriving this month. For a business with PII obligations, the strategic read is: stop negotiating better promises from cloud vendors and start building the architecture where the promises don't matter. The December ADM transparency deadline is the natural forcing function — you can't disclose what you haven't mapped, and you can't map what you don't control.
