**Belitsoft enterprise survey (recent)** — Enterprises run an average of 12 AI agents; half operate autonomously. Developers use AI for ~60% of their work but can fully hand off only 0–20% of tasks. The human oversight layer isn't going away — it's becoming the high-value layer.

## 🎙️ Podcasts & Newsletters

**AI Daily Brief — "The AI Engineering Skills Map for Knowledge Workers"** (Aug 18, 2026, NLW)
→ https://aidailybrief.beehiiv.com/p/the-ai-engineering-skills-map-for-knowledge-workers

The anchor episode for this week. Inspired by Andrew Ng's map for software developers, NLW builds the version for everyone else. Five skills on top of a foundation of domain judgment:

1. **AI Capability Mapping** — knowing the shape of the jagged frontier for *your* work. Which tasks AI nails, which it fumbles, how to help it where it's weak. Nobody hands you this; it's discovered through trial and error in your specific function.
2. **Context and Harness Management** — setting the AI up to succeed before it starts. Past performance data, analytics, customer feedback, instructions, tool access, memory. Best practices here will look different in six months.
3. **Problem and Product Prototyping** — not becoming engineers, but using code to do the job. A marketer building a cross-channel analytics dashboard wired to live APIs instead of dumping numbers into Sheets and decks. Domain judgment is still the translation layer; the freed time goes into judgment work.
4. **New Opportunity Identification** — not "how can AI help" but "what's now possible." Imagine someone handed you a team of engineers and said do whatever you want. The first answers automate existing work; the interesting answers are new products and capabilities that didn't exist before.
5. **Rapid New Skill Acquisition** — the meta-skill. Recognising which new capabilities have suddenly become valuable, creating space to experiment in real environments, assessing whether to integrate the output. Continuous, not a semi-regular seminar.

**The Apprenticeship Question** (new, from this episode): If experienced workers use AI to do what juniors used to do, the pipeline for developing domain judgment breaks. NLW flags this as unsolved and suggests the answer may involve AI as *multiplayer* — moving the unit of AI use from the individual to the small team.

**Ethan Mollick — "One Useful Thing" (recent)**
— "Real AI Agents and Real Work": the era of AI as chatbot is ending; what's arriving is AI you assign work to, wait on, and review. The skill is assigning well, not prompting well.
— "The Shape of the Thing": the shape of knowledge work is changing faster than our mental models of what "a job" is.

## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**Knowledge Worker AI Engineer** (emerging title) — Not a job for the IT department. Andrew Ng's skills map, reinterpreted for knowledge workers by NLW, describes a role that every function is growing internally: the person who can capability-map, build harnesses, prototype solutions, and spot opportunities. No clean job title yet, but this is the "maker for your function" archetype made concrete.
→ *Architecture-of-work map: Prototyper + Scout + Builder, all in one.*

**Claude Skills Seller / AI Workflow Packager** (practitioner-led) — A micro-economy forming around people who package their daily Claude workflows as sellable products. Nate Herk's video shows the template: one workflow you already use → product listing → Stripe checkout → $200–$1000 per sale. Not a traditional job title, but a new income archetype.
→ *Architecture-of-work map: Builder + Grower + Evangelist.*

**Agent Integrator** (enterprise role) — Belitsoft's enterprise data (avg 12 agents per company) implies a new coordination role: the person who decides which agents get which tasks, owns the harness configuration, and monitors the autonomous half. Sometimes called "Agent Orchestrator" or "AI Ops."
→ *Architecture-of-work map: Orchestrator + Risk Steward.*

**Graph Engineer** (technical, emerging) — AIDB flagged "What the Heck Is Graph Engineering" (Aug 10) as a nascent technical discipline for wiring agent networks together with structured knowledge representations. Still early, but this is the plumbing layer of the multi-agent era.
→ *Architecture-of-work map: Builder + Conductor.*

**The Deputisation Auditor** (self-directed) — From AIDB Aug 14: "How to Decide What Work AI Should Do for You: The AI Deputization Audit." The name is clunky but the role is real — systematically deciding which tasks to hand to agents and under what oversight conditions. This is the Risk Steward archetype operationalised.
→ *Architecture-of-work map: Risk Steward.*

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

Both Altman and Amodei have walked back earlier predictions of catastrophic near-term job displacement — timed, critics note, around impending IPOs. A16z called the apocalypse framing "unhelpful marketing, bad economics and worse history." The data, as of now, supports the patient camp: Yale Budget Lab's tracker through March 2026 shows stable employment share across high-AI-exposure and low-AI-exposure occupational groups; June 2026 nonfarm payrolls added 57K jobs, unemployment held at 4.2%. High-AI-adoption firms grew headcount 10% over two years, with 12% growth at entry level — slightly ahead of low-adoption firms that stayed flat. The debate isn't settled, but the evidence-to-date favours disruption-by-transformation over disruption-by-elimination.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The AIDB episode from yesterday is the sharpest single piece of content this thread has produced in weeks: NLW's five-skill map takes the abstract "become the maker for your function" move and turns it into a curriculum anyone can execute. The Apprenticeship Question it closes on is the most important open problem in the field — domain judgment is the one thing AI can't yet develop in the next person, and the pipeline for growing it is cracking. The community's response to all of this (Nate Herk packaging workflows for sale, Matt Wolfe documenting the agent race, practitioners debating which coding agent to trust) is that the adaptation is happening ground-up, function by function, not top-down from a re-skilling programme. The week's dominant theme: **the skills map exists, the tools are here, and the gap between people doing it and people waiting to be told to do it is widening fast.**
