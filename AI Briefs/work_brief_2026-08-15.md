## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**Forward-Deployed Engineer (FDE)** — The week's biggest structural signal. AWS announced a $1 billion investment to build an entire organization around this single job title; Microsoft launched Microsoft Frontier Company ($2.5B, ~6,000 FDEs) days later. FDEs embed inside customer organizations to build and ship AI systems in days, not months. The role borrows from military deployment language (Palantir coined it) — the "forward" is the customer site.
→ *Architecture-of-work lens:* FDE is a compound archetype — Builder + Evangelist + Risk Steward, all deployed externally. The $3.5B combined bet from two major platforms suggests this is the dominant commercialization model for AI deployment in enterprise through at least 2027.

**Algorithm Auditor** — Now among the fastest-growing AI ethics roles. Procurement mandates (especially under EU AI Act, high-risk obligations in effect August 2026) require bias testing and governance documentation as a purchasing condition. The auditor is not a watchdog; they are a quality-control function that makes the sale possible.
→ *Architecture-of-work lens:* Risk Steward, institutionalized. The EU timeline is creating a hard market-entry requirement that generates demand whether or not firms believe in the role philosophically.

**AI FinOps** — Emerging as the cost-management complement to ML engineering. As compute costs and per-task pricing become material line items (40¢/task on the Artificial Analysis Gemini 3.7 Flash run, per today's AIDB), organizations need people who can optimize AI spend the way cloud FinOps optimized AWS bills in 2018–2020.
→ *Architecture-of-work lens:* Maintainer + Sweeper hybrid, focused on the economics of running an agent fleet rather than its outputs.

**AI Deputization Auditor** — NLW's episode introduces the concept without naming a job title. But the "8/10 score" framework and the structured rubric it implies suggests this will crystallize into a role: someone who runs the recurring audit of which work has been delegated, whether the handoff is still appropriate, and whether human oversight has quietly atrophied.
→ *Architecture-of-work lens:* Risk Steward at the workflow level, not the model level. This is distinct from the Algorithm Auditor (who audits the model's outputs) — the Deputization Auditor audits the *decision* to delegate.

**AI-Success Orchestrator** — Emerging from customer success functions as tier-one troubleshooting automates away. Reported compensation ~$190K+. The role is about outcomes with AI systems, not configuration — closer to account management than engineering.
→ *Architecture-of-work lens:* Grower + Conductor hybrid, facing outward toward customers.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

Yale Budget Lab finds no meaningful change in unemployment for AI-exposed roles since ChatGPT launched. The NBER working paper concurs. A16z has called the "job apocalypse" framing "unhelpful marketing, bad economics and worse history." Both Altman and Amodei have walked back their strongest predictions. The honest complication: global tech layoffs hit 115,000 by May 2026 (nearly matching all of 2025), with Meta, Amazon, and Snap citing AI as a driver — and entry-level tech hiring at top firms is down 30–50% vs. 2023. The macro numbers and the micro experience are pointing in different directions. The aggregate may be fine; the first rung of the ladder is genuinely narrowing.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The community's center of gravity this week is the *handoff decision* — not whether AI will eventually do most work, but how a practitioner decides, right now, which specific tasks to deputize and to what standard. NLW's deputization audit, GrokBot's teach-a-task, Mollick's "theory of mind" frame, and Allie Miller's 34-agent workforce are all answering the same question from different angles: the bottleneck has moved from capability to context and trust. That's the architecture-of-work shift in one sentence. The roles emerging this week — FDE, Deputization Auditor, AI FinOps — are the institutional answers to that bottleneck, at scale, with money behind them.
