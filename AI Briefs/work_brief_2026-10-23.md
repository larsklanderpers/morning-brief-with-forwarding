## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**The redesigned first rung: junior engineers with sales and product rotations**
Source: Harvard Business School Working Knowledge | [Link](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/ai-hasnt-spurred-widespread-layoffs-yet-why-not)
In a Harvard Gazette piece republished by HBS on 20 Oct, Joseph Fuller describes an unnamed large tech company that, rather than stop hiring junior software engineers, rewrote the role so new hires still write code but also get structured exposure to sales and product management, the gaps it found in its senior engineers' reviews. Fuller says the entry-level job may now look like a second or third job did five years ago.
💡 Constructive read: This is the apprenticeship being redesigned rather than abolished: the first rung is now wider, not missing. For anyone starting out, the move is to ask for the rotation before it is offered, because the Builder who can also sell and scope is the one the archetype map says will be in demand.

**The outsider operator: non-experts running a science project through agents**
Source: Interesting Engineering | [Link](https://interestingengineering.com/ai-robotics/claude-scorpion-toxin-protein-binders)
Secondary coverage of an Anthropic announcement dated 20 Oct says Citrini Research, a financial research firm, worked with Anthropic's life sciences team as non-experts to see whether Claude could design a drug candidate. Reported figures: about 318,000 designs in 64 hours, 211 sent to labs, and 17 that met the binding criteria. The binders have not been tested for neutralising the toxins, lab turnaround still took about two months, and the primary Anthropic post was not read. Anthropic is the vendor, so this is its own showcase.
💡 Constructive read: The Conductor archetype in its purest form: the operator did not know protein chemistry, they knew how to set a goal, fan out the work and send the shortlist to people with pipettes. The bottleneck that remains is the wet lab, which is a reminder that the physical check is still where the human job lives.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

**Goldman: AI could displace about 9% of workers, but the cuts likely wait for a recession**
Source: Fortune | [Link](https://fortune.com/2026/10/22/goldman-sachs-forecast-how-many-jobs-lost-to-ai-9-percent/)
Goldman economist Joseph Briggs wrote in a 22 Oct note, as reported by Fortune, that under Goldman's assumption of a 15% AI productivity lift, up to 9% of US workers could be displaced during the transition, with slower hiring weighing on employment by another 3%. His larger finding is timing: historically, automation layoffs cluster in downturns, and job growth in the most AI-exposed industries has run about 10,000 below trend over the past three months. He also says only 23% of US establishments have built AI into regular workflows. These are forecasts, not measured outcomes, and the note itself was not read. Harvard's Doug Elmendorf, in the 20 Oct HBS piece, makes a similar case for patience: today's labour data says little about the next five to fifteen years.
💡 Constructive read: A forecast that says "the storm comes with the next recession" is a gift of lead time, the way a weather service that names the week lets you board up before it hits. The practical response is to do the reskilling while the labour market is still tight enough to move jobs on your own terms.

## 📌 Today's Signal

Today's thread is about who gets to do expert work once the expertise can be rented. A financial research firm ran a protein-design project, Mollick reports a model out-reviewing human referees, and YC spent an episode on the one thing generation cannot supply, which is taste. Read through the architecture-of-work lens, the growth is in the Conductor who frames and fans out the work and the Editor who decides what is good enough to ship. Harvard's Fuller shows what that means at the bottom of the ladder: a junior role that is wider, not gone. Goldman's note is the honest counterweight, saying the real test comes in the next downturn, which makes this the cheap time to learn the conductor's and editor's skills rather than the expensive one.
