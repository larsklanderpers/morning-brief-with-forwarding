## 🧑‍💻 Practitioner Ground Truth (Reddit)
*Reddit API access blocked in sandbox today. No hot-post data from r/singularity, r/artificial, r/ExperiencedDevs, or r/cscareerquestions.*

Web signal as proxy for practitioner sentiment: The dominant question in hiring forums has shifted from "will AI take my job?" to "what skills do I need?" — a sign the anxiety phase is giving way to adaptation. The most-cited gaps: **specification precision** (writing instructions that produce consistent AI output) and **AI literacy combined with domain expertise** — not model-building, but knowing which workflow to hand off and how to verify the result.

IBM's 2026 move is worth tracking: tripling entry-level intake but rewriting job descriptions to *de-emphasize* tasks that AI can handle, and prioritizing client engagement, complex problem-solving, and collaborative project management. The entry-level rung isn't gone — it's being redesigned in real time.

## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

The hiring data is getting specific enough to map directly to the architecture-of-work lens:

| Role | Growth Signal | Archetype Fit | Salary Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Forward Deployed Engineer** | 729% YoY growth (Indeed); 643 postings → 5,330 in one year | Orchestrator / Conductor — sits at the boundary between AI platform and enterprise ops | $170–200K+ |
| **AI FinOps Specialist** | Fastest-growing AI finance role | Risk Steward — reads cost curves, decides when agent runs beat human runs | Emerging |
| **Algorithm Auditor** | Described as "business necessity, not optional safeguard" | Risk Steward / Editor — QA for AI decisions, fairness, compliance | Emerging |
| **AI Agent Architect** | Gartner: 40% of enterprise apps embed task-specific agents by end of 2026 | Conductor — designs multi-agent systems coordinating across business functions | Senior IC range |
| **AI Agent Workflow Designer** | Named in hiring guides; distinct from engineer or PM | Orchestrator — doesn't manage people, manages the logic and permissions of digital agents | Emerging |

**Context:** AI has added >1.3 million new roles total (AI Engineer, Forward-Deployed Engineer, Data Annotator, plus ~600K AI-enabled data center jobs). Workers with AI skills earning 28–56% wage premium vs peers. "New-collar" hiring rising: up to 60% of new roles may not require a traditional degree by 2030 — experience and demonstrable output over credentials.

**The most underrated skill surfacing across hiring data:** *specification precision* — the ability to write instructions that produce consistent, correct AI output at scale. Not prompt engineering as a title. The craft of giving a clear enough brief that an agent can execute it without handholding.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

The big reversal of May 2026 is still the backdrop: both Altman and Amodei walked back their "AI jobs apocalypse" warnings (Amodei's 2025 "50% of entry-level white-collar jobs in 5 years / 20% unemployment" prediction; Altman's "entire categories of entry-level knowledge work at risk"). Both are now saying AI expands work rather than eliminates it. Yale Budget Lab (May 2026): no meaningful change in unemployment through March 2026 for workers in high-AI-exposure jobs. The cynical read: both companies filed IPO paperwork in May 2026, and a calmer jobs narrative is better for a public listing. The more generous read: the data actually doesn't show mass displacement yet, and they're updating on evidence. Probably both.

The honest version for any piece: the displacement debate is real, the labor data through Q1 2026 doesn't show it yet, and the incentives of those making pronouncements are worth naming.
