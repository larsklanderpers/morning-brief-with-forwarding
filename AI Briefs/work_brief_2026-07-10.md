## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

The market keeps minting titles for the archetypes NLW sketched — and the volume is now hard to wave off:

- **The numbers got serious.** Agentic-AI job postings are up ~280% year-over-year, roughly 90K openings in 2026, averaging ~$190K. This isn't a thought-leadership slide anymore; it's a hiring req.
- **Forward-Deployed Engineer (FDE)** → **Prototyper + Evangelist** fused. Embeds in a customer to build and tune agentic workflows on their stack; listings up 800%+ in 2025, with Anthropic scaling its applied team ~5x and OpenAI hiring thousands into enterprise-facing roles.
- **Agent Orchestrator** → the **Orchestrator/Conductor** archetype as a paycheck. Designs multi-agent systems that chain tools and actions — the engineering core of agentic ops, support, and commerce.
- **The quiet one: the Grower.** Today's junior-hiring debate is really a fight over who staffs the *Grower* archetype — the person who builds the human pipeline that AI can't. The firms defending entry-level hiring are betting the orchestrators of 2029 have to come from somewhere.

The signal: the outward-facing archetypes (orchestrator, evangelist, steward) are still where the money's moving, but the community is starting to defend the *inward* ones too — grower and maintainer — as the deliberately-protected soil the rest grows from.

## 📌 Today's Signal

Two threads that look opposite are actually the same lesson. The devs warning that "nobody can explain what they built anymore" and the ones insisting "you can't stop hiring juniors" are both defending the *human* half of the architecture of work — the judgment that inspects the machine's output, and the pipeline that grows the people who'll someday orchestrate it. Stack that against Mollick's "specs, not tricks" and today's read is unusually concrete: the agent era doesn't reward the fastest typist or the cleverest prompter — it rewards the manager. The one who can define the job, judge the result, and grow the next person who can. That's not a role you wait to be assigned; it's a posture you adopt this week. Pick the workflow you own, rebuild it around a small team of agents, keep your ability to explain it sharp, and you've crossed from the half of the workforce that's waiting to the half that's compounding.
