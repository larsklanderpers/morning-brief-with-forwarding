## 🔒 Privacy, PII & Data Sovereignty

**Australia: Privacy Act automated decision-making requirements now confirmed for 10 December 2026**
Sources: FlowWorks / PADISO | https://flowworks.com.au/blog/ai-privacy-act-2026-changes
https://www.padiso.co/blog/australian-privacy-act-2026-reform-ai-implications/

New APP 1.7, 1.8, and 1.9 obligations take effect 10 December 2026. Covered entities must disclose in their privacy policies: which types of personal information are used in automated decisions, the nature of decisions made by computer programs, and where decisions could significantly affect individual rights or interests. The OAIC is currently conducting its first-ever compliance sweep across 60 organisations.

⚠️ This directly affects any Australian business using AI for hiring, lending, insurance underwriting, customer analytics, or credit decisions.

💡 Strategic take: Four months to December 10. If your AI pipeline touches customer decisions and your privacy policy doesn't currently describe the automated decision logic, you're already behind. The OAIC's compliance sweep is active now, not post-deadline — and the penalty regime runs to AUD 50M or 30% of adjusted turnover. The disclosure obligation is actually simpler than it sounds: name the types of decisions, name the types of data, and say where significant rights impacts could occur. But it needs to be in the privacy policy, not in a technical document nobody reads.

**Australia data residency: "Australian hosting" claims often route inference offshore**
Source: Lorikeet / Amaze | https://www.lorikeetcx.ai/articles/ai-support-australian-data-residency-guide

A pattern is emerging: vendors claim "Australian data residency" but route inference through Singapore or US regions during peak loads. For APRA-regulated entities, CPS 234 (information security) and CPS 230 (operational risk) extend obligations to material service providers, including AI vendors. The distinction between *where data rests at rest* and *where inference actually runs* is where most due-diligence gaps sit.

⚠️ Flag: if your AI vendor's contract says "Australian hosting" but doesn't explicitly constrain inference geography, the data sovereignty claim is incomplete.

💡 Strategic take: "Australian hosting" is a marketing claim until it's a contractual guarantee with specific carve-outs for inference routing, model updates, and log storage. For businesses under CPS 230's operational risk framework, the AI vendor audit should include a specific question about inference path under load. This is a category of risk that didn't exist two years ago and isn't yet consistently covered in standard vendor due-diligence frameworks.

## 🌏 AU/EU AI Ecosystem

**Australia PM announces legislation for AI standards; National Cabinet review in August**
Sources: IAPP / Inspirepreneur | https://iapp.org/news/a/australia-unveils-ai-policy-roadmap
https://inspirepreneurmagazine.com/technology/ai-regulation-australia-2026/

On 15 July, PM Albanese announced plans to legislate the Australian Standards for AI and established an Office of AI within the Department of Prime Minister and Cabinet. National Cabinet is expected to consider the approach this month, with standards targeted for legislation in early 2027. Australia does not yet have a standalone AI Act — governance is currently through Privacy Act amendments, the Consumer Law, and sectoral overlays (TGA for health AI; ASIC and APRA for finance AI).

💡 Strategic take: The National Cabinet timing matters: if the approach is agreed in August, the drafting body that writes the standards will begin work in Q3 2026. That's the window to engage — standards consultations are where the compliance detail gets set, not the legislation itself. The pattern in Australia is to legislate the framework and regulate the substance, so the standards body will be more consequential than the enabling Act.
