## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE):** Job postings up 1,165% YoY. OpenAI launched "The Deployment Company" — a large-scale initiative of FDEs embedding within enterprises. Total comp: $300K–$1.2M at the top. The role requires three skill buckets: core software engineering, applied AI (LLMs, agent orchestration, RAG), and customer-facing judgment. In 2026 the bar has shifted to agentic orchestration specifically.
→ *Architecture-of-work map: this is the Builder + Orchestrator archetype in its most fully-formed commercial shape.*

**AI Agent Operations Engineer:** The new role sitting at the intersection of MLOps and Cloud FinOps — managing token economics, model routing, caching strategy, and guardrails for production agent systems. The AI FinOps Specialist is its twin: focused on whether agent fleets are actually generating ROI.
→ *Architecture-of-work map: Sweeper + Risk Steward. The person who keeps the fleet running and audits what it costs.*

**AI Governance Manager / Responsible AI Lead / AI Ethicist:** All now appearing in UK and US hiring platforms as formal titles, none of which existed five years ago. EU AI Act is the forcing function for the compliance-facing roles.
→ *Architecture-of-work map: Risk Steward.*

**AI Engineer:** LinkedIn's #1 fastest-growing US job title in 2026. Postings up 143% YoY from 2025. Workers with AI skills commanding a 56% wage premium over peers (PwC).
→ *Architecture-of-work map: Builder.*

**WEF macro frame:** 170M new roles created globally by 2030, 92M displaced. Net positive, but the disruption is real in the transition window.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

Both Altman and Amodei have walked back their apocalyptic predictions from May 2025 (Amodei had warned of 10-20% unemployment from AI within "one to five years"). The Yale Budget Lab found no statistically significant changes in occupational mix or unemployment duration for high-AI-exposure jobs through March 2026 — the labor data doesn't support the headline claims. That said, tech-sector layoffs hit 115K+ through May 2026, with some companies citing AI as a driver (and Deutsche Bank warned of "AI redundancy washing" — using AI as cover for cuts with other causes). The aggregate is stable; the pockets of disruption are real, particularly at entry-level white-collar.
