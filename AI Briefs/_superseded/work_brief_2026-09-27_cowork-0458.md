## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**Forward-Deployed Engineer (FDE) — the fastest-growing AI role in the market**
224 open positions across 39 AI companies in a mid-2026 market scan. Total comp $300K–$1.2M. Anthropic describes FDEs as engineers who "work within customer systems to build production applications with Claude models," deliver MCP servers and agent skills, and travel 25–50% of the time.
*Architecture-of-work mapping:* FDE = **Builder + Orchestrator** in one body. They build what the customer can't, then hand off patterns for the org to replicate.
Source: https://hashnode.com/blog/a-complete-2026-guide-to-the-forward-deployed-engineer

**"Seniorization" of entry-level roles**
PwC/Revelio data: Roles requiring 10+ traditionally senior skills (judgment, people management, empathy) grew 35% between 2019–2025 while non-seniorized equivalents in the same categories fell 10%. New tasks added to AI-exposed roles are 2.5× more likely to require empathy, judgment, and creativity than before.
*Architecture-of-work mapping:* The market is collapsing the **Scout → Editor → Conductor** progression — you arrive already expected to do all three.

**AI Orchestrator & Agent Architect**
Gartner projects 40% of enterprise applications will include task-specific AI agents by end of 2026 (up from <5% in 2025). The gap between pilot and production is where Agent Architects work: multi-agent orchestration, tool-use design, safety guardrails, human-in-the-loop checkpoints.
Source: https://dev.to/sidonnie_hinton_042e3478f/five-open-ai-agent-roles-that-show-where-the-work-is-moving-in-2026-5f8d

**Mollick's framing — org design as the new AI skill**
Integrating agents isn't a software project anymore; it's answering: what does this firm do vs. what does the agent network do? The people who can answer that coherently are the **Conductors** in NLW's archetype map.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

Both Dario Amodei and Sam Altman have walked back their AI jobs apocalypse predictions in 2026 — Amodei now says automation may actually expand the work people do; Altman called himself "pretty wrong" about economic impact. The macro labor data supports the revision: Yale Budget Lab finds no significant unemployment signal in high-AI-exposure occupations since Nov 2022. Aggregate BLS numbers are holding.

The real displacement signal is concentrated and structural: entry-level tech hiring at top firms is down 30–50% vs. 2023, and the Alliance for Secure AI's JobLoss.ai has documented 125K+ affected positions. The jobs aren't disappearing — the ramp into them is getting steeper, and the people who can't climb it faster are being left at the base.

## 📌 Today's Signal

Sunday quiet, but the week's evidence points one direction: the organization chart is not being flattened by AI — it's being **inverted at the bottom and extended at the top**. Entry-level roles are acquiring senior-skill requirements before anyone gets hired; new roles like FDE pay partner-track money on day one; and the org design question — who decides what the agents decide — is becoming the highest-leverage thing a person or team can own. The architecture-of-work framework (Prototyper through Risk Steward) isn't a futures exercise anymore; it's a vocabulary for job descriptions being written right now. The people learning that vocabulary, and mapping themselves onto it, have a head start measured in months, not years.
