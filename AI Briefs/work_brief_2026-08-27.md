## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE)** → **Builder / Orchestrator archetype**
The fastest-growing named AI role in 2026. 224 open positions across 39 AI companies; comp $300K–$1.2M; postings grew 19x YoY. The FDE is embedded inside customer organisations to scope, build, and deploy AI systems end-to-end. Required skills have shifted from RAG and fine-tuning to agentic orchestration (LangGraph, CrewAI), evaluation frameworks, and AI observability. EY's UK launch confirms it has crossed from startup-native to enterprise mainstream.

**Agent Orchestration Designer** → **Orchestrator / Conductor archetype**
Appearing in open job listings at hyperscalers and large enterprises. Designs and deploys multi-agent systems — defines goals, sets guardrails, monitors performance, ensures inter-agent coherence. Different from the generalist "AI engineer" in that the output is a *running system*, not a model. Compensation tracking toward $250–$350K at large enterprises.

**AI FinOps Specialist** → **Risk Steward archetype**
Emerging as a standalone function as inference costs scale. The role asks whether the AI system is making money or consuming resources — cost-per-accepted-task, not cost-per-token, is the unit of measure. Still early (few formal job postings), but showing up in enterprise org charts alongside the CFO function.

**"Frontier Professional"** (Microsoft WTI frame) → **Conductor / Scout archetype**
Not a job title, a designation. The 20% of workers who have redesigned their workflows around agent output, not just bolted AI onto existing workflows. The 8.3X productivity gap runs between Frontier Professionals and everyone else. This week, OpenAI's data confirms the same divide. Worth watching: whether this becomes a formal hiring criteria ("must have frontier AI workflow experience") in the next two quarters.

**AI Job Title Proliferation note:** In a single week in mid-2026, open roles appeared for 40+ different AI-related titles: AI Engineer, Applied AI Engineer, Generative AI Engineer, Agent Engineer, Agentic AI Engineer, RAG Engineer, AI Systems Engineer, Forward-Deployed Engineer, and more. The proliferation reflects an industry still searching for which archetypes to formalise. The Cherny/NLW map is still the most coherent attempt to cut through the naming chaos.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

**Altman and Amodei walking back the apocalypse.** Both CEOs have softened earlier catastrophic job-loss predictions as of mid-2026. Altman (June 2026): companies that have adopted AI most deeply are among those hiring the most; some firms citing AI for layoffs may be using it as a convenient cover. Amodei, who previously warned of a 50% wipe-out of entry-level white-collar jobs, has moderated publicly ahead of Anthropic's IPO.

**What the labor data actually shows (so far).** Yale Budget Lab: "No major macroeconomic effects" from AI on employment through early 2026. BLS: each 10-percentage-point increase in AI exposure maps to roughly a 0.6-point reduction in projected employment growth — measurable, not catastrophic. The clear exception: **Stanford "Canaries in the Coal Mine" (August 2026)** finds young workers in high-AI-exposure occupations are 19% below their expected employment trajectory versus less-exposed peers. The entry-level gap is real and widening, even if the aggregate numbers don't show it yet. Also: tech and finance sectors — fastest AI adopters — are losing approximately 28,000 jobs per month per Bloomberg / BLS data.

**Bottom line of the data:** Not an apocalypse. Not nothing. A selective pressure, strongest at entry level and in the most AI-exposed cognitive roles. Workers with AI fluency in those same roles are, per the data, doing better than their less-fluent peers — not just surviving, gaining scope.
