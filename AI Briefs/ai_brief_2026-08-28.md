## 🔒 Privacy, PII & Data Sovereignty

**⚠️ Australia Privacy Act — Dec 10 deadline: automated decision-making transparency**
From 10 December 2026, amended Privacy Act obligations for automated decision-making come into force. Scope: AI used in hiring, lending, insurance, and customer analytics — precisely the use cases Lars's regulated-industry audience is already running. OAIC has published two guidance documents on AI and the Australian Privacy Principles (AI model development + commercially available tools).
💡 *Strategic take:* Organisations that haven't audited their AI decision pipelines for automated decision-making coverage have fewer than 15 weeks. Any AI touching lending, insurance, or employment decisions needs provenance documentation now — not after December.
Sources: [Landers](https://landers.com.au/legal-insights-news/australian-privacy-law-update-what-app-entities-need-to-know-in-2026) | [FlowWorks](https://flowworks.com.au/blog/ai-privacy-act-2026-changes) | [Gilbert + Tobin](https://www.gtlaw.com.au/insights/oaic-ai-guidance-regulating-ai-to-maintain-privacy)

💡 *Strategic take:* The double-fine exposure (GDPR + EU AI Act simultaneously) is now live for EU-market operators. **APRA CPS 230/CPS 234 — AI vendors now in scope**
For APRA-regulated entities, CPS 234 (information security) and CPS 230 (operational risk) now explicitly extend to AI vendors as material service providers. Data residency for AI is an architectural question: where do training data, inference compute, model weights, and operational logs physically sit? Many vendors claiming "Australian hosting" route inference through Singapore or US regions under load.
💡 *Strategic take:* The "Australian hosting" claim needs vendor verification against peak-load routing, not just default configuration. If your AI vendor can't answer where inference runs at peak capacity, that's a CPS 234 gap, not a vendor preference question.
Source: [Lorikeet](https://www.lorikeetcx.ai/articles/ai-support-australian-data-residency-guide) | [Ryderkai](https://ryderai.com.au/insights/sovereign-ai-australia-queensland-data-residency)

## 📌 Today's Signal

The dominant theme across every lane today is **who controls the AI**. Open-weight models have reached a quality tier where running inference on your own hardware is no longer a performance compromise — it's a governance choice. Australia's December 10 Privacy Act deadline and the EU AI Act's August 2 enforcement activation mean regulated industries are now facing real legal exposure, not theoretical future risk. The ASIC deepfake alert, the APRA vendor-scoping provisions, the EU's 78% non-compliance figure, and the surging on-premise inference adoption rate all point to the same pressure: cloud AI's default data flows are incompatible with the regulatory posture that financial services, health, and government are being held to. For a decision-maker with PII obligations, the strategic read is: the window to treat AI sovereignty as optional is closed. The question is no longer whether to control where your data goes — it's whether you have the architecture, vendor contracts, and governance documentation to prove you already do.
