## 🎥 What Creators Are Saying (YouTube)

Tracked channels: AIDB posted on Anthropic's public benefit fund (see Podcasts). Berman, Wolfe, AI Explained, YC and Ramonov had nothing new on work in the window; Herk's newest is a tools ranking. Detail below comes from video descriptions and chapter lists, not transcripts.

**"Claude Engineering Lead: Stop Using Claude Like It's 2024" - Silicon Valley Girl (20 Oct, about 53K views)**
Source: YouTube | [Link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D-SVFneJ_Nk)
Felix Rieseberg, who has led engineering for Claude.ai, Cowork and Claude Code Desktop at Anthropic, argues most people still hand AI tasks it has already outgrown. Chapters cover handing over the goal beyond the task, catching confident mistakes, how to stop being your company's bottleneck, whether Anthropic still hires junior engineers, and what to build and learn to stay valuable.
💡 Constructive read: Asking a frontier model to summarise your email is like hiring a senior consultant to staple your reports. The cheapest upgrade available to most knowledge workers this week is not a new tool, it is a bigger ask: give the agent the outcome you want and the reason you want it, then spend your own time checking the answer.

**"Let Your AI Agent Do ITS OWN Harness Engineering (Here's How)" - Cole Medin (20 Oct, about 15K views)**
Source: YouTube | [Link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TVhhmhvCovg)
Medin defines harness engineering as improving the rules, skills and memory around a model each time the agent gets something wrong, and demos an agent that learns from escalations and proposes changes to its own harness, with a human approving every lesson, plus versions and rollbacks. The video is a paid collaboration with LangChain and the demo runs on LangChain's own products.
💡 Constructive read: Strip out the sponsor and the pattern stands on its own: every mistake becomes a written rule, and a person signs off on what the agent is allowed to learn. That approval step is a real job, and it looks a lot more like a team lead running retros than like programming.

**"Anthropic Just Revealed What's Happening to Its Own Workers" - AI Copium (20 Oct, about 102K views)**
Source: YouTube | [Link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=X8Qwp_naMzg)
A reaction video on Anthropic's "How employment is changing at Anthropic" report (covered here 18 Oct). What moved is the reach and the framing: at 102K views in a day it is the most-watched work video in today's searches, and it ties the report to Anthropic's public benefit fund and a SpaceX share pledge by Elon Musk under the question "the beginning of UBI?".
💡 Constructive read: The report is now travelling as a story about income, not about tasks, which is the less useful half for anyone deciding what to learn. The task data still says the work is shifting toward specifying, reviewing and monitoring agents, and that is something you can start practising before any policy arrives.

## 🐦 The Conversation on X

Claude in Chrome was connected. The item below comes from Mollick's feed; it was not covered yesterday.

**@emollick (Ethan Mollick) - "Multiplayer AI needs to move past the group chat phase" (19 Oct)**
Source: X | [Link](https://x.com/emollick/status/2112289593286352976)
Mollick says shared AI chats are chaotic, everyone sees everything, which creates social and management problems, and they are bad for long projects; worse, they keep AI trapped inside existing teams when its real promise is working across them. In a follow-up he says better multiplayer options exist but need serious experiments with organisational form, not just adding AI to existing chat. About 47K views and 490 likes at time of check.
💡 Constructive read: Dropping an agent into the team channel is the 2026 version of putting the company intranet on a fax machine. The organisations that get the gain will be the ones willing to redraw who talks to whom, and the person who designs that wiring (what the agent sees, who it reports to, which teams it bridges) is doing the Orchestrator's job at the level of the org chart.

## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**The conjurer: Mollick's third way of working with AI**
Source: One Useful Thing | [Link](https://www.oneusefulthing.org/p/30-months-on-the-exponential-in-charts)
Alongside the centaur (work divided between human and AI) and the cyborg (work blended), Mollick adds the conjurer, who summons agents when needed, assigns the work and evaluates what comes back. He calls it the most powerful way to use AI and says it carries new risk because the person loses sight of exactly what the AI did to get the result.
💡 Constructive read: The conjurer is the Orchestrator archetype given a name normal people will remember, and the risk he flags is the job description of the Risk Steward. The two travel together: anyone who learns to summon agents should learn in the same week how to audit what they did.

**The harness engineer, or the agent's team lead**
Source: YouTube, Cole Medin | [Link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TVhhmhvCovg)
Medin and Rieseberg land on the same shape from different ends: the human's work is writing the rules, memory and goals the agent runs inside, then approving what it learns and catching its confident errors. Medin's version is sponsored by LangChain, so treat the tooling claims as a vendor demo.
💡 Constructive read: This is the Maintainer archetype moving up a level, maintaining the agent's instructions rather than the code. It rewards people who already write good checklists and runbooks, which is a much wider pool than people who write software.

**Forward-deployed engineer: now there is a number**
Source: FourWeekMBA | [Link](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-awss-1b-fde-push-meets-openais-4b-deployment-company/)
A secondary write-up of a CNBC report dated 15 Oct says US postings for the role rose from under 200 in 2023 to over 7,300 in the first nine months of 2026, citing Lightcast data, and notes postings are advertised roles, not hires. The same piece reports AWS, OpenAI and Anthropic programmes behind the role; those figures were not checked against CNBC.
💡 Constructive read: What moved since yesterday's FDE item is a growth curve, if it holds up against the original. A thirty-fold rise in postings is the market voting that domain knowledge plus a little building is worth paying for, which is the most hopeful entry point in this whole thread.

## 📌 Today's Signal

Today's thread converges on one move: ask for more, then check harder. Rieseberg says most people hand AI tasks it has outgrown, Mollick names the conjurer who summons agents and judges what comes back, and Medin shows the rules and memory around an agent becoming something a person curates. Read through the architecture-of-work lens, that is the Orchestrator and the Risk Steward arriving as one role, not two departments, and it is open to anyone who can describe a good outcome and spot a plausible wrong one. Meanwhile the public debate is drifting toward who owns the gains, which matters, but the lever an individual actually holds this week is the size of the job they are willing to hand over and the quality of the review they bring back.
