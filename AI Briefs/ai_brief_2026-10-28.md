The same post reports partner results: at Enveda, Claude reviewed 1,061 pages of manufacturing documentation, caught all 11 issues human reviewers found plus seven more, and cut review time by 89%. **Liquid AI releases LFM3: six open-weight 2B-class models across text, vision, audio and decision tasks, with llama.cpp and MLX support on day one**
Source: Liquid AI | [Link](https://www.liquid.ai/blog/lfm3)
Liquid's blog lists the release on 27 October. Per its announcement as relayed by the POND newsletter, the text model LFM3-1.7B fits in about 1 GB at 4-bit with a 256K-token context, is post-trained for agent harnesses, and every model can be fine-tuned on a single GPU; Liquid itself concedes larger models still win on knowledge and agentic coding. ## 🔒 Privacy, PII & Data Sovereignty

**⚠️ Check Point finds "Yoba" malware hiding a crypto miner behind genuine, signed ChatGPT and Claude installers on fake GitHub projects**
Source: AI Chat Daily | [Link](https://www.aichatdaily.com/ai-security/yoba-malware-hides-crypto-miner-behind-signed-chatgpt)
Check Point Research's 27 October report says ZIPs pair a real signed OpenAI or Anthropic Setup.exe with a malicious winmm.dll that Windows sideloads, and the fake repos ranked near the top of Google results. The observed payload was the XMRig miner, but the loader can deliver any executable or PowerShell script; no OpenAI or Anthropic compromise was found, and no victim counts were given.
💡 Strategic take: This is shadow AI with a supply-chain twist: the employee who searches for "Claude desktop" and installs whatever ranks first now hands an attacker a signed, trusted process. A valid signature vouches for one file, not the folder it shipped in. The control is boring and effective: approved AI clients via your software portal only, and no local admin for the people most likely to go looking.

**OAIC, APRA and the EU AI Office: no fresh primary in the last 24h; Australia's automated-decision disclosure duty starts in six weeks, on 10 December**
Source: OAIC | [Link](https://www.oaic.gov.au/news/media-centre/australians-more-concerned-about-privacy-as-trust-in-ai-languishes%2C-survey-finds)
From 10 December, APP 1.3 requires privacy policies to disclose where a computer program makes, or substantially and directly contributes to, decisions that significantly affect individuals. Secondary sources disagree on whether the OAIC's final guidance on "substantially and directly related" has landed; check the OAIC site before relying on any summary.
💡 Strategic take: Apple's assistants and every other embedded copilot widen the inventory you need for this. If a model drafts the letter that declines a customer, that is a decision you may have to disclose.

## 🌏 AU/EU AI Ecosystem

**ECB deadline Saturday: the 110 banks it directly supervises must submit plans against AI-enabled cyberattacks by 31 October**
Source: Euronews | [Link](https://www.euronews.com/2026/07/07/ecb-tells-europes-biggest-banks-to-prepare-for-ai-powered-cyber-threats)
Supervisory board chair Claudia Buch's July letter asked for immediate and longer-term measures, prioritising faster patching, AI-enabled monitoring, internet-facing systems and tighter oversight of third-party providers, including open-source components. Coverage cites Anthropic's Mythos as the trigger. This is secondary reporting of the letter; the ECB's own text was not opened in this run, and nothing yet shows what the ECB will do with the plans.
💡 Strategic take: A central bank has turned "AI threat model" from a slide into a dated deliverable, and third-party oversight is on the list. APRA already has a third-party regime in CPS 230, so Australian financial services firms have the hook a similar request would hang on; writing the plan now, while it is still voluntary, is cheap insurance.

**Australia: no fresh federal AI primary in the last 24h; the Joint Select Committee on AI's full report is still due 30 November**
Source: Corrs Chambers Westgarth | [Link](https://corrs.com.au/insights/five-decisions-that-will-define-australias-ai-future)
The operative instruments remain the Voluntary AI Safety Standard and National AI Centre guidance, neither binding, per Corrs; mandatory Australian Standards for AI are proposed, not legislated.
💡 Strategic take: Quiet weeks are when the useful work gets done. A firm that can show its own AI incident log and third-party register before 30 November will be writing submissions, not reacting to them.

## 📌 Today's Signal
Every story this morning is about the wrapper, not the model. Yoba wraps malware in a genuine signed installer; Apple wraps OpenAI inside Pages and opens a protocol door for outside agents; Meta shops for a containment layer around its consumer agents; the ECB wants banks' plans for the third parties wrapped around their estates by Saturday. The model in the middle is increasingly trustworthy and increasingly commoditised, as a 1 GB LFM3 doing real agent work shows. What decides whether customer PII stays where it should is everything around it: where the installer came from, which model a document assistant silently calls, what an agent can reach, and whether a regulator would accept your answer to "who else touches this?" For a decision-maker, the capability question is settling; the perimeter question is the one to staff.
