## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**The agentic job market got measured this week:** ~90,000 agentic-AI job postings in 2026, up 280% YoY, averaging $190K (jobsbyculture). Forbes/Box/McKinsey/LinkedIn converge on a list of ~20 new agentic roles. The standouts, mapped to the lens:

- **Forward-Deployed Engineer** — listings up 800%; OpenAI, Anthropic, Google all hiring. This is Evangelist + Builder fused: someone who lives at the boundary between the platform and the customer's messy reality. The most concrete new job title of the era.
- **Agent Orchestrator / Agent Supervisor** — designs and runs teams of agents: goals, guardrails, monitoring. Note the tension with our baseline thesis: the market is posting it as a *job*, but Mollick's data (a quarter of OpenAI running 4+ agents) says it's becoming a *skill*. Expect the title to dissolve into everyone's title — the way "computer operator" did.
- **AI FinOps** — Mollick's $251-for-14-hours anecdote is the seed of a discipline: when compute is labor, someone budgets the labor. Risk Steward with a spreadsheet.
- **"AI Co-Founder" as product category** (Gusto) — not a human role, but it creates one: the sole practitioner who runs a company of agents. The Conductor as a one-person firm.

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

The doom camp is retreating from its own headlines: Altman now says he was "pretty wrong" about entry-level wipeout; Amodei has reframed his 50%-of-white-collar warning as preparation, not prophecy (Fortune, May 26; Forbes, May 29). Yale Budget Lab still finds no unemployment break in AI-exposed occupations since ChatGPT. The honest asterisks: global tech layoffs crossed 115K by May, and entry-level tech hiring is down 30–50% versus 2023 — the transition is real, it's just concentrated at the entry ramp, not spread across the workforce.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The week's four model releases and today's community chatter point at the same conclusion from opposite ends: the models are now changing faster than any individual tool loyalty can survive, which means the durable skill is the layer above the tools — specs, orchestration, judgment. Mollick's twin posts are the intellectual anchor (management is the skill), the 280%-growth agentic job data is the market confirmation (someone will pay $190K for it), and Nate Herk's six-hour free course is the on-ramp (anyone can start Monday). For a reader trying to adapt: stop asking which model to learn and start writing specs for work you already understand — the Orchestrator seat is being filled right now by whoever sits in it first. This maps cleanly onto the architecture-of-work frame: the archetypes aren't future job titles, they're this quarter's time allocation.
