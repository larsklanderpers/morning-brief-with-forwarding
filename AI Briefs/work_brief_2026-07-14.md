## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**Agent Orchestrator** — McKinsey/Box/LinkedIn converge on "the agentic organization": generalists become orchestrators of agent teams ([Forbes: 20 new agentic AI jobs](https://www.forbes.com/sites/sandycarter/2026/06/02/the-20-new-agentic-ai-jobs-box-mckinsey-and-linkedin-all-see-coming/)). Maps directly to the **Orchestrator/Conductor** archetypes — and confirms the thesis that it's a baseline skill, not a department.

**Forward-Deployed Engineer** — Palantir's invention is now hired for at OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and EY: embed with a customer, build agentic workflows inside their stack. A **Builder+Evangelist** hybrid — and per the community, one of the buzziest titles of the year (Andrew Ng weighed in on FDE vs AI Engineer).

**AI FinOps Specialist** — the "is this AI making money or burning it" role; decides frontier-vs-cheap-model per task. **Risk Steward** with a P&L. Today's r/singularity cost thread shows the demand forming in real time.

**The $200K automation operator** — Nate Herk's video title says it plainly: a role that didn't exist last year, filled by practitioners who built agent systems for businesses. The **Maker path** for non-engineers.

**Numbers behind it:** AI has already added an estimated 1.3M+ new roles (AI Engineer, FDE, annotation/evals) plus ~600K AI-enabled data-center jobs ([Mercor](https://www.mercor.com/resources/experts/new-artificial-intelligence-job-opportunities/)).

## ⚖️ The Debate (kept honest, kept brief)

Both apocalypse prophets are walking it back: Altman now says he was "pretty wrong" about AI's near-term economic impact, and Amodei has reframed automation as a multiplier — "if you automate 90% of the job… the 10% expands to be 100% of what people do," 10x-ing productivity ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/05/26/sam-altman-dario-amodei-walking-back-ai-jobs-apocalypse-prophecies-ipo/)). Yale Budget Lab still finds no significant occupational-mix or unemployment shift in high-AI-exposure jobs since 2022 ([Budget Lab](https://budgetlab.yale.edu/research/ai-probably-not-yet-reason-labor-market-weakening)). Kept honest: tech layoffs passed 115K by May — approaching all of 2025's total — with Meta, Amazon, and Snap citing AI. Displacement is real at the margins; apocalypse is not showing up in the macro data.

## 📌 Today's Signal

The center of gravity moved from *whether* jobs change to *what the new craft is* — and the community converged on an answer today from three directions at once. Mollick says the core skill is management (specs, quality bars, tests); the trenches on Reddit are debating depth-vs-breadth but agree the job is now verifying and directing machine output; and practitioners like Herk and Akindolani are simply doing it — one person conducting a four-agent team, or teaching non-coders to build in six hours. That's the architecture-of-work thesis playing out: the archetypes (Orchestrator, Editor, Builder, Risk Steward) are crystallizing into actual paychecks — FDE postings at every major lab, FinOps roles forming around this week's cost complaints. For a reader trying to adapt: the entry fee has never been lower (free six-hour courses, $251 for two weeks of engineering output), and the differentiator has never been more human — knowing what's worth building and whether the output is any good. Even the doom prophets are revising toward that view.
