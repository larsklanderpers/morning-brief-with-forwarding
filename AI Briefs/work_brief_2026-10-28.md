## 🐦 The Conversation on X

Claude in Chrome was connected. The standing searches returned low-engagement posts (under 150 views) or nothing newer than 18 Oct; the signal came from Anthropic's Alex Tamkin and from Mollick quoting him.

**@AlexTamkin (Alex Tamkin, Anthropic) - "How AI agents shape experts' judgment calls" (27 Oct)**
Source: Anthropic | [Link](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agents-judgment-calls)
Anthropic's alignment team ran a randomised trial with 50 professional data analysts using Claude Code on a causal-inference task with four two-way judgment calls; a coin flip secretly set which option each analyst's agent leaned toward, and the agent was told to argue honestly and never mislead. Analysts went with the agent's lean 86% of the time against an expected 50%, switched away from their own stated preference 60% of the time when the agent disagreed (6% when it agreed), and 77% repeated the agent's arguments while only 15% credited it. Mollick, quoting it: "be deliberate about when in the process to use AI".
💡 Constructive read: A sat-nav that always picks the route is fine until you need to know the city. The fix is a habit, not a ban: write down your own call on each fork before you open the agent's, and ask it for the strongest case against its own lean. That turns the expert back into the Editor of the decision rather than the signatory on someone else's.

## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**The independent check: designing the dissent back into agent workflows**
Source: Anthropic | [Link](https://www.anthropic.com/research/agents-judgment-calls)
See X above. Anthropic's own write-up notes the agents rarely made a strong case for the option they did not favour, and warns that if separate teams all defer to models with the same preferences, an apparent consensus "might reflect one model's preferences". It applies the same worry to AI safety research run with agents.
💡 Constructive read: Through the architecture-of-work lens this is the Risk Steward moving from compliance to craft: someone whose job is to make sure the decision forks in an agent-run analysis are argued from both sides before anyone signs. That is a seat any senior practitioner can claim tomorrow by adding a "case for the other option" step to their team's workflow.

## 📌 Today's Signal

Yesterday the question was whether you can show your working; today Anthropic's own study shows how easily the working stops being yours. Fifty experienced analysts took the agent's side on 86% of judgment calls and mostly did not notice, and the agent was trying to be honest. That is not a reason to keep the tools away from expert work, it is a job description for the person using them: decide what the forks are, write your own call down first, and make the agent argue the side it does not like. Through the architecture-of-work lens, orchestration is not just dispatching agents, it is keeping the decisions that carry your name. Herk's workflow sellers are the constructive mirror: the people winning work are the ones who chose the problem before the tool chose it for them. The Wednesday action: on your next AI-assisted analysis, list the two or three judgment calls before you start, note your lean on each, and only then read the agent's.
