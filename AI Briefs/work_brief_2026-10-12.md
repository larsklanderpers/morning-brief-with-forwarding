**"5 Concepts to Know for Coding with AI Agents" - IBM Technology (12 Oct, about 1.2K views)**
Source: YouTube | [Link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hTAWHgQI4xg)
Cedric Clyburn walks through five working concepts for building with coding agents: spec-driven development, context engineering, loop engineering, sandboxing and AI security. The stated thesis is that developers "move from implementation to orchestration". Per the video description; IBM sells agent tooling and training, and the description links to both.
💡 Constructive read: Think of the switch from typing letters to dictating them: the job did not vanish, it moved up a level to deciding what the letter says. Four of the five concepts are about specifying and checking work, not producing it, and none of them requires a computer-science degree to start learning.

**"The AI Paradox: 5 Human Skills That Are More Valuable Than Ever" - Nate B Jones (11 Oct, about 24K views)**
Source: YouTube | [Link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=huWizSgmRf0)
Jones argues that as models get smarter, five human skills gain value rather than lose it: taste, agency, focus, resourcefulness and follow-through. He lays out how he would build each for 2027 and how to pick which shelved project deserves a second look. Per the video description and its linked post.
💡 Constructive read: When the oven gets cheap, the recipe and the decision to cook matter more. The list is notable for what is missing: not one of the five is a technical skill, which is the opportunity frame in plain words for anyone outside engineering.

## 🆕 New Roles & Archetypes Watch

**The corrections owner**
Source: X, Ethan Mollick (12 Oct 2026) | [Link](https://x.com/emollick/status/2109497942717632662)
Building on the post above: when a model flags errors as a by-product of other work, someone has to confirm each one, rank it and route it. This extends last week's verifier thread from maths to every field that runs on documents: audit, compliance, research, legal.
💡 Constructive read: This is the Sweeper and the Risk Steward in one seat. The model finds the cracks; a person decides which ones hold up the building, and that decision is where the judgement and the pay sit.

**The agent supervisor, under four names**
Source: AI Codex (May 2026) | [Link](https://www.aicodex.to/articles/agent-operator-job-market-2026)
A job-market guide finds the role surfacing as AI Agent Manager, AI Ops Manager, Agent Supervisor and Agent Operator: someone who oversees a portfolio of deployed agents, watches outputs for accuracy and odd behaviour, and runs evals, cost and change management. It claims operations, project management and HR backgrounds are preferred over pure technical ones. A secondary blog, not fresh, and its salary bands are one source's estimates, so not repeated here.
💡 Constructive read: Four names for one job is what a new profession looks like before the industry agrees on a business card. Orchestration as a baseline skill for everyone is the frame; this is the version of it that becomes a full-time role, and the door is open to people from operations, not just engineering.

## 📌 Today's Signal

A quiet Monday with one consistent thread: the work is moving from producing to deciding. IBM tells developers their job is shifting from implementation to orchestration; Nate B Jones lists five skills gaining value and none of them is technical; Mollick describes models flagging errors faster than anyone can confirm them, and frontier tools pointed at only three fields while the rest wait. Read together, last week's verifier story is generalising: wherever AI output piles up faster than people can judge it, a new seat opens for the person who sets the spec, owns the queue of flagged issues and decides what matters. The practical move this week is to pick one place in your own function where AI could be pointed and nobody has pointed it yet, and be the one who does.
